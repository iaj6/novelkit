# Panel Audit
*Auditor: skeptic agent, 2026-08-14. All factual claims checked against draft/01–08.md.*

---

## 1. IS THE PANEL TOO GENEROUS?

Yes, moderately. The score band of 7–8 across seven lenses with genuinely different priorities is suspicious, as flagged. The synthesis defends it by arguing the book is "genuinely good at a consistent level" — which is a reasonable defense only if the concrete errors found don't warrant score depression. They do.

The specific pressure point: **verisimilitude** gave 7/10 while finding five concrete errors: a geometric impossibility in the altitude sequence, a headcount contradiction on page one, a disputed year arithmetic, a frame-count labeling ambiguity, and a mule-weight phrasing that admits an impossible reading. A 7/10 — meaning "good, worth reading, recommended" — is hard to justify for a manuscript where a bowl on a cliff face is placed six hundred feet above the mountain's own summit. That isn't an editorial oversight in the same category as inconsistent chapter headers. It is a geographic impossibility in the terrain the entire expedition traverses.

The synthesis acknowledges the altitude problem in its fix list (item A4) and recommends raising Mariano's summit estimate. It does not acknowledge that this is a large fix: if the bowl floor is at 17,900 feet and the entrance at 18,200 feet, SM-7's summit must exceed 18,200 feet. Mariano's stated estimate of "perhaps eight hundred feet above" from the 16,500-foot ledge places the summit at 17,300 feet — an underestimate of at minimum 900 feet, probably over 1,000. For an experienced Andean arriero who has been on this mountain before (his March attempt reached 17,200 feet), a 1,000-foot error on a feature directly above him in clear conditions is not a rounding error. It is a world-building failure that requires more than changing one number; it requires deciding how tall SM-7 actually is and checking that all altitude references across five chapters are consistent with that decision.

None of the seven lenses names this consequence. Verisimilitude identifies the impossibility and scores it 7/10. The synthesis's fix list understates what fixing it actually requires.

The other generous pressure: all six "would finish" lenses finish with enthusiasm, and the one "reluctantly" lens (airport) kept reading because "the writing is too good to put down" — which is a compliment dressed as a reservation. The panel produced 35 weaknesses and zero votes to abandon the manuscript. That is a coherent result only if the weaknesses are all at the level of execution rather than conception. Most are. The altitude geometry is not.

## 2. GROUNDED CLAIMS VS. PADDING

### Grounded — verified against the manuscript

**The opening sentence is doing real work.** "He found none of this humiliating. He found it accurate." is in chapter 1, page 2. It does what all seven lenses claim: establishes Holroyd's epistemological register through grammar (not sentiment, not claim, just finding) in six words. The repetition of "found" converts emotional position to data-processing. This is a specific and checkable craft observation and it is correct.

**The gorge sequence is the book's best-sustained passage.** Chapters 1–2 verify every element praised: Mariano requests two hours to splice the frayed rope; Holroyd refuses; the fraying is shown at the anchor post ("Even from where Holroyd stood, eight feet away, the fraying was visible: the outer fibers worn through to bare strands, the inner core showing pale against the weathered brown of the working rope"); walking-load resonance is explained correctly and specifically; the mule falls; the crossing the next morning takes exactly two hours. The "Two hours. The same two hours he'd refused Mariano the day before." sentence closes the chapter without annotation. All five lenses that praise this are describing what is actually there.

**"He was aware of not writing it" is in chapter 2.** Chapter 2 contains both this sentence and "He was aware of standing and watching." Literary quotes the first; airport, authenticity, and comparative quote the second. Both are real, both are in the same chapter, both are doing what the respective reviewers claim. These are correctly attributed.

**Mariano's crampons reveal is structurally earned.** Chapter 4 confirms: he bought them "from a climbing club in Cusco — secondhand, scratched, the straps replaced with new leather — three months before Isobel came to him with the commission." He puts them on in chapter 5 "No announcement, no ceremony, no prior mention at any point in the expedition." The retroactive reframing of his qualifications, his March attempt, and his standing at the bowl center is real. The literary and airport lenses are right about this.

**"His attention given to it without remainder" is the best phrase.** Chapter 7, verified: Mariano standing at the bowl center while Holroyd and Riedel work. The accounting register inverted — Holroyd is always a man with remainder, Mariano is not — is a genuine structural observation and the phrase is real and exact.

**"He meant both sentences equally" is over-explanation.** Chapter 7 ends with Riedel's "Don't let them make it what it isn't. Neither will I." followed by "He meant both sentences equally." Authenticity is correct: the doubleness of Riedel's exit is legible without this sentence. It explains what the reader already processed. The synthesis recommends deleting it. That is the right call.

**The chapter header inconsistency is real.** Verified across all eight files: ch01 = "# The Bottom of the List" (no number), ch02 = "# Chapter 2 — Selva Alta" (Arabic + em-dash), ch03 = "# Chapter 3: Puna" (Arabic + colon), ch04 = "# The Snowline" (no number), ch05 = "# Chapter Five: The Storm" (spelled-out numeral + colon), ch06 = "# Rititambo" (no number), ch07 = "# What It Proves" (no number), ch08 = "# The Descent" (no number). Three formats across three numbered chapters; five chapters with no number. Production artifact, not formal choice. All seven lenses are right.

**The headcount contradiction in chapter 1 is real.** Same scene, same source: "He saw a German party of four cross the trailhead heading east" and then, in response to Holroyd's follow-up question, "Five men, no women." The rest of the book confirms four (ch02: "Four men was Riedel's party"; ch03: "Three of them. A Peruvian man with them"; ch05: three figures plus Riedel visible through binoculars). "Five men" is a drafting residue. Multiple lenses catch this. ✓

**Riedel enters chapter 7 and exits it.** He has been established through boot-prints, a campfire hollow, a cartridge case, and binocular sightings across four chapters. His exit line carries weight; literary is right that "it lands as a line; it doesn't quite land as a revelation about a person." The characterization base is thin relative to the moral weight his exit is asked to deliver.

### Padding — unfalsifiable or hedged into meaninglessness

**"Not something most literary fiction manages, even with the best intentions" (airport on Mariano).** Comparator undefined. What class of literary fiction? Recent? Anglophone? Expedition fiction specifically? This is not a claim, it is a superlative without a scale.

**"At the level of Robert Macfarlane's best landscape writing" (comparative on altitude prose).** Comparative uses this in a strength item for chapters 4–6. The altitude prose in chapters 4–6 is genuinely good — wind-packed crust that holds or doesn't, verglas creaking under front-points, shadows that fall "slightly wrong" at 17,000 feet. But "at the level of" a named author's "best" is a claim that would require reading Macfarlane against the passage and is doing no critical work here. It is a dust-jacket superlative placed inside a reviewer's notes.

**"Among the most carefully executed indigenous characters in recent expedition fiction" (implied by airport, literary).** The airport lens says Mariano's transformation from functional guide to moral center is "not something most literary fiction manages." The literary lens says he is "the most fully realised character in the novel." Neither makes a comparison to an actual other book with an actual other Andean or indigenous guide character. This is aspiration-as-evidence.

**"Holroyd is entirely constructed through the management of his field notebooks" (literary).** Plausible, but the literary lens doesn't account for what Holroyd is constructed through besides the notebooks: his posture toward Mariano's route advice, his management of the gorge disaster, his binocular watch of the German camp at 5:30 AM, his crouch at 16,000 feet checking his compass. The notebooks are a significant thread but "entirely" is not accurate. Holroyd is constructed through several overlapping behavioral patterns, not one object.

## 3. DID ANY REVIEWER FAIL TO READ THE WHOLE BOOK?

No lens shows the classic failure signatures — vagueness about late chapters, misattributed events, structural arithmetic that doesn't survive counting. All seven lenses cite specific details from chapters 6, 7, and 8 that require having been there: the sealed chamber, Riedel's exit line, the Leica in the Cusco hotel room, Mariano's departure south to "the woman who keeps the knowledge." The quotes are all verified exact.

However, there is one imprecision worth naming.

**The ordinary lens claims the India backstory is referenced "by the fifth allusion."** Checking the manuscript: the India/Assam material appears as — (1) Finch's cable in chapter 1: "Know your India situation"; (2) the November 1931 anchor and "seven years" in chapter 2; (3) "bad accounting" in chapter 8. There is also an implied reference when Holroyd notes he was the fourth name on Finch's list and the first man died. That gives at most four references, of which two are substantive. The ordinary lens says "by the fifth allusion it starts to feel like a withheld explanation" — the count is off by one or two, which is not a serious reading failure but is not careful counting either. Minor.

## 4. ARE ANY TWO REVIEWS SUSPICIOUSLY SIMILAR?

The most conspicuous pattern is the unanimous convergence on the opening sentence: all seven lenses quote "He found none of this humiliating. He found it accurate." from the first two pages. The synthesis presents this as evidence of genuine independent reading — seven different critical frameworks arriving at the same line. That argument works for the gorge sequence, where airport, propulsion, and ordinary quote different sentences from the same passage and ordinary quotes a sentence the others don't ("The walls were intact.") — that is genuine convergence.

For the opening sentence it is weaker. The sentence is on page 2, it is the most obvious candidate quote from the entire chapter, and any reader who read the first page would have it available. A reader who skimmed chapter 1 and read carefully from chapter 2 onward would still have it. Universal agreement on the easiest quote to find is not strong corroboration; it is what you'd expect even if some lenses read chapter 1 less carefully than the others. I am not saying any lens skimmed — I cannot establish that from the evidence — but I am saying the synthesis should not use the opening-sentence consensus as positive evidence of independence.

The gorge-sequence consensus IS genuine corroboration. Airport quotes "He was aware of standing and watching"; propulsion quotes "Two hours. The same two hours he'd refused Mariano the day before"; ordinary quotes the chapter-closing version of that sentence; authenticity quotes "Walking loads rope differently from standing." Four different lenses, same passage, four different sentences. That is reviewers reading the same chapter and finding different things in it. That is what independent reading looks like.

The frame-count finding is convergent but not suspicious: airport identifies the "frame fourteen" labeling as inconsistent with the three-shot east-wall description; verisimilitude identifies it independently from the frame-counter perspective ("Chapter 7 confirms 12 exposures... three approaches to the east wall are described before 'frame fourteen'"). The two analyses are different enough in their angle to be genuine rather than echoed.

## 5. THE MOST DAMAGING TRUE THING THE PANEL IS COLLECTIVELY UNDERWEIGHTING

**The verisimilitude reviewer's central "arithmetic inconsistency" may be the reviewer's own error — and the synthesis inherited it without checking.**

The verisimilitude lens identifies as a concrete error: "He'd been doing that arithmetic for seven years" when the expedition is August 1936 and Assam was November 1931 — that is approximately four years and nine months, not seven.

I checked the manuscript. The expedition year is **never stated anywhere in the text**. Not in chapter 1, not in the field notes in chapter 8, not in the cable from Urubamba. The verisimilitude reviewer derived 1936 from the Leica IIIa's introduction date (1935) as the earliest year the camera could appear. But this gives a floor, not the actual year.

The manuscript does contain a year anchor: "an office in Assam in November 1931... He'd been doing that arithmetic for seven years." If you take "seven years" as the author's stated fact rather than an error, the expedition is in 1938 — which is consistent with every piece of period equipment (Leica IIIa available from 1935, Rolleiflex available, Ahnenerbe active, Kodak Super-X panchromatic available from 1933), with Holroyd's 1934 survey, and with the Ahnenerbe's pseudo-archaeological activities, which were most aggressive in 1936–1939.

The synthesis accepted the 1936 assumption, listed the "seven years" as item A3 in the production artifacts fix list, and recommended changing "seven" to "five." That recommendation rests on an unverified assumption. Before anyone changes that number, someone needs to establish what year the manuscript intends. The answer may already be in the text and the answer may be 1938.

This is the one case in the panel where a reviewer's confidence in a claimed error was not warranted by the evidence, the synthesis amplified the error, and the fix list codifies it. It is the smallest of the "errors" in consequence, but it is the only one where the reviewer may be the one who got it wrong.

---

**A second thing the panel tiptoes around: the Arce contradiction.**

The literary reviewer notes that "Arce turned back at the rock step; he never entered the bowl." That is correct per chapter 6: "Arce, sensible as always, had turned back here" (the verglas step below the cliff passage). The literary reviewer uses this only to argue that the 360-year figure is off in a "different direction."

But there is a harder problem no lens names. Chapter 3 describes Isobel reading her father's marginalia in Arce's bound account: her father cross-referenced "each specific description in Arce — the approach to the sealed bowl, the passage through the rock, **the proportions of the interior, the style of the doorframes**." If Arce turned back at the rock step below the cliff passage and never entered, he could not have described interior proportions or doorframe styles from inside the bowl. Those details require entry.

Either Arce entered (contradicting chapter 6) or the "interior proportions and doorframe styles" in the marginalia are from oral traditions her father cross-referenced with Arce, not from Arce alone (which the ch03 prose does not clearly support). The text is genuinely ambiguous — it could be read as "descriptions of all the above that appear in Arce" or as "the things her father found, spanning Arce and oral tradition." But the natural reading of "each specific description in Arce — [list including interior proportions]" is that Arce documented those things.

This matters because Arce's account is the load-bearing historical document in the novel. Holroyd and Riedel are both working from it. Isobel's father spent thirty years correlating it against valley oral tradition. The sealed chamber's significance rests partly on Arce's having identified it from the outside without opening it. If Arce's documented knowledge of the interior is internally inconsistent — he turned back before entering but somehow described interior proportions — the foundation of the record-keeping argument is quietly compromised. No reviewer names this. The synthesis does not include it anywhere.

---

**Third underweighted problem: the gap-closing arithmetic.**

The ordinary reviewer flags this: "the gap between the Germans and Holroyd's party closes from nine days (at the puna herder, Chapter 3) to four to six hours (at the snowline campsite, Chapter 4) in roughly one day of travel — the storm explains some of this, but the math is silent on the rest."

I checked the text. The storm (chapter 5) occurs AFTER the campfire evidence (chapter 4) establishes that Riedel is four to six hours ahead. The storm cannot account for the gap closing before it hits. For the gap to go from nine days to four to six hours in approximately one to two days of walking above the puna, the German party must have been effectively stationary at the snowline for approximately seven days — resting, waiting for weather, or pinned. This is possible but nowhere stated. Chapter 4 shows only that Riedel's camp was made "this morning."

The synthesis does not include this in the fix list. The propulsion reviewer, who is specifically responsible for tracking this kind of narrative arithmetic, does not flag it — propulsion describes the gap-closing as "rigorously tracked" and praises the race engine. That is wrong. The gap from nine days to four to six hours in one or two days of travel is the one moment in the race-engine that is not tracked. Propulsion missed it.

---

**Summary on what the panel is collectively avoiding:**

The panel inherited the book's own framing — that Arce's account is a reliable historical anchor, that the timeline is coherent, that the race's arithmetic works — and tested none of it systematically. Verisimilitude came closest, caught real errors, but made an assumption about the expedition year that isn't in the text. The synthesis accepted that assumption and others without checking.

The single most damaging true thing about this manuscript that the panel is tiptoeing around is not a prose problem or a pacing problem. It is a structural question: **the historical document the entire novel depends on may be internally inconsistent about what Arce actually saw, and no one — neither the panel nor the synthesis — has noticed.** If Arce described interior proportions he could not have observed from below the rock step, then either the text quietly contradicts itself in its most load-bearing historical claim, or the text is genuinely ambiguous and requires a revision pass that decides what Arce actually documented, from where. That revision pass is not on the synthesis's fix list at all.

## WHERE THE SYNTHESIS IS WRONG

**On the "seven years" fix:** The synthesis lists this as production artifact A3 and recommends changing "seven" to "five." This is premature. The expedition year is not established in the manuscript. The "seven years" is the only explicit year-delta in the text and may be correct. Check the year first. If the text intends 1938, the number is right and the year is wrong only by a few months.

**On the altitude geometry fix:** The synthesis recommends raising Mariano's summit estimate "to at least 19,000 feet." This is correct as a direction but understates the work required. If SM-7 is above 19,000 feet, then Mariano's estimate from 16,500 feet changes from "eight hundred feet above" to "twenty-five hundred feet above." All altitude references across chapters 4, 5, and 8 need to be checked for internal consistency with a mountain of that height. The repair is not one number; it is a consistency pass across five chapters.

**On the ending:** The synthesis mediates between literary/authenticity (earned ambiguity) and propulsion/comparative (evasion) and recommends a middle path: Holroyd commits to the necessity of deciding without deciding. That is a sensible editorial solution. But the synthesis understates comparative's structural observation: Riedel's exit in chapter 7 is more resolved than anything in chapter 8. The antagonist is more closed than the protagonist. This asymmetry is not fixed by changing "He would know" to a more forward-leaning formulation — it is fixed by making the chapter 8 final movement Holroyd's, not Riedel's posthumous residue.

**On the gap-closing arithmetic:** Not in the fix list. Should be, even at low priority. The ordinary reviewer's instinct was correct and the propulsion reviewer's confidence was misplaced.

**On the score-spread defense:** The synthesis says the narrow spread "reflects [the book's quality] more than it reflects panel failure." This is unfalsifiable in the dismissive direction. The correct answer is: the spread is consistent with both explanations and we cannot distinguish them from the scores alone. The synthesis should have left the uncertainty on the table rather than resolving it in the book's favor.

## WHAT THE SYNTHESIS GETS RIGHT THAT DESERVES PRESERVING

The synthesis's structural diagnosis of the ending disagreement is correct and the recommended fix (commitment to necessity of deciding, not resolution of what is decided) is precisely targeted.

The call to not touch the gorge sequence, the opening sentence, "his attention given to it without remainder," or the sealed chamber decision is correct. These are the manuscript's load-bearing successes and they are correctly identified as such.

The identification of Riedel's thinness as connected to the missing Ahnenerbe ideological context is the synthesis's best structural insight and is not present in any single lens at that level of specificity. The recommended fix (one scene where Riedel makes a specific ideological claim, dramatizing his framework before his concession) is the right level of intervention.

The synthesis's willingness to disagree with individual lenses (e.g., siding with airport/propulsion/ordinary against literary on the two-hour symmetry) demonstrates more independence than a synthesis that simply averaged the panel would. That independence is earned in the cases where the evidence supports it.
