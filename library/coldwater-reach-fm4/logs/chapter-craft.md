
## 01-the-dock-and-after
**Ending mode:** turn
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Short declaratives (4-8 words) at the moment of decision: 'She turned back.' 'She went back inside.' These carry the full weight of the chapter's moral action.
- Professional vocabulary as interiority: Eira's registry of the coat ('not oilskin, not canvas, not any kind of watch coat') enacts her character knowledge while doing the work of alarm.
- Free indirect interior embedded mid-scene without tag: 'A cold reached her that wasn't the wind off the harbor mouth. It was in her hands, which had nothing wrong with them.' No attribution; the coldness and the self-reassurance land as physical fact.
- The gap-as-turn: the chapter's pivot is purely structural — the reader sees the tide log entry contain nothing about the dock. The gap does the work; no sentence glosses it.
- Owen's presence introduced through objects, not statement: the latch trick, the requisition book page 37 changeover, the chart table — the ghost of the post established through specific named facts, never named as grief.
**Recurring constructions:**
- `the harbor went on`
- `she did not write anything else`
- `nothing about the far dock`
**POV register:**
- **eira-bowman:** Spare, declarative, 10-16 words average. Professional vocabulary throughout — mooring lines, chafing gear, requisition books, gauge readings — doing double duty as both the world and the interiority. Feelings arrive as physical facts in the hands, not as named emotions. Dialogue clipped; she asks what kind of something and does not elaborate. Interior observation disguised as external: what she notices about the coat, the calluses, the gap in the log IS the chapter's emotional content, never stated as such.
**Craft notes:** This is the novel's inciting chapter and its entire function is to establish what Eira will carry: not guilt, exactly, but the knowledge of what the log says and what it doesn't. The chapter resists naming this for the reader — the gap in the log is the turn, not a sentence about the gap. The Parsons scene was deliberately extended (he asks three follow-up questions) to let Eira's 'nothing I need to note' echo through more of the page before the end. Next Eira chapter should be careful not to reuse 'she did not look' or 'she went back inside' as structural moves — both appear twice here and have used up their welcome. Owen is present through objects only; no direct memorial language.

## 02-the-icehouse
**Ending mode:** literary-fade
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Untranslated German embedded in prose without gloss or bracket translation ('Fang an', 'Danke', 'Wir hätten früher gehen sollen') — meaning left for the reader to absorb from context; withholding translation is structural, not inaccessibility.
- Free indirect interiority mid-scene without tag: Hannah's thinking about 'Danke' and what it means to her surfaces inside action paragraphs without attribution, treated as physical fact rather than named reflection.
- The barn window as recurring visual anchor: the icehouse is always visible from this vantage; Hannah always looks even when she says she didn't mean to. Establishes persistent low-grade attention without stating its nature.
- Sensory grounding before interiority in every section: smell of silage and hide, cold on the face, the flat dry sound of Lonzo's newspaper page-turn — the physical world comes before Hannah's interior opens in each beat.
- 'Nothing had happened' as structural frame: the chapter's accumulation technique is the small thing that is only what it appears to be but is charged anyway — the fence moment is the clearest instance, built toward and then deflated deliberately.
**Recurring constructions:**
- `the way she always did`
- `she didn't know`
- `she went back to work / she went back to the drill`
- `she hadn't meant to look`
**POV register:**
- **hannah-pfeiffer:** Porous, interior, adolescent permission to follow a thought further than an adult would. Sentences longer and more recursive than Eira's — average 15-22 words, some reaching 30-35 with interruptions or pivots. German surfaces at moments of high affect ('Fang an' in the barn; 'Danke' replaying on the walk; 'Wir hätten früher gehen sollen' at the close) without translation. Sensory farm world as primary anchor. Interiority named once, not rehearsed: 'He is a person. This felt obvious and also like something she was just now getting to.' Feelings arrive as observation and fact, not taxonomy.
**Craft notes:** This chapter needed to establish Hannah's voice as distinctly different from Eira's — longer, more interior, more willing to follow a thought into its complication. The recurring barn-window view of the icehouse is doing structural work across the chapter: she always looks even when she says she doesn't mean to, which is the whole of her relationship with Karl at this stage. The indirect discourse lesson in Marcus's class (mentioned only as background) has unannounced thematic resonance with Magda's sentence embedded in the kitchen scene — but this connection is left entirely for the reader. Next Hannah chapter should not open in the barn at dawn: the morning-milking opening has established the rhythm but two consecutive uses would make it Hannah's structural default rather than her world.

## 03-the-second-bedroom
**Ending mode:** coda
**Opening texture:** observation
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Subordinate clauses building to conclusions: Marcus's signature move — qualification arrives before commitment in nearly every paragraph ('not because X so much as because Y'; 'which was a warmth Marcus had come to understand, over two years, as belonging specifically to this room and these people — not warmth in general, which was a different thing')
- Grammar as perceptual frame: the ablative absolute and dum haec geruntur reorganize Marcus's perception throughout the day without ever being named as metaphor — the chapter's structural resonance is left entirely for the reader
- Compression in the Henry exchange: 'That you teach Latin too. Figured that was the useful part.' / 'That is accurate.' — eleven words do the thread-6 work; no gloss added
- Physical warmth as enacted fact: the kitchen warmth stays in his body in the cold second bedroom; the contrast is never named, only rendered through temperature and the muffled sound of 'a life being lived in a warm room, which was where he had just been'
- Free indirect interiority embedded in action: 'He also knew that this was not an explanation he could introduce into a conversation in the teachers' room at Coldwater High School' — unmarked, inside action paragraph, no attribution tag
**Recurring constructions:**
- `which was a different thing`
- `he had learned`
- `not X but Y (qualification pattern)`
- `which was where he had just been`
**POV register:**
- **marcus-klein:** Formal, measured, avg 16-22 words. No free contractions. Subordinate clauses arrive to qualify — 'not because X so much as because Y.' Builds to conclusions through structure. Analytical where other POVs would feel: the warmth of the kitchen arrives as anatomical fact ('in the chest, in the way that warmth settled'). Slightly aware of the medium: the grammar-book scene has the quality of a man reading a letter from himself. Dialogue held one register above the conversation he is in; Henry and Lillian close that gap without Marcus noticing it as effort.
**Craft notes:** Opening texture varied from routine-procedure (which the outline specified) to observation — Clara Walsh watching the board — to break the pattern-pressure flag from ch01 and ch02. The ablative absolute is not deployed as symbol; it's Marcus's professional instrument, and if it resonates with the chapter's structure (completed prior actions as background), that resonance is the reader's work. The grammar book functions as a compressed memoir: it opens to the middle from habit, and the 1937 Paris handwriting is the trace of a prior self without requiring any declared grief. The final coda withholds the kitchen-vs-bedroom contrast as interpretation — warmth diminishing, voices below, the room is cold but he is not yet. Next Marcus chapter should vary the walk/arrival structure; the three-block walk is established and need not be re-walked at the same pace.

## 04-official-report
**Ending mode:** resolved-beat
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Short declaratives and sentence fragments to carry structural weight: 'Neither in this seam.' / 'Nothing in the log.' — these do the chapter's moral work without elaboration
- Professional preparation as character revelation: Eira has organized the tide charts chronologically and tabbed them before Parsons arrives — her competence shown through a physical act, not stated
- Free indirect interiority embedded in action without tag: 'I've thought about it.' (her acknowledgment, to Parsons, that she's spent three weeks working the tidal data — doing double duty as professional competence and load-bearing silence about what else she spent three weeks thinking about)
- The scope-of-the-question technique: every answer accurate within the exact scope of the question asked; Parsons doesn't ask to see Tuesday's page; the log is open on the table; the entry is there; the reader holds all of this simultaneously without any of it being stated
- Final log-reading enacted as rhythm: she reads the entry three times; the third reading is described as the same as the first and second; the prose refuses to analyze why she is reading it a third time
- Repeated object: Owen's chart table names Owen once in the opening (pen in the groove) and once in the final section; his presence is structural, not memorial
**Recurring constructions:**
- `nothing unusual to note`
- `she had been on this breakwater`
- `the format she had used for eight years`
**POV register:**
- **eira-bowman:** Spare, compressed, declarative — average 10-16 words per sentence; drops to 4-5 words at key moments ('Neither in this seam.', 'Nothing in the log.'). Professional vocabulary throughout (tide gauge, seam, fleet status, mooring check) doing double duty as both the harbor's content and Eira's interiority. No named emotions; feelings arrive as physical facts (the cold easing, her hand flat on the closed log). Dialogue clipped to interrogative or declarative. Free indirect interiority arrives unmarked: 'She knew what a good method looked like.' / 'She had not known this seam the way she knew it now before October.' The competence is real and unironic.
**Craft notes:** The chapter's central technical problem was making Parsons's thoroughness feel like genuine pressure without making him suspicious — he's not trying to catch her, but thoroughness is precisely what she must survive at exactly the right distance. The tide log open on the table during the previous-night questions is the chapter's load-bearing image: the entry is visible, he can ask for it, he doesn't ask. The chapter never states that both what is in the entry and what is not in it are now permanent — it enacts this through the three readings and the closing of the log. The final line ('Nothing unusual to note') echoes 'I didn't enter anything unusual' without pointing to the echo — the reader holds the resonance without the text announcing it. Next Eira chapter should vary from the office-as-container: this is the second consecutive Eira chapter (after ch01) in which the office is the primary physical setting; the Portland chapter (ch07) provides the break.
