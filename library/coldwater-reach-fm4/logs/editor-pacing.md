# Editor — Pacing Pass
## Macro arc assessment

### Overall arc shape

*Coldwater Reach* is a 30-chapter, four-phase novel (Setup 1–10, Middle 11–20, Turn 21–27, Close 28–30) braiding three POVs around a central mystery that the structure promises will stay partially in shadow. The arc design is sound: the Setup is disciplined, the Close is appropriately lean for the novel's aesthetic, and the Turn has genuine escalation energy. The main structural risks are concentrated in a specific pocket of the Middle and in two places where the same emotional beat plays twice.

---

### Midbook sag: chapters 11–13 (Jan–March 1944)

This is the novel's most vulnerable stretch. From chapter 10 to chapter 14 — a four-chapter run — **Thread 1 (the Horak spine) is dormant**. Chapter 11 (Owen's Ledger) is introspective and single-threaded. Chapter 12 (The School Board) is strong as an institutional set-piece but resolves cleanly and advances no forward pressure on the central mystery. Chapter 13 (The Gift) advances only Thread 4 (Hannah and Karl) and is described as a single-thread chapter even in the thread design notes.

Three quiet chapters in a row — each individually warranted, none individually fat — compose a stillness that, cumulatively, reads as a sag. The school board hearing is the Middle's most dramatic action beat, but it resolves (ch12 ends in resolved-beat), and the two chapters framing it are atmospheric retreats. A reader arriving at chapter 14 (Horak named) after this stretch will have gone roughly 7,500–9,000 words without spine advancement. This is where midbook sag typically takes root: not in any single underperforming chapter, but in a sequence of individually defensible quiet chapters that compound into lost momentum.

**Recommended check at the per-chapter pass:** Verify that chapters 11 and 13 are pulling their maximum narrative weight. In ch11, ask whether Owen's ledger precedent is doing enough dramatic work or whether it is primarily setup for ch26's payoff (making it feel thin in place). In ch13, the carved bird is a load-bearing object, but the chapter is 2,500–3,200 words — if the scene is dwelling in atmosphere at the expense of pressure, the sag is structural, not stylistic.

---

### Doubled beat: Marcus's drawer (chapters 19 and 22)

Chapter 19 (*After Eira*) ends with Marcus holding the assembled picture and deciding to put it away — specifically located in "the drawer." Chapter 22 (*The Unfinished Letter*) ends with Marcus drafting a letter he cannot send and putting *it* in the drawer. Both end on the identical image: a man choosing not to act, the thing going in a drawer. Both end in deliberately non-urgent modes (coda, elliptical). The emotional note — Marcus arriving at the edge of action and retreating — is played twice in a three-chapter span (with only ch20 D-Day between them).

The draft intends these as different choices (the first is about protecting the Pfeiffers; the second is about protecting Eira and the Pfeiffers from official exposure). But from a pacing perspective, both chapters deliver **Marcus in stasis after assembly**. The reader who has watched him assemble the picture carefully across five chapters (6, 9, 15, 18, 19) and then watches him not-act at ch19 has already understood his choice. Chapter 22 then repeats the not-action with a different object, and the drawer reappears. This risks making Marcus feel passive across the Turn's opening rather than making a meaningful second decision.

**Recommended check:** If the per-chapter pass confirms ch22 largely restates ch19's emotional position, consider whether the chapter earns its length or whether its function (the letter as evidence that Marcus has things to protect) could be accomplished with significantly fewer words.

---

### Hannah's ending-mode uniformity

Seven of Hannah's nine chapters end in literary-fade (2, 5, 8, 13, 16, 27, 29). The chapter map's non-consecutive rule is respected, but because Hannah's chapters are not consecutive, the cumulative pattern still creates a register imprint: her chapters collectively feel like they recede. The two departures — ch21 (resolved-beat, Karl's English sentence) and ch24 (mid-action, harvest/Albrecht warning) — are the chapters where something changes in Hannah's arc. The literary-fade dominance in her other chapters is consistent with her porous, immersive register, but it means her chapters as a type create less forward pull than Eira's or Marcus's. Per-chapter passes should flag any Hannah chapter where the atmospheric close is substituting for a forward hook rather than genuinely completing the chapter's emotional beat.

---

### Close section balance (chapters 28–30)

Three chapters for the resolution of all three character arcs, the Albrecht non-reveal, and the thematic landing is compact. This leanness is almost certainly correct given the aesthetic — this is a novel that trusts restraint, and an over-extended coda would betray it. But the compactness means any one of these three chapters that underperforms cannot be rescued by the others. The per-chapter pass should verify that ch28 (Marcus's loss and the kitchen) is doing full emotional work for Thread 3's resolution and not merely gesturing at it; that ch29 (Hannah's farewell) is complete as written and not relying on the reader's assumed investment; and that ch30 (Eira at the breakwater) carries enough specific physical and narrative detail to function as the book's final resting place rather than a summary of what has already been established.

---

### D-Day placement (chapter 20): a defensible risk

D-Day arrives at the end of the Middle as a single-thread chapter advancing only Eira's arc through professional procedure. The restraint is tonally correct and thematically honest — this is a home-front novel. But this is the novel's historically largest moment and it is filtered through the most procedurally spare POV in the book. The risk is that the novel has been careful to avoid the war-as-backdrop problem everywhere except here, where the war's biggest event *becomes* the backdrop by design. The per-chapter pass should confirm that ch20 has sufficient specific weight — concrete details particular enough to make the D-Day chapter feel like the novel's D-Day chapter, not just a competent execution of its concept.

---

### Summary of priority concerns

1. **High — midbook sag at ch11–13**: spine dormant for four chapters; compound quietness without compound pressure.
2. **High — doubled drawer beat at ch19/ch22**: same emotional position played twice; risks making Marcus feel passive rather than deliberate.
3. **Medium — Hannah's literary-fade uniformity**: seven of nine chapters recede; check that no individual chapter's fade is unearned.
4. **Medium — Close section thinness**: three chapters for three arc resolutions; each must land cleanly without rescue from its neighbors.
5. **Low — D-Day as procedural chapter**: the novel's largest historical moment handled in the most spare register; verify the chapter has enough specific gravity.
