## 01-the-dock-and-after.md

**Fix applied (line-level, no sentence removed):**

Original: *"She stood there for the length of time it takes to know you've already decided."*  
Revised: *"She stood there for longer than she needed."*

Reason: The original named Eira's decision-making process as a metacognitive event — "the length of time it takes to know you've already decided" makes the interior mechanism explicit. Revised to a brief duration-marker that preserves the pause without naming what the pause means.

But it sits closer to the post-scene subtext-naming failure mode than anything else in the chapter. If the writer judges it is explaining too much, the simplest trim is to stop the sentence at *"if she were being precise about it"*.

---

**Overall:** Chapter is well-tuned to Eira's register. No "felt" for emotion, no exclamation marks, no forbidden dialogue tags, no dialogue glosses. Fragments are used correctly. **1. "the same grammar underneath" (break scene, Portland harbor window)**

> "Five times the capacity of Coldwater Reach, three times the commercial traffic, but the same grammar underneath."

"Grammar" used as a structural metaphor (underlying operational rules) is the one word that gives pause. In this book "grammar" is Marcus's professional domain — his register, his vocabulary, the word his chapters would reach for. Eira thinks in load limits, hull draft, catch tonnage, mooring capacity; the abstraction "grammar" as metaphor for organizational logic is a small register-step away from her usual vocabulary. A word from her domain ("the same work underneath," "the same operations") would be less likely to be Marcus-coded. However, the line is compressed and precise enough that it may be intentional — "grammar" does capture something more exact than "logic" or "structure." Writer judgment needed on whether to replace or leave.

---

**2. Two Varnum appositive psychological glosses**

First instance (arrival scene):
> "Varnum from Pemaquid — a large man going somewhat to heaviness, with the particular unhurried quality of someone who has worked a stretch of coast long enough that its recurring things have arranged themselves into a known sequence — had spoken with her in the spring about maintenance costs..."

Second instance (breakwater exchange):
> "He set down his pen. He had a specific quality of stillness — the stillness of a man who has seen enough of a recurring thing that each instance settles into the larger category almost before he has finished looking at it."

Both phrases are long appositive clauses with the same shape: *the [quality] of [someone/a man who has worked long enough] that [recurring things fall into pattern]*. That parallelism suggests intention. The observations are professional-to-professional (Eira reading a peer's working competence), which keeps them within the "interior observation disguised as external observation" mode. But both run long for Eira's compression register and edge toward psychological analysis she typically avoids. The compression pass has already run, so cutting isn't in scope here — raising it in case the writer wants to tighten one of the two on revision. If one is kept at full length, shortening the other would reduce the analytical weight.
