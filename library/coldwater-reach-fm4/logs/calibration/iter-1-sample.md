# Calibration Sample — Iteration 1
## Chapter 1: "The Dock, and After" (Opening, ~400 words)

---

Eira noted the barometer at 2300 — climbing since suppertime, the blow over or near enough — and closed the tide log.

The smacks still needed checking. She would have gone regardless of the barometer.

She pulled on her coat, lit the lantern from the desk lamp, and went down to the dock.

November was a week away. The harbor smelled of rockweed and diesel and the cold salt that follows a clearing blow. The lobster boats knocked against their fenders in the leftover chop. She went to each in order — port side to starboard — testing the cleat hitches and the spring lines. Hector Brann's *Cormorant*. The *Callie*, which always wanted the stern line tightened after weather. She tightened it. The lantern threw her shadow long across the planking.

At the far end of the commercial dock, near the granite bollard where the charter boats tied off, a man was sitting.

She stopped.

He was not dressed for the harbor. She could see that from thirty feet, in the lantern's edge-light. A heavy coat, dark, cut straight across the shoulder — not oilskin, not work-wear, nothing a Coldwater man wore to a dock. He was watching the water. Beside him, or rather turning away, a second figure was already moving toward the street end of the dock — walking fast, back to Eira's light, already gone.

Her mind ran its check. Unauthorized persons on the commercial wharf after dark — a matter for the harbormaster. She had a clipboard. She had, technically, a whistle.

Her feet did not move.

The man had not looked at her. She could not see his face. He held himself very still — not the stillness of cold or exhaustion but the stillness of a man listening to a place with his whole body.

She looked at him a moment longer. Then she walked back along the dock, checked the last line on the *Callie*, and went up to the office.

She did not write anything about him in the tide log. She closed up, walked home through the post-blow dark, and went to bed.

The 0558 tide reading was unremarkable. The harbor lay flat and grey, the sky coming pale over the breakwater end. Eira was at the chart table with her coffee when Hector Brann came to the door.

He stood in the frame with his cap in his hands.

"There's something on the breakwater," he said. "You'll want to see."

She already knew. She had known it at *something*. She put down her coffee and pulled on her coat.
