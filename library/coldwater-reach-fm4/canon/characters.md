# Characters

## POV Characters

---

### Eira Bowman
**Age**: 52 in October 1943  
**Role**: Harbormaster, Coldwater Reach  
**Background**: Born in Coldwater, daughter of a lobsterman. Married Owen Bowman in 1913; they had no surviving children. Learned the harbor's rhythms as Owen's shadow, took the post formally after his death in November 1935 when the selectmen ran out of other options. Has held it for eight years. She is the only woman holding a harbormaster post on the Maine coast. Nobody mentions this to her face; everyone has an opinion about it.

**Drives**:
- Competence for its own sake. She does the job better than the men who might have done it, and she knows this with the dispassion of someone who has long since stopped needing to be told.
- Authority held at cost. The question beneath all of Eira's chapters: what does it cost to fill a role built for someone else's body, someone else's name? Not just gender — Owen's ghost is in the post itself.
- The body as professional problem and personal burden. She knows what she saw. She cannot unknow it. She has not decided what that knowledge obligates her to do.

**Voice and register**: Spare, procedural, observant in a working way. Sentences built for information transfer, not reflection. She thinks in tidal schedules, load limits, weather probability. Her grief — for Owen, for the accumulated missing of the war — does not announce itself; it appears in what she notices and when she notices it. Interior monologue is rare and tends to arrive disguised as professional observation. Dialogue is clipped, often interrogative.

**Physical**: Strong-handed, weathered, thick-coated. Not physically remarkable to herself. She dresses for weather and ignores the mirror.

**Key relationship**: Dead Owen, whose presence is structural throughout. The Coast Guard Lieutenant (a recurring contact, unnamed initially). Hannah's father Albrecht, who sometimes brings milk to the harbor store — one of the crossing points with that thread.

**Arc**: What it costs to hold a post built for someone else; whether authority exercised honestly is its own justification; whether she has the right to bury what she knows in order to keep the harbor running.

---

### Hannah Pfeiffer
**Age**: 17 in October 1943; turns 18 in March 1944  
**Role**: Daughter, student, farm laborer  
**Background**: Born in Maine, daughter of Bavarian immigrants. Speaks German at home and English everywhere else. Attends Coldwater High School, one of Marcus Klein's students in Latin and (unofficially) German. Works the farm every morning before school and every evening after.

**Drives**:
- Witness and accumulation. Hannah's chapters are the most porous — they take in what the others don't see or won't let themselves see. She is seventeen and still assembling the person she will be; the novel is about that assembly.
- Her parents' silence as weight. Albrecht's German-only household rule and Magda's deeper withdrawal are existential weather for Hannah. She does not understand them fully; she understands them acutely.
- Karl Reinmar. The youngest prisoner is from fifty kilometers from where her father was born. This is not a romance in the adult sense. It is something younger and more dangerous: recognition across a wall she was raised not to look over.

**Voice and register**: The most interior of the three POVs. Adolescent permission to follow a thought further than an adult would. German words and phrases surface at moments of high affect — her father's language bleeding through. The farm's seasons are her sensory world: calf smell, frozen ground, the particular silence of a snowed-in barn. Her prose has more movement than Eira's, more instability than Marcus's. She sees things the other two have learned to walk past.

**Physical**: Tall, practical-handed, Bavarian-fair. In winter she wears her father's old coat to the barn.

**Key relationships**: Albrecht Pfeiffer (father, receding); Magda Pfeiffer (mother, withdrawn); Karl Reinmar (prisoner, the thread she'll have to choose about); Marcus Klein (her teacher, one of the thread-crossings); Lonzo Reed (the guard she observes more carefully than he observes the prisoners).

**Arc**: The accumulation of small choices that constitutes adolescence; what she does with the permission — or the transgression — of crossing the lines her parents and the town have drawn; what she becomes on the other side of them.

---

### Marcus Klein
**Age**: 47 in October 1943  
**Role**: Latin and German instructor, Coldwater High School  
**Background**: Born in Vienna. Left Austria in 1936. Paris (1936–38), London (1938–39), Canada (interned at Sherbrooke, Quebec, 1939–41, under the British policy of interning "enemy aliens," including Jewish refugees). Released through the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars. Cambridge, Massachusetts (1941–42), then hired by Coldwater High School when the previous instructor enlisted. He does not discuss Sherbrooke.

**Drives**:
- Displacement as condition, not event. Marcus does not wait to stop being displaced; he has learned, at cost, that the waiting itself is a trap. The question in his arc is different: what is left of a person, and what obligations survive, when the displacement has gone on long enough that there is nowhere else to return to?
- Language as survival instrument. He weighs every word for what it reveals about him. His formal, slightly overcorrect English is armor. Teaching language — the grammar and declension of it — is the one place he can be wholly competent.
- Henry and Lillian Crow. He recognizes a variant of his condition in theirs — not the same, not equivalent, but recognizable. The household is the one place he is not a specimen.
- The body on the breakwater intersects something in Marcus's past — not through plot-mechanics but through the kind of knowledge that accumulates in a man who has moved through wartime Europe: a certain type of person, a certain type of end.

**Voice and register**: Precise, slightly formal, with the characteristic careful pace of someone translating his thinking as it occurs. The academic's habit of measuring sentences. His narration has subordinate clauses that arrive to qualify or correct; he rarely commits to an absolute statement unless it concerns grammar or the calendar. His chapters sometimes have the quality of a letter written in a language not his own — not because his English is wrong, but because he is always slightly aware of the medium.

**Physical**: Slight, carefully dressed (the old European habit of dressing as a form of dignity). Wears a coat hat in autumn that looks distinctly not from Maine. Glasses. The Crows' house has the only room in town where he fully takes his coat off.

**Key relationships**: Henry Crow (landlord, recognition, something approaching friendship); Lillian Crow (who feeds him and asks him about Vienna); Hannah Pfeiffer (student, the site of one thread-crossing); the school board (threat, background pressure); Karl Reinmar (via Hannah, and via his own knowledge of what certain German boys' paths through the war look like).

**Arc**: Whether displacement ends, and if it does, what the displaced person becomes when the ground stops moving. Whether a man who has lost everything consecutive still has obligations to places, people, languages he no longer belongs to.

## Secondary and Supporting Characters

---

### Owen Bowman (deceased)
**Age at death**: approximately 47 (drowned November 1935)  
**Role**: Eira's husband; previous harbormaster  
**Background**: Fourth-generation Coldwater fisherman. Took the harbormaster post in the late 1920s. Drowned in the harbor during the November storm of 1935 — went out to secure a vessel that was breaking loose; was not found for three days. Eira found him.  
**Function**: Owen is structural throughout Eira's chapters — not as a ghost in the literary sense, but as the shape of the post she holds. The tools he used, the ledger in his handwriting, the men who still call the office "Owen's." He is the precedent she is always both following and revising.

---

### Albrecht Pfeiffer
**Age**: 56 in 1943  
**Role**: Hannah's father; dairy farmer  
**Background**: Born in Bavaria, near Nuremberg. Emigrated to Maine in 1922 with Magda. Naturalized citizen. He came to America wanting to leave Germany, and now Germany has followed him in a different form. Since the declaration of war he has retreated into German at home — not as defiance, but as a kind of grief. He does not speak of Bavaria specifically; what surfaces is the language, not the memory.  
**Voice**: His English is grammatically correct and formally old-fashioned. His German is the German of the region he left. The prose does not phonetically render either — it renders the fact of them.

---

### Magda Pfeiffer
**Age**: 53 in 1943  
**Role**: Hannah's mother  
**Background**: Also from Bavaria. Magda's withdrawal is more complete than Albrecht's retreat into German — she has gone quiet in ways that cannot be explained solely by the war. There is something older and more personal in her silence. She works the household, tends the kitchen garden, cooks. She does not speak much in any language. Hannah watches her.

---

### Karl Reinmar
**Age**: 19 in 1943  
**Role**: German POW, the youngest of four assigned to the Pfeiffer farm  
**Background**: Wehrmacht, captured in North Africa (Tunisia, spring 1943). From a town in Franconia, fifty kilometers from Albrecht Pfeiffer's birthplace. He is not ideological — at nineteen, drafted at seventeen, he has been a soldier long enough to have lost most of the beliefs the uniform was given with. He speaks a German that Albrecht recognizes down to the vowels. He is learning English from newspapers left in the icehouse.  
**Function**: The crossing point between Hannah's interior and the history she is surrounded by. His presence forces the question she cannot avoid: what side is she on, if sides are where the question lives.

---

### Lonzo Reed
**Age**: 63 in 1943  
**Role**: Coastal Patrol private, armed escort for the Pfeiffer farm POWs  
**Background**: From a Maine fishing family, too old for the regular service and assigned to the Coastal Patrol. He sits on his stool with the Springfield rifle, reads the Bangor Daily News, and pays attention to more than he shows. He is not unfriendly to the prisoners. He is not friendly either. He has a son in the Pacific who does not write often enough.  
**Function**: A dry, careful observer who occasionally appears as a perspective character in short interstitial sections if the structure calls for it. He knows more about what happens on the farm than anyone attributes to him.

---

### Henry Crow
**Age**: 51 in 1943  
**Role**: Chief custodian, Coldwater High School; Marcus's landlord  
**Background**: His family came north from Norfolk, Virginia, in 1924. Henry has been at the school for sixteen years. He knows every pipe, every furnace trick, every student by name. He is not a man to whom the town pays close attention. He notices this with the calm of someone long accustomed to being underestimated.  
**Voice**: Direct, warm at home, more reserved in public. He and Marcus have developed a friendship built on the recognition that both of them are in this town on sufferance — a recognition neither has stated directly.  
**Function**: The Crow household is the moral anchor of Marcus's chapters. Henry may carry a chapter or two from his own POV if the structure requires — his perspective on the school, the town, and Marcus's position is different from Marcus's own.

---

### Lillian Crow
**Age**: 48 in 1943  
**Role**: Henry's wife  
**Background**: Born in Maine (Waterville), one of the small number of Black families in the inland mill towns. She came to Coldwater Reach when she married Henry in 1924. She works some hours at the school as well (cleaning, cooking for events) though less formally than Henry.  
**Voice**: The warmest register in the novel. She asks Marcus about Vienna — actually wants to know. She has the quality of a person who has decided, actively, that the life available to her is worth inhabiting fully.  
**Function**: Grounds the Crow household in warmth rather than simply solidarity. Her relationship with Marcus is easy in a way that Coldwater Reach otherwise is not.

---

### The Body on the Breakwater (the unknown man)
No name is given in early chapters. Male, somewhere between 35 and 50. His clothing is unusual — not a fisherman's. His hands have calluses in the wrong places. The Coast Guard takes him. Official cause: drowning, storm exposure. The three POV characters accumulate understanding of who he might have been and how he came to be on that breakwater in ways that do not converge into a single clean answer before the book ends.
