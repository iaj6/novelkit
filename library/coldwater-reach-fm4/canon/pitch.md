# Pitch

Autumn 1943. A man's body washes up on the breakwater at Coldwater Reach, a working fishing harbor on the Maine coast. He has no papers. He is not from the town. The Coast Guard removes him within the day and takes most of the official silence with it.

Three people in Coldwater Reach end up carrying the weight of that body, none of them by plan:

**Eira Bowman**, fifty-two, the town's harbormaster — the only woman in her position on the Maine coast, widowed eight years before when the previous harbormaster (her husband) drowned. She saw something on the dock the night before the body came in.

**Hannah Pfeiffer**, seventeen, inland on her family's dairy farm, where four German prisoners work the fields under armed escort. The youngest prisoner is from near where her father was born. Her parents have been disappearing, slowly, in different directions, since the war started.

**Marcus Klein**, forty-seven, Austrian-Jewish, who arrived in Coldwater in 1942 to teach German and Latin at the town high school after years of displacement across Paris, London, and a Canadian internment camp. He boards with the Black custodian Henry Crow and his wife Lillian — the only household in town that treats him as a guest rather than a curiosity.

None of the three meets the other two together. Their threads cross. The question of who the dead man was, and what happened that night, moves through all three of them across eighteen months, assembling in the reader before it assembles in any of the characters. The war ends in the background. Their resolutions are smaller and older than that.
