# Glossary

Terms, names, places, and objects for Coldwater Reach. Alphabetical. Downstream phases should add to this file as new terms are introduced.

---

**Albrecht Pfeiffer** — Hannah's father. Born in Bavaria near Nuremberg. Emigrated to Maine 1922. Naturalized citizen. Has reverted to speaking only German at home since the war began. Age 56 in 1943.

**Battle of the Bulge** — German Ardennes offensive, December 1944. Within the novel's narrative period. The town loses two more men.

**BC-1206** — The standard wartime radio communications set. Civilian shortwave use was restricted during the war.

**Beal's General Store** — Coldwater Reach's general store on Main Street. Where the radio is on June 6, 1944.

**Body on the Breakwater, the** — Unnamed male, not from Coldwater Reach, found on the breakwater after a three-day blow in October 1943. No papers. Coast Guard takes him. The spine object of the novel.

**Camp Houlton** — Real German POW internment camp in Aroostook County, Maine. Prisoners from Houlton were assigned to satellite farm-labor placements across the state under the Emergency Farm Labor program.

**Coldwater Reach** — Invented coastal Maine fishing town, the setting of the novel. On the Penobscot Bay periphery, south of Bangor, north of Portland. Population approximately 800.

**Coastal Patrol** — Wartime volunteer patrol organization, mostly older men. Responsible for watching Maine coastal beaches and harbor approaches. Lonzo Reed is a Coastal Patrol private.

**D-Day** — June 6, 1944. The Allied landings at Normandy. Within the novel's narrative period. Arrives on the kitchen radio.

**Eira Bowman** — Harbormaster of Coldwater Reach. Age 52 in 1943. Widow of Owen Bowman. The only woman in her post on the Maine coast. POV character.

**Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars** — Real American organization that helped displaced European academics find positions in U.S. institutions. Marcus Klein was on their rolls from autumn 1940.

**Emergency Farm Labor Program** — Federal program that assigned German POWs to agricultural labor to replace the wartime farm-labor shortage.

**Enemy alien** — Wartime classification for foreign-born nationals of Axis countries, including (unjustly) Jewish refugees. German-born noncitizens were required to register and faced movement restrictions. The Pfeiffers, as naturalized citizens, were not classified as enemy aliens, though the social weight of the designation still touched them.

**Hannah Pfeiffer** — Seventeen-year-old daughter of the Pfeiffer dairy farm family. POV character. Turns 18 in March 1944.

**Henry Crow** — Chief custodian, Coldwater High School. Age 51 in 1943. Marcus's landlord. Family came north from Norfolk, Virginia, in 1924. Black American. Not a POV character in the main rotation, but may carry occasional short chapters.

**Karl Reinmar** — Youngest of the four German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm. Age 19. From Franconia, approximately fifty kilometers from Albrecht Pfeiffer's birthplace. Captured in Tunisia, spring 1943.

**Lillian Crow** — Henry's wife. Age 48. From Waterville, Maine. Treats Marcus as a guest rather than a specimen. The warmest register in the novel.

**Lonzo Reed** — Coastal Patrol private. Age 63. Armed escort for the Pfeiffer farm POWs. Has a son in the Pacific. Pays more attention than he is attributed with. May carry occasional short chapters.

**Magda Pfeiffer** — Hannah's mother. Age 53. Born in Bavaria. Increasingly withdrawn. Not speaking much in any language.

**Marcus Klein** — Latin and German instructor, Coldwater High School. Age 47 in 1943. Viennese, Jewish, displaced through Paris, London, and the Sherbrooke internment camp. POV character.

**Owen Bowman (deceased)** — Eira's husband and previous harbormaster. Drowned November 1935 in a storm, securing a vessel that was breaking loose. Eira found him after three days.

**Ration book** — Government-issued booklet authorizing civilian purchase of rationed goods: sugar, meat, coffee, fuel oil, rubber, shoes. Renewed periodically at the rationing board (Legion hall in Coldwater Reach).

**Route 9** — The road on which the Pfeiffer farm sits, three miles inland from Coldwater Reach.

**Sherbrooke, Quebec** — Real Canadian city where an internment camp held "enemy aliens," including Jewish refugees from Europe, under British wartime emergency policy. Marcus was interned there 1939–1941. He does not discuss it.

**Springfield rifle** — The M1903 Springfield bolt-action rifle. Lonzo Reed's weapon of record while escorting the Pfeiffer farm POWs.

**V-Mail** — Victory Mail: the microfilm-based letter system used to communicate with U.S. servicemen overseas. Letters were photographed, the film transported, and prints made at the destination. The dominant form of communication between Coldwater residents and their men in service.

**VE Day** — Victory in Europe Day, May 8, 1945. The story ends before it arrives.

**WLBZ** — Real radio station out of Bangor, Maine. The source of the nightly war news in Coldwater Reach homes.

**Hector Brann** — Lobsterman in Coldwater Reach. Fishes the Irene M. (green hull, third slip on the commercial dock). The man who found the body on the breakwater in October 1943.

**Irene M.** — Hector Brann's lobster boat. Green hull. Third slip on the Coldwater Reach commercial dock.

**Lt. Arthur Parsons** — Coast Guard lieutenant. Took the official report on the body found on the Coldwater Reach breakwater in October 1943. A recurring official contact for Eira Bowman. Approximately 40 years old. Manner: brisk, thorough, by the book.

**Requisition book** — The harbormaster's supply requisition log. Owen Bowman's handwriting through page 37; Eira Bowman's from page 38 onward. Kept in the harbormaster's office.

**Tide log** — The official harbor record kept by the harbormaster: gauge readings, mooring notations, notable events. The spine of Eira's professional record and the site of the novel's central gap — her Tuesday night entry contains no mention of what she saw on the dock.

**Pfeiffer Farm** — Working dairy farm on Route 9, three miles inland from Coldwater Reach. Property of Albrecht and Magda Pfeiffer and their daughter Hannah. Twenty-two Holsteins. Red barn, icehouse beside it (now renovated as POW quarters). Stone walls from 1903. Corn stubble in October.

**Pfeiffer Icehouse** — Former icehouse on the Pfeiffer farm property, beside the barn. Renovated by Camp Houlton personnel in autumn 1943 into sleeping quarters for four German POWs: whitewashed inside, partitioned, two cots and two pallets. Visible from the barn window above the third stall.

**Karl Reinmar** — German POW, age 19; youngest of four assigned to the Pfeiffer farm under the Emergency Farm Labor program. Captured in Tunisia (spring 1943). From Franconia, approximately 50 km from Albrecht Pfeiffer's birthplace near Nuremberg. Wind-chapped face, gray-blue eyes, short hair; shorter than the other three prisoners.

**Lonzo Reed** — Coastal Patrol private, age 63. Armed escort (Springfield rifle) for the four German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm. Arrives at 7 a.m. daily, sits on a stool at the barn door, reads the Bangor Daily. Has a son in the Pacific. Not unfriendly to the prisoners; not friendly either.

**Albrecht Pfeiffer** — Hannah's father; Bavarian-born dairy farmer, from near Nuremberg; emigrated to Maine in 1922; naturalized U.S. citizen. Since the war, speaks only German at home. Reads the German-language Bangor Daily (received by post Thursdays) for war news in the evenings. Age 56 in 1943.

**Magda Pfeiffer** — Hannah's mother; Bavarian-born; emigrated to Maine in 1922; naturalized U.S. citizen. Largely silent since the war started — rarely speaks in any language. Works the household, tends the stove. Age 53 in 1943. In late October 1943 she spoke one sentence aloud: 'Wir hätten früher gehen sollen' (We should have left sooner).

**Wir hätten früher gehen sollen** — German phrase (We should have left sooner) — spoken once by Magda Pfeiffer in the Pfeiffer farmhouse kitchen, late October 1943. Not translated in the prose. A load-bearing sentence in Thread 5.

**oratio obliqua** — Latin for indirect discourse — the grammatical embedding of a reported statement in another clause, which shifts the verb form. The topic of Marcus Klein's Latin lesson in this chapter; thematic resonance with the novel's embedded knowledge and secondhand accounts.

**Clara Walsh** — Student at Coldwater High School; the strongest student in Marcus Klein's Latin class in 1943. Grasped the ablative absolute on his second explanation, identifying it as 'like circumstances.'

**Raymond Foss** — Student at Coldwater High School in Marcus Klein's Latin class; repeatedly commits a third-declension dative plural error. His father works at the cannery.

**Einführung in die deutsche Grammatik** — Marcus Klein's German grammar text, published in Leipzig in 1929. He has carried it since Vienna; the cover is water-damaged from a crossing he does not discuss; the spine has been tape-repaired. His marginal notes were written in Paris in 1937 on the Rue des Ecoles.

**The Prater** — Large park in Vienna with a main chestnut-tree avenue. Marcus walked there Sunday mornings before he left Vienna in 1936. He tells Lillian Crow about the Prater — its October color and the roasted-chestnut cart at the eastern entrance — but stops before 1938.

**Crow House** — Henry and Lillian Crow's white clapboard house on Elm Street, Coldwater Reach, three blocks from both the school and the harbor. The kitchen is the social center and the warmest room; Marcus rents the second bedroom on the upper floor.

**Coldwater High School** — WPA-era brick building in Coldwater Reach handling grades 1-12, enrollment ~130. Marcus Klein teaches Latin and German here. Henry Crow is chief custodian. The school has multiple furnaces; Henry tends them.

**Edna Price** — Teacher at Coldwater High School; present in the teachers' room in ch03, discussing the body-on-the-breakwater theory.

**Ralph Garland** — Teacher at Coldwater High School; present in the teachers' room in ch03; confidently advocates the U-boat-agent explanation for the body on the breakwater.

**Dorothy Mead** — Fishing smack moored at Coldwater Reach harbor; owned by Alderman; had unresolved engine trouble in early November 1943.

**District office, Portland** — The Coast Guard district administrative office in Portland, Maine; where official case records for the Coldwater Reach inquiry were filed.

**Ruth Ann, Mabel, Cornelia Best** — Three fishing smacks named as being out from Coldwater Reach on the November morning of Parsons's official inquiry.

**Feuchtwangen** — Town in Franconia, Bavaria; Albrecht Pfeiffer's birthplace; approximately 40 km south of Ansbach. First named in chapter 05 when Hannah asks Albrecht where exactly he was born.

**Post-driver (fence post driver)** — Heavy iron pipe with capped ends used to drive fence posts into the ground; Hannah and Albrecht use it on the south fence of the near field in ch05.

**Landsmann (Landsmann)** — German word meaning a person from the same region or home district — a term of regional affiliation, not merely national identity. Implies shared Gegend (home ground) specifically: a man from Munich would not naturally call a man from Hamburg his Landsmann. First defined by Marcus Klein in ch06, in response to Hannah Pfeiffer's question.

**Gegend** — German word for home district, home region, local ground — the specific geography implied by the word Landsmann. Used by Marcus Klein in his definition of Landsmann in ch06.

**Landsleute** — German word for countrymen in the broader national sense — distinguished from Landsmann (which implies specific regional origin) by Marcus Klein in ch06. Landsleute is national; Landsmann is regional.

**Michael Bates** — Student in Marcus Klein's third-year Latin class at Coldwater High School. Sits third row from left. Struggling with the irregular passive fertur in November 1943.

**Ralph Garland** — Teacher at Coldwater High School. Uses the teachers' room; reads the Bangor Daily sports section during free periods.

**Donna Packard** — Student in Marcus Klein's Latin class at Coldwater High School.

**Philip Varnum** — Harbormaster of Pemaquid, Maine. Large man, going to heaviness; running his harbor since at least the mid-1930s. Eira has spoken with him at three quarterly coordination meetings. In December 1943 he names 'the refugee crossings' as the probable category for the Coldwater breakwater body.

**Fitch** — Fuel allocation officer, Portland District office. Runs the regional figures and reads the final allocation recommendation at the quarterly harbormasters' coordination meeting.

**Hartley** — Coast Guard liaison at the December 1943 quarterly harbormasters' meeting in Portland (not Parsons). Younger, administrative, runs the incident summary and communications network review.

**Portland Harbor Coordination Office** — Third-floor office in a brick building one block from the Portland waterfront. Site of the quarterly Maine coastal harbormasters' meetings — fuel allocation, communications maintenance, incident summary, security protocols.

**The refugee crossings** — Philip Varnum's phrase for unofficial wartime transit of people along the Maine coast — passengers off vessels that did not file manifests, traveling by means that generate no documentation. More common than the official record reflects. First named to Eira at the December 1943 quarterly meeting; changes the frame she holds the breakwater body in.

**Inge** — Magda Pfeiffer's mother; Hannah's maternal grandmother. Bavarian. Died 1937. Hannah met her once around age five or six. One photograph exists in the Pfeiffer farmhouse hallway. Possible source of the phrase 'Wenn das Wasser steigt, verlässt man das Dorf.'

**Wenn das Wasser steigt, verlässt man das Dorf** — German phrase meaning 'When the water rises, you leave the village.' Surfaces in Hannah's interior for the first time in ch08 (December 1943). Source unlocatable — possibly her grandmother Inge or Magda before the silences started. Recurs later in the narrative. Has the shape of advice or of justification after the fact.

**Magda's spells (three kinds)** — Hannah's private taxonomy for her mother's episodes of withdrawal. Kind one: distress, required the doctor (twice: Hannah ages 12 and 14). Kind two: unnamed middle. Kind three: worst, requires only time and presence. The December 1943 spell in ch08 is the worst in the narrative to date.

**Rockland feed cooperative trip** — Albrecht Pfeiffer's twice-yearly overnight supply run to the Rockland feed cooperative (December and June). He leaves before dawn and stays one night.

**Whitmore** — Marcus Klein's contact at the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars, Cambridge. Handles the displaced scholars correspondence file. Known for letting things accumulate before responding — methodical rather than prompt. First named in ch09.

**Mrs. Aldrich** — Counter clerk at the Coldwater Reach post office on Main Street. Manages named-letter sorting. First named in ch09.

**Coldwater Reach Post Office** — Located on Main Street, Coldwater Reach. Mrs. Aldrich is the counter clerk. Marcus picks up his (sparse) overseas correspondence here.

**Grammar of handled** — Marcus's internal name for the compression technique — the statement that is accurate and complete within a decided frame, answering the question asked without extending to the question behind it. He recognizes Eira Bowman using this form in ch09; he has used it himself in Paris, London, and Sherbrooke.

**James Reed (Jimmy)** — Lonzo Reed's son. Serving on a Buckley-class destroyer escort in the Pacific theater; assigned February 1943. Writes home every six weeks when things are going well. His last letter (as of December 1943) contains the phrase 'don't worry, Dad' and is in Lonzo's jacket pocket.

**Fischer** — The heavyset German POW at the Pfeiffer farm. Always first out of the icehouse; sets the pace for the other three prisoners. (Identified first in ch10 from Lonzo's POV; Hannah had previously described him as 'the heavyset one with a rolling walk.')

**Brunner** — Thin German POW at the Pfeiffer farm. Habitual left-side lean. Works heavy tasks together with Krebs. (Identified first in ch10; previously 'the thin one who leans slightly to the left.')

**Krebs** — Oldest German POW at the Pfeiffer farm. Bald on the crown; missing upper third of right ear from a pre-Tunisia injury. Works heavy tasks with Brunner. (Identified first in ch10; previously 'the older one with a bald patch.')

**Buckley-class destroyer escort** — Class of destroyer escort used in the U.S. Navy during WWII. Jimmy Reed (Lonzo's son) serves on one in the Pacific theater, 1943–1945.

**Coastal Patrol daily report form** — Form Lonzo Reed fills out at day's end at the Pfeiffer farm. Blanks for date, prisoner count, hours worked, incidents, violations. The document through which Lonzo's editorial decisions (what counts as an incident) are institutionally recorded.

**Elias Foss** — Fisherman from Vinalhaven, Maine; reported missing from his vessel September 29, 1934; found dead at the end of the commercial dock in Coldwater Reach on October 14, 1934, by Elias Hollis's night crew. Death ruled drowning from extended exposure. Subject of Owen Bowman's October 1934 harbormaster log entry, which also contains a post-hoc note about J.T.

**J.T.** — Unidentified person referenced in Owen Bowman's 1934 harbormaster log note (below the Elias Foss entry). Came to the dock on October 12, 1934, in a bad way; Owen met him and held his statement from the official record until he was ready, then entered a complete account late with a notation explaining the delay. Never further identified in the novel.

**Harbormaster Ames** — Eira's predecessor-once-removed as harbormaster of Coldwater Reach; died 1929. His annual logs (kept in the harbormaster's office cabinet before Owen's) had approximate record-keeping that Eira has had to verify against District office copies.

**J.H. Pell** — Medical examiner who handled the Elias Foss case in 1934; notified at 0700 on October 14, examination completed at 0900, ruled death by drowning consistent with extended exposure.

**Elias Hollis** — Lobsterman in Coldwater Reach; his night crew recovered Elias Foss's body at the end of the commercial dock in October 1934. Also present in Eira's 1943 log (fuel allocation check, October 26).

**Dwight Hatch** — Selectman and school board member, Coldwater Reach. In his fifties, cautious rather than hostile. Presided over the February 1944 review of Marcus Klein's appointment; voted 3-0 to continue it. May be referenced in later chapters but does not need to recur in person.

**Agnes Vane** — School board member and school parent, Coldwater Reach. Composed, concerned in a way that is articulate but refuses to state itself fully. At the February 1944 board review her objection was about 'the language itself, at this particular time.' Voted to continue Marcus's appointment.

**Gordon Sears** — School board member, hardware man, Coldwater Reach. Direct, impatient with formalities. Could not see why a German-language instructor was necessary; said so plainly. Voted to continue Marcus's appointment. Said to Marcus: 'You understand our position.' Marcus: 'I understand it perfectly.'

**Harrington** — Whitmore's predecessor at the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars, Cambridge. Signed Marcus Klein's enrollment letter in November 1940. Background figure only.

**Legion Hall** — American Legion hall, Coldwater Reach. Rationing board offices by day (9 a.m. to 4 p.m. weekdays); school board meeting space when needed. Interior: folding tables in two rows, American Legion banner across the back wall, gold fringe fading at the folds, two ceiling fixtures, three north windows.

**The Carved Bird** — A small carved wooden bird made by Karl Reinmar from birch, approximately three inches long; wings folded, head angled down, tail notched, with a corrected faint ridge on the breast. Left by Karl at the south fence corner post of the Pfeiffer farm on or before March 15, 1944. Found and kept by Hannah Pfeiffer. A load-bearing object recurring in chapters 13, 24, and 29.

**Jan Václav Horak** — Czechoslovak national, displaced person, identified as the remains recovered from the Coldwater Reach breakwater, October 1943. Last known location: Halifax, Nova Scotia, October 1942. No next of kin on record. Identified through Allied civilian records, communicated by Coast Guard regional office Portland, April 12, 1944.

**Idabelle** — Lobster boat belonging to Robert Cates; moored at Coldwater Reach berth 14 since summer 1938; berth released February 1944 after Cates shipped as merchant vessel mate.

**Robert Cates** — Coldwater Reach lobsterman; held berth 14 since 1938; shipped as merchant vessel mate September 1943; fate uncertain.

**Harold Vose** — Coldwater Reach lobsterman; held berth 9; registration returned to his mother in Searsport (named next of kin) December 1943.

**Archie Lunt** — Lobsterman from Tenants Harbor; moved to Coldwater Reach after Price fish house folded February 1944; assigned berth 14. Brother of Eddie Lunt.

**Eddie Lunt** — Lobsterman from Tenants Harbor; moved to Coldwater Reach after Price fish house folded February 1944. Brother of Archie Lunt. No relation to Port Clyde Lunts.

**Roy Tremblay** — Lobsterman from Spruce Head; vouched by Elias Hollis; assigned berth 9 provisionally, spring 1944.

**Price Fish House** — Fish house in Tenants Harbor, Maine; closed February 1944, displacing at least two lobstermen (Archie and Eddie Lunt) who relocated to Coldwater Reach.

**Franconian-Czech Borderland** — The eastern Upper Franconian region including Feuchtwangen, Dinkelsbühl, and the communities running east toward the Bohemian Forest and the Czech border. Both Albrecht Pfeiffer's family and Karl Reinmar's dialect origin are from this area. Marcus Klein identifies it as the probable source of the émigré network that may have moved Jan Václav Horak south from Halifax.

**Upper Franconian harvest idiom (Karl's phrase)** — A regional Franconian harvest idiom overheard by Hannah Pfeiffer and brought to Marcus Klein for translation. Marcus renders it as: 'The field takes what it is owed, and the man who worked it walks home lighter.' The idiom's dialect form pinpoints Karl Reinmar's origin to the Feuchtwangen-Dinkelsbühl corridor in eastern Franconia — not Ansbach, as Hannah had believed.

**Die Erde hier ist schwer** — German phrase: 'The earth here is heavy.' Recurring in Hannah's chapters; first appears in ch08 from a Bavarian grammar or farm prayer; recurs in ch16 as ambient, Hannah unable to locate its origin. Associated with the clay subsoil of the north field and with the weight her parents carry.

**Marcus Klein's Classroom (Mr. Klein's Room)** — Second-floor classroom at Coldwater High School where Marcus teaches Latin and German. Twenty desks in four rows, eleven with student names carved into the wood. Shelf behind the front desk: Latin dictionary, mark book (green cover), spare chalk. Blackboard with next day's date in upper right corner. Henry Crow thinks of it as 'Mr. Klein's room,' never by its subject designation. South window latch stuck since March 1944, freed by May warmth.

**East Wing Furnace (Coldwater High School)** — Coal furnace serving the east wing of the WPA-era school building. Has a baffle plate in the air-change fitting that sits wrong and restricts the draw on cold mornings; Henry shimmed it in December 1943 and managed it all winter. Can be banked down in May when the radiators are no longer needed.

**South Corridor Radiator (Coldwater High School)** — Main-corridor radiator, slow to heat all winter 1943-44 due to air in the feed-line fitting. Bled by Henry in May 1944 (eight seconds of air before running clear); tightened and tagged with red tape for summer work order. Henry's maintenance notation: red tape = summer priority repair.

**Henry's maintenance notation (red tape)** — Henry Crow's system for flagging deferred repairs at Coldwater High School: a strip of red tape on the fitting or fixture marks it for the summer work order. Referenced in maintenance book entries with 'summer priority.'

**School rowing dock** — Twenty-foot timber platform at the foot of Coldwater High School's east slope, facing the Reach, downstream of the commercial wharf. Used by the school's rowing club. Pre-1937 construction, mortised stringers. Anchor registration lapsed 1941. Platform four inches below post-1937 tidal compliance height. Two fiberglass rowing shells, matched pair, school name on bow. Managed by Mr. Folger (athletics director).

**Folger** — Athletics director at Coldwater High School. Responsible for the rowing dock and its mooring registration.

**Mooring anchor registration fee** — Two dollars per year, payable at the harbormaster's office, required for any moored vessel or dock anchor in the harbor jurisdiction of Coldwater Reach.

**Ellis (supply boat)** — Unnamed supply boat contact who provides occasional goods to the Crow household; in late May 1944 gave Lillian two bags of real coffee found at the back of the locker.

**Coldwater High School Rowing Team** — School rowing team that ceased activity in 1942 when three members enlisted: Hennessey's boy and the two Vance brothers. Two matched fiberglass shells remain at the school's east-slope dock, mooring registration expired 1941.

**desiderium** — Latin: the longing specific to absence — the ache of missing a particular person, distinct from general sadness (tristitia) or grief (dolor). Used correctly by a student in Marcus Klein's afternoon Latin class, late May 1944, in a passage about soldiers far from home.

**Lawrence Cary** — Harbormaster at Rockland; on the Portland coordination relay; called Eira at 03:02 on June 6, 1944 to relay that the Normandy landing was underway. Functional contact only.

**Galen Fuller** — Coldwater Reach man, age 22 in June 1944; Army infantry in the Normandy landing; former crew of the Helena; Calvin Fuller's son. Killed June 17, 1944 — confirmed in ch23, telegram arriving months late.

**Robert Cates** — Coldwater Reach man, age 24 in June 1944; Army infantry in the Normandy landing; third-generation dock family; his father runs the Bethia (dock 7). Survives, though this is not known until later.

**Calvin Fuller** — Galen Fuller's father; owns the fishing vessel Helena; has been working the boat alone since Galen enlisted in spring 1943.

**Bertrand Hatch** — Western Union clerk on Main Street, Coldwater Reach; has delivered 43 casualty telegrams since December 1941. Present at Beal's on D-Day, coat on, looking grey.

**Helena** — Fishing vessel owned by Calvin Fuller; crewed by Galen Fuller before he enlisted spring 1943; Helena's berth now occupied by Winslow's Penelope Ann on a monthly arrangement since autumn 1942.

**Bethia** — Fishing vessel owned by Robert Cates's father; dock 7, Coldwater Reach; run by the father alone.

**Margaret Jean** — Fishing vessel owned by Hollis Vane; noted June 6, 1944 with suspected loose cooling-system fitting.

**Penelope Ann** — Fishing vessel owned by Winslow; occupies the Helena's former berth on a monthly arrangement since autumn 1942.

**Brunner** — One of the three older German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm. Reacts to the D-Day news with a low, wordless sound before returning to work.

**Fischer** — One of the three older German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm. On D-Day news: looks at the field in silence, returns to work without comment; already has his gloves on when he comes out of the icehouse.

**Krebs** — The quietest of the three older German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm. Says nothing in response to the D-Day headline.

**Professor Emil Stern** — Cambridge professor; member of the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars routing committee. Helped move Marcus Klein from Sherbrooke internment to Cambridge (1941), and placed his name with the Coldwater High School hiring notice spring 1942. Marcus's occasional correspondent. Not a character who appears in person; the intended (unsent) recipient of the unfinished letter of July 10, 1944.

**The Unfinished Letter** — A letter drafted by Marcus Klein on July 10, 1944, addressed to Professor Emil Stern in Cambridge. Four paragraphs asking whether Jan Václav Horak was known to the displacement networks moving persons through the Halifax-to-New England corridor. Never sent; placed in the right-hand drawer of Marcus's desk at the Crow house. Fills one and a half pages of composition paper; includes a crossed-out fifth paragraph (a failed redraft). Stays in the drawer through the rest of the novel.

**Abel Holt** — Coldwater fisherman; present at the dock working his stern post on July 10, 1944. Minor background figure.

**Henry Cates** — Coldwater lobsterman; captain of the Bethia, dock 7, over twenty years without significant incident. Robert Cates's father. First introduced by name in ch23 when he brings news of Galen Fuller's telegram to the harbormaster's office.

**Ruth Fuller** — Galen Fuller's wife; has operated the Fuller dragger herself on fuel-allocation days since Galen enlisted spring 1943. Her sister lives in Tenant's Harbor. Widowed August 1944 when the telegram arrives. Met Eira briefly at Beal's harbor counter; their exchange is the chapter's emotional weight: 'He was a good man with a boat.' / 'He was.'

**Nancy Marie** — Sardine seiner out of Spruce Head, Maine; moored at Coldwater Reach during the August 1944 sardine run. One of two seasonal boats that displaced the Halcyon and Eda L. in the mooring rotation.

**Ida May** — Double-ended lobster boat belonging to a fisherman named Kell, from Penobscot Bay; moored at Coldwater Reach outside staging during August 1944 sardine run season.

**Beal's harbor counter** — Beal's secondary harbor-end account at the commercial wharf — flour, salt, kerosene wicks, tobacco, canned goods for harbor workers. Distinct from the full Beal's general store on Main Street. Eira shops here Thursday mornings.

**Karl's carved bird** — Small wooden bird carved by Karl Reinmar and left at the south fence for Hannah Pfeiffer on her eighteenth birthday in March 1944 (ch13). Hannah kept it as her first deliberate secret about him. By autumn 1944 she carries it habitually in her coat pocket. The bird is still in her possession at the novel's end (ch29: 'keep it').

**Harbor Log Late Entry (October 13, 1943)** — The late notation Eira Bowman added below the October 13, 1943 entry in the Coldwater Reach harbor log, written in October 1944 during the annual log review. Records the unidentified individual at the far bollard and the second party who walked inland — the dock observation Eira omitted at the time. Signed 'E. Bowman, Harbormaster, October 1944.' Does not name Horak; does not identify the second party. Resolves Eira's Thread 1 and Thread 2 arcs by completing the professional record.

**Ardennes offensive (German counterattack)** — The German counteroffensive beginning December 16, 1944 in eastern Belgium (the Ardennes forest region); known later as the Battle of the Bulge. In the novel's present (December 1944), the American press and Bangor Daily are using 'German counterattack.' Referenced in ch27 via Albrecht Pfeiffer's radio listening.

**Calvin Wilkes** — Coldwater High School student, first-year Latin under Marcus Klein, 1942–43. Enlisted spring 1943, aged 17. Killed in the Ardennes (Battle of the Bulge), December 1944–January 1945. His card on the school servicemen's board receives a black border.

**Servicemen's Board** — Framed corkboard in the hallway of Coldwater High School, right of the main entrance. White cards for each serviceman since spring 1942; Bertrand Hatch places a thin black border on a card when the casualty telegram arrives.

**Kessler** — Marcus Klein's Parisian classicist colleague; at the Sorbonne since 1933; arrested Paris, August 1942; status unknown. Referenced briefly in ch28 as one item in Marcus's accumulation of losses.

**Ned Harker** — Coldwater Reach resident, Mast Street; father of the second Coldwater man killed in the Ardennes. His son was on Galen Fuller's dragger crew before enlisting; unnamed in the novel.

**Battle of the Bulge** — American popular name for the German Ardennes counteroffensive, December 1944–January 1945; two Coldwater Reach men confirmed killed. Name establishing itself in American usage by early January 1945.

**Brunner** — One of four German POWs assigned to the Pfeiffer farm under the POW labor program. Present at the farm from autumn 1943 to spring 1945. Named only at departure.

**Krebs** — One of four German POWs assigned to the Pfeiffer farm. Present from autumn 1943 to spring 1945. Named only at departure.

**Fischer** — One of four German POWs assigned to the Pfeiffer farm. Present from autumn 1943 to spring 1945. Named only at departure.

**Pfeiffer farm icehouse** — Renovated outbuilding on the Pfeiffer property that served as sleeping quarters for the four German POWs, autumn 1943 to spring 1945. Padlocked and dark after their departure.

**Danny Jessup** — Young Coldwater fisherman, age 19 at spring 1945; registered his first vessel — a twenty-two-foot trap boat — at the spring 1945 harbor opening; assigned berth 7, main float.

**Severance** — Owner of a lobster dragger relocated from Owls Head when that operation closed; registered in the Coldwater fleet at the spring 1945 opening; assigned the outside berth on the east pier.

**Haskell** — Former Coldwater fisherman; his sloop was sold to a Rockland man in November 1944 and removed from the Coldwater harbor registry.

**Hanson brothers** — Regular Coldwater lobstermen who open their season each spring; hold three reserved main float slips, the same positions annually.

**Spring opening (harbor)** — Eira's term for the annual spring harbor inventory: depth soundings, mooring condition checks, and fleet registration review, performed in that order each spring.
