# Chapter 09 — The Breakwater

**POV**: Marcus Klein  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: December 1943  
**Target words**: ~2,800  
**Chapter shape**: Multi-beat (errand near the harbor, chance encounter with Eira, walk home)  
**Ending mode**: Turn  
**Opening texture**: Transit

---

## One-line summary

Marcus passes the breakwater, stops; a chance exchange with Eira reveals to him — by what she does not say — that she knows more about the body than the official record contains.

---

## Setting

Coldwater Reach — the main street, the harbor road, the foot of the commercial wharf, the breakwater end (viewed from outside), the walk home to Elm Street. December: the harbor cold, the breakwater granite black and grey, the harbor empty of summer boats.

---

## Goal / pressure

Marcus is walking to the post office — a domestic errand, not plot-driven. The pressure comes from the coincidence of his route past the breakwater and Eira's harbormaster office being open. The chapter's work is what two careful people discover about each other through what neither one says.

---

## Action beats

**Beat 1 — The breakwater:**
1. Marcus walks down the harbor road on a late-afternoon errand to the post office (he is expecting a letter from Cambridge — the Emergency Committee contact he last wrote in November). He passes the commercial wharf.
2. He pauses at the base of the breakwater. The body was found here, two months ago. The place is just the place: granite blocks, November-December algae, cold water working against it. He looks for two minutes, not dramatically — the way a person looks at the site of something they've been thinking about.
3. He has been thinking about it. Since the conversation with Hannah and the word *Landsmann*, the probable shape of the thing has been assembling itself in his mind against his preference. A Czech man on the Maine coast. The refugee crossings (he doesn't have Varnum's phrase; he has his own knowledge of what those crossings looked like). The Franconian coincidence at the Pfeiffer farm.

**Beat 2 — The harbormaster's office:**
4. He walks past the office and Eira is at the door — she has just stepped out to check something on the dock; their paths intersect at the bottom of the wharf steps.
5. They have seen each other around town but not spoken before. He introduces himself — "Marcus Klein, at the school" — not because she doesn't know who he is, but because it is the correct form. She says her name, which he also knows.
6. He says something about the weather (accurate and genuine; December harbor weather is significant). She agrees with the substance of what he said.
7. He mentions, because it is on his mind and he is standing fifty feet from the breakwater: "I heard about the remains found here last fall. I wondered if there was any identification."
8. She says: "The Coast Guard handled it. Accidental drowning." Her tone is precise and flat. Professionally flat. Not cold — not unfriendly — but compressed in a way that is one register narrower than the topic requires.
9. He says: "Of course." He means this. He is not pressing.
10. She asks if he gets much mail from overseas — making conversation, or because teachers sometimes receive things. He says he gets some. Rarely now.
11. They part. He continues to the post office. His letter from Cambridge is not there.

**Beat 3 — The walk home:**
12. He walks back through town, past the school, toward Elm Street. The December dark is arriving at four-thirty.
13. He is thinking about her tone: "The Coast Guard handled it." The compression. He knows that particular compression — he has used it himself about Sherbrooke, about Vienna, about Paris. It is the compression of a person who has answered the question accurately, fully, and completely within the scope of what they have decided to answer.
14. He has no certainty. He has a recognition. He has been in conversations where people carry knowledge they have decided not to surface, and he knows what that sounds like. He has been the person in that conversation.
15. So: she knows something she hasn't said. And he knows something about what the body's presence suggests that he hasn't said. They are, without having agreed to it, each carrying a piece.
16. He wonders, briefly, if this is the thing that accumulates. He has seen that too — in Vienna, in Paris, in Sherbrooke — the way people carry pieces of the same thing separately until the pieces become different things in different hands.
17. He arrives home. The kitchen is warm. Lillian is doing something with a canned tomato. He hangs up his coat.

---

## Outcome / turn

The turn is Marcus's recognition: Eira is carrying knowledge she's chosen not to surface, the same way he is. They are parallel. The chapter has not plotted anything — no plan is made, no information exchanged — but Marcus now knows there is another partial holder of this matter in town, and he knows where her office is.

---

## New question raised

Two people in this small town are carrying partial knowledge of the same dead man. Neither has spoken. Neither knows exactly what the other holds. What happens when partial knowledge accumulates in separate hands?

---

## Continuity notes

- First pairwise crossing between Marcus and Eira — a chance encounter, not arranged. Introduce them properly; they both know each other by sight and reputation, but have not spoken before.
- Eira's compression is the tell. Marcus recognizes it because he uses it. Do not have the prose name what he's recognizing — let the parallel do its work.
- Marcus's letter from Cambridge: not there. This is small — his contact with the outside world is sparse, slow, uncertain.
- Marcus does not know Varnum's phrase "the refugee crossings." He has his own framework for the same knowledge — from his own experience of those networks — but he has not named it to himself in those terms.
- The breakwater is just a place: granite, algae, cold water. Do not make it atmospheric or symbolic beyond what Marcus would actually observe.
