# Chapter 17 — Mr. Klein's Room

**POV**: Henry Crow (fourth POV)  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: May 1944  
**Target words**: ~1,700  
**Chapter shape**: Multi-beat (morning walk to school; work in the building; observation of Marcus's classroom)  
**Ending mode**: Resolved-beat  
**Opening texture**: Transit

---

## One-line summary

Henry's walk through the school shows him what Marcus's displacement looks like from the outside; he reads the change in the classroom, and then he does his own work, which is its own answer.

---

## Setting

Elm Street to Coldwater High School — the three blocks; the school's hallways and furnace room; Marcus's classroom (empty, between periods or after school). May — the school's furnaces can finally be cut back; the windows open for the first time since October.

---

## Purpose of this fourth-POV chapter

Henry's perspective does what none of the three primary POVs can: it reads Marcus from the outside, with the particular clarity of a man who recognizes a variant of his own condition. Henry has been in Coldwater Reach for twenty years; he has been chief custodian for sixteen. He knows what it is to be in a town on sufferance. He has not named this parallel to Marcus; the chapter's work is to let the reader see what Henry sees, and what he does with it.

---

## Action beats

1. **The walk to school**: Henry leaves Elm Street at 6:45. He knows every house on this route, every board that needs painting, every yard. He has walked this route for sixteen years. The May morning is the best morning of the year — the cold and the light at once.

2. **Who he sees**: The fishermen going down to the harbor. The woman at number 14 who is always at her window when he passes. He nods. She nods. They have exchanged this nod for eight years.

3. **He thinks about the town**: Coldwater Reach has known him for twenty years and still sometimes looks at him as though deciding. He knows this with the patience of someone who has been patient long enough that the patience is its own form of knowledge.

4. **At the school**: He opens the building at 7. He does his rounds: the furnaces (which can be banked down now, finally — he's been managing the east wing's flue all winter), the hallway floors, the boiler room log. This is precise, mechanical knowledge; he has it entirely.

5. **Marcus's classroom**: Between his rounds he passes Marcus's classroom. The door is open — or he opens it to check the window (there's a latch that sticks). He looks in.

6. **What he sees**: The German grammar texts have been moved from the shelf to the desk. Not packed away — in use. The window is open for the first time since October. On the desk: a composition book with student names visible on the cover (not private; just papers). A pencil with a broken tip.

7. **He has been watching Marcus change**: Over the winter, since the school board meeting, something in Marcus has been different. Henry doesn't name it as loosening or arriving; he would say, if asked (and no one has asked), that Marcus has stopped holding himself quite as far from the door. He is still careful; he is careful in a different way.

8. **He thinks about the school board meeting**: He sat in that room along the wall. He has sat in rooms along the wall before, for different reasons, with different people at the table. He knows what it costs. He also knows what Marcus's particular version of the cost is — and that it's not the same as his own version, though they rhyme.

9. **He does not say any of this to Marcus.** He has learned to hold things without saying them. This is not suppression. It is the skill of a man who has watched his own observations become invisible to the people who benefit from them.

10. **The furnace room**: He finishes the morning rounds. He bleeds a fitting on the south radiator that has been slow to heat all winter. He makes a note about the pipe for summer repair. He does this work exactly and completely.

---

## Outcome / resolved-beat

Henry does his work. That's the resolved-beat. Not a revelation, not a confrontation — he looks at Marcus's room, holds what he knows, and goes back to the furnace room. His work is what he does with what he has, and he does it well. This is not presented as heroic or tragic; it is presented as his day, exactly.

---

## New question raised

What does Henry know about what Marcus is becoming — and does he have a name for what he himself already is? What would he say to Marcus if the parallel were ever spoken aloud?

---

## Continuity notes

- Henry's voice: direct, warm when he's at home, more reserved in the public-facing work. In this chapter he is at work, so the register is precise and observational, not intimate. His interior is present but not given to self-declaration.
- The parallel with Marcus: both men in this town on sufferance, both living with the specific form of being-known-but-not-recognized that their respective positions produce. Do not name this parallel; let it run through Henry's observations.
- Henry does not have a son overseas; his emotional throughline here is the work itself and his reading of Marcus.
- The furnace room detail is accurate: chief custodian of a WPA-era school would manage coal furnaces, boiler systems, and maintenance schedules. Henry's expertise is real and specific.
- This chapter is shorter than primary-POV chapters by design. It is a lens, not an arc. Do not extend Henry's interiority beyond what the lens needs.
- Resolved-beat: the resolution is Henry completing his rounds — not an emotional resolution, but a completion of the day's work. This is deliberate.
