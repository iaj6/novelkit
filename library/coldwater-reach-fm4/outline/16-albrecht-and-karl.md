# Chapter 16 — Albrecht and Karl

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: May 1944  
**Target words**: ~2,700  
**Chapter shape**: Interiority-heavy (Hannah witnesses a conversation she cannot fully hear; the interior is in the witnessing)  
**Ending mode**: Literary-fade  
**Opening texture**: Observation

---

## One-line summary

Hannah overhears Albrecht and Karl speaking the same Franconian dialect across the fence; something in her father comes unstuck; the conversation is completely ordinary, and not.

---

## Setting

Pfeiffer farm, May 1944. Planting season — the fields being turned, the soil dark and giving. The fence between the near field and the barn yard. Early morning, before the full work day starts.

---

## Goal / pressure

Hannah's goal is observation — she doesn't plan to watch, she just finds herself watching. The pressure is what she sees: her father and Karl, in the same language, doing something that has no name for it. Not reconciliation. Not friendship. Something more like recognition across a wall that she was told existed.

---

## Action beats

1. **Early morning**: She is out before the full light, checking the near fence (the frost-heave is still pushing posts in some sections). She hears voices — two voices, German, from around the side of the barn where the field fence runs.

2. **She stops.** She can see through the gap between the barn wall and the fence. Her father is at the fence; Karl is on the field side, near his morning row of work. They are speaking.

3. **The language**: It is her father's German, which is the German of home — but it sounds different in the May morning than it does at the dinner table. Out here it has the dialect's full vowels. And Karl is answering in the same dialect — the same deep-A sounds, the particular way the final consonants work.

4. **What they are talking about**: Soil. They are talking about the soil. Albrecht is describing the drainage problem in the north field — the clay subsoil that holds water and makes the planting difficult. Karl is comparing this to soil in his home region — the different drainage, the clay mix in Franconia, what the farmers there do with the early-spring wet.

5. **She cannot hear every word.** She catches: the comparison between soils; a mention of a specific technique for raised ridges; Albrecht asking something she doesn't catch; Karl's answer, which includes the name of a town she doesn't recognize.

6. **Albrecht's reaction to the town name**: He goes still — not the sharp pause of the first fence moment (Chapter 5), but a slower stillness. He looks at Karl. He says something she cannot hear. Karl nods.

7. **They stand there for a moment.** Then Albrecht turns back to the field. Karl goes back to his row. The conversation is over. Nothing has happened; everything has happened.

8. **Hannah in the barn**: She does the morning milking as usual. She is thinking about what she saw. Her father knows where Karl is from — not from Franconia generically, but specifically, because the town name meant something to him. They share something she has never been told about. The geography of her father's first country is more specific than she knew.

9. **German threading**: *"Die Erde hier ist schwer."* — The earth here is heavy. She thinks of this phrase again, from nowhere. Whether it is Albrecht's thought about the Maine clay or something older and heavier, she can't tell.

10. **Magda, at the window**: When Hannah comes back toward the house, she looks up and sees Magda standing at the kitchen window, looking out at the field. Looking toward where Albrecht was standing. Hannah doesn't know if Magda saw the conversation. Magda goes back inside.

11. **The morning continues.** The planting work. The Holsteins in the field. Lonzo arrives at 7 a.m. and nothing in Lonzo's demeanor suggests he saw anything.

---

## Outcome / literary-fade

No confrontation, no revelation spoken aloud. The chapter ends on the May morning opening up — the last frost risk past, Albrecht back at his regular work, Karl in the far field, Hannah going to school with mud on her boots. Her father has something in common with the prisoner that she is not part of. The chapter fades on the field, the light, the ordinary day continuing around what just happened.

---

## New question raised

What town did Karl name that stopped Albrecht? What did Albrecht know — or know of — that the town name confirmed? And does Magda know? Has she always known?

---

## Continuity notes

- This chapter does not resolve what the town name was or what Albrecht knew. That is deliberate. The reader has Marcus's probable picture (Chapter 15) to assemble against this scene.
- Hannah's German is fluent but she is positioned at the edge of hearing; she catches content but misses key details. This is accurate to her position (around the barn corner) and to the novel's structure (no single character gets the full picture).
- Magda at the window: her presence is visual only. She may have seen; she may not have. The prose does not confirm. This is Magda's function — she watches, she witnesses, she doesn't explain.
- The soil conversation is completely plausible as an actual conversation between a Maine dairy farmer and a Franconian prisoner during spring planting. It is not a coded exchange; it is two men talking about what they know in the language they share. The resonance is all below the soil conversation itself.
- *"Die Erde hier ist schwer"* recurs from Chapter 8. Hannah doesn't know where it comes from; it surfaces again here because the earth is literally heavy and because she is thinking about weight.
