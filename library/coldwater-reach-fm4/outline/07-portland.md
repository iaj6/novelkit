# Chapter 07 — Portland

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: December 1943  
**Target words**: ~2,800  
**Chapter shape**: Transit (bus to Portland, meeting, bus back — travel as the structure)  
**Ending mode**: Resolved-beat  
**Opening texture**: Transit

---

## One-line summary

Eira attends the quarterly harbormaster coordination meeting in Portland; another harbormaster mentions the Coldwater body; she returns unchanged, which is a kind of resolution.

---

## Setting

The bus from Coldwater Reach south to Portland. The Portland harbor coordination office — a municipal building near the waterfront. The return bus in the dark. December — the coast road, the light going early.

---

## Goal / pressure

A professional errand: the quarterly coordination meeting covers winter fuel allocations for the harbor, harbor security protocols, maintenance of the war-period coastal communications network. Eira attends because it is her job and she does her job. The hidden pressure: the body on the breakwater is in the regional record, and it may come up among the other harbormasters. She is going to have to be the Coldwater Reach harbormaster, which means being the person who found the body and filed the official report, in a room full of colleagues.

---

## Action beats

**Beat 1 — The bus south:**
1. The bus from Coldwater through Rockland, Camden, down to Portland. Four hours each way. Eira has a window seat and watches the coast road. The December light lasts until about four.
2. She has her ledger with her — not Owen's, hers — and she reviews the winter mooring allocations and the fuel numbers. She has a proposal for a small increase in the fishing fleet's fuel ration, supported by her catch records. This is the work she is going to present.
3. She is the only woman on the bus going to this meeting. She will be the only woman in the meeting room. She has been to three of these; the men have largely stopped looking when she enters. "Largely" is doing work in that sentence.

**Beat 2 — The meeting:**
4. Twelve harbormasters from Maine coastal towns. A Coast Guard liaison (not Parsons — a different officer, regional). Two men from the fuel allocation office.
5. The first hour: winter fuel rations. Eira presents her numbers; they are received without comment. She knows her proposal will be noted. She does not know if it will be approved.
6. Midway through the meeting, the Coast Guard liaison runs through the quarterly incident summary. He mentions two matters from the fall — a vessel fire in Camden, a collision in the Sheepscot River. And: "Unidentified remains, Coldwater Reach breakwater, late October. Classified accidental drowning, case closed."
7. **The harbormaster from Pemaquid**: an older man, Philip Varnum, who has run his harbor since before the war. He says: "I had one like that in '42. Came in thinking it was a downed pilot. Turned out to be a fisherman from New Brunswick." He pauses. "Your man was different? The clothing, they said."
8. **Eira**: "European cut. Not military." A beat. "Coast Guard handled it."
9. Varnum nods. "Likely a passenger off something that didn't file a manifest," he says. "There's more of that than people think. The refugee crossings."
10. She says: "Yes." She doesn't say anything more. Varnum has named something she didn't have a word for before — *the refugee crossings* — and she files it.
11. The meeting continues. The fuel allocation for the winter is approved as proposed. The meeting breaks at four.

**Beat 3 — The return bus:**
12. Dark by the time the bus leaves Portland. She watches the coast road going north, now visible only in the bus headlights' throw. She thinks about the fuel numbers, the winter mooring allocations, the Sheepscot collision report.
13. She thinks about Varnum's phrase: *the refugee crossings*. More of that than people think. A man in European clothes, not military. She has been assuming he was alone, isolated, inexplicable. The possibility that he was part of a larger movement — people moving through unofficial channels — is new.
14. She gets off the bus in Coldwater at 9 p.m. The harbor is dark and working. She walks to the office, checks the night log, goes home.

---

## Outcome / resolved-beat

The professional question resolves: the fuel allocation is approved; she has done her harbormaster work in that room and it was received. The personal question — the body, the dock, her silence — returns to Coldwater with her exactly as it left. She has gained one thing: the word *refugee crossings*, from Varnum. She does not do anything with this yet. The resolved-beat is the professional one; the personal question is unchanged.

---

## New question raised

If the man was part of a refugee movement — not alone, but traveling — then there are other people who know something about him. There are networks. Someone expected him to arrive somewhere.

---

## Continuity notes

- Philip Varnum (harbormaster, Pemaquid) introduced as a functional secondary character for this chapter; he may be referenced later but does not need to appear again.
- The phrase "the refugee crossings" is Varnum's; it is the first time anyone near Eira has offered a non-spy explanation for the body. She registers this.
- The fuel allocation subplot is real: fuel rations for Maine fishing fleets were a genuine wartime constraint. Her proposal being approved is a professional win rendered without fanfare.
- The December coastal road: render the physical reality of bus travel on the Maine coast in wartime (fuel rationing affects bus schedules too; this bus runs twice weekly). 
- Eira is the only woman at the meeting. This fact is registered by the prose through her body's awareness and the small calibrations she makes — not through complaint or commentary.
