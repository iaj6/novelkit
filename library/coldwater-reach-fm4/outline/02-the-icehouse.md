# Chapter 02 — The Icehouse

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: Late October 1943  
**Target words**: ~2,700  
**Chapter shape**: Multi-beat (morning farm work, school day, evening at home)  
**Ending mode**: Literary-fade  
**Opening texture**: Routine-procedure

---

## One-line summary

The prisoners have been on the farm three weeks; Hannah observes Karl for the first time in close proximity; Magda speaks one sentence and then goes silent again.

---

## Setting

Pfeiffer dairy farm, three miles inland on Route 9. The barn, the near field, the icehouse (now whitewashed inside, partitioned for sleeping), the farmhouse kitchen. October — corn stubble, frost mornings, the silage smell strong.

---

## Goal / pressure

Hannah's goal is the ordinary one: get through the day. The pressure is what the day contains — the prisoners as a constant presence she must navigate around without knowing the rules; her father's silence; her mother's deeper withdrawal. This chapter establishes the farm as a world unto itself before the story does anything with that world.

---

## Action beats

**Beat 1 — Morning milking:**
1. Hannah rises at 5 a.m. and goes to the barn. Twenty-two Holsteins. She works efficiently — this is her body's knowledge, not thought. Her father is already there but they don't speak much; his German-only rule applies even here.
2. Through the barn window she can see the icehouse. A lamp is lit inside. The prisoners are awake.
3. Lonzo Reed arrives at 7 a.m. and takes his post at the barn door. He nods to her. She nods back. This exchange is their whole relationship.
4. The four prisoners come out of the icehouse and go to the field. She watches from the barn door. Three of them she can identify by gait now: one is heavyset (Brunner), one walks with a slight list (Krebs), one is old enough to have a bald patch (Fischer). The fourth — Karl Reinmar — is the youngest and the smallest. He walks quickly, like someone trying to keep up.

**Beat 2 — The dropped tool:**
5. She is bringing feed to the far end of the barn when she hears something drop near the fence line. A small cultivating tool has fallen into the gap between the fence and the barn wall — inside the fence, but where Karl, working in the near field, cannot reach it without going around.
6. She picks it up. Karl is standing on the other side of the fence, two feet from her. She has not been this close to any of them. He is shorter than she thought. His face is unremarkable — young, tired, a raw wind-chapped quality. He says, in German: "Danke." She hands the tool through the fence gap without speaking.
7. That's all. Lonzo is reading the paper on his stool. Nothing has happened.

**Beat 3 — School:**
8. She walks the three miles to Coldwater High School. In Latin class (Marcus Klein's class, though we don't focus on Marcus here — just his classroom as routine background). The lesson is on indirect discourse.
9. At lunch someone has heard about the body on the breakwater — it's been town news for a week. A friend says "Coast Guard thinks it's a German agent." Hannah eats her sandwich and doesn't say anything.

**Beat 4 — Evening, the farmhouse:**
10. After the evening milking, she sits at the kitchen table doing homework. Albrecht is at the far end reading a newspaper (Bangor Daily, German-language edition he gets by post). Magda is at the stove.
11. Albrecht says something to himself in German — a single word, something under his breath. He is reading about the war in Europe. He doesn't look up.
12. Magda, at the stove, says a full sentence in German: *"Wir hätten früher gehen sollen."* (We should have left sooner.) Albrecht looks up. They look at each other across the room. Three seconds, four. He goes back to the paper. She goes back to the stove.
13. Hannah writes this sentence in the margin of her homework paper without thinking, then crosses it out.

---

## Outcome / turn

No dramatic turn. Accumulation. Hannah has been close enough to Karl to see that he is a tired nineteen-year-old. Her mother has spoken for the first time in weeks, and what she said is about leaving. The farm is sealed in with these people and these silences.

---

## New question raised

What is Karl Reinmar, to this farm and to her? What did Magda mean by leaving sooner — leaving Bavaria before the war, or leaving something else?

---

## Continuity notes

- Introduces Hannah's voice: interior and porous, sensory-grounded in farm life, German threading in at moments of affect.
- Introduces the four prisoners: Brunner, Krebs, Fischer, Karl Reinmar. Only Karl is named in the prose at this point (the others identified by visual habit, not name).
- Introduces Lonzo Reed: described, not named yet (or named once, functionally). His quality of watching more than he appears to watch is present from the start.
- The prisoners arrived approximately three weeks before this chapter — they are settled, not new. Don't render the arrival; render the established rhythm.
- Magda's sentence: *"Wir hätten früher gehen sollen."* — not translated in the prose. The reader absorbs it from context (Hannah writes it down, crosses it out).
- The body on the breakwater is town gossip in this chapter; Hannah has no special knowledge of it. It exists as background noise here.
- Establish that Hannah and her father speak German at home (his rule) and English everywhere else. Her German is fluent; her English at home is unspoken.
- The literary-fade ending should come from the sensory close: silage smell from the icehouse direction, the barn sounds at night, the lamp going out.
