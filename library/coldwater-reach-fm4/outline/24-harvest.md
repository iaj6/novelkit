# Chapter 24 — Harvest

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: September–October 1944  
**Target words**: ~2,700  
**Chapter shape**: Multi-beat (harvest work; the fence meetings; Albrecht's warning)  
**Ending mode**: Mid-action  
**Opening texture**: Routine-procedure

---

## One-line summary

Full harvest on the farm; Hannah's brief fence exchanges with Karl have become a quiet pattern; Albrecht warns her in German to be careful; she doesn't yet know if she'll listen.

---

## Setting

Pfeiffer farm, September and early October 1944. Harvest season — the most labor-intensive time, the days long, the farm at its most alive. Corn, the kitchen garden's last produce, the late-hay baling. The prisoners work the hardest they've worked all year.

---

## Goal / pressure

Hannah's arc is moving toward a decision she doesn't know she has to make yet. The pressure in this chapter comes from two directions: the fence meetings with Karl, which have become a comfortable transgression she has been telling herself is not a transgression; and her father's warning, which requires her to acknowledge that he has been watching.

---

## Action beats

**Beat 1 — The harvest:**
1. Harvest on the Pfeiffer farm: all four prisoners working from dawn, Lonzo at his post. The corn comes in first — twenty-two rows, two to three days of work. Then the late hay. Then the kitchen garden's last produce (squash, potatoes, the few remaining tomatoes).
2. Karl works the hardest of the four. He has been doing this since June in a way that the other prisoners — older, more fatigued — have not. He is burning something off; she can see it in the pace.
3. Lonzo notices this too. She can tell from the way he looks at Karl sometimes — not with suspicion, but with something closer to recognition. An older man watching a young man run at the work the way young men run at things when they don't have another way to be.

**Beat 2 — The fence meetings:**
4. Over the summer, Hannah and Karl have developed a pattern: when she is working the south fence section and he is near that part of the field, they exchange a few words. Sometimes in German — his dialect, which she can mostly follow; her German, which he follows completely. Sometimes in English, which he is getting better at steadily.
5. What they talk about: the farm, mostly. The soil, what's growing, the cows. He knows farming; she knows this farming. They are two agricultural people comparing notes across a fence. This is what she tells herself it is.
6. She also knows it's something else, and that she is choosing to call it notes-comparing because that is easier. She has the carved bird in her coat pocket still, most days. She has not given it back.

**Beat 3 — Albrecht:**
7. One evening in early October, after the day's harvest work, Hannah is in the kitchen doing dishes. Albrecht comes in and sits at the table. He says, in German: "You should be careful."
8. She says: "About what?" She knows about what.
9. He says: "The fence." Just that.
10. She turns from the dishes. She looks at him. He is looking at the table. He says: "I'm not angry. I'm telling you to be careful."
11. She asks: "Have you said anything to Lonzo?" He says: "No." A pause. "And I don't plan to. But Lonzo is not the only person who walks past this farm."
12. She goes back to the dishes. He goes to his chair. They don't discuss it further.

**Beat 4 — What Lonzo is not doing:**
13. The next morning she watches Lonzo more carefully than usual. He is reading his paper, sitting on his stool, doing exactly what he always does. He looks up once and catches her watching him. He nods. She nods. He goes back to the paper.
14. She thinks: Lonzo knows. He has known for months, probably. He hasn't done anything with it. What does that mean? Is she in his report? Is she not in his report? She doesn't know.

---

## Outcome / mid-action

The chapter ends without resolution — mid-action in the sense that Hannah has received her father's warning and has not yet decided what to do with it. She is at the fence the next day, and Karl is on the other side, and they don't speak but they are there. The question of whether she stops is open. This is the mid-action ending: not crisis, but forward pressure with the outcome unknown.

---

## New question raised

What will Hannah do with Albrecht's warning? And what does Albrecht know — or suspect — about Karl that makes this specifically about "be careful" rather than "stop"?

## Continuity notes

- Hannah has been having fence exchanges with Karl since approximately June (after Chapter 21's "Karl Speaks"). These meetings are brief, agricultural in topic, never physically transgressive — but they are unauthorized in the sense that a female civilian having unescorted conversations with a POW is outside the spirit of the escort arrangement.
- Karl's improving English: by September-October, he can hold a short conversation. He still makes errors; the errors are consistent with someone learning from newspapers.
- Albrecht's warning: "be careful," not "stop." This distinction is important. He is protecting her, not forbidding her. He also presumably has reasons for not going to Lonzo or to any authority, which connects to the assembled picture Marcus has (the probable Pfeiffer farm connection to Horak's network).
- Lonzo's non-action: established in Chapter 10, consistent here. He knows and is choosing not to act. This is his judgment call, as established in his chapter.
- The carved bird in her pocket: still there. This is the object the chapter will return to in Chapter 29.
