# Chapter 18 — What Language

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: Late May 1944  
**Target words**: ~2,900  
**Chapter shape**: Single scene (Eira comes to the school; their exchange)  
**Ending mode**: Turn  
**Opening texture**: Dialogue

---

## One-line summary

Eira brings Marcus the sounds she heard on the dock that night; he identifies them as Czech; the name Horak confirms it; they are both holding partial pictures of the same dead man, and they know it now.

---

## Setting

Coldwater High School — the side entrance near the rowing club mooring, then outside, then briefly in the hallway. Late May — the school year almost over, the afternoon warm enough to stand outside without coats.

---

## Goal / pressure

Eira has been carrying the dock knowledge for seven months. She has Horak's name and his Czech nationality from the official letter. She has the fragment of language she heard on the dock — something she's never mentioned to anyone — and she has a growing sense that Marcus Klein, a Viennese polyglot, might be able to tell her what language it was. The official reason for the visit is a mooring rights question for the school's rowing dock. The real reason is the language question. The pressure: she is going to have to say, out loud, that she was on the dock that night.

---

## Action beats

1. **The official reason**: She comes to the school at 3:30 p.m. on a Tuesday — after classes. She has an actual mooring question: the school's rowing club has a small dock downstream of the commercial wharf, and the registration on the mooring anchor is out of date. She knows this is Marcus's building and someone will know the right person to ask. This is true and also a reason to be at the school.

2. **She finds Marcus**: He is leaving his classroom. She says she's looking for the person who manages the school dock. He says he doesn't know the dock matters but he can walk her to it — it's around the east side of the building.

3. **They walk to the dock**: It is a small, unpretentious dock, clearly used by students learning to row. She looks at the mooring anchor — it has an old tag on it, she makes a note — and she has the actual information she needed.

4. **She turns to him**: She says: "I want to ask you something else. Not about the dock."

5. **She explains, briefly**: In October, on the night before the body came in, she was doing a late mooring check. She saw a man on the commercial dock. She heard him say something — a few words, quietly. Not English. She didn't understand it.

6. **She tries to reproduce it**: She has held this in her memory for seven months. She gives him what she has — a few syllables, the shape of them, the way the stress fell. It is imperfect. She knows it's imperfect.

7. **He listens**. He asks her to say it again. He does not show what he's thinking. He asks: "Can you give me more?" She gives him what she has.

8. **He says**: "I think it's Czech. I can't be certain from that fragment, but the consonant cluster and the stress pattern — I would say Czech, possibly Slovak, but Czech is more likely."

9. **She says**: "The Coast Guard identified the body as Jan Václav Horak. Czech national." She is watching his face.

10. **His reaction**: He nods. He says: "Yes. That would be consistent." She asks: "Consistent with what?" He pauses. He says: "With a certain kind of person, in a certain kind of movement, in wartime." He doesn't say more than this. She doesn't ask him to.

11. **What she knows now**: The man on the dock was Czech. He was speaking Czech. He was not a German agent, not a U-boat case. He was a displaced person, probably, moving through unofficial channels. Someone was with him. She still doesn't know who.

12. **She asks**: "Did you know him?" He says: "No." A beat. "I knew the kind of person he might have been." She says: "So did I, after a fashion." They are standing by the rowing dock in the late May afternoon and both of them are telling the truth in very precise ways.

13. **She says**: "Thank you." He says: "Of course." She goes back to the harbor.

---

## Outcome / turn

The turn: they have both named their partial knowledge to each other, obliquely, without full disclosure. Eira now knows the language she heard on the dock was Czech; she now knows what Horak was. She has not told Marcus what she saw on the dock — she has told him she was there, that she heard something. He has told her what kind of person Horak might have been without explaining how he knows. They have traded partial pictures without completing each other's.

---

## New question raised

Now that Eira knows the language, and Marcus has confirmed the probable shape of what Horak was, does Eira carry the dock knowledge differently? And Marcus — does he want to know more, or has he decided his piece is the piece he has?

---

## Continuity notes

- This is the pairwise crossing between Eira and Marcus — their only real conversation in the novel. It should feel earned by what both characters have been carrying since Chapter 9.
- Eira tells Marcus she was on the dock — not as a confession, but as a statement of fact needed to get to the language question. She does not tell him she chose not to report what she saw.
- Marcus does not tell Eira what he has assembled (the probable network logic, the Pfeiffer farm connection, the Franconian dialect). He tells her the language and "the kind of person." This is enough.
- The rowing dock is the physical setting that earns the conversation — a neutral, outdoor space that gives them room to be overheard if they're not careful (they're not overheard; the school is quiet). It also lets them look away from each other if needed.
- "So did I, after a fashion" — Eira's line. She means: she knew what it looked like to be a person moving through the world without belonging to it. She does not explain this.
- The chapter's ending is the turn: they each have more than they had before, and neither has the full picture. Their parallel is now acknowledged, quietly, without being named as such.
