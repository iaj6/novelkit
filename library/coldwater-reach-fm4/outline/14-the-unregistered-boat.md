# Chapter 14 — The Unregistered Boat

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: April 1944  
**Target words**: ~2,800  
**Chapter shape**: Multi-beat (spring harbor work, the anomaly, the Coast Guard letter)  
**Ending mode**: Mid-action  
**Opening texture**: Routine-procedure

---

## One-line summary

Spring harbor activity surfaces an unregistered boat; an official letter from the Coast Guard names the body for the first time — Jan Václav Horak, Czechoslovak national — and the name opens more than it closes.

---

## Setting

Coldwater Reach harbor — the commercial wharf, the registry office, the dock. April: the spring thaw has opened the harbor fully, ice gone, the fleet resuming. The cold is still present but a different cold — working-season cold, not locked-in cold.

---

## Goal / pressure

The spring opening is Eira's professional peak — the harbor coming back to life, registrations, mooring assignments, depth soundings. Her goal is the ordered restart of the season. The pressure comes from two anomalies that arrive on the same day: an unregistered boat she can't account for, and an official letter that gives the dead man a name.

---

## Action beats

**Beat 1 — Spring harbor:**
1. The April morning: the commercial wharf active for the first time since February. She is going through the spring mooring assignments — the fleet has shifted since autumn (two boats gone for good, their owners not coming back; two new entries from fishermen who moved here from further up the coast).
2. She registers mooring changes, checks the depth soundings from the winter survey, updates the tide log for the new season. This is satisfying work. The harbor in April has a specific quality: the cold and the activity at once.
3. She knows every registered boat by sight. She can walk the moored fleet and name each one without consulting the registry. She has done this for eight years.

**Beat 2 — The unregistered boat:**
4. At the end of the commercial dock, a small lobster boat is moored at a post she would not have assigned it to. Registration number on the stern: she checks it against the Maine registry in her office. Not there.
5. She asks Hector Brann (who is nearby rigging): whose boat is that? He shrugs — "Think it's someone from Owls Head. Been there a few days."
6. She goes back and checks the Owls Head harbor list (she has contacts with several coastal offices). The number doesn't match any boat from Owls Head either.
7. She makes a notation in her log: unregistered vessel, dock 7, first observed April 14th, registration not on record. She will follow up.
8. Two days later, the boat is gone. She enters: "Unregistered vessel, dock 7. Departed approx. April 16th, unseen." She will send an inquiry to the regional office but has no expectation of response.

**Beat 3 — The letter:**
9. The same day the boat disappears, she finds a letter in the official mail — Coast Guard regional office, Portland. A follow-up communication regarding the October 1943 remains.
10. The letter states: through liaison with Allied civilian records, the remains have been identified as those of Jan Václav Horak, Czechoslovak national, status: displaced person, last known location: Halifax, Nova Scotia, October 1942. No next of kin information available. The official record is amended to note his identity; the classification of the death remains accidental drowning.
11. She reads this twice. Jan Václav Horak. Czech. Halifax, 1942. He had been in Nova Scotia a year before he came to this breakwater.
12. She sits at the chart table. She knows more now and understands less. A Czech displaced person in Halifax in 1942, dead on the Coldwater breakwater in 1943. Between those two facts: a year of movement she has no account of.

---

## Outcome / mid-action

The chapter ends without resolution — mid-action, in the sense that Eira has a name and it has opened rather than closed the question. The unregistered boat appeared and vanished; Horak's name has arrived but doesn't explain what he was doing at the dock. The chapter is explicitly unresolved: she knows more and the knowing is a new pressure, not a relief.

---

## New question raised

Jan Václav Horak was a displaced Czech national, in Halifax a year before his death. What was he moving toward — or running from? And is the unregistered boat connected, or coincidence?

---

## Continuity notes

- **Jan Václav Horak**: Czechoslovak national, displaced person, last known location Halifax, Nova Scotia, October 1942. This is now canon. Record it.
- The unregistered boat: appears April 14th, gone by April 16th, registration not on record with any coastal office Eira checks. She does not identify it. It may be a coincidence; the novel does not resolve this. Do not have Eira conclude it's connected to Horak. She notes it; the reader can connect it or not.
- The mid-action ending: Eira has a name but no explanation. She is sitting at the chart table at the end of the chapter. She has not made a decision about what to do with the name.
- The letter's routing: through "liaison with Allied civilian records" — this is accurate; by 1944 there was interagency cooperation on displaced person identification. The mechanism is plausible without being specific.
- Halifax as Horak's last known location: he was in Canada, then moved south to Maine. The refugee crossing implied by Varnum's phrase in Chapter 7 now has a plausible route.
