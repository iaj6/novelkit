# Chapter 01 — The Dock, and After

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: Late October 1943, night into the following morning  
**Target words**: ~2,800  
**Chapter shape**: Single scene (two continuous movements: the dock night, then the morning after)  
**Ending mode**: Turn  
**Opening texture**: Routine-procedure

---

## One-line summary

Eira sees a man on the commercial dock late at night and says nothing; by the following morning his body is on the breakwater.

---

## Setting

The Coldwater Reach commercial wharf and breakwater. October — a three-day blow has been subsiding; the harbor is still rough. The harbormaster's office at the foot of the wharf. The breakwater's granite end, facing open water.

---

## Goal / pressure

Eira is doing a standard late mooring check after the blow — a professional errand, unremarkable. The pressure comes from what she sees, and from the choice she makes in the moment of seeing it.

---

## Action beats

1. **Eira leaves the office at approximately 11 p.m.** after a final weather check. The blow is subsiding; she wants to confirm the lobster smacks are secured. Professional habit, not emergency. She carries a lantern.

2. **At the far end of the commercial dock**, she sees a figure — a man, sitting on the bollard or standing at the dock's edge, not working, not from any boat she can identify. He is wearing a coat that is wrong for the harbor — European cut, not oilskin. He is speaking quietly with a second figure she cannot make out. The second person has their back to her and leaves quickly, walking inland, before she reaches them.

3. **She slows. Then stops.** The man on the bollard doesn't notice her, or pretends not to. He is watching the water. She cannot see his face well in the lantern light. Her professional instinct runs a checklist: trespassing on commercial dock is a violation; unauthorized persons are to be challenged. Her other instinct, or her body, says: leave this alone.

4. **She turns back.** She checks the far smack, confirms the mooring, and walks back to the office. She does not enter anything in the tide log. She goes home.

5. **Morning**: She is back at the office before dawn for the 6 a.m. tide reading. Everything normal. At mid-morning, Hector Brann (lobsterman) comes to the office door: "Something on the breakwater end. You'll want to see."

6. **She goes out to the breakwater.** The man from the dock is there — face-up in the granite seam, clearly dead, the sea having brought him around the harbor mouth and lodged him. She checks for pulse. She notes what she can see: European clothing, no papers visible, calluses on the hands that are wrong for a fisherman. His face is blue-white from cold exposure. She goes back to the office and calls the Coast Guard on the field telephone.

7. **While waiting for the Coast Guard**, she stands at the breakwater and looks at him and knows he is the man from the dock. She knew it when Hector said "something on the breakwater" — she knew it the way you know a thing you've been holding at the edge of your mind.

8. **The Coast Guard arrives** — Lt. Arthur Parsons, brisk and thorough. He takes notes. He asks if she found anything unusual in the harbor last night. She says: the fleet is in, the blow is over, nothing she needs to note. She hears herself say this.

9. **The body is removed.** Lt. Parsons says they'll be in touch.

---

## Outcome / turn

The chapter ends with Eira back at her chart table, the harbor quiet, the breakwater empty. She opens the tide log to the previous night's entry. There is nothing there about the dock, the man, the second figure, the coat. She reads her own handwriting and understands exactly what it doesn't say. This is the turn: not dramatic, not loud — she understands what she has done. The official record and her knowledge are already two different things.

---

## New question raised

What does her silence make her? What obligation does the knowledge of the dock impose — or is it the kind of knowledge that dissolves under the weight of what can be proven?

---

## Continuity notes

- Introduces Eira's voice and register: spare, procedural, physical feelings arriving as professional observations.
- Establishes the body as the spine object. He has no name yet, no papers.
- Introduces Lt. Arthur Parsons (Coast Guard) as a recurring official contact.
- Introduces the dock knowledge as Eira's load-bearing secret. It is *not* explained directly; the reader understands it from the gap in the log.
- The second figure on the dock: not identified, not described, essential. Their back was turned; Eira could not see their face. They walked inland.
- The body's clothing is European-cut and non-fisherman. His hands have calluses in the wrong places.
- Establishes the breakwater's physical reality: granite blocks, north shore, approximately 200 feet, harbor mouth exposure.
- Do not have Eira think explicitly about what her silence means. The chapter earns this through the gap in the log, not through interior analysis.
