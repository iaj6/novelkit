# Chapter 05 — Fifty Kilometers

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: November 1943  
**Target words**: ~2,700  
**Chapter shape**: Multi-beat (farm morning, a moment at the fence, the schoolday, home again)  
**Ending mode**: Literary-fade  
**Opening texture**: Routine-procedure

---

## One-line summary

Karl says a phrase in German that stops Albrecht mid-swing; Hannah asks her father for the first time where in Bavaria he was born, and learns the town name.

---

## Setting

Pfeiffer farm and Coldwater High School. November — the corn stubble grey, first light frosts, the cows' breath visible in the mornings. The fence between the field and the near barn becomes the chapter's recurring boundary.

---

## Goal / pressure

Hannah is still watching. The chapter's pressure comes from noticing: she sees something happen between Albrecht and Karl that she doesn't have language for. Her question to her father at the end is the first time she has reached across his silence in months. The goal is simply to keep watching until she understands enough to ask.

---

## Action beats

**Beat 1 — The fence moment:**
1. Hannah and Albrecht are repairing fence posts on the near field's south side — a morning task, the two of them working in their usual near-silence. Albrecht swings the post-driver; Hannah holds the post.
2. Karl is working in the field nearby, close to the fence line. He's moving rocks from a section that needs to be tilled next spring. He mutters something to himself as he lifts a particularly stubborn one — a complaint, an oath, something in German.
3. The phrase is regional — Franconian, a specific idiom that would not come from a Berlin or Munich speaker. The vowels are shaped wrong for standard German. Albrecht hears it. He stops mid-swing.
4. He does not speak or turn. He holds the post-driver above the post for two full seconds — Hannah counts without meaning to. Then he swings. Then he takes the driver off the post and walks to the next one.
5. Karl doesn't seem to notice. He moves the rock.
6. Hannah watched her father's pause. She files it.

**Beat 2 — After the morning work, before school:**
7. She is washing her hands at the pump. Albrecht is doing the same beside her. She says: "Vater, where exactly were you born? In Bavaria — which town?"
8. He looks at her. The question surprises him — she has not asked this before. He says the name: Feuchtwangen.
9. She doesn't know it. She says: "Is it near Ansbach?" (She has seen the name Ansbach on a map at school — it is the nearest city-name she knows from Franconia.) He says: "Forty kilometers or so, south." He doesn't ask why she wants to know.
10. That's the whole conversation.

**Beat 3 — School:**
11. In the afternoon, at school, she looks at a wall map in the geography classroom. She finds Ansbach, then Feuchtwangen. She finds Ansbach again and thinks about the fifty kilometers in both directions — her father's town and wherever Karl is from. The map doesn't tell her Karl's town; she doesn't know it. But the geography is visible: this is a small region.
12. She mentions the map to her friend Ruth without explaining why. Ruth says: "Bavaria? You doing a report?" Hannah says no.

**Beat 4 — Evening:**
13. Evening milking. Karl is filing back to the icehouse at the end of the work day. He passes within ten feet of where Hannah is standing at the barn door. He doesn't look at her.
14. But she notices: he nods to Lonzo Reed — a small, unforced nod. Lonzo nods back, eyes still on his paper. The prisoners and the guard have settled into a rhythm. She wonders what Lonzo's version of all this is.
15. She goes in for supper. At the table, Albrecht says grace in German — he always has. Magda is present and quiet. The radio in the corner is on but low.

---

## Outcome / literary-fade

No dramatic turn. Accumulation: Hannah has a piece of geography that locates Karl in relation to her father, though she can't yet say what that means. The evening closes on the sensory ordinary — silage smell through the kitchen wall, the lamp on the table, her father's hands around his soup bowl. The chapter fades on texture.

---

## New question raised

What does the geographic coincidence — Albrecht's pause, the shared regional idiom — mean? And who is Karl Reinmar apart from the uniform he was taken in?

## Continuity notes

- Albrecht's birthplace: Feuchtwangen, Bavaria (Franconian region, approximately 40 km south of Ansbach). This is canon; record it.
- Karl is from Franconia, approximately 50 km from Albrecht's birthplace — not yet stated directly in the prose, but the geographic inference is available to the reader.
- Albrecht's pause at the fence is a key moment: rendered physically, not psychologically. He stops mid-swing. The prose does not explain what the phrase was or why it stopped him.
- Hannah does not yet know what *Landsman* means — that comes in the next Marcus chapter. But the proximity of the two men's dialects is visible to her as a fact, even without the word.
- Lonzo Reed's nod to Karl: small, human, important. Lonzo is paying attention in his own way.
- Literary-fade ending: sensory close on the farm evening, no action resolution.
