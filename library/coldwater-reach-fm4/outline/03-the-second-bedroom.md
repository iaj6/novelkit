# Chapter 03 — The Second Bedroom

**POV**: Marcus Klein  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: Late October 1943  
**Target words**: ~2,700  
**Chapter shape**: Multi-beat (school day, then Crows' kitchen, then his room)  
**Ending mode**: Coda  
**Opening texture**: Routine-procedure

---

## One-line summary

Marcus teaches Latin, hears the town's theory about the body on the breakwater, and finds — in the Crows' kitchen — the only room in Coldwater where he is not a specimen.

---

## Setting

Coldwater High School (classroom, teachers' room), the walk to Elm Street, the Crow house kitchen and second bedroom. Late October — the school's furnaces running, leaves down, first real cold.

---

## Goal / pressure

Marcus's opening chapter does three things: establishes his voice and register; introduces the Crow household as his true home base; and plants his first piece of the mystery — the town's body-is-a-German-agent theory, which Marcus knows is almost certainly wrong. His goal in the chapter is simply to get through a teaching day and arrive at the kitchen table. The pressure is the quiet one: he is always being measured, and the measurement never stops, even when no one is actively doing it.

---

## Action beats

**Beat 1 — Latin class:**
1. Marcus is teaching a third-year Latin class — the ablative absolute, a construction he finds elegant. He explains it twice, differently. One student (the strongest in the class) gets it on the second explanation. Most don't. He assigns a passage of Livy for the following week.
2. His English in the classroom is formal, slightly above the conversational register, technically correct in a way that is neither stiff nor pretentious — it is simply the English of a man who learned it on the page. Students have stopped noticing this; it was more visible in 1942.
3. He corrects a student's declension three times, patiently, with the particular patience of someone who knows the ending matters even when the student cannot yet feel why.

**Beat 2 — The teachers' room:**
4. Between classes, Marcus sits in the teachers' room with a cup of tea. Two other teachers are discussing the body on the breakwater — a week's worth of speculation has now hardened into confident theory: "U-boat agent, obviously. What other explanation is there?"
5. Marcus says nothing. He knows several other explanations — he has lived through the period of history that generates them. A man in European civilian clothes washing up on the coast in 1943: the first explanation should be displacement, not espionage. But he has learned that correcting this kind of certainty costs more than the correction is worth.
6. He drinks his tea. He thinks about the calluses in the wrong places (he's heard this detail secondhand). He files this away.

**Beat 3 — After school, the walk:**
7. He walks to Elm Street — three blocks from school, three blocks from the harbor. He passes the harbor road without going to it. He has walked this route every school day since March 1942. He knows which boards in which sidewalk are loose.
8. He is thinking about the Livy passage he assigned: *dum haec geruntur* — while these things were being done. The ablative absolute that puts one action in the background of another. He finds this grammatically useful and humanly accurate.

**Beat 4 — The Crows' kitchen:**
9. Lillian has made fish chowder. She has found actual milk for it — not the reconstituted powder. She explains this with satisfaction. He asks where she found milk. She names a farm. He thanks her as though the chowder is a gift, which it is.
10. She asks him about Vienna. He has been asked this before; he has learned to answer a piece of it. Tonight he tells her about the Prater — the park, the chestnut trees in October. He does not get to 1938. He has learned not to get there at this table.
11. Henry comes home. He is late because the school's east furnace flue needed clearing. He brings the smell of coal in on his jacket. He sits down and they eat together.
12. Henry mentions that someone in town — doesn't say who — was asking about "the German teacher." Henry says it neutrally, informationally. Marcus says: "What did you tell them?" Henry says: "That you teach Latin too." A beat. Marcus says: "That's accurate."
13. They eat. Lillian starts on the dishes. Henry reads the paper. Marcus is quiet.

**Beat 5 — The second bedroom:**
14. He goes upstairs. His room is tidy, kept with the European habit of order as a form of dignity. A single bookshelf. A desk. The window faces the street.
15. He takes a German grammar text from the shelf — one he has had since Vienna, its cover water-damaged from a crossing he does not dwell on. He opens it to the middle and reads a paragraph of notes he wrote in the margin in 1937. His own handwriting from before.
16. He closes it.

---

## Outcome / coda

The coda is structural: the warmth of the Crows' kitchen and the smell of the chowder still exist in his body as he sits in the cold second bedroom. The kitchen is the chapter's moral center; the bedroom is the chapter's close. He is at home, which is not the same as home.

---

## New question raised

What does it mean to have arrived somewhere and still be in transit? And: the town has a theory about the body; he has the beginning of a different one. What does he do with the distance between them?

---

## Continuity notes

- Introduces Marcus's voice: precise, formally constructed, subordinate clauses arriving to qualify, slightly aware of the medium he's using.
- Establishes the Crow household as Marcus's anchor. Henry and Lillian are vivid and specific; their warmth is not performance.
- Marcus does not discuss Sherbrooke. The internment camp is not mentioned; the water-damaged grammar book carries the trace of a crossing without naming it.
- The body on the breakwater is present here as town gossip — Marcus has the beginning of a different interpretation (displacement, not espionage) but does not state it to anyone.
- Henry's mention of someone asking about "the German teacher" introduces the school board pressure as background threat. It is not named or escalated here.
- Lillian asks about Vienna every few chapters; each time Marcus answers a different piece of it. The full answer is never given in a single chapter.
- The coda ending: the warmth doesn't reach the second bedroom in the same way. This is a physical fact, not a metaphor the prose should name.
