# Chapter 26 — The Harbor Log

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: October 1944  
**Target words**: ~2,900  
**Chapter shape**: Single scene / bottle (the harbormaster's office; the annual review; the late entry)  
**Ending mode**: Resolved-beat  
**Opening texture**: Routine-procedure

---

## One-line summary

One year after the body; Eira does the annual log review, opens to October 1943, and writes the late entry — not a confession, not a report, but a complete record in her own handwriting.

---

## Setting

The harbormaster's office — the cedar-shingled building at the foot of the commercial wharf, chart table, tide log, kerosene stove. October 1944. The harbor going into its autumn rhythm: fleet reducing, the summer boats gone, the lobster smacks back as the center of operations.

---

## Goal / pressure

One year has passed since the body. Eira is doing the annual log review — the same task she does every October, which requires going through the previous year's entries to close out the season's records. She will reach October 1943. She has known, since finding Owen's ledger in January, that the blank in that entry is different from Owen's deferred-but-complete notation.

The pressure is the year of accumulation: she knows Horak's name now, she knows his nationality, she has spoken to Marcus and knows the language she heard on the dock. She has more than she had a year ago. The question of whether that changes what the log should say has been present for twelve months.

---

## Action beats

**Beat 1 — The annual review:**
1. October — the fleet turnover. She is going through the year's logs, as she does every autumn: confirming all entries are complete, closing out the season's mooring registrations, checking that every incident notation has been properly followed up.
2. She starts at January 1944 and works forward. Her entries are complete and accurate throughout. Good year for the harbor, accounting for the war.
3. She reaches October 1944 — present time — and continues working backward to close out the review.
4. She reaches October 1943.

**Beat 2 — The entry:**
5. October 1943. She reads her entries for that month: the blow and its aftermath, the discovery of the body, the Coast Guard notification, the official report filed. All accurate.
6. She turns back one day: the night before. October 13th entry (or the appropriate date). What she wrote: tide reading, fleet secure, mooring check completed. Clean.
7. She holds the pen. She has been holding this for a year.
8. She thinks about Owen's ledger — his J.T. entry, his "this not entered at time; entering here as complete account." She has been thinking about this since January.
9. She thinks: the difference between Owen's approach and hers. His was deferred completeness. Hers has been omission. He came back to it. She has not. Until now.

**Beat 3 — Writing the entry:**
10. She writes in the dated space for October 13th, 1943 — a late notation, clearly added below the original entry. She writes in her own hand, in the harbor log's standard format:

*"Late entry, dated October 1944: 2300 hours, 13 October 1943: completed mooring check, commercial dock. One unidentified individual present at the far bollard, civilian appearance, not a known registered vessel operator. Observed in the company of a second party who departed walking inland before I could make identification. No approach made. Not entered at original time. Entering now in accordance with complete record."*

*— E. Bowman, Harbormaster, October 1944*

11. She reads what she has written. It is precise. It is accurate. It says what happened without saying what she decided in the moment, without saying what she has carried for twelve months, without making any accusation or conclusion. It says: there was a man, there was a second person, she saw them, she didn't approach, she didn't enter it.
12. This is a complete record.

**Beat 4 — After:**
13. She closes the log. She sits at the chart table for a few minutes. The stove ticks. The harbor outside the window is going about its afternoon.
14. She thinks: this doesn't change what happened. Horak is still dead. The second person on the dock is still unknown. The Coast Guard still has its accidental drowning ruling. This entry is in a log that no one reads except her, except in the case of an official inquiry, which has not been reopened and will not be.
15. What changes is: the record is complete. In her own handwriting, by her own hand, in the log she is responsible for. She has done what Owen did — what a harbormaster does, when they are doing it honestly. She has made the record complete.
16. She opens the log for the current day and writes the afternoon's tide entry.

---

## Outcome / resolved-beat

This is Eira's resolution — the chapter's and her arc's. Not a confession, not a reactivated investigation, not a dramatic act. A late entry in a professional log, in the standard format, that says what happened. The resolved-beat is the completion of the record: the professional obligation she has been carrying since October 1943 is met. Her personal grief and questions remain; they are hers. But the log is complete.

---

## New question raised

The record is complete. But the second person on the dock — the one who walked away before Eira reached them — is still unknown. That question is not in the log and is not resolved by the log. Does Eira need it to be?

## Continuity notes

- The late entry text: the drafter should render this in full, in the appropriate professional register. It should not read as confession or dramatic statement — it should read as a harbor log entry with a late notation, because that is what it is.
- Owen's precedent (Chapter 11) is explicitly present in Eira's thinking here. This is the payoff of that chapter.
- The entry does not name Horak — she didn't know the man on the dock was Horak. She describes what she saw: an unidentified individual, a second party. This is accurate.
- The second person on the dock: not identified in this entry, not identified by the novel. This is the deliberately unresolved thread (continuity fact #32: the breakwater question is not fully resolved). Eira's log does not answer who the second person was.
- Eira's arc resolves here. Her chapter 30 will extend this resolution into the harbor's spring, but this is the act that resolves her relationship to the dock knowledge.
