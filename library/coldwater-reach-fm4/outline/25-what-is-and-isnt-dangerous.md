# Chapter 25 — What Is and Isn't Dangerous

**POV**: Marcus Klein  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Season / time**: October 1944  
**Target words**: ~2,800  
**Chapter shape**: Single scene (Hannah and Marcus, the classroom; extending into Marcus's brief reflection afterward)  
**Ending mode**: Resolved-beat  
**Opening texture**: Dialogue

---

## One-line summary

Hannah tells Marcus about her unauthorized fence meetings with Karl; he tells her what is actually prohibited and what is simply human; the chapter's central question is answered.

---

## Setting

Marcus's classroom at Coldwater High School — after school, October. The school year is underway; Hannah is in her senior year. The classroom in autumn afternoon light.

---

## Goal / pressure

Hannah comes to Marcus because she has no one else to bring this to: she can't tell her parents (Albrecht warned her; Magda is cycling through her spells), she can't tell her friends, she can't tell anyone in a position of official authority. She comes to the one person who might know both the rules and what they mean in practice. Marcus's goal is to give her an accurate answer. The pressure is what that accuracy requires him to know — about the Geneva Convention, about his own judgment of what is actually dangerous, and about his assessment of Hannah, Karl, and Lonzo.

---

## Action beats

1. **Hannah comes in**: She sits down at the desk nearest Marcus's, not the desk she normally occupies during class. This is a different kind of being in this room.

2. **She tells him**: Over the past several months — since June, approximately — she has been having conversations with Karl Reinmar at the fence. Brief. Five, ten minutes at a time, when Lonzo is not watching that specific section. Topics: the farm, the soil, his English, occasionally his home region. She gave him nothing and received nothing (she pauses before saying this, and Marcus notes the pause). She has not facilitated any escape, not passed any messages, not provided any contraband.

3. **And then she says**: Her father knows. He warned her to be careful. She doesn't know what Lonzo knows or has reported.

4. **Marcus listens.** He asks two questions, in sequence:
   - "Has Private Reed filed any incident report that you know of, or has any official come to the farm about this?" She says no.
   - "Have you passed anything — food, documents, any written communication — to or from Karl Reinmar?" She says: well, he left something for her once. She takes the carved bird from her pocket. A small wooden bird, three inches. Not a message; not useful to anyone.

5. **He looks at the bird.** He gives it back. He says: "That's not contraband."

6. **He explains**: The regulations governing POW labor placements prohibit assisting escape, passing military intelligence or information that could aid the enemy, providing weapons or materials with military application, and romantic or sexual relations between civilian women and prisoners. Unauthorized conversation is technically a violation of the spirit of the escort arrangement, not of the statute. In practice, he says, every farm with prisoners has moments like this; the Coastal Patrol knows it and manages it.

7. **He says**: "If Lonzo Reed has chosen not to report it, it's because he's decided it isn't worth reporting. That's his judgment. I think it's probably correct."

8. **She asks**: "Did I do something wrong?"

9. **He considers.** He says: "You did something human. Those are sometimes the same thing. In this case, I don't think they are."

10. **He adds**: "But I would stop. Not because it's wrong. Because it isn't worth the risk to you or to him. The war is ending — he will be repatriated. Between now and then, neither of you benefits from being noticed."

11. **She says**: "I think my father knows more about why I should be careful than he's told me." She says this tentatively, testing whether Marcus will confirm it.

12. **He says**: "I think your father is a man who has made some decisions about what he knows and what he doesn't need to say. Like most people." He does not say more. This is exactly what he means and not what he means.

13. **She leaves.** He sits at his desk.

14. **After**: He thinks, briefly, about the assembled picture he has carried since May — the Pfeiffer farm, the Franconian network, Horak. He has told Hannah nothing of this. He has given her what she needed: a practical assessment of risk and a true sentence. That seems like the right thing to have done.

---

## Outcome / resolved-beat

The chapter's central question resolves: Hannah has an answer. She knows what is prohibited, what is not, and what Marcus thinks she should do. She has a decision to make; this chapter gives her the information she needs to make it. The resolved-beat is Marcus's, too: he has found the right thing to do with what he knows, which is to give Hannah what she needs and keep the rest of what he carries in its drawer.

---

## New question raised

Will Hannah stop the fence meetings? And Marcus's line about her father — "a man who has made some decisions about what he knows" — will she hear what that means?

## Continuity notes

- This is the pairwise crossing between Marcus and Hannah that matters most for Hannah's arc.
- The carved bird: Marcus sees it, confirms it is not contraband, gives it back. The bird remains hers.
- Marcus's knowledge of the Geneva Convention and POW labor regulations: accurate. He has had reason to study the legal frameworks around prisoner status (his own internment at Sherbrooke was under a different but related framework).
- "You did something human. Those are sometimes the same thing. In this case, I don't think they are." — This is Marcus's characteristic precision: the subordinate clause qualifying the absolute, arriving at a clear judgment. It should land as his voice, not as aphorism.
- Marcus does not tell Hannah about Horak, the network, or what he suspects about Albrecht. He gives her the practical answer she came for.
- Lonzo's non-action: confirmed again by Marcus's reasoning — Reed has made a judgment that this isn't worth reporting. This is consistent with Chapter 10.
