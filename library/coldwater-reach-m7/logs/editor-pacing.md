# Editor — Pacing Pass
## Macro arc assessment

### Arc shape — overall

*Coldwater Reach* has a clean four-phase plan (Setup 1–8 / Development 9–16 / Turn 17–23 / Resolution 24–30) and the structural bones are sound. The opening eight chapters do efficient work introducing three POVs plus Henry Crow, and the resolution phase (Chs 24–30) is the book's best-woven section: threads resolve in staggered sequence, the mystery protection holds, and the final two chapters (29, 30) earn their weight. The main problems are in the middle: a real midbook sag in the mystery threads, one risky consecutive-chapter pair, and an ending-mode pattern that could flatten the development escalation curve.

---

### Midbook sag — Chapters 17–22 (the mystery goes quiet)

The central spine of Threads 1 and 2 (What Eira Knows / Who Was the Dead Man) makes no meaningful forward movement for six consecutive chapters — Chs 17 through 22, covering May through November 1944. The weave check marks Thread 1 advancing at Ch 17 (twelve words between Eira and Marcus: "Nothing from Portland") and again at Ch 20 (a brief dock exchange), but the story-arc summaries confirm both are gestures rather than advances. Thread 2 marks nothing in this stretch at all.

Six chapters — roughly a fifth of the novel — carry the book on Hannah/Karl, Hannah/father, and Marcus/Henry threads only. That's legitimate dramatic material, but the mystery's engine having gone entirely cold for that span creates a low-pressure corridor precisely where accumulation should be building toward the December wallet revelation (Ch 23). The risk is that when the wallet arrives, readers who remember the mystery primarily as "what Eira knows" will feel the six-chapter gap as the book having moved on — and the cliffhanger mode at Ch 23 will land softer than intended.

**Flag:** Chapters 17–22 need at least one additional mystery-thread beat somewhere in the run. Ch 20 (Mail Boat) is the natural host — it already has an Eira/Marcus exchange; extending that contact toward the proximity of the mystery (without prematurely surfacing it) could sustain the thread without disrupting structure. The fix is a single paragraph of deepened interiority or a withheld detail, not a new scene.

---

### D-Day pair — Chapters 18–19 (identical emotional shape)

Chapters 18 (June Radio) and 19 (The Zenith) cover the same event — June 6, 1944 — from Hannah's POV and Marcus's. Both are high-concept ideas executed individually. The problem is that they land on nearly the same emotional note: in both, the POV character is near a radio, the war is arriving as sound, and the chapter closes on something that cannot be said (Hannah's father's single German sentence to no one; the four sentences across twenty minutes of silence on the porch). Both end in the "silence is the thing" mode — Ch 18 elliptical, Ch 19 literary fade. The content differs, but the emotional gesture is cognate.

Consecutive chapters should not register as emotional echoes of each other. One of the two needs a distinct texture at its close. Ch 18's closing image (the father's single German sentence) is the more surprising and should probably be preserved. Ch 19 has more latitude — Marcus's chapter could close on action, information, or even the briefest forward implication, rather than the porch-silence fade. This is a low-word fix, not a restructure.

---

### Ending-mode clustering in the Development phase

Of the nine resolved-beat closings across the novel, five fall in Chs 9–17 (the Development phase and early Turn): Chs 9, 13, 15, 17, and then Ch 20. The run 13 → 15 → 17 is three resolved beats in five chapters (with one elliptical in between). For a novel working in restraint throughout, this could feel like the book repeatedly arriving at the same small clearing before walking back into the forest. Escalation should be visible even in a quiet novel — each chapter's closing beat should feel *further along* than the last, not equivalently settled.

Chapters 15 (Sprechen) and 17 (Ice Out) are the two most likely candidates for mode adjustment if an edit is warranted. Ch 15's resolved beat (Hannah and Karl are now in each other's language — something shifts) is a genuine advance and should stay. Ch 17's resolved beat (dock dispute resolved, twelve words with Marcus) is the weakest close in the Development phase — the chapter is doing Eira-thread maintenance and its resolution is administrative. If Ch 17 closed elliptically instead — leaving the harbor's ordinary-on-the-surface texture slightly open — it would not repeat the same mode across Chs 13/15/17 and would hold more tension before the D-Day pair.

---

### Thread 6 scaffolding risk

Henry Crow's two chapters (Ch 8, 1,500 words; Ch 22, 1,500 words) are separated by fourteen chapters and roughly eleven story-months. Thread 6 is meant to accumulate through the margins of Marcus's chapters in between, but those margins depend on the reader tracking a friendship that has only two named anchors in the entire novel. The Ch 28 resolution moment — Henry staying until midnight after the liberation news, and the two sentences that mean what they mean — is the most emotionally compact resolution in the book. If the friendship hasn't accumulated enough visible weight across the Marcus chapters, that moment won't carry what it's meant to.

No structural change needed, but the per-chapter pass on Chs 19, 22, and 28 should verify that the friendship's texture is present — not explained but *felt* — in the Marcus POV. The craft log shows Ch 19 (The Zenith) carries the friendship well. Ch 22 (Henry's chapter) and Ch 28 are the checkpoints.

---

### Climax/coda balance — sound

The resolution phase (Chs 24–30) is properly weighted. Threads resolve across seven chapters in different registers — fragment/identification (Ch 24), daughter/father (Ch 25), the addendum (Ch 26), repatriation foretold (Ch 27), liberation/recognition (Ch 28), spring log (Ch 29), departure (Ch 30). The two deliberately unresolved threads (Thread 2 and Thread 6) are held with discipline. Ch 30's coda mode is the right close. The only small concern is that Eira's thread resolves formally at Ch 26 but she appears again substantively at Ch 29 — that's appropriate (the spring log as emotional aftermath), not padding, provided Ch 29 doesn't re-settle what Ch 26 already settled. The story-arc summary suggests it doesn't: Ch 29 gives Eira Owen, not the addendum, as its center.

---

### Summary priority list

1. **(High)** Chs 17–22: mystery sag; find one beat in this run, probably Ch 20, to keep Thread 1/2 warm without resolving.
2. **(Medium)** Chs 18–19: D-Day pair's closing notes are too cognate; Ch 19's porch-silence fade could be varied.
3. **(Medium)** Ch 17: resolved-beat ending in the weakest-close spot of the arc; consider elliptical.
4. **(Low)** Thread 6: verify the friendship's texture lands in Marcus's Chs 19, 24, and 28 before the per-chapter pass closes.
