# Calibration Sample — Iter 1
## Chapter 01: The Breakwater (Eira POV, opening)

---

The tide clock wanted four minutes. It always wanted four minutes by Monday, and Monday was what the calendar said, so Eira set it.

Six forty-seven, the tide. She wrote it in the log.

Wind NNW at nine knots, barometer steady at 30.12 and holding. The blow of the previous three days had moved off toward Nova Scotia and left behind a fog that the breakwater light was doing nothing about. She noted this too, in the column where she noted such things.

The shack was lit by a single bulb and the smell of it was kerosene and old rope and something she had stopped identifying eight years ago. The log went back to June 1891. Her handwriting ran in from the current month toward Owen's, and Owen's stopped on a Tuesday in November 1935 in the middle of a sentence about the *Agnes Maud*'s draft: *bilges pumped and forward hold dry; sea running 12 ft, barometric pressure fall—*

She did not linger on this page. She had not lingered on it in eight years.

The radio sat on the shelf above the chart table, a BC-1206 the Coast Guard auxiliary had loaned the harbor office in February. She turned it on at six-fifty and ran through the check: Portland, Rockland, Bar Harbor, back to Portland. Static where the fog was heaviest. She logged the check.

Outside, the dock ran dark. October had taken the early light and would keep it until March. She could hear the *Callie B.* shifting in her slip, the water against the pilings carrying a sound that suggested less than what the tide gauge confirmed. The breakwater extended two hundred yards into the fog and ended in a red fixed light she could see from where she sat — which meant this was a harbor fog and not an offshore bank, and the morning would clear.

She had a half-hour before the first boats came in. She used it to go through Farber's delivery schedule and the fuel account and the November watch rotation. There was a pencil shortfall she had been meaning to address with the harbormaster's supply requisition for six weeks. She addressed it now.

Farber's boy came at quarter to seven. He was breathing as if he'd run.
