# Story Arc

One tight line per drafted chapter, in chronological order.

- **01-the-breakwater** — A man's body is found on the Coldwater Reach breakwater on October 12, 1943; Eira processes it as harbormaster, files an official record that omits what she saw the night before, and closes the log.
- **02-the-icehouse-in-october** — Hannah's world is established: the four-thirty milking, her father's icehouse-window German, the body arriving as town rumor, and what sits under both.
- **03-the-crow-house** — Marcus overhears "no papers, no name" in the school hallway, settles into the Crow house's quiet acceptance, and his shortwave vigil surfaces an oblique recognition of the dead man's condition.
- **04-statement** — Lt. Ennis takes Eira's formal statement on October 20; her answers are technically accurate and collectively incomplete; the case is filed in Portland; she sets the tide clock back four minutes.
- **05-tuesday-german** — German class: Marcus identifies Hannah's Bavarian phrasing and says "Yes" and nothing more; she walks home past the breakwater; her mother's silence fills supper; her father's voice reaches her through the floor, talking to Karl in the dark.
- **06-at-the-harbormasters** — Marcus collects a school book shipment from the harbor and, in seven careful minutes with Eira, reads the precise shape of what she is not saying about the breakwater body.
- **07-november-log** — A November blow keeps Eira in the shack all night; she finds Owen's unfinished 1935 entry and, for the first time, holds the October 11 memory fully — the unlighted boat, the man she watched walk to his death.
- **08-school-hours** — Henry Crow's day at the school — furnace, hallways, the window exchange with Marcus — and an evening at home: he carries an informal complaint about Marcus privately and says nothing.
- **09-across-the-fence** — Karl speaks to Hannah directly for the first time across the paddock fence — ninety seconds, a frost-heaved post, thirty-two words — and Hannah resolves to keep noticing what she notices without deciding what it means.
- **10-a-letter-from-new-york** — Dr. Feld's Emergency Committee letter arrives describing Felix K., last documented Yarmouth NS, September 1943; Marcus writes in his German notebook "the coast of Maine is between" but makes no connection to the body.
- **11-january-harbor** — Reg Farber tells Eira he saw an unlighted boat on October 11; she now carries a second witness's knowledge alongside her own and does nothing with either.
- **12-found** — Hannah finds a page from Karl's mother's letter in the barn hay, reads its key phrase in Bavarian German, and keeps it instead of returning it — a small choice made without deliberation.
- **13-what-the-letter-said** — Hannah brings Karl's mother's letter to Marcus after class; he reads more than she intends, translates the key phrase, and gives back a true but partial answer — keeping what he hears about the letter's date, its missing header, and the adjacency to Yarmouth.
- **14-a-report-from-portland** — A Coast Guard District letter routes a Yarmouth NS missing-vessel inquiry to Eira; the dead man was in transit heading south — she files the letter privately and the mystery reframes from who to where he was going.
- **15-sprechen** — Hannah speaks Bavarian German to Karl directly in the milking barn during three minutes of Lonzo's absence; Karl answers; they are now in each other's language for the first time.
- **16-the-notebook** — Marcus assembles his probable theory in his German notebook — the dead man, Felix K., the arithmetic of Yarmouth — and closes it on a question he cannot yet answer.
- **17-ice-out** — Ice clears from the inner cove in early May; Eira resolves Reg Farber's dock-fee dispute with a legitimate ledger maneuver, and exchanges twelve words with Marcus at the Heron's slip — "Nothing from Portland."
- **18-june-radio** — D-Day news arrives by radio on June 6, 1944; Albrecht sits in his coat through the day; Hannah sees Karl unreachable in the south field; her father ends the evening with one German sentence to no one.
- **19-the-zenith** — D-Day evening: Marcus, Henry, and Lillian listen at the Zenith; Marcus translates a BBC German broadcast; on the porch afterward, four sentences cross twenty minutes of silence.
- **20-the-mail-boat** — Summer 1944: Eira manages the harbor's peak season, watches the Heron for yellow envelopes, exchanges three words with Marcus at the dock, and writes one more non-operational line in the log.
- **21-the-second-october** — One year since the body; Hannah is eighteen, Karl is twenty, the farm's distributed German exchanges have become ordinary; her father watches her at the fence and says nothing.
- **22-at-the-school** — Henry Crow learns of Mrs. Vose's formal complaint against Dr. Klein, tells Marcus in two sentences in a hallway without stopping, and goes to bed having done what he could.
- **23-december** — Reg Farber pulls a waterlogged wallet from his winter net off the inner bar; Eira finds partial German script in a protected pocket, locks it in the middle drawer, and tells no one.
- **24-the-fragment** — Marcus examines the wallet fragment at Eira's request, cross-references the Feld letter that night, and concludes the dead man was probably Felix K. — then decides not to tell her.
- **25-zuhause** — Deep winter on the Pfeiffer farm; Hannah speaks German to her father for the first time in years, and he answers — then says her name in English, the first English he has spoken to her in more than two years.
- **26-the-addendum** — Eira writes and submits the addendum to the October 1943 harbor record, formally entering what she saw; the file receives it and closes again; the wallet and fragment stay in the drawer.
- **27-march** — Hannah and Karl's fence conversation confirms his departure in weeks; she retrieves his mother's letter from her notebook and decides she will return it before he leaves.
- **28-april** — Liberation news arrives through the Zenith; Marcus sits with what he has managed not to know for seven years; Henry stays until midnight; Marcus writes one line in his German notebook and goes to bed.
- **29-spring-log** — Late April 1945: Eira runs a full spring day at a busy harbor, turns back to Owen's unfinished 1935 entry, then writes her own final line in the log — "Harbor in order. Nine years, five months. All boats accounted for."
- **30-komm-rein** — Hannah returns Karl's mother's letter before the prisoners' truck comes; she and her father watch it leave from the porch; he puts his hand on her shoulder and says komm rein; the kitchen, the farm, the war in the radio, not yet over.
