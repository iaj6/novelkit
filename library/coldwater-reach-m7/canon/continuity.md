# Continuity

Hard facts that downstream drafting must not contradict. Numbered for reference.

1. Eira Bowman is fifty-two years old in autumn 1943. She has been harbormaster of Coldwater Reach since November 1935.
2. Eira's husband, Owen Bowman, was the previous harbormaster. He drowned in the November storm of 1935. His handwriting appears in the harbor log through approximately November 12, 1935, where it stops.
3. A man's body washes up on the Coldwater Reach breakwater on or about October 12, 1943, after a three-day blow. He carries no papers. He is not from Coldwater.
4. Eira was at the dock the night before the body came in. She saw something she has not disclosed to the Coast Guard or to anyone else.
5. The Coast Guard takes the body and removes the record. The mystery is not solved by institutional means. The investigation, such as it is, closes without a name.
6. Hannah Pfeiffer is seventeen years old in autumn 1943. She lives on the Pfeiffer dairy farm, three miles inland from Coldwater Reach.
7. Albrecht Pfeiffer (Hannah's father) and Magda Pfeiffer (Hannah's mother) emigrated from Bavaria to Maine in 1922.
8. Albrecht has stopped speaking English at home since the war began. Magda has stopped speaking altogether. Both conditions are present when the story opens in October 1943.
9. Four German prisoners of war have been housed in the renovated Pfeiffer icehouse since April 1943 and work the farm under armed escort.
10. Karl Reinmar is nineteen years old, one of the four POWs at the Pfeiffer farm, and the youngest of them. He is from a town in Bavaria within fifty kilometers of Albrecht Pfeiffer's birthplace.
11. Lonzo Reed is an aging Coastal Patrol auxiliary who serves as the daily escort for the POW work party at the Pfeiffer farm. He carries a Springfield rifle; he has not fired it since the First World War.
12. Dr. Marcus Klein is forty-seven years old in autumn 1943. He was born in Vienna in 1896.
13. Marcus's trajectory: University of Vienna (Germanist, eighteenth-century philology) until the Anschluss, 1938 → Paris → fall of France → London → Canada (scholars' visa, early 1940) → interned at Camp Sherbrooke, Quebec, June 1940–August 1941 → Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars rolls from autumn 1940 → Coldwater Reach, March 1942.
14. Marcus does not speak about Sherbrooke.
15. Marcus boards with Henry Crow and Lillian Crow. Henry is approximately fifty, is Black, and came north from Norfolk, Virginia with his family in 1924. Henry is the chief custodian of the Coldwater school. Their household is the only one in town where Marcus is treated as a guest rather than a specimen.
16. The three primary POV characters — Eira, Hannah, and Marcus — never meet simultaneously as a group of three.
17. The story spans from autumn 1943 to late April or early May 1945, concluding just before VE Day (May 8, 1945). The war's ending occurs in the background; the story's resolutions are smaller and older than it.
18. There are no spy thriller elements: no Nazi agents operating in or near Coldwater, no submarine landings, no coded messages, no covert networks. Any chapter that moves toward these mechanics contradicts the brief.
19. The POW labor program is historically real. Camp Houlton in Aroostook County, Maine was the primary POW facility in the state. German enlisted prisoners were paid eighty cents per day in canteen coupons. The program was governed by the Geneva Convention.
20. At least one thread must remain deliberately unresolved at the story's close. The question of the body's full identity and exactly what happened on the dock the night before is a candidate — but whichever thread is chosen as the unresolved one must be protected throughout drafting.
21. The Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars was a real organization. Do not invent a fictional substitute; use the correct name.
22. Real place names may appear (Portland, Bangor, Houlton, Rockland, Cambridge, Sherbrooke). Real people's names should not appear.
