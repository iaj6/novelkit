# Style

## POV and tense

Third-person limited past tense throughout. The narration is bound tightly to whichever character the chapter belongs to — their vocabulary, their observational frame, their emotional register. No authorial intrusion above the POV character's head. No information the POV character couldn't have.

## Three distinct registers

The manuscript uses three distinguishable prose registers, one per POV character. They must remain audibly distinct even when the chapter heading names the POV. An editor should be able to identify who is narrating from a passage alone.

---

### Eira's register

Spare, procedural, load-bearing. Eira thinks about tide, weather, draft, and load before she thinks about anything else. Her chapters open close to work: the log, the dock, the radio, the weather. Her sentences are short and declarative except when working out a complex observation, in which case they extend carefully, clause by clause, as if she is running a calculation rather than expressing a feeling. She does not produce interior monologue in the conventional sense. Her emotional life reaches the prose only through the precision of her professional notation — a barometer reading that holds a beat too long, a log entry that repeats what doesn't need repeating. The sea is her workplace. It is never a metaphor. Her grief for Owen is present through objects and gaps, not through reflection.

*Target rhythm*: 14–18 words average per sentence. Short declaratives (3–4 in sequence) are characteristic rhythm. Longer sentences are for complex observations, not for feeling.

---

### Hannah's register

Closer to the interior, more permeable. Adolescent consciousness permitted: the mind that picks up and puts down without deciding. Hannah's chapters are the most sensory of the three — body-close, barn-close, cold-close. German words and phrases appear naturally where her father's voice has reached her: single words or short phrases, untranslated, not italicized, treated as part of the narrative's texture rather than foreign interruptions. Her chapters tend to open in the physical (what she hears at four-thirty a.m., what the barn smells like) before rising to perception. Her register is the one most likely to let things accumulate without resolving them.

*Target rhythm*: 16–22 words average per sentence. More associative syntax than Eira. Longer sentences accumulate sensory detail. Short sentences mark discovery or shock.

---

### Marcus's register

Precise and slightly formal. The voice of a man who learned English on the page and continues to translate his thinking as it occurs. He uses subordinate clauses and qualifying phrases where a native speaker would use a gesture or a shrug. His narration is careful, not cold; the formality is a protective habit, not a personality trait. When something genuinely moves him, the prose shortens rather than elongates — the qualifying structure drops away, and what remains is brief, exact, and without gloss.

*Target rhythm*: 18–24 words average per sentence. Subordinate clauses are characteristic. But the longest sentences should be the most analytical, not the most emotional; emotion is rendered by *compression* relative to his established norm.

---

## Dialogue

Clipped in Eira's chapters. Busier (more interior-adjacent) in Hannah's. Measured in Marcus's, with occasional significant pauses. Dialogue tags default to *said* and *asked*; other verb-of-manner tags only when the manner is not already clear from context or surrounding action. Do not gloss dialogue. Do not write "as if to say" or "which meant." The reader hears the line; they do not need the stage direction.

**German dialogue and phrases**: Albrecht's English is grammatical and slightly formal, with Germanic syntax, not phonetic caricature. Karl's English is poor and improving. Marcus's English is careful and slightly overcorrect. None of these should read as stage accents. German phrases in Hannah's narration surface as vocabulary items, not performance.

## What the prose trusts the reader to do

The story trusts the reader to feel what is not said. Eira's log entries, Hannah's silences, Marcus's careful sentences — the reader is meant to feel the weight behind these without assistance. The prose notes what is observable and moves on. The reader assembles the interior.

---

## Failure modes

The AI-author patterns most tempting in this story's register. The drafter should actively avoid these; the compression pass should cut them.

- **Sentence-after-the-sentence** — an image or observation arrives complete; the next sentence must not gloss it. Eira in particular: the harbor note *is* the feeling. Trust it and stop.
- **Meta-commentary on cognition** — most dangerous in Marcus's chapters: "he noted," "he observed," "he registered the fact that." The observation is already rendered in the prose; the label is redundant.
- **Emotional structural analysis** — dramatize the feeling; do not then describe its architecture. "She felt something she might have called grief, or something close to it, or a version of it she had not named yet" is the failure mode. Hannah's chapters are most at risk.
- **Post-scene subtext naming** — the mystery element creates strong pressure to close a scene by stating what it was "really about." None of the three characters should narrate their own symbolism. End the scene, then end the chapter.
- **Thesis restatement** — displacement, authority, belonging, the war's texture are the story's deep concerns. An abstract sentence re-stating them at scene's end is the single most reliable way to make them inert. Name the concrete; trust the reader to feel the abstract.

---

## Lean-into patterns

Patterns the drafter and editors should not treat as failures, because this register depends on them.

- **Free indirect discourse sustained across paragraphs** — the prose regularly moves into the character's framing and vocabulary without announcing it. What looks like authorial statement is the character's thought rendered in third-person. This is the primary vehicle for interiority in all three POVs, and it should feel seamless.
- **Procedural precision as emotional container** — specifically in Eira's chapters: the specific number (fourteen feet of draft, the tide at 6:47 a.m., wind NNW at twelve knots) is doing emotional work. Do not convert it into simile or metaphor. The precision is the precision.
- **Accumulation without analysis** — in Hannah's chapters, things are permitted to pile up without being architectured. The reader will find the structure. Trust the accumulation.
