# Glossary

Initial entries. Subsequent phases will append.

---

**Albrecht Pfeiffer** — Hannah's father. Bavarian immigrant, arrived Maine 1922. Dairy farmer, ~55 in 1943. Has stopped speaking English at home since the war began.

**BC-1206** — The radio set installed in the Coldwater Reach harbormaster's office. A period-accurate maritime transceiver.

**Breakwater, the** — Granite structure extending two hundred yards into the Coldwater Reach harbor cove. WPA-built, 1937. Ends in a fixed red light. Where the body is found in October 1943.

**Camp Houlton** — The primary German POW facility in Maine, located in Aroostook County. Source of the satellite work groups placed on farms and at canneries statewide, including the Pfeiffer farm detail.

**Coldwater Reach** — The invented fishing village on the mid-coast of Maine where the story is set. Population approximately nine hundred in 1943.

**Coast Guard auxiliary station** — The local federal maritime presence in Coldwater. The agency that takes custody of the body found on the breakwater in October 1943.

**Dr. Marcus Klein** — Viennese refugee scholar. Born Vienna 1896. Teacher of German and Latin at the Coldwater school. Forty-seven in 1943. Boards with Henry and Lillian Crow.

**Eira Bowman** — Harbormaster of Coldwater Reach. Fifty-two in 1943. The story's primary POV anchor. Has held the post since November 1935.

**Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars** — Real organization (New York) that placed refugee European academics in American positions. Marcus was on their rolls from autumn 1940.

**Hannah Pfeiffer** — Seventeen-year-old daughter of Albrecht and Magda Pfeiffer. Lives on the dairy farm; junior at Coldwater school; one of Marcus Klein's three German students.

**Henry Crow** — Chief custodian of the Coldwater school. Approximately fifty in 1943. Black; his family came north from Norfolk, Virginia in 1924 as part of the Great Migration. Marcus's landlord. A private, exact man.

**Karl Reinmar** — Nineteen-year-old German POW. Youngest of the four prisoners at the Pfeiffer farm. From a town in Bavaria within fifty kilometers of Albrecht Pfeiffer's birthplace. Farm-raised before conscription. His English is poor and improving.

**Lillian Crow** — Henry Crow's wife. Warmer to Marcus than Henry in expression, though both are welcoming. Runs the household.

**Lonzo Reed** — Aging Coastal Patrol auxiliary. Late fifties. Former fisherman. Escorts the four Pfeiffer farm POWs twice daily. Carries a Springfield rifle; has not fired it since the First World War.

**Magda Pfeiffer** — Hannah's mother. Bavarian immigrant, arrived 1922. Approximately fifty-two in 1943. Has stopped speaking altogether since the war began.

**Merrill's** — The diner in Coldwater Reach.

**Owen Bowman** (deceased) — Eira's husband. Previous harbormaster of Coldwater Reach. Drowned in the November storm of 1935. Present in the story through objects: the peacoat, the harbor log (his handwriting through November 1935), the tide clock.

**POW labor program** — Federal program placing German prisoners of war in supervised farm and industrial labor. Governed by the Geneva Convention. Enlisted prisoners paid eighty cents per day in canteen coupons. See also: Camp Houlton.

**The Reach** — The tidal river at the base of the Coldwater valley that meets the harbor at the dock. Gives the town its name.

**V-Mail** — Wartime correspondence system. Servicemen's letters photographed to microfilm and printed on arrival to conserve shipping weight.

**Callie B.** — Reg Farber's dragger, moored at the Coldwater Reach dock. Eira was checking its bilge pump on the night of October 11, 1943 — her legitimate reason for being at the dock when she observed the night boat.

**Felix K.** — Probable identity of the man found dead at the Coldwater breakwater on October 12, 1943. German-Jewish linguist, formerly of Leipzig. Last documented Yarmouth, Nova Scotia, September 22, 1943 — wrote to the Emergency Committee stating intention to travel south to Massachusetts. Never confirmed; the character's full surname is withheld throughout the manuscript.

**Reg Farber** — Local Coldwater lobsterman. His son found the body on October 12, 1943. Farber independently saw the night boat on October 11. He later pulls a waterlogged wallet from his net off the inner bar in December 1944.

**Lt. Ennis** — Coast Guard officer from Portland who takes the body and official record in October 1943 and conducts Eira's formal statement interview (Ch 04). Does not appear again on-page.

**Dr. Feld** — Marcus's contact at the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars, New York. His December 1943 letter (Ch 10) describes displaced scholars lost in transit, including Felix K.

**Heron, the** — The mail boat serving Coldwater Reach, running from Rockland twice weekly (Tuesdays and Fridays).

**inner bar** — Submerged formation approximately eighty yards from the breakwater's cove-side base. Where Reg Farber nets the wallet in December 1944.

**zuhause** — German: 'at home.' Key word in Ch 25 (chapter title) and in Hannah's interiority. Represents her relationship to her Bavarian-inflected language and her parents' world.

**Komm rein** — German/English: 'Come inside.' Albrecht Pfeiffer's words to Hannah at the close of the final chapter (Ch 30), as they watch the truck carrying Karl and the other prisoners drive away. Deliberately ambiguous as to which language he speaks them in.

**Nun ist es fertig** — German: 'Now it is finished.' Albrecht Pfeiffer's single sentence, spoken to no one in particular on the evening of June 6, 1944 (D-Day). Ch 18.

**frost heave** — Seasonal ground-heaving from freeze-thaw cycles, which shifts fence posts on the Pfeiffer farm. The term Karl learns from Hannah at the fence in December 1943 (Ch 09). Recurs in Ch 27.

**Harbormaster's shack** — Eira Bowman's office at the head of the Coldwater Reach dock. Contains the BC-1206 radio set (south wall), the tide clock (shelf above the radio), and the harbor log. Salt-weathered exterior. The location from which she conducts the morning radio check and maintains all port records.

**Farber's boy** — Reg Farber's youngest son, approximately fifteen, unnamed. He discovered the dead man at the breakwater's seaward end the morning of October 12, 1943, and ran to the harbormaster's shack to report it.

**Fresnel lens housing** — The optical fixture on the breakwater's seaward light standard. Inspected and certified by Eira Bowman in September 1943; found intact on October 12, 1943.

**Albrecht Pfeiffer** — Hannah's father, ~55, Bavarian-born dairy farmer at Coldwater Reach inland. Emigrated from Bavaria to Maine in 1922. Has stopped speaking English at home; speaks to farm animals and to Karl Reinmar in German; says 'Morgen' to Hannah in the barn instead of good morning.

**Magda Pfeiffer** — Hannah's mother, ~52, Bavarian-born. Emigrated 1922. Has stopped speaking altogether since approximately April 1943; communicates only by gesture and the quality of her silence. Rockland doctor found no physical cause.

**Lonzo Reed** — Coastal Patrol auxiliary, late fifties, former fisherman. Escorts the four German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm from icehouse to work and back, twice daily, with a Springfield rifle. Commands about three German words. Figure of adequate, unhurried presence.

**Mabel Trask** — Runs the counter at the Coldwater general store and post office. The practical center of town information; knows about the breakwater body before Hannah arrives Wednesday.

**Pfeiffer dairy farm** — The Pfeiffer family property, three miles up the inland road from Coldwater Reach. 1880s farmhouse, red barn, silo, renovated icehouse. 45 dairy cows. Since April 1943, the icehouse houses four German POWs.

**Pfeiffer icehouse** — Renovated outbuilding on the Pfeiffer farm, quartering four German POWs since April 1943. Wood stove, four cots, one window with a hasp on the outside; kerosene lamp authorized June 1943. In the evenings the window is cracked 2-3 inches. Smells of pine sap from new boards.

**die Kühe** — German: 'the cows.' Part of Albrecht Pfeiffer's barn German — his working language for the animals, distinct from his kitchen silence and from the German he speaks at the icehouse window in the evenings.

**Morgen** — German: 'morning.' Albrecht Pfeiffer's barn greeting to Hannah, replacing his former 'good morning' at some undated point. Hannah has been saying it back since August 1943.

**Morrissette brothers** — Two unnamed local brothers, recurring figures at the Coldwater general store; contribute unreliable local commentary on events. Minor background color.

**Crow house** — White clapboard house at 14 Elm Street, Coldwater; second-from-end on Elm Street; home of Henry and Lillian Crow; Marcus Klein's boarding residence since March 1942. Kitchen table with three established chairs: Marcus faces east window, Henry faces door, Lillian can see both.

**Henry Crow** — Chief custodian of the Coldwater school; Black man; family came north from Norfolk, Virginia in 1924; approximately fifty in 1943; boards Marcus Klein and is his most significant friendship in Coldwater. Reads the Rockland paper. Economy of gesture and response: his quiet receipt of information is not incuriosity but precision about what requires response.

**Lillian Crow** — Henry Crow's wife; runs the Crow house; warmer to Marcus in expression than Henry though Henry's offer of boarding was genuine. Makes real coffee (not grain substitute). Calls Marcus 'Marcus' at home, 'Dr. Klein' in public — distinction not discussed.

**Doris Hatch** — Junior at Coldwater High; one of Marcus Klein's three German students (with Hannah Pfeiffer and Louise Pelletier). Translates competently and without interest. Holds her breath when reading in a second language.

**Louise Pelletier** — Junior at Coldwater High; one of Marcus Klein's three German students (with Hannah Pfeiffer and Doris Hatch). Capable student; independently solved a dative of purpose without prompting in October 1943.

**Marcus's Zenith portable shortwave** — Zenith portable shortwave set on Marcus Klein's desk; acquired in Bangor, spring 1942; used most evenings to find BBC World Service (around 9.41 megacycles, shifting). Dial glows amber when warm. Distinct from the Zenith console in the Crows' parlor.

**Marcus's German notebook** — Marcus Klein's current notebook, written in German; begun April 1943; not yet half full by October 1943; kept on the shelf above his desk between an English journal and a grammar workbook. Spine written in his English hand — a quietly significant detail first noted in Chapter 03.

**Minna von Barnhelm** — Play by G.E. Lessing; used by Marcus Klein as a German class text in October 1943. Military setting made it timely; grammatical difficulty of Act One kept students occupied without requiring Marcus to contextualize the content.

**Mabel Lena** — Harbor vessel at Coldwater Reach; owner Clarence Pelletier; winter registration due November 15, 1943.

**Osgood** — Petty officer at the Coldwater Reach Coast Guard auxiliary station; wrote the scheduling note for Lt. Ennis's October 20 formal-statement visit to Eira; minor character, does not otherwise appear.

**harbor-log-oct20-statement** — Registered record: the harbor log entry Eira wrote October 20, 1943, noting the formal statement interview. Canonical text: 'October 20, 1943. Interview conducted with Lt. R. Ennis, USCG, re: body found October 12. Formal statement submitted. No additional information at this time.'

**Philco (Pfeiffer kitchen radio)** — Old Philco radio on the counter of the Pfeiffer farmhouse kitchen. Signal is uneven — some nights clear, some nights static. Distinct from the Zenith portable shortwave in Marcus Klein's room and the Zenith console in the Crow parlor. Hannah uses it for evening news; turns it off at eight.

**Pfeiffer south field** — One of the working fields of the Pfeiffer dairy farm. Mentioned in Albrecht's supper German (Ch 05) as needing attention before school — a recurring seasonal farm task.

**Heron** — The mail boat serving Coldwater Reach. White-sided, approximately 60 feet. Runs Tuesdays and Fridays from Rockland; turns around at four-thirty p.m. First named in Chapter 06.

**freight manifest, 7 November 1943** — Freight document for the school's book order (one crate, 43 lbs, textbooks, language classroom); Eira countersigned and added a non-required annotation ('Collected 7 November 1943, 14:10'); Marcus keeps it. Establishes his habit of keeping things.

**Agnes Mae** — Lobster boat home-harbored at Coldwater Reach; captained by Gilmore; one of two boats out in the November 14, 1943 storm, twelve miles south-southwest when Eira called them in.

**Carrie Holt** — Lobster boat home-harbored at Coldwater Reach; captained by Brewer; one of two boats out in the November 14, 1943 storm, seven miles out when Eira called them in.

**Gilmore** — Local lobsterman, captain of the Agnes Mae. Minor recurring harbor figure; responsive to harbormaster authority.

**Brewer** — Local lobsterman, captain of the Carrie Holt. Minor recurring harbor figure; stands off the breakwater correctly when instructed.

**Mrs. Thibodeau** — Town seamstress who altered Owen Bowman's peacoat for Eira in spring 1936 (shoulders taken in, hem raised one inch, cuffs re-sewn). She said nothing about whose coat it was.

**November 14, 1943 storm** — First hard storm of the season at Coldwater Reach; southwest blow, 22 knots gusting to 28, barometer 29.4 falling to 29.2, easing by 0218 November 15. The chapter in which Eira first holds the October 11 memory fully in mind.

**Vulcan No. 6 Furnace** — Coal-fired furnace in the basement of the Coldwater school, installed 1921. Runs lean on the right side near the upper draft plate in cold weather below 20 degrees — a characteristic Henry Crow manages with a specific scooping and damper technique. Henry's primary mechanical responsibility and the school's equivalent of Eira's tide clock.

**Edna Parsons** — School secretary at Coldwater school. In early October 1943 she received an informal parent letter questioning whether a German teacher (Marcus Klein) was appropriate 'given the times.' She responded citing Emergency Committee credentials; nothing formal was filed. She informed Henry Crow of the letter in late November 1943. Henry did not tell Marcus. Precursor to the formal complaint in Ch 22.

**Mr. Reedy** — Teacher at Coldwater school. Has not acknowledged Henry Crow in the hall since September 1932. A detail Henry has noted and filed as survivable.

**Mrs. Morse** — Teacher at Coldwater school. Says good morning to Henry in the hall. One of two women who speculate about the October body in the teachers' room in November 1943.

**Miss Tatum** — Teacher at Coldwater school. Says good morning to Henry in the hall. One of two women who speculate about the October body in the teachers' room in November 1943.

**Frost heave** — Seasonal phenomenon on the Pfeiffer farm and in the Maine winter landscape: deep freezing pushes fence posts (and other anchored objects) upward by several inches. Hannah repairs a frost-heaved paddock fence post in early December 1943; Karl Reinmar learns the term from her during their first direct conversation.

**Pfeiffer farm paddock fence** — Paddock fence at the Pfeiffer farm; southeast corner post frost-heaved in early December 1943 and repaired by Hannah Pfeiffer. Site of first direct exchange between Hannah and Karl Reinmar.

**Dr. Feld** — Marcus Klein's contact at the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars, New York office. Corresponds with Marcus about displaced scholar placements and the wider network. His December 1943 letter introduces the Felix K. case (Ch 10).

**Dr. Felix K.** — German-Jewish linguist, formerly of the University of Leipzig. Last documented in a letter postmarked Yarmouth, Nova Scotia, September 22, 1943, stating his intention to travel south by sea to Boston by late October 1943. Not heard from since. Full surname never given in the manuscript — protected thread. Probable identity of the man found on the Coldwater breakwater, but never confirmed. Marcus suspects this in Ch 24 via the wallet fragment.

**Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars** — Real historical organization. Marcus Klein has been on their rolls since autumn 1940. They placed him in Coldwater Reach in March 1942. Dr. Feld is his contact. Marcus keeps their correspondence in a dedicated folder in his room. The Committee's phrase 'last documented in' — bureaucratic language for a scholar gone missing — recurs in Ch 10 and resonates with Marcus's own experience.

**Emergency Committee correspondence folder** — Marcus Klein's folder of letters from the Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars, kept on the shelf in his second-floor east room at the Crow house, between his English journal and a volume of Stifter. Holds 13 letters by December 1943. Reappears in Ch 24.

**Vienna Philology Conference, 1936** — Conference held in Vienna in the Anatomiegebäude, 1936. Marcus Klein attended. He has a partial, unconfirmed memory of being introduced to a man who may have been Felix K. — Leipzig-accented German, Germanic philology / historical linguistics specialty. The name does not surface.

**Margaret Rose** — Dragger owned by the Pelletier family of Coldwater Reach; newer than Reg Farber's Callie B. by six years; moored at the outer dock in winter months.

**Inner Cove** — The sheltered inner portion of Coldwater Reach harbor, where fresh water off the Reach mixes with salt water at the margins; ices over in hard winter by late January (approximately four inches of opaque ice by January 22, 1944).

**Dock Extension** — The far end of the Coldwater Reach dock, past the regular slips, not visible from the road; the location where the unlighted night boat tied on October 11, 1943.

**karl-mother-letter (the letter)** — A single page of a letter to Karl Reinmar from his mother, written in Bavarian German with careful narrow handwriting. Domestic content: neighbor's fence, potato prices, cousin Els's October wedding, the cellar, the dog sick in December. Key phrase: Die die versucht haben zu gehen, sind weg. Wir beten für sie. ('Those who tried to go are gone. We pray for them.') Found by Hannah Pfeiffer in the milking barn after Karl stumbled on the icy sill patch, February 1944. Hannah keeps it rather than returning it; it passes to Marcus Klein's attention in Ch13. Canonical record: karl-mother-letter-page.

**Els (cousin)** — Named figure in Karl's mother's letter: a cousin who married in October (year unspecified), small family gathering. Karl's mother could not attend but sent a gift with Els's sister.

**pfeiffer-milking-barn** — The Pfeiffer dairy farm's milking barn. Hannah milks six Guernseys on the near side beginning at 4:30 a.m.; Albrecht milks three Holsteins in the far stalls. German prisoners arrive at 6 a.m. with Lonzo Reed for can-carrying and stall cleanup. Chronic icy patch at the barn-door sill in winter.

**die die versucht haben zu gehen, sind weg. Wir beten für sie.** — Bavarian German phrase from Karl Reinmar's mother's letter. Canonical text: 'Those who tried to go are gone. We pray for them.' Appears between the October-cousin-wedding paragraph and the firewood paragraph. Translated for Hannah Pfeiffer by Marcus Klein in Ch13. Recurs in Marcus's German notebook (Ch16) and in his final notebook entry (Ch28).

**Hannah-Marcus letter exchange** — Event in Ch13: Hannah Pfeiffer brings a single page of Karl Reinmar's mother's letter to Marcus Klein after German class (February 1944). Marcus reads the full page, translates the key phrase, makes his first personal disclosure (the route; 'the leaving is not the hard part'). Hannah leaves with the letter. Marcus sits with the adjacency to the Yarmouth/Felix K. thread.

**adjacencies (Marcus Klein's usage)** — Marcus Klein's internal vocabulary for connections that are real and probably significant but not yet proven — held in suspension rather than closed. Applied in Ch13 to the cluster of the Karl-mother-letter / Felix K. / Yarmouth / October breakwater body. A habit developed over six years of careful movement across borders.

**Yarmouth, Nova Scotia** — Harbor town in southwest Nova Scotia, across the Bay of Fundy from the Maine coast. The man who became the body on the breakwater departed Yarmouth approximately September 28, 1943, in a small wooden motorboat (~18 feet) heading south toward Massachusetts. The port authority filed an inquiry (received by Coldwater Reach via Portland, April 1944) about the missing boat and occupant.

**First Coast Guard District letter (March 1944)** — Official government letter dated March 19, 1944, from First Coast Guard District Headquarters, Boston, redirected through the Portland auxiliary. Routes the Yarmouth NS port authority's missing-vessel inquiry to affected Maine coastal stations. Arrives in Eira's Friday mail, April 1944. She files it in the desk drawer un-responded-to. Canonical record id: coast-guard-portland-yarmouth-inquiry-mar1944.

**continental European appearance** — The official language of the Yarmouth NS missing-persons inquiry as reproduced in the First Coast Guard District letter, March 1944. Used to describe the unknown motorboat occupant. Not Eira's language — she marks the phrase as the letter's, not hers. The identity of the described man is NOT confirmed by this letter.

**seltsam** — Bavarian/German adjective: strange in a way that is also true or right — not simple wrongness but the quality of an unexpected thing that is also somehow appropriate. Albrecht Pfeiffer uses it for early warm days in March, new calves, things wrong in a way that is not wrong. Karl uses it in his final line to Hannah in the barn conversation (Es ist seltsam, das hier zu hören — Strange, to hear it here). Not italicized in the prose; treated as part of Hannah's vocabulary.

**Es ist seltsam, das hier zu hören** — Karl Reinmar's final line to Hannah in the April 1944 barn exchange: 'It is strange to hear this here.' The closing line of the canonical barn-exchange-april44 record. Hannah reads an implication of gladness beneath the strangeness.

**Einiges** — Hannah's answer when Karl asks whether she understands the icehouse window German: 'Some of it.' Honest answer to an honest question; she has enough German for most of it and not all.

**Pfeiffer north field** — The north field of the Pfeiffer dairy farm. Low corner subject to waterlogging in spring thaw; the drainage ditch below the elbow chokes each winter. Site of the April 1944 POW field-preparation work that places Hannah and Karl in proximity for the barn conversation.

**Marcus's notebook theory (April 24, 1944)** — The full assembled theory written by Marcus Klein in his German private notebook on April 24, 1944: unknown man at Coldwater breakwater (October 1943) = probable Felix K. (Leipzig linguist, Feld letter, Yarmouth September 22 1943, heading to Massachusetts by sea, crossing 10-14 days, arrival mid-October). Registered as record marcus-notebook-theory-apr24. The question 'What do I intend to do with this?' is written below and left unanswered.

**Gut. Weiter so.** — German phrase written by Marcus at the bottom of Hannah Pfeiffer's April 1944 composition exercise: 'Good. Keep on like this.' Marks his decision to stop correcting her Bavarian dialect divergences from standard Hochdeutsch.

**June M.** — Reg Farber's lobster boat — a 32-foot wooden trap hauler, 11 years old in May 1944. His second vessel alongside the Callie B. dragger.

**The Heron** — Twice-weekly mail and freight boat running between Rockland and Coldwater Reach (Tuesdays and Fridays). Assigned to the outermost slip in Coldwater harbor since 1942. Mate: Ayers.

**Dock cleat #7** — Cleat number seven, midpoint starboard of the outer float, Coldwater Reach dock. Flagged for wear in Eira's fall 1943 maintenance survey (October 11, 1943 note); replaced after January 1944 mooring-line failure; inspected and confirmed sound May 11, 1944.

**Coldwater inner cove** — The sheltered inner portion of Coldwater Reach harbor. Ices over in hard winters (by late January); northwest corner opens first in spring thaw. Ice-out date spring 1944: May 3.

**D-Day (June 6, 1944)** — Allied amphibious landing on the Normandy coast of France (Operation Overlord); the largest sea operation in history at the time. News arrived by radio to the Pfeiffer kitchen during the morning of June 6. Central event of Ch18.

**Pfeiffer parlor radio** — Radio set in the Pfeiffer farmhouse parlor; better signal and larger speaker than the kitchen Philco; BBC reception without static. Albrecht moves to it by four o'clock on D-Day.

**Pfeiffer parlor winter chair** — Armchair in the corner of the Pfeiffer parlor by the window; gray slipcover put on each November, taken off each April. Associated with Albrecht's periods of deep listening (Pearl Harbor 1941; D-Day 1944).

**Nun ist es fertig** — German: 'Now it is finished.' Albrecht Pfeiffer's single spoken sentence on the evening of D-Day (June 6, 1944), addressed to the kitchen window and to no one. The word fertig carries the weight of completion without possibility of reversal. Left untranslated in the text of Ch18.

**The Selected Poems of Robert Frost** — Poetry collection assigned to Hannah Pfeiffer by Marcus Klein at the end of spring term 1944; she continues reading it after the term ends. Present at the kitchen table on the evening of June 6.

**Zenith (parlor console)** — The Zenith console radio in the Crow house parlor; the household's shared radio used to receive CBS and BBC broadcasts. Distinct from Marcus's personal portable shortwave set in his room. The D-Day chapter (Ch19) centers on this object.

**Klopstock monograph** — Marcus Klein's unfinished academic monograph on Klopstock, the eighteenth-century German poet. A chapter was in draft when Marcus fled Vienna in 1938 and was left in a drawer in his Vienna apartment. Its current status is unknown to him.

**BBC European Service** — The BBC's German-language radio service broadcast to occupied Europe during WWII; intended for listeners in occupied territories with clandestine receivers. Marcus briefly finds this signal overlapping with the English BBC frequency on June 6, 1944, and translates its formal German in real-time for Henry and Lillian.

**D-Day porch exchange** — The exchange between Marcus Klein and Henry Crow on the porch of the Crow house on the evening of June 6, 1944: four sentences across approximately twenty minutes — 'You have people there?' / 'I did. I don't know.' / 'It's a long way to go.' / 'It is.' The canonical image of Thread 6 (the friendship that neither names).

**Mrs. Pelletier** — A neighbor who lives across the back street from the Crow house. Referenced as having her light on late on the evening of June 6, 1944.

**E.J. Hatch (Earl Junior Hatch)** — Doris Hatch's older brother; served in the U.S. Navy; killed in action, WWII; War Department telegram received by his family on the second Tuesday of July 1944, delivered via the Heron mail boat. His name appears in Eira's August 1944 private log annotation.

**Eaton farm** — A farm in the Coldwater Reach valley road area. Albert Eaton's youngest son enlisted after Pearl Harbor (November 1941) and was killed in action; his War Department telegram arrived second Tuesday of July 1944 via the Heron.

**Burnham** — A Rockland man who brought his day sailer to Coldwater Reach for a summer mooring in late June 1944. Initially startled to find a woman harbormaster; repeated his mooring fees without incident. Minor recurring background character of the summer harbor.

**Herzlichen Glückwunsch** — German: 'Heartfelt congratulations' / 'Happy birthday.' Used by Albrecht Pfeiffer to wish Hannah a happy birthday in October 1944, and her 'Danke, Vater' response — both in German — marks a subtle threshold in their communication.

**north pasture upper wall** — Stone-and-mortar field wall along the highest edge of the Pfeiffer farm's north pasture. Required repointing in autumn 1944 before ground freeze. Karl and Fischer were assigned the work in October 1944 under Lonzo Reed's escort.

**fence-exchange-oct1944** — The canonical October 1944 German exchange between Hannah and Karl at the north pasture fence: four lines about mortar curing time before ground freeze. First exchange observed by Albrecht Pfeiffer, who watched from the field's edge without speaking.

**Vose, Mrs. Almira** — Coldwater parent who filed a formal written complaint to the school board against Dr. Klein in October/November 1944, citing his German nationality as unfitting for a school whose boys are dying in France. Her son served in France and was previously one of Marcus's Latin students. (Ch22)

**Pease, Mrs. Ingrid** — Coldwater school secretary since 1931. She tells Henry Crow about the Vose complaint before Marcus hears of it, and carries the information with evident distress. (Ch22)

**The German-teacher question** — Recurring phrase used in both the informal (Ch08) and formal (Ch22) complaints about Marcus Klein — referring to the fitness of a German-national to teach at a school whose boys are dying in the war. Not Henry's phrase; the phrase of those who complain.

**Breakwater Wallet** — A waterlogged leather wallet pulled from Reg Farber's winter lobster net off the inner bar on December 19, 1944, approximately eighty yards from the breakwater's cove-side base. Fourteen months in the water (from approximately October 1943). Center divider seam held, protecting a piece of heavy card stock with partial German script (three legible letters, possibly four). Locked by Eira in the middle desk drawer of the harbormaster's shack alongside the Yarmouth letter. Not disclosed to anyone as of December 19, 1944.

**Inner Bar** — Submerged sandbar in the inner cove of Coldwater Reach, approximately eighty yards from the cove-side base of the WPA breakwater. Tidal set runs north-northwest on the ebb; a natural settling point for objects drifting from the breakwater area. Reg Farber's winter lobstering ground (long lines, June M., from Thanksgiving onward).

**Battle of the Bulge** — The German Ardennes counteroffensive, which began December 16, 1944. News arrives continuously at the Coldwater harbor shack radio from December 16 onward. Historical time-anchor for Ch23 (December 19, 1944).

**The Breakwater Wallet** — A leather wallet recovered from the inner bar by Reg Farber's lobster net in December 1944, approximately eighty yards from the breakwater's cove-side base. Fourteen months in the water. The billfold and rear pockets failed; the center divider seam held. Inside: a piece of heavy card stock, folded twice, partially legible. The visible letters in the center third of the card — F and e (clearly), followed by damaged characters consistent with l or i, then x or k — are examined by Marcus Klein in January 1945. He reads them as consistent with the name Felix, cross-referenced against the Feld letter. Eira returns the wallet to her locked middle drawer after Marcus returns it to her.

**zuhause** — German: 'at home.' Used in Hannah's interior narration at the close of Chapter 25 as her name for what the kitchen in January 1945 means after the German-English exchange with her father. Appears untranslated and without italics throughout the novel.

**Bauer** — One of the four German POWs on the Pfeiffer farm work detail; the oldest of the group; surname only used. Has a winter cough worsened by cold; Lonzo Reed informally keeps him in the icehouse on mornings below ten degrees.

**Ardennes** — The forested region on the German-Belgian border where the German Army's Ardennes Counteroffensive (the Battle of the Bulge) began December 16, 1944. The news of the German push hits Albrecht Pfeiffer hardest of any war news, undoing the relative equilibrium that had settled over the farm since D-Day.

**Bulge, the** — Colloquial for the Battle of the Bulge (German Ardennes Counteroffensive, December 16, 1944 – late January 1945). Resolved in the Allies' favor; dramatized in Chapter 25 through Albrecht's radio listening and its effect on him.

**addendum-oct11-1943** — Eira Bowman's supplementary statement (February 28, 1945) to the October 12, 1943 harbor record, disclosing her observation of the unlighted vessel and single male occupant at the dock's eastern end at approximately 23:00 on October 11, 1943. Filed at USCG Aux Station Coldwater, forwarded to First District Portland. Canonical record registered; must be quoted verbatim in any later reference. The official case gains no name.

**Maisie K.** — Lobster boat working the outer bar off Coldwater Reach in winter 1944–45, arriving periodically at the dock alongside Reg Farber's June M.

**Kerrigan** — USCG petty officer on duty at the Coldwater Reach auxiliary station; transferred from Bath, Maine station in September 1944; received Eira's addendum and Yarmouth reply on February 28, 1945.

**Repatriation directive (March 1945)** — War Department directive forwarded through Camp Houlton to satellite work details: on German surrender, prisoners assemble at Camp Houlton for processing and staged transport back to Germany in phases. Lonzo Reed delivered it to the Pfeiffer farm on a Thursday in March 1945.

**Hast du Angst zurückzugehen?** — Hannah's German question to Karl at the fence in mid-March 1945: 'Are you afraid to go back?' Untranslated in the text; available from context. Karl's answer is honest and uncertain: he doesn't know what he's going back to.

**Wir sind noch hier / Du bist** — Exchange at the north-pasture fence, March 1945. Hannah: 'We are still here.' Karl: 'You are.' The asymmetry — you, not we — is present in the text without commentary.

**Jakob R.** — Marcus Klein's closest philology colleague at the University of Vienna for six years before 1938. Habit in seminar: begins disagreements with a question rather than a rebuttal. Last correspondence November 1940 (Berlin address). Fate unknown. Named only in Marcus's interior, Ch28.

**Grete H.** — Marcus Klein's German-language department assistant at the University of Vienna for seven years before 1938. Last known location: Vienna, 1938. Emergency Committee had no current documentation as of late 1942. Fate unknown. Named only in Marcus's interior, Ch28.

**Bergen-Belsen** — German concentration camp liberated by British forces on April 15, 1945. Featured in the Zenith parlor broadcast in Ch28; described by a radio correspondent in careful wire-dispatch language that grew more careful the more specific it became.

**Rockland Courier** — The Rockland, Maine newspaper Marcus receives through the school office. In Ch28 (approximately April 12-20, 1945), it carries Buchenwald and Bergen-Belsen liberation photographs and dispatches. Marcus opens the paper to page three.

**Lily B.** — Dragger (fishing vessel) operating out of Coldwater Reach harbor. Holds the third commercial float by seasonal assignment with the Mackerel Sky.

**Mackerel Sky** — Dragger (fishing vessel) operating out of Coldwater Reach harbor. Holds the third commercial float by seasonal assignment with the Lily B.

**Pelletier brothers** — Two brothers who own a lobster boat in Coldwater Reach harbor. In a mooring dispute with Reg Farber resolved by Eira on April 25, 1945; assigned the second float's outside mooring for the season.

**float 2 / float 3** — Float numbering at Coldwater Reach harbor. Float 3 (east side) is the commercial dragger float, seasonal assignment May–October. Float 2 is the adjacent dock for lobster boats; Farber (June M.) and the Pelletiers assigned inside and outside moorings respectively as of April 25, 1945.

**maple stand (south bend, Pfeiffer farm road)** — The stand of maple trees at the south bend of the road below the Pfeiffer farm. The road curves here and goes out of sight toward the highway south to Houlton. Used as a visual limit in Hannah's chapters — the point where the truck disappears in Chapter 30.
