# Chapter 02 — The Icehouse in October

**One-line summary**: Hannah hears about the body from town talk while her own world continues on its usual rhythms: the four-thirty milking, the prisoners in the barn yard, her parents' parallel silences.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Pfeiffer dairy farm and, briefly, the general store
**Time**: October 13–15, 1943

**Chapter shape**: Multi-scene
**Ending mode**: Elliptical
**Word target**: ~2,600

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: No single goal — this chapter establishes Hannah's world before her arc begins moving. She is curious about the body in the way of a seventeen-year-old who has heard something interesting; she is also trying to understand what her parents are.
**Obstacle**: Her parents' withdrawal makes every question feel wrong to ask at home. The farm's routine is the container everything else happens inside.

---

## Scenes

### Scene 1: Four-thirty (the barn)
The alarm. Hannah up before light. The milking barn: the sound of the cows that she has heard every morning of her life. The smell — hay and manure and the warm animal steam. She works efficiently, alone in the first part of it, before her father arrives. Albrecht moves through the barn without speaking English. He speaks to the cows in German; this is normal; he has always done this. What is not normal — or what became normal in the past two years — is that he does not switch back. Hannah answers him in English. He does not acknowledge this mismatch. The four German prisoners are still locked in the icehouse at this hour.

**German texture**: a single word from Albrecht — *Morgen* — that he says to Hannah the way he might say good morning. She has started saying it back because it feels less like a gulf than standing on the English side of one.

### Scene 2: The daily escort (the barn yard)
Lonzo Reed arrives at eight a.m., Springfield on his shoulder, to let the prisoners out. The four men are not dramatic about emerging into the cold — they put their jackets on and go to work. Karl Reinmar is the youngest, clearly. He is also the one who has learned the most English, which is still not much. He and Albrecht do not acknowledge each other in front of Lonzo, but Hannah has seen them at the icehouse window in the evenings. The morning work detail is ordinary to the point of absurdity. Lonzo stamps his feet and watches his breath.

### Scene 3: The general store (news arrives)
Hannah goes into town Wednesday afternoon for feed supplies. At the general store / post office, the body is what everyone is talking about — a man found on the breakwater, no name, no papers. She learns: the Coast Guard took him away; Reg Farber's boy found him; nobody knows who he was; no one from Coldwater is missing. She listens. She does not contribute what she knows, which is nothing. She carries the feed sacks to the truck.

### Scene 4: Evening (the icehouse window)
After supper, Hannah hears her father's voice through the kitchen wall — the low, careful cadence of his German, not the German of the cows but the German of a man speaking to another man. She goes to the window: Albrecht at the icehouse window, Karl on the other side of it. They are speaking, both of them, with a quality of attention she hasn't seen her father use in a long time. Her mother, Magda, is at the table behind Hannah, sewing, making no sound that is language.

**Close (elliptical)**: Hannah goes to bed. The body is still in her mind — an unknown man, no name — but what sits beneath it, the thing she can't shape yet, is the sound of her father talking to Karl through the window in the dark. She does not know what to do with any of this. She does nothing.

---

## Outcome

The chapter establishes Hannah's world and its specific arrangements before her arc begins. The icehouse, the farm's routine, her parents' silences, Lonzo's inadequacy, Karl's existence — these are the materials of her next eighteen months.

---

## New Question Raised

Why is Albrecht's German with Karl different from the German of his daily withdrawal? What are they talking about?

## Continuity Notes

- Four prisoners at the Pfeiffer farm since April 1943 (continuity #9). Karl is nineteen and the youngest (continuity #10).
- Lonzo Reed, Coastal Patrol auxiliary, Springfield rifle (continuity #11).
- Albrecht stopped speaking English at home; Magda has stopped speaking at all (continuity #8).
- The icehouse: renovated, wood stove, four cots, one window with a hasp (world.md).
- Milking begins at four-thirty a.m. — this is Hannah's physical anchor throughout.
- Karl's town of origin (Bavaria, within 50km of Albrecht's birthplace) is what shifted things between them after Albrecht told him; Hannah has witnessed the window conversations but has not joined them.
- The body is known about in town by Oct 13; Hannah learns it at the general store.

## Voice Notes

Hannah's register: sensory-close, the farm before perception rises toward thought. German words as texture: *Morgen*, possibly *die Kühe* (the cows) from her father — untranslated, not italicized. The chapter accumulates without architecturing the accumulation. The ending does not name what she feels; it describes what she does (goes to bed) and what she doesn't (say anything).
