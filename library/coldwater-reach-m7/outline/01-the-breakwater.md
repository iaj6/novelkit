# Chapter 01 — The Breakwater

**One-line summary**: A man's body is found at the seaward end of the breakwater on the morning of October 12, 1943; Eira Bowman processes the discovery as harbormaster and says nothing about what she saw the night before.

**POV**: Eira Bowman
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Coldwater Reach harbor dock and breakwater; the harbormaster's shack
**Time**: Morning, October 12, 1943

**Chapter shape**: Single scene (compressed into one long morning)
**Ending mode**: Turn
**Word target**: ~2,400

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Manage the morning properly — log the tides, get the radio check done, keep the dock in order.
**Obstacle**: Reg Farber's boy comes running from the breakwater before seven a.m. with news that there's a man at the end of it.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Opening**: Eira at the harbormaster's shack before dawn. She winds the tide clock (it loses four minutes a week; she resets it every Monday). She opens the log and enters the night's observations — wind NNW at nine knots, barometer steady, tide at 6:47 a.m. The handwriting is hers; Owen's entries stop on a page from November 1935 that she has never removed.

2. **The report**: Farber's boy (fifteen, unnamed) appears at the shack door. There's a man at the breakwater end. He thought the man was sleeping. He is not sleeping.

3. **The walk**: Eira takes her lantern and walks the breakwater. Two hundred yards of granite. The morning fog thick. She keeps the red fixed light to her left and does not hurry. She has learned not to run toward things.

4. **The body**: The man is at the seaward end, against the light-base. Middle-aged or older; impossible to say more. No identification visible. He is not dressed like a fisherman. His clothes are soaked through. His hands are empty. She notes: the body is positioned as if he sat down and did not get up.

5. **The Coast Guard**: She goes back to the shack and radios the Coast Guard auxiliary. Lt. Ennis (Coast Guard, Portland) arrives by nine a.m. with two enlisted men. She shows him what she found. She answers his questions with what the official record holds: she was at the dock around eleven p.m. the previous night checking the bilge pump on the *Callie B.* (Farber's dragger) after the previous day's chop; she saw nothing unusual; she left around eleven-thirty.

6. **The removal**: The Coast Guard photographs the scene, takes the body, and takes the log. Ennis tells Eira there will be a formal interview later in the week. He says they'll be in touch about the record. He does not say thank you. He is not unkind; he is efficient.

7. **The turn (close)**: Eira watches the Coast Guard truck drive up the hill from the dock. She goes back into the shack. She opens the log to where Ennis's men removed the pages and writes a new entry for October 12, 1943: weather, tide, the discovery, the Coast Guard's arrival time and departure time. She does not write what she saw at eleven p.m. the previous night. She closes the log. The tide clock reads four minutes fast.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The chapter turns at its close: the reader understands the official record has been filed and the official record is incomplete. What Eira saw the night before — not yet shown to the reader — is the thing the whole book carries forward.

## Continuity Notes

- The harbor log's earliest entry is June 1891; Owen's handwriting stops November 1935.
- The tide clock loses four minutes a week; Eira resets it Mondays.
- The breakwater is WPA-built, 1937, granite, two hundred yards, ends in a fixed red light.
- Lt. Ennis is the Coast Guard officer; he will return in Ch 04 for the formal statement.
- Reg Farber is established here as a local lobsterman; he returns in Ch 11 and Ch 23.
- Eira checks the bilge pump on the *Callie B.* (Farber's dragger) — this is her stated reason for being at the dock that night. It is true. It is not the whole truth.
- **Do not reveal what Eira saw on Oct 11.** The glimpse comes in Ch 07. The full picture assembles across the book.

---

## Voice Notes

Eira's chapter: procedural, short declaratives, the harbor as workplace. The body is a problem the office doesn't authorize her to solve. No interior monologue about what she's withholding — that remains entirely below the prose surface. The tide clock and the log and the specific weather readings do the emotional work.
