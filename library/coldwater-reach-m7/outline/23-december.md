# Chapter 23 — December

**One-line summary**: Reg Farber brings Eira a waterlogged wallet pulled from his net off the inner bar; inside, a scrap of paper with partial German script; she locks it in the log drawer; she tells no one.

**POV**: Eira Bowman
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The harbormaster's shack; the Coldwater dock
**Time**: December 1944

**Chapter shape**: Single scene (high weight, compressed)
**Ending mode**: Cliffhanger
**Word target**: ~2,000

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Manage the harbor in December — winter again, the season turning over, the harbor quieting.
**Obstacle**: Reg Farber brings something up from the sea that the official record says doesn't exist.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Context: December 1944**
   The Battle of the Bulge has begun in Europe (December 16, 1944); the news has been grim again after the relief of summer and autumn. The harbor radio brings it: German counteroffensive, American casualties. Eira logs the weather and the tide and keeps the radio on. There is nothing to do about the Battle of the Bulge from the harbormaster's shack in Coldwater, Maine. She does her work.

2. **Farber comes in**
   A Tuesday morning. Farber has been running a long line off the inner bar — winter lobstering, the water cold enough now that the lobster are moving deeper, but there are still traps out. He brings his catch in and then comes to the shack.

   He has a small object wrapped in a piece of tarp. He sets it on her desk. He says he pulled it up in his net off the inner bar — maybe eighty yards from where the breakwater meets the cove bottom. It's a wallet. Or what's left of one: leather, waterlogged and warped, the stitching gone on one side. He says he thought she should have it since she's the harbor authority. He doesn't say why he thinks it's relevant. He probably doesn't need to. October 1943 is not so far away.

3. **Eira examines it**
   She unwraps the tarp. The wallet is beyond identification in the usual sense — the leather cracked and shrunk, impossible to say what color it was. She opens what remains of it. Empty: whatever it contained has been in water for fourteen months, which is sufficient. But there is one small thing: a piece of heavy paper — heavier than ordinary paper, a note card or the flap from an envelope — that has been compressed by the water into a legible shape, because the wallet's damaged seam held it protected in a pocket that remained mostly sealed.

   The paper: a partial word in what looks like German script. Not printed — handwritten, in ink that has partly bled but which remains readable in its outlines. She can make out three or four letters clearly; the rest is smeared. The letters are consistent with German but she cannot read German.

   She looks at it for a long time.

4. **What she does**
   She wraps the wallet back in the tarp. She opens the log drawer — the desk drawer she uses for the log pages and the freight files — and puts it in. She locks the drawer. She has a key on her keyring and the spare is at her house. There is no other key.

   She thanks Farber. She says she'll look into it. He says he figured as much. He goes back to his boat.

5. **Close (cliffhanger)**
   She sits at the desk. The radio: more from the Bulge. The day's work is in front of her — the log entries, the radio check schedule, the February dock assignments that need filing. She does not open the drawer again. She goes home at the end of the day and the wallet is in the drawer and she knows it is there and she does not tell anyone.

   The chapter closes on the fact of what's in the drawer. The wallet came from the water where the body may have gone in, or where the man dropped it, or where it fell. It is in her possession. She is the only person who knows. The reader's question is sharp and simple: what is she going to do?

## Outcome

The chapter functions as a cliffhanger: Eira has found a material connection to the October mystery. The wallet may belong to the dead man (the inner bar, eighty yards from the breakwater, is consistent with where something would drift and settle). The German script fragment is something she can't read but which someone can. She locks it up and tells no one. The cliffhanger is genuine: the reader knows she will have to do something with this, but not yet.
