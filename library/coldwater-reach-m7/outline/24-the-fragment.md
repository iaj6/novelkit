# Chapter 24 — The Fragment

**One-line summary**: Eira shows Marcus the wallet fragment; he reads what he can of the German script; he cross-references Dr. Feld's letter in his room that night; he thinks he knows who the man was; he does not tell Eira what he thinks.

**POV**: Dr. Marcus Klein
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The harbormaster's shack; Marcus's room at the Crow house
**Time**: January–February 1945

**Chapter shape**: Single scene (the meeting) and its immediate aftermath
**Ending mode**: Turn
**Word target**: ~2,200

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Marcus has been asked by Eira to come to the shack — she has something she wants him to look at, briefly, in his capacity as someone who reads German. He comes without knowing what he is walking into.
**Obstacle**: What the fragment may be telling him, and what he owes Eira when he reads it.

---

## Action Beats

1. **The request**
   Eira comes to the school in January — not to the classroom but to the school office, where she leaves a note for Marcus: could he stop at the harbormaster's shack at his convenience, as she has a document in German she would like him to translate. The note is in her compressed, exact handwriting. He reads it three times.

   He comes on a Thursday afternoon.

2. **At the shack**
   She opens the drawer and unwraps the tarp. She sets the wallet on the desk and opens it to the protected pocket. She does not explain beyond what she has to: it came from Farber's net, off the inner bar, in December. She asks him to read what he can of the paper.

   He picks up the fragment with the same care he would give an archive document. The paper: heavy, partially bled, the handwriting visible in outline. He looks at it for a long time.

3. **What he reads**
   The letters he can make out: five letters, two fully legible, three partial. Together they suggest — suggest, not confirm — the beginning of a German surname. The first two letters are clear: *Fe*. The next is ambiguous: could be *l* or *i*. The last two letters are heavily bled but the ink's ghost suggests *x* or possibly *k*.

   He says, carefully: "I can make out part of what may be a name. The first two letters are *F* and *e*. The subsequent letters are damaged." He does not say what the letters suggest to him. He asks: "Where did you say this was found?" She repeats: Farber's net, the inner bar, December.

   He sets the fragment down. He says: "I'm not certain it's legible enough to identify. I'll need to look at it more carefully. May I keep it overnight?" She looks at him. She takes his measure. She says: "Yes." She wraps it back in the tarp and hands it to him.

4. **His room (the cross-reference)**
   That evening: Marcus at his desk. The fragment, unwrapped, under his lamp. His Emergency Committee correspondence folder. Dr. Feld's December 1943 letter.

   He reads: *Felix K., linguist, formerly of Leipzig. Last documented Yarmouth, Nova Scotia, September 22, 1943.*

   Felix. *Fe* — *l* or *i* — *x*.

   The probability is very high. He knows it. He sits with it.

   He gets his German notebook. He writes: *Fe–?–x. Felix K. Yarmouth, September 1943. Coldwater, October 1943. Inner bar, recovered December 1944.* He draws a line. He writes: *If this is correct, then Felix K. is the man on the breakwater, and has been for fourteen months, and no one has known his name.* He draws another line. He writes: *I do not know for certain. The fragment is not sufficient.*

   He closes the notebook. He looks at the fragment under the lamp for a long time.

5. **Close (turn)**
   He puts the fragment back in the tarp. He does not open the shortwave tonight. He thinks about what he owes Eira: not a name — he doesn't have a confirmed name to give her — but a theory. He thinks about whether a theory helps or harms. He decides he will return the fragment in the morning and tell her what he can honestly say: that the letters are consistent with a German-speaking name beginning *Fe*, and that he cannot determine more without additional documentation.

   He will not tell her about Dr. Feld's letter. Not yet. He is not sure why not yet. He goes to bed.

   The turn: the reader now knows what Marcus thinks he knows. The dead man was probably Felix K. The name stays unconfirmed. The question is no longer who the man was but what Marcus will do with what he probably knows and has decided not to say.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The chapter turns on Marcus's decision to withhold his theory from Eira — not permanently (he will tell her eventually, probably, or she will find the Feld letter in Ch 26's filing) but for now. He has the probable name; he does not give it. The turn is: the reader knows more than Eira does, and Marcus is choosing this. This is the chapter that brings the mystery as close to resolution as it will ever come — and then holds.

---

## New Question Raised

Will Marcus tell Eira? Will he tell anyone? What does it mean that the man's name is probably Felix K. — and that no one, including Marcus, will say it aloud with certainty?

## Voice Notes

Marcus's chapter: precise, careful, subordinate clauses. The shack scene: the dialogue between Eira and Marcus should be exact and restrained — she asks, he reads, he asks to keep it, she agrees. No more than necessary. The cross-reference scene in his room: the notebook entries should be brief and exact (as in Ch 16). The close: his decision not to tell Eira comes without drama or self-congratulation — it arrives as a practical conclusion he is not entirely comfortable with. The prose shortens at the key moment (when he sees the letters suggest Felix K.) — the qualifying architecture should drop for one beat and then reassemble.
