# Chapter 22 — At the School

**One-line summary**: A formal parent complaint about Dr. Klein reaches the school board; Henry learns of it before Marcus does; he handles what needs handling; what he carries home.

**POV**: Henry Crow
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Coldwater school; the Crow house
**Time**: October–November 1944

**Chapter shape**: Single scene across a day
**Ending mode**: Resolved beat
**Word target**: ~1,500

**Note**: Second fourth-perspective chapter. Short. Henry's voice is direct and exact.

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Henry's goal, as always, is the building — furnace, supplies, maintenance, the school's daily running.
**Obstacle**: A formal complaint from a parent (Mrs. Almira Vose) has gone to the school board about Dr. Klein. The complaint: that a man who is technically a German national, however long ago he left Germany, should not be teaching the children of a town whose boys are dying in France. The complaint is foolish and Henry knows it; it is also not nothing, in 1944, and he knows that too.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Morning (the furnace)**
   Henry at the school by six, same as always. The furnace in October is familiar work. He knows the building's sounds by now as Eira knows the harbor's sounds. The school filling up at eight. This is the Tuesday after the formal complaint was submitted to the board on Monday.

2. **Henry hears**
   The school secretary, Mrs. Ingrid Pease, tells Henry about the complaint when he comes through the office to pick up the supply order. She is distressed about it. The complaint was submitted in writing by Mrs. Vose, whose son is in France. The board will take it up at the November meeting. Mrs. Pease does not think it will go anywhere. She wants Henry to know because she thinks he ought to know, and she's probably right.

   Henry thanks her. He does not make a face. He asks if the supply order includes the new chalk for the second floor. She says yes. He takes the box and goes.

3. **Henry and Marcus in the hallway**
   Midmorning. Marcus is between classes. Henry passes him in the second-floor hallway — their usual nod, the window check ("the east window sticking again?" "No, fixed"). Then Henry says, without pausing: "Mrs. Vose sent a letter to the board." He says her name because Marcus will have heard it; her son is one of his former Latin students. "About the German-teacher question." He keeps moving. He does not look back.

   Marcus says, after a beat: "I see." Henry says: "November meeting." He's at the stairwell. He goes down.

   This is not a warning in any formal sense. Henry has said two things that are true in a hallway. What Marcus does with them is Marcus's business.

4. **What Henry carries**
   He finishes his work. He does not think about the complaint with the portion of his mind he uses for the furnace and the supply orders. He thinks about it with the other portion — the one that has learned, in eleven years in this town, which things are survivable and which require action. The board will likely not act on the complaint; Marcus is the only German and Latin teacher in the county, and they know it. But likely is not certain.

   He also knows that this information — the complaint, the board, the November meeting — is not his to carry alone without giving Marcus the chance to prepare. He has given Marcus the chance. What happens next is not Henry's to manage.

5. **Evening (Lillian)**
   Henry home. Lillian has supper. He tells her about the complaint. She says: "Does he know?" Henry says: "I told him." She says: "Good." They eat. She says: "It won't go anywhere." Henry says: "Probably not." She puts more food on his plate.

6. **Close (resolved beat)**
   Henry at the table after supper. He has done what he can do: told Marcus what Marcus needed to know, in the space that allows Marcus to hear it without being humiliated. The thing is handled as much as it can be handled by the person he is in the position he holds. The board will meet in November. He does not know what they'll decide. He has done his part. He goes to bed.

---

## Outcome / Resolved Beat

Henry has navigated a genuinely difficult situation with his characteristic precision: the compliment is in the handling. He told Marcus without telling Marcus; he protected without protecting in a way that costs either of them the specific dignity each requires. The resolved beat is genuine: he has done what he could.

---

## New Question Raised

What does the board decide? What does Marcus do with the information? Does Marcus know Henry went out of his way to tell him?

---

## Continuity Notes

- The parent complaint: Mrs. Almira Vose, whose son is in France. A formal written complaint to the school board about Dr. Klein's German nationality.
- The November board meeting: it will likely not result in Marcus's dismissal (the school has no other German/Latin teacher), but it is real and has a real cost to Marcus's position.
- Henry's notification to Marcus: done in the hallway in thirty seconds, in two pieces of information, without stopping. This is exactly how Henry would do it.
- The informal complaint mentioned in Ch 08 (School Hours) was from a different parent, earlier in the year, and was not formally filed. This is a separate, formal complaint — an escalation.
- Henry does not discuss Marcus's Sherbrooke history or anything personal about Marcus; he deals only in facts (the complaint, the date of the board meeting).
- Lillian's response: "Does he know?" / "Good." She has her own views and they are also handled in two words.
- The board's eventual decision is not dramatized in any chapter; it is implied (Marcus is still teaching in Ch 28, April 1945).

---

## Voice Notes

Henry's register: direct, exact, no editorializing. His interior should be minimal — he thinks about what needs doing, he does it, he goes to bed. The chapter's resolution is in what Henry accomplishes, not in what he feels about accomplishing it. The Lillian scene: four sentences, genuine, quick. The close should be brief — Henry's chapters are the book's shortest and most compressed.
