# Chapter 17 — Ice Out

**One-line summary**: Ice gone from the harbor; a dock-fee dispute with Reg Farber resolved at human scale; Eira and Marcus exchange twelve words at the dock; the harbor opens into spring.

**POV**: Eira Bowman
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Coldwater Reach harbor and dock
**Time**: May 1944

**Chapter shape**: Multi-scene
**Ending mode**: Resolved beat
**Word target**: ~2,800

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Get the harbor operational for the season — dock fees collected, mooring assignments confirmed, the draggers back out.
**Obstacle**: Reg Farber is behind on his dock fees; he's had a bad winter and is asking for consideration. The request is reasonable and puts Eira in the position of exercising the particular authority she holds: not rule-following, but judgment.

---

## Scenes

### Scene 1: The ice out (early May)
The inner cove clears fully in the first week of May. Eira has been watching the ice pull back for weeks; now it's done and the harbor is open water, the salt-tang back in the air after the winter's cold suppression of it. She opens the shack door in the morning and it smells like the harbor she runs. She notes the ice-out date in the log: May 3, 1944. The tide clock, re-set again.

The boats go back out. The lobster season starting. The two draggers making their first runs of the year. Eira runs the radio check in the morning and the dock is alive with the seasonal resumption that she finds, each year, slightly startling — how quickly the harbor becomes itself again.

### Scene 2: The Farber dispute
Reg Farber comes to the shack on a Friday. He is behind on the seasonal dock fee for his two boats — he took a hard winter, one mooring line snapped and damaged the dock cleat, he's been paying the repair out of his own account. He asks if she can defer one boat's fee to July. He is not a difficult man and the request is reasonable.

This is an authority question. The fee schedule is fixed; the harbormaster doesn't have official discretion to defer. She could tell him this and enforce the schedule. She could refer him to the harbormaster's general fund for hardship consideration, which is a bureaucratic process that will take until August.

She does neither. She writes in the log that the fee for the second Farber boat is under review pending inspection of the cleat damage (which is real and is her legitimate business anyway). This creates a legitimate reason for delay without creating a lie in the record. The fee will be paid in July. The record will show it was reviewed and appropriate.

Farber thanks her. She says the cleat inspection will need to be this week. He says he'll be at the dock Thursday morning. She notes it.

This is authority exercised at human scale. She does not congratulate herself on it. She goes back to the radio check.

### Scene 3: Marcus at the dock
Marcus comes to the dock in mid-May — a different Friday, not Farber's. He is there for the mail boat, as before. Eira is checking lines. They are within speaking distance for perhaps three minutes as she moves down the dock toward where the *Heron* is tying up.

Marcus says: "Warmer." She says: "Finally." He says: "The boats are back." She says: "They are." He says: "Any word — " She says: "Nothing from Portland." He nods. She keeps walking. He collects his package from the *Heron*.

Twelve words. Neither of them stops moving. The exchange carries the weight of the whole winter in it and releases it without anyone acknowledging this. What was said: warmer, finally, the boats are back, they are, any word, nothing from Portland. What was meant is the same and also more.

### Scene 4: Close (the log, evening)
She writes the day's entries. Weather, tide, traffic. The Farber inspection (cleat assessed, repair confirmed, fee review noted). Then, at the day's close, she writes one more line in the log that is also the chapter's close — not the kind of line that will mean anything to a future harbor archivist but which she writes because she has not let herself write anything like it since October:

*May. Ice out. Harbor open. All boats accounted for.*

The phrase is operational. It is also something else. She closes the log.

---

## Outcome / Resolved Beat

Two things resolve: the Farber dispute (handled cleanly, with judgment and legality in balance) and the long winter of distance between Eira and the harbor's spring (which returns, and she is here for it). The exchange with Marcus is small and exact and resolves one note of their relationship's wariness — they are now people who have spoken to each other more than once and know how to handle the space around what they're not saying.

## New Question Raised

What does the harbor's reopening release in Eira? And: "nothing from Portland" — is the Yarmouth inquiry still sitting in her desk drawer?

## Continuity Notes

- Ice-out date: approximately May 3, 1944.
- Reg Farber: lobsterman, established in Ch 01 and Ch 11. His dock fee situation is original to this chapter.
- The Farber fee resolution: logged as "under review pending cleat inspection" — legitimate, not fraudulent. This is Eira's authority exercised correctly.
- Marcus's "any word" = any word about the body/the October case. "Nothing from Portland" = the official investigation has not been reopened. Eira does not mention the Yarmouth letter from Ch 14, which is still in her desk drawer.
- "All boats accounted for" — the log phrase resonates with the October mystery (a boat was unaccounted for) without naming it.
- D-Day is approximately one month away (June 6, 1944); this chapter is set just before that event.

## Voice Notes

Eira's chapter: procedural, short declaratives. The Farber scene should feel like exactly what it is — a harbor-management problem solved with competence and judgment — without any authorial commentary on what this represents for her arc. The twelve words with Marcus: render them in their brevity. The chapter's log entry at the close is the emotional punctuation; it should be exact and operational and carry the weight without being explained.
