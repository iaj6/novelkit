# Chapter 26 — The Addendum

**One-line summary**: Eira writes and submits an addendum to the October 1943 harbor record: she observed a vessel and a single figure on the night of October 11; the addendum will be filed and unread; something changes for her anyway.

**POV**: Eira Bowman
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The harbormaster's shack; the Coast Guard auxiliary station
**Time**: February–March 1945

**Chapter shape**: Single scene
**Ending mode**: Elliptical
**Word target**: ~2,800

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Eira has been carrying her knowledge of the night of October 11, 1943 for sixteen months. After the fragment (Ch 23–24) she has arrived, without dramatic decision, at the point of formally recording what she knows.
**Obstacle**: She cannot put anything in the record that wasn't there before without acknowledging why it wasn't there before. The addendum is also a confession of omission — not a crime, but not nothing.

---

## Action Beats

1. **The decision forms (without being a decision)**
   February 1945. The war in Europe clearly ending — the Russians from the east, the Allies pushing from France. The harbor radio brings this daily. She does her work. The wallet is back in her desk drawer (Marcus returned the fragment; she has both the wallet and the Yarmouth letter and the fragment in the locked drawer now). She has thought, since the autumn, about what she is going to do with these things. The answer has been arriving slowly since December, the way the ice-out arrives — not a moment but a process.

   She is going to write the addendum.

2. **Writing it**
   She takes out harbormaster's stationery — the official form she uses for supplementary reports. She writes it in a single sitting, at the desk in the shack, on a Wednesday morning in February when the harbor is quiet.

   The addendum, in full: she was present at the Coldwater Reach dock on the night of October 11, 1943, at approximately 23:00 hours, performing routine inspection of the *Callie B.* (Farber's dragger). She observed a small wooden motorboat, running without lights, approach from the south and tie at the dock's eastern end near the breakwater access. She observed a single occupant, male, of approximately middle age, who left the vessel on foot and proceeded in the direction of the breakwater. She did not engage with the individual and observed him for less than one minute. She was unable to identify the individual or the vessel. The vessel was absent when she returned to the dock the following morning. She did not include this observation in her October 12 statement to Lt. Ennis because she judged it insufficiently documented and uncertain. She submits it now as supplementary material.

   She signs it: Eira Bowman, Harbormaster, Coldwater Reach. Date: February 28, 1945.

3. **The Yarmouth letter**
   She also drafts a formal reply to the Yarmouth, NS, inquiry (the letter she received in Ch 14 and put in the drawer). The reply directs them to Lt. Ennis's case file at the Coast Guard First District, Portland, and notes that the supplementary material (the addendum) has been submitted to the same file. She does not speculate. She does not name anyone.

4. **At the auxiliary station**
   She walks up the hill to the Coast Guard auxiliary station and submits both documents — the addendum and the Yarmouth reply — to the petty officer on duty. He logs their receipt and gives her a copy. He will forward them to Portland. The case file exists; the file will receive these documents; the file will be closed again.

   She asks: has anything come of the October 1943 inquiry? He checks his records — he looks through the log — and says: no, nothing from Portland. She says: thank you. She walks back down the hill.

5. **Close (elliptical)**
   Back at the shack. The harbor outside the window — late February, still cold, the cove not yet thinking about thawing. She sits at the desk. The drawer is locked but lighter now — the Yarmouth letter and the addendum are filed; only the wallet and fragment remain. She did not include the wallet and fragment in her submissions because they are physical objects that belong to the dead man and should go to the official file, but she is not sure she is ready to give them up. She is not sure why.

   She does not feel better. She does not feel worse. She feels something she doesn't name: the particular quality of having done what she can do from where she stands, which is not the same as having done everything and is not the same as having done nothing.

   She opens the log. She writes the day's conditions. Tide, barometer, weather. She writes the formal notation of the addendum's submission: Supplementary statement re: October 12, 1943 incident submitted to USCG Aux Station Coldwater, forwarded to 1st District Portland. Then she closes the log. She is still harbormaster. The harbor is there outside the window. She goes back to work.

---

## Outcome

Eira's central arc resolves here — she has done the thing the arc required: acknowledged what she knew, put it in the record, given the coast its accounting even though the accounting changes nothing recoverable about what happened to the man. The elliptical close is right because the personal question (could she have saved him?) is not answered. It is not answerable. What is resolved is her relation to her own authority: she exercised it correctly within its limits, and then she exceeded those limits — slightly, without permission — and she has now made the official record of what she did. The case stays open, technically. She has done what she can.

---

## New Question Raised

The addendum is filed; the case gets no name. Will anyone ever read it? What is the wallet and fragment still in her drawer?

## Continuity Notes

- **PROTECTED THREAD**: The addendum does not name the dead man. The official case is supplemented but not solved. The identity stays open.
- The addendum text is rendered in full above — this is the canonical version. When drafting, the drafter should render it (or something very close to it) as Eira's written document. **Register this as a canonical record in the world store** so any later reference to the addendum reproduces it accurately.
- Eira's reason for the original omission: she "judged it insufficiently documented and uncertain." This is the harbormaster's language for what she could not quite say to Ennis in October 1943. It is true enough.
- The Yarmouth, NS, reply: she sends it to the Yarmouth port authority via Portland. This completes the paperwork chain from Ch 14.
- The wallet and fragment remain in her drawer. She does not submit the physical objects at this time. This is deliberate (she doesn't explain it) and is Eira's own small choice.
- The harbor in late February 1945: still cold, cove not thawing. The spring log is Ch 29 (late April); there is still time.
- The Coast Guard auxiliary station petty officer on duty: unnamed, administrative, not a character.
- Lt. Ennis: not present here; his name appears only in the documentation.

---

## Voice Notes

Eira's chapter: the addendum should be rendered in the chapter's text — either directly (she writes it; we read what she writes) or as close to direct quotation as the prose permits. It should read like what it is: an official supplementary report in Eira's compressed, exact, harbormaster's English. The close should be short and not reflective. "She is still harbormaster. The harbor is there outside the window. She goes back to work." Trust this. Don't add an internal monologue about whether she did the right thing.
