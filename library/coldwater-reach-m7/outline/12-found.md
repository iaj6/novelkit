# Chapter 12 — Found

**One-line summary**: Hannah finds a page from a letter in the milking barn — it fell from Karl's pocket during morning work — reads it in German before returning it, then keeps it instead.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Pfeiffer milking barn
**Time**: February 1944

**Chapter shape**: Single scene (high weight)
**Ending mode**: Turn
**Word target**: ~2,200

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Morning milking, same as always. No particular goal.
**Obstacle**: Something falls from Karl's pocket during the work. Hannah finds it. The obstacle is the decision of what to do with it, which takes about thirty seconds to make and is not made carefully.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Opening: the barn before light**
   Four-thirty a.m. The alarm. Hannah in the barn: the cows, the dark, the steam from their breath, the familiar smell. February — the coldest month; the frost inside the barn door. She works her section without thinking about it, the way she works everything in this barn.

2. **The prisoners are brought in at six**
   Lonzo Reed arrives with the four men. The early morning work is shared: the prisoners muck the far stalls and carry the milk cans while Hannah and her father manage the milking. Albrecht works in silence; Karl's work is becoming more competent with the farm routine. At some point during the can-carrying, Karl moves quickly — a stumble on the icy patch inside the barn door, one of the cans knocked sideways, the men righting it. A piece of paper falls from his jacket pocket. He doesn't notice. He picks up the milk can. He goes.

3. **Hannah finds it**
   She picks up the paper. It's a single folded page — worn at the folds, the paper thin with handling. Karl carries this in his pocket. This knowledge comes before she unfolds it. She looks up: Karl is outside, the door is closed, Lonzo Reed is not in view. She unfolds the page.

4. **The letter (what she reads)**
   It's in German — a woman's handwriting, careful and slightly formal, the kind of German Hannah recognizes as Bavarian. A mother's letter to her son. Domestic and specific: the neighbor's fence, the cost of potatoes, the news about a cousin who married in October. And then one phrase that stops her: *Die die versucht haben zu gehen, sind weg. Wir beten für sie.* ("Those who tried to go are gone. We pray for them.")

   She doesn't know who "those who tried to go" refers to. She knows her German well enough to know the phrase is specific — not an abstraction about the war but a reference to particular people the mother knows. She doesn't ask. There's no one to ask.

5. **The decision (thirty seconds)**
   She should give it back. Karl came in and went out without noticing; she should leave it on the ledge where it's visible when he comes back in. Instead she folds it, puts it in her own pocket, and goes back to the milking.

   She does not narrate this decision to herself. She makes it without the vocabulary to describe what she's doing. She finishes the morning work.

6. **Close (turn)**
   That afternoon she takes the page out of her pocket at the kitchen table and reads it again. It is still Bavarian, still a mother's letter. *Die die versucht haben zu gehen* still stops her. She folds it again and puts it in her school notebook, between the pages of her English literature notes.

   She has taken something that is not hers. The chapter's turn: a small choice has been made with consequences she can't predict yet, and the fact of it sits plainly on the page — she took it, she knows she took it, she did not give it back.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The turn is Hannah's act of keeping the letter. It is not a morally clear act; it's not entirely clear to Hannah what her reason is; the prose does not supply a reason. The letter, with its phrase about "those who tried to go," is now in Hannah's possession, where Marcus will eventually read it.

---

## New Question Raised

What will Hannah do with the letter? And what does "those who tried to go" mean?

---

## Continuity Notes

- Karl's letter: from his mother, Bavarian German, careful handwriting. She writes about domestic matters and includes the phrase *die die versucht haben zu gehen, sind weg. Wir beten für sie.* ("Those who tried to go are gone. We pray for them.")
- This phrase is the key mystery artifact. In Ch 13, Marcus hears something in it that Hannah doesn't. The phrase refers, in the mother's context, to people from their Bavarian community who tried to emigrate or flee before the war closed the borders. It is not about the dead man specifically but resonates with the mystery.
- Karl is carrying this letter page folded in his jacket pocket — it is the letter his mother wrote that he has kept to carry with him.
- The barn: icy patch inside the door — this is what causes Karl's stumble.
- Hannah does not tell anyone she found the letter, took it, or kept it. She shows it to Marcus in Ch 13 by presenting it as "found" and asking about one word.
- **The letter page is a load-bearing document.** Register it as a canonical record when drafting so its text is preserved for any later re-quotation.

---

## Voice Notes

Hannah's register: physical, close, without analysis of what she's doing. The chapter should move quickly — it's a single morning's scene. The thirty-second decision should be rendered in exactly that time, without the internal deliberation that would make it more legible and less true. "She folds it, puts it in her own pocket, and goes back to the milking" — this sentence is the turn. Trust it. The close (she reads it again at the kitchen table) should carry the same quality: she reads it, she keeps it, the chapter ends.
