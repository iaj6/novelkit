# Chapter 03 — The Crow House

**One-line summary**: Marcus hears about the body at school; the Crow house and his rented room are established; the shortwave finds and loses signals from across the Atlantic.

**POV**: Dr. Marcus Klein
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Coldwater school; the Crow house at 14 Elm Street
**Time**: October 14–16, 1943

**Chapter shape**: Interiority-heavy
**Ending mode**: Literary fade
**Word target**: ~2,800

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Marcus is trying to get through another week — German to three students who would rather not be there, Latin to twelve who find it merely difficult — while assembling the self he is still assembling.
**Obstacle**: The body on the breakwater has introduced a distraction he didn't plan for. He is curious about it with the precision of someone for whom unexplained things are a professional hazard.

---

## Scenes / Movement

### Scene 1: At school (the classroom)
Marcus's German class: three girls, all juniors, one of whom (Hannah Pfeiffer) is not there today. He notices her absence because she is the only one who doesn't look slightly embarrassed to be there. The other two — Doris Hatch and Louise Pelletier — translate competently and without interest. He sets them a passage from the textbook and listens to them construe it with the accuracy of people who have no idea what they are translating.

In the hallway afterward, he overhears two teachers discussing the body on the breakwater — the dead man, the Coast Guard, the closed record. Marcus listens from around the corner, which is not something he would have done in Vienna but which he has learned to do here, where conversations stop when he enters a room. He learns: no papers, no name, not from Coldwater. He files this.

### Scene 2: The Crow house (evening)
Marcus walks up the hill from school. The house: white clapboard, second-from-the-end on Elm Street, a porch where Henry sits in the last hour of light if the weather allows. Tonight it doesn't; October closing in. Inside: Lillian has coffee (real coffee, not the grain substitute; this is one of the things Marcus has stopped asking about). Henry is at the kitchen table with the Rockland paper.

The Crow house is the only place in Coldwater where Marcus is not a problem to be managed. This is rendered through its specific textures: the arrangement of chairs, who pours what, the way Lillian says his name (Dr. Klein in public, Marcus at the table, the distinction is not discussed). Henry's economy of gesture — a nod that means *sit down*, a silence that means *I heard you*.

Marcus mentions the body. Henry says: "Found him yesterday?" Marcus confirms. Henry returns to the paper. This is not incuriosity — it is Henry's way of indicating that some information requires no particular response. Marcus understands this. He is still learning to do it.

### Scene 3: His room (the shortwave)
Marcus's room: second floor, east-facing, smells of woodsmoke and cold books. He has a shortwave set — a Zenith portable — that he works most evenings. He finds the BBC World Service; he loses it; he finds it again. The news from Europe comes in waves: Italy, the Pacific, the convoy situation. He doesn't write in his German notebook tonight. He listens to the static and thinks about the man on the breakwater.

He does not know why the man interests him. He is aware this is not a rational interest. A dead man with no papers, no name, found at the edge of the sea — he is aware, without stating it, that this description has some formal similarity to his own situation in 1941, when he emerged from Sherbrooke with paperwork that described him as resident alien enemy and a suit that no longer fit. He does not pursue this thought. The shortwave finds a clear signal, then loses it.

**Close (literary fade)**: The room. The radio hissing. The window east-facing, and the harbor somewhere down the hill through the dark, and somewhere on the shelf above his desk his German notebook, and his English handwriting has become better than his German handwriting, and this is still something that startles him when he notices it. He turns off the radio. The room is dark and cold and is, for the moment, where he lives.

## Outcome

Marcus is established: his situation, his household, his mind's habits. The body is filed as a curiosity he can't stop thinking about. The Crow house is established as a specific physical and social fact.

---

## New Question Raised

Why does the man on the breakwater interest Marcus so specifically? What does he recognize in the description (no papers, no name) that he has not yet let himself name?

## Voice Notes

Marcus's register: precise, slightly formal, subordinate clauses as protective habit. The chapter is interiority-heavy because this is his mode — but the interiority reaches us through observation of specific things (the house arrangement, the shortwave frequencies, the notebook on the shelf) rather than through named reflection. When he notices the parallel between himself and the dead man, the prose should shorten, not lengthen. The observation arrives complete; the drafter does not gloss it.
