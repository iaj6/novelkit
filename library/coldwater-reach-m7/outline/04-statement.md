# Chapter 04 — Statement

**One-line summary**: Lt. Ennis returns to take Eira's formal statement; the Coast Guard closes the case without a name; the record is gone; Eira has left something out.

**POV**: Eira Bowman
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The harbormaster's shack, Coldwater Reach
**Time**: October 18–20, 1943

**Chapter shape**: Single scene (the interview, with before and after)
**Ending mode**: Resolved beat
**Word target**: ~2,600

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Get through the official interview correctly. Satisfy the Coast Guard's procedural requirements. Protect her post.
**Obstacle**: She knows more than she intends to say, and the interview is designed to surface what she knows.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Before**: Eira arrives at the shack Monday morning to find a note from the Coast Guard auxiliary station: Lt. Ennis will come Wednesday at nine a.m. She has two days. She does not spend them reconsidering what she'll say; she spends them doing the harbor work, running the radio check, recording the weather. The note sits on the desk. She glances at it twice.

2. **Ennis arrives**: He comes with a yeoman who types. The shack is too small for this; the yeoman's typewriter takes up half the desk. Ennis is correct and efficient, not hostile. He is from Portland; this is a paperwork matter to him.

3. **The statement**: He takes her through the night of October 11 and the morning of October 12. Her answers are exact and limited. She was at the dock from approximately 22:45 to 23:30, checking the bilge pump on the *Callie B.* after Monday's weather. She saw no vessel she hadn't seen before. She saw no individual on the dock or the breakwater. She went home. She found nothing unusual in the log from that night.

    The reader knows: the boat was without running lights. She may have told herself she wasn't certain it was a boat she hadn't seen before. She saw an individual but not clearly, and it was brief, and she said nothing to him. None of these things are lies. Together they constitute an omission she has decided on without quite deciding.

4. **One moment of pressure**: Ennis asks whether the dock's dim lighting would have made it difficult to observe activity at the far end near the breakwater steps. She says: yes, the lights are kept low. He nods. He writes something. She notes that he is not suspicious of her — he is covering the question because the form requires him to cover it. She is not as reassured by this as she expected to be.

5. **What Ennis tells her**: The man carried no papers. No wallet. No tags. His clothing was civilian, nothing military or naval. He was not, by appearance, a local man. He had been in the water or the weather for some time before he stopped at the breakwater. The investigation is not closed, but there is at present no identifying information sufficient to pursue it further. The record will be held in Portland.

6. **After**: The yeoman packs the typewriter. Ennis shakes Eira's hand — the handshake of a man who has processed a paperwork problem. He leaves. The shack is empty and she is alone with the tide clock, which has gained another few minutes since Monday.

7. **Close (resolved beat)**: Eira writes in the log: October 20. Interview conducted with Lt. R. Ennis, USCG, re: body found October 12. Formal statement submitted. No additional information at this time. She dates it and signs it. The case, officially, is closed. She sets the tide clock back four minutes. This is the most regular thing she can think of to do and she does it.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The official chapter is closed. The institutional machinery has processed this and moved on. What remains is Eira's private record — what she knows that the statement doesn't contain — and the reader's growing awareness that this private record is the book's actual subject.

The chapter title's irony is available to the reader without the prose naming it: she gave a statement. She gave a partial statement. Both things are true.

---

## New Question Raised

What will happen when what Eira knows cannot stay private? What does she believe she is protecting by withholding it?

---

## Continuity Notes

- Lt. Ennis is the Coast Guard officer; he has the record now. He does not appear again until Ch 26 (when Eira submits the addendum).
- The dead man: no papers, no wallet, no military or naval identification, civilian clothing, not a local man. He had been in the water or weather before reaching the breakwater (this suggests he had been traveling in difficult conditions — consistent with the Nov storm preceding his arrival, which was a three-day blow).
- Eira's official position: she saw nothing unusual on the night of October 11. This is what the formal record holds.
- The *Callie B.* is Reg Farber's dragger; Eira checking its bilge pump is her legitimate reason for being at the dock that night.
- The dead man's clothing: civilian, nothing military or naval. This rules out the easy interpretations (deserter, shipwrecked sailor in uniform). It deepens the mystery.

---

## Voice Notes

Eira's chapter: spare, procedural. The interview itself should feel ordinary — Ennis is efficient, the forms are correct, nothing is dramatic. The pressure is entirely below the surface, rendered through the precision of her answers (each one technically true) and the tide clock's gaining of minutes. The resolved beat at the close is genuine — she has managed this successfully by her own measure — and also slightly hollow in a way the prose does not editorialize about.
