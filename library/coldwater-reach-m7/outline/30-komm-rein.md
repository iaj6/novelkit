# Chapter 30 — Komm Rein

**One-line summary**: The truck comes for the four prisoners; Karl and Hannah exchange the letter before he goes; she and her father watch the truck leave from the porch; he puts his hand on her shoulder and says two words.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Pfeiffer farm — the yard, the porch
**Time**: Late April–May 1, 1945

**Chapter shape**: Single scene (coda)
**Ending mode**: Coda
**Word target**: ~2,200

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: None that can be stated as a goal. The chapter is aftermath: the truck comes, the prisoners go, what remains.
**Obstacle**: No obstacle — this is the coda. The story's tensions have resolved in the previous chapters; what remains is the specific texture of this morning.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Before the truck: the letter returned**
   In the days before the truck comes — or the morning of, in the barn at the end of the last work day — Hannah gives Karl the letter. She says, in German: "This is yours. I found it in the hay in February of last year." She has had it fourteen months.

   He takes it. He looks at it. He says: "I wondered where it went." She says: "I'm sorry." He says: "No." He holds it for a moment. He says: "You could read it." It is not a question. She says: "Yes." He nods. He puts it in his jacket pocket.

   He doesn't say: why did you keep it? She doesn't explain. She doesn't know how to explain and he may know anyway and it doesn't change what the letter was or what she did.

   She says: "Your mother. *Wir beten für sie.*" (We pray for her.) He looks at her. She says: "I hope she's all right." He says: "I think so. I hope so." He puts the letter in his pocket. That is all.

2. **The morning of the truck**
   Early May. The truck arrives mid-morning from Camp Houlton — the same camp administration truck that comes occasionally for supply deliveries. Two soldiers from the camp to supervise the transfer. Lonzo Reed is at the yard's edge for the last time, Springfield at his side, the same coat he's worn all winter.

   The four prisoners carry their few things out of the icehouse. Karl has his jacket, his small bag, his letters. He does not look at Hannah when they come out; he looks once, and she sees it, and she looks back. That is enough. The prisoners load into the truck bed.

3. **The porch**
   Hannah is on the farmhouse porch. Her father is standing next to her — she did not hear him come out; he is simply there. This is the first time they have stood next to each other watching something since she cannot remember. Her mother is somewhere inside; she did not come out.

   Lonzo Reed says something to the camp soldier. The truck starts. It pulls out of the yard, the rutted mud lane, and turns onto the road south toward Houlton. The dust it raises is small; it has been raining this week and the road is soft. The truck moves away. In perhaps thirty seconds it is out of sight past the maple stand at the south bend.

4. **The hand on her shoulder**
   Her father puts his hand on her shoulder. The weight of his hand is specific — the farmer's hand, the hand that has been milking cows and turning hay and holding fence posts for twenty-two years. She feels it through her coat.

   He says two words. In German or in English — the chapter leaves this genuinely ambiguous, because Hannah hears it as both and it is both: *Komm rein.* Come inside.

   She stands for a moment longer, looking at where the truck was. Then she turns. They go in.

5. **Close (coda)**
   Inside: the kitchen, the stove, her mother at the table. The farm is still the farm. Sixteen cows in the barn. The south field will need turning next week. The icehouse is empty. Somewhere on a road south, the truck.

   The radio on the kitchen counter is off. Somewhere in the radio — she could turn it on and find it — the war in Europe is ending, has been ending for weeks, will end in days. Germany will surrender. The prisoners will go home to whatever home is now. The man on the breakwater will stay in the supplementary file in Portland, without a name.

   She sits at the kitchen table. The chapter ends here, in the kitchen, in the ordinary morning of the farm, in what remains after what was going to end has ended. Her father sits across from her. Her mother makes coffee. The war is in the radio. It is not yet over. It will be. The farm is here.

---

## Outcome (Coda)

The book's final chapter is its quietest. Nothing resolves dramatically; everything has resolved in the chapters before this. The coda gives the reader the specific texture of the morning after Karl and the prisoners leave: the hand on the shoulder, two words, the kitchen, the farm continuing. This is what Hannah's arc of small choices has been moving toward — not a grand moment but this ordinary morning, in which she is in the kitchen with her parents and the war is almost over and the farm is still the farm.

## Connections to the Rest of the Book

- The letter returned to Karl: completing the thread from Ch 12 (Found) and Ch 13 (What the Letter Said) and Ch 27 (March). *Wir beten für sie* — she prays for his mother.
- *Komm rein* echoes the chapter title; the phrase is Albrecht's return to a shared language with his daughter.
- "The man on the breakwater will stay in the supplementary file in Portland, without a name" — the book's explicit protection of the unresolved thread, rendered without drama.
- The Zenith/radio: off. The war in the radio. Hannah can turn it on; she does not. The story does not need to see VE Day; it ends before VE Day.
- The icehouse is empty. This is a physical fact and also the close of eighteen months of the farm's strange arrangement.
- Lonzo Reed's last appearance: at the yard's edge, Springfield at his side, the same coat. He has done exactly what his job required. He waves or he doesn't; the chapter doesn't need this detail to be specified.

## Continuity Notes

- Karl's departure: he and the three other prisoners are transferred to Camp Houlton. The repatriation process is just beginning; they will be shipped back to Germany in phases after the surrender. The process takes months; Karl does not go home immediately.
- Karl's mother's letter: returned by Hannah in the scene before the truck, or the morning of. The exchange is brief: *You could read it. Yes. I hope she's all right. I think so.*
- *Komm rein*: this can be read as German ("Come inside") or heard as both German and English simultaneously — the ambiguity is intentional and should be preserved. Do not translate it in the text; do not specify which language Albrecht speaks it in. The chapter title is the same phrase.
- VE Day (May 8, 1945) is after this chapter's close. The book ends before Germany's surrender.
- Magda does not speak in this chapter. She makes coffee. This is a resolution of its own kind.
- The icehouse: empty for the first time since April 1943. The farm returns to its pre-1943 arrangement.
- The breakwater is not mentioned by name in this chapter. The man on the breakwater is mentioned in a single subordinate clause. This is correct: he is the spine of the book, not its final word.

## Voice Notes

Hannah's register: quiet, sensory, close. The coda chapter should feel like a breath after eighteen months. The letter return should be brief — she has been carrying it fourteen months and the scene of returning it should take the same weight as the scene of keeping it (Ch 12): quick, physical, done. The hand on her shoulder: the specific weight of it, felt through the coat, the farmer's hand. *Komm rein.* The kitchen. The farm. The radio, off. End.
