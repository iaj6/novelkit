# Chapter 28 — April

**One-line summary**: First reports of concentration camp liberations come through the shortwave; Marcus in his room, then with Henry and Lillian in the parlor; Henry stays until midnight; what neither of them says; the lamp, the notebook, the page.

**POV**: Dr. Marcus Klein
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: Marcus's room; the Crow house parlor
**Time**: April 1945 (approximately April 12–20, 1945)

**Chapter shape**: Bottle (one location across one evening, then his room)
**Ending mode**: Literary fade
**Word target**: ~3,200

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Marcus did not plan for this news. No one planned for this news. He is sitting with what the radio is saying about the camps.
**Obstacle**: The news from the liberated camps is the thing he could not let himself know until it was too late to not know. The photographs in the newspapers. The names being read. The architecture of what happened to the Europe he left, becoming visible now in the worst possible resolution.

---

## Action Beats

1. **The news arriving (his room)**
   Mid-April 1945. The camps are being liberated one by one by the advancing Allied armies — Buchenwald, Dachau, Bergen-Belsen. The American press is beginning to publish the photographs. Marcus has the Rockland paper on his desk and cannot open it fully. He opens it to page three, which is far enough, and closes it again.

   He has known, in the way that people who fled knew and did not know: that the deportations were real, that the camps were not just labor facilities, that the architecture of what was happening to European Jews was systematic. He knew this the way you know the structure of something before you can see the complete thing. Now the complete thing is becoming visible and it is worse than the structure suggested, or it is exactly as bad as the structure suggested if you let yourself follow the structure to its conclusion, which he did not let himself do.

2. **What he cannot not think about**
   The colleagues from Vienna. The people he knew in Paris. The members of the Emergency Committee's extended network he has heard about through Feld's occasional letters — the ones who did not make it out in time, the ones whose last documented location was a city now shown in photographs that Marcus cannot look at directly.

   Felix K. He thinks about Felix K. the way he has been thinking about Felix K. since January — with a specific quality of probability that is not the same as certainty and is not the same as comfort. Felix K. who tried to go. Who tried to reach Massachusetts by sea. Who ran out of road at Coldwater Reach on October 11, 1943.

   If it was Felix K. — if the man on the breakwater was Felix K. — then he died trying. He did not die in a camp. He died in the Atlantic coast dark, in October, and no one knew his name. Marcus does not know whether this is better or worse. He thinks it is neither. He thinks it is what it is.

3. **The parlor**
   He carries the newspaper downstairs. Henry and Lillian are in the parlor — the Zenith on, the same chairs as June 6, but a different news now. Lillian is not listening; she is knitting with her hands very still. Henry is in his chair. Marcus sits on the sofa.

   The Zenith: the reporters describe what the troops are finding. Names. Numbers. The specific geography of what was built in Europe to kill people. Marcus translates nothing tonight. He just sits.

   After a while Lillian says, quietly, "I'm going to bed." She goes. Marcus and Henry are left.

4. **Henry stays until midnight**
   They do not talk much. Henry says, at some point: "Is there anyone you're trying to find out about?" Marcus says: "Several. I don't know how to find out." Henry says: "There will be lists." Marcus says: "Yes." Henry says: "That will take time." Marcus says: "Yes."

   Henry gets up once to make coffee and comes back. He stays until nearly midnight. He does not say: I'm sorry. He does not say: I can imagine. He says, when he finally rises to go to bed: "I'll be up." Which means: I'll be here in the morning. Marcus says: "I know." He means: thank you. These are the same two sentences.

5. **His room (the notebook)**
   Marcus upstairs. The lamp on his desk. The German notebook. He opens it to the last entry from January: *Fe–?–x. Felix K. Yarmouth, September 1943. Coldwater, October 1943. Inner bar, recovered December 1944.*

   He reads this. He takes his pen. He writes one more line: *Die die versucht haben zu gehen.* ("Those who tried to go.") He closes the notebook.

6. **Close (literary fade)**
   He turns off the lamp. The room is dark. East-facing window: the April dark outside, which is also, somewhere across the Atlantic, the camps, the photographs, the lists that haven't been published yet. His German notebook on the desk with what he wrote. His English handwriting, which has become better than his German handwriting, which is still not the same thing as having arrived somewhere.

   He does not open the shortwave. He sits in the dark for a while. He goes to bed. The chapter fades on the room: lamp off, window east, the notebook closed.

---

## Outcome

Marcus's most significant private moment in the book: sitting with the news from the camps, with what he has been not-knowing about Europe for years, with the probable identity of the man on the breakwater, and with Henry's company, which is the kind of company that doesn't try to be more than what it is. The literary fade at the close is earned — his German notebook holds the story's most complete assembly of the mystery, and what he writes in it (*die die versucht haben zu gehen*) is the phrase that connects him, Felix K., Hannah's world, and Karl's mother's letter across the book's full span.

## Continuity Notes

- April 1945: camp liberations historically: Ohrdruf (April 4), Buchenwald (April 11), Dachau (April 29), Bergen-Belsen (April 15). These dates cluster in mid-to-late April. The chapter is set approximately April 12–20.
- The Rockland paper: regional newspaper Marcus receives at the school.
- Felix K.: Marcus writes the phrase from Karl's mother's letter in his notebook here — *die die versucht haben zu gehen* — as a final notation beneath the January assembly. This closes the book's most explicit mystery-assembly.
- Marcus's people in Europe: colleagues, friends, community members from Vienna. He has not known what happened to them. Now the shape of what happened is becoming visible.
- Henry's sentences ("I'll be up" / "I know"): these should be rendered exactly. The whole midnight scene is in the specific grain of this friendship — nothing declared, everything present.
- Lillian's exit: she goes to bed; this is not avoidance but the household's division of who stays. Henry is the one who stays.
- **PROTECTED THREAD**: Marcus writes *die die versucht haben zu gehen* in his notebook, next to the Felix K. notes, but this does not constitute a naming of the dead man. The identity stays unconfirmed. Marcus thinks he knows; the reader thinks they know; the book does not confirm.

## Voice Notes

Marcus's chapter, interiority-heavy in the room scenes, dialogue-minimal in the parlor. The prose should be at its sparest here — when something genuinely moves Marcus, the qualifying architecture drops (style.md). The camp news should not be described in graphic detail; Marcus is not describing what he reads, he is sitting with the fact of it. The notebook entry at the close: one line, in German, exact. The room going dark: the chapter's final image, not a metaphor, not a symbol, just the room going dark.
