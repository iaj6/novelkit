# Chapter 08 — School Hours

**One-line summary**: Henry Crow's day at the Coldwater school — early furnace work, the building's social weather, Marcus in the hallway — and an evening at home with Lillian; a coda to the setup phase.

**POV**: Henry Crow
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Coldwater school; the Crow house at 14 Elm Street
**Time**: November 1943

**Chapter shape**: Single scene across a full day (with evening coda)
**Ending mode**: Coda
**Word target**: ~1,500

**Note**: Fourth-perspective chapter. Shorter than standard. Henry Crow's voice is direct and exact; he does not editorialize about his circumstances or anyone else's.

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Henry's goal is the same as every other day: maintain the building, keep what needs keeping.
**Obstacle**: There is no dramatic obstacle. The chapter's shape is observational — the building and the town through Henry's eyes.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Opening: before anyone arrives**
   Henry at the school before six. The furnace is his first job: he knows this furnace the way Eira knows the tide clock — its particular habits, what it wants in cold weather, how much coal to bank for a Tuesday when the temperature drops below twenty. He has worked this building eleven years and the furnace fifteen more. He feeds it; it works.

2. **The school in morning**
   The building fills: students by eight, teachers by eight-thirty. Henry moves through the hallway with the mop, the repair kit, the supply closet keys. He has a specific route, efficient, the same as every day. People greet him or they don't. He knows which teachers say good morning and which ones don't notice him. He does not catalog this for the reader; he has simply learned which facts are survivable.

3. **Marcus in the hallway**
   Marcus passes Henry mid-morning, going from German to Latin. Their exchange: Henry asks if the window in the German classroom is still sticking. Marcus says yes. Henry says he'll get to it this afternoon. Marcus says: the cold comes in on the north side. Henry says he knows. They move in opposite directions. This is a friendship rendered entirely in maintenance logistics, and both men know it, and neither mentions it.

4. **The body still in the air**
   By November the gossip has not fully settled. Henry overhears a conversation in the teachers' room: two of the women, speculating about who the man was and whether the Coast Guard will ever know. He does not contribute. The body is not his business, and even if it were, speculating about it in the teachers' room is not something he would do. He moves on.

5. **One specific fact**
   Late afternoon: Henry speaks to the school secretary about a supply order. She mentions, in passing, that someone submitted a complaint about Dr. Klein earlier this fall — not formal, not filed, just a parent who thought the school board should know they'd hired a German. The secretary is embarrassed to be relaying this; she clearly thinks the complaint was foolish. Henry listens and thanks her and goes back to fixing the German classroom window.

   He does not tell Marcus.

   (This plants the seed for the more formal complaint in Ch 22.)

6. **Evening: the Crow house**
   Lillian has soup. Henry comes home cold from the building's last check. They eat. Lillian asks how Marcus seemed today. Henry says: fine. She asks: is he still running the shortwave every night? Henry says: I expect so. This is a full conversation for them on this subject. Lillian refills his bowl without being asked.

7. **Close (coda)**
   Henry at the kitchen table after Lillian has gone to bed. The paper. The quiet. A paragraph of what he is not saying: not to Lillian, not to Marcus, not to the school secretary. The chapter ends on Henry's stillness, which is not resignation and is not contentment and is the specific condition of a man who has determined exactly which expenditures of himself are survivable and which are not, and who manages the rest with precision. He goes to bed.

---

## Outcome

This is the quiet chapter that closes the setup phase. Henry's vantage gives the reader what none of the three POV leads can provide: the town's social texture as a Black man in 1943 Maine sees it. His rendering of Marcus's situation (that there is already a complaint filed against Marcus, however informally) is the book's first indication that Marcus's position in Coldwater is more precarious than Marcus himself knows.

The chapter also establishes the specific quality of Henry and Marcus's friendship — transactional in surface, genuine in the only way they allow themselves.

---

## New Question Raised

What is the cost of what Henry holds privately? What will happen when the informal complaint against Marcus becomes formal (Ch 22)?

## Continuity Notes

- Henry has been at the Coldwater school eleven years (he arrived in Coldwater with his family in 1924; he's been at the school since approximately 1932). His family came north from Norfolk, Virginia, in 1924.
- The supply complaint about Marcus is informal and not yet filed; it comes from a parent (unnamed) who didn't want a German teacher. Henry files it as information.
- Henry does not tell Marcus about this complaint — this becomes significant in Ch 22.
- Lillian's warmth: she asks about Marcus; Henry's answer is minimal; this is their established dynamic.
- The furnace is Henry's particular province and competence — the equivalent of Eira's tide clock.
- This chapter may reference the body only obliquely (the teachers' room speculation) — Henry's chapter is about his world, not the mystery.

---

## Voice Notes

Henry's register: direct, exact, no editorializing. His narration does not comment on the unfairness of his situation or the town's racial dynamics — it notes facts and moves on. The reader is meant to feel what Henry's precision costs without the prose naming it. "He has learned which facts are survivable" is the register's key phrase — it should appear in some form without being a thesis statement. The chapter's coda quality comes from its quietness after eight chapters of setup: this is the stillest moment in the book so far, and it should feel earned.
