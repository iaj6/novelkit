# Chapter 25 — Zuhause

**One-line summary**: Deep winter; Hannah speaks German to her father in the kitchen, initiating, with something she has needed to say for a year; he answers in German; and then says her name in English.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Pfeiffer farmhouse kitchen; the barn
**Time**: December 1944–January 1945

**Chapter shape**: Multi-scene
**Ending mode**: Resolved beat
**Word target**: ~2,800

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Hannah has been circling something with her father for the better part of a year. The winter is the right time — the farm is quiet, the prisoners are in, the nights are long. She wants to say something to her father that she has not said.
**Obstacle**: Her father's withdrawal, which has been total and systematic and which she has learned to work around rather than through. The question is whether what she wants to say is possible to say to a man who has made a language of silence.

---

## Scenes

### Scene 1: December on the farm (the Battle of the Bulge)
The Pfeiffer farm in December 1944: the coldest point of the year, the prisoners mostly in, the farm's production at its winter minimum. The radio is on again as it was in June — Albrecht at the kitchen set, or the parlor set, listening to the Bulge news. The German counteroffensive has undone something in him that June rebuilt; he sits with his coffee and his stillness and Hannah recognizes the quality of what he's listening to, which is the war in the country he came from being killed by the country he is in.

She does not know how to name this. She does the farm work. She cooks what she can cook given the rationing. She goes to school on the days she goes. Karl and the prisoners work the minimal winter detail. Lonzo Reed has two pairs of wool socks now.

### Scene 2: January (what she wants to say)
By January the Bulge is resolved in the Allies' favor — the Germans pushed back — but the damage to Albrecht's reserves is visible. He has become quieter even for him. He sits with the radio less. He is in the barn more, which is where he goes when something is too large for the house.

Hannah has been thinking for months about what she wants to say to him. Not what she wants to ask (she has questions) but what she wants to say: that she knows what he left and knows she doesn't know what he left, and that she has spent this year learning to hear what his German sounds like from the inside and she knows it is not nothing, and she is not going to pretend it is nothing anymore.

She has been saying things to Karl in German for eight months. She has not said anything to her father in German except *Morgen* and the routine words of the farm.

### Scene 3: The kitchen (the conversation)
A Thursday evening in January. Albrecht at the kitchen table, not by the radio, just sitting. Magda at the far end, darning. Hannah comes in from the barn.

She sits down across from her father. She says, in German — his German, the Bavarian she grew up hearing in this house: *Papa. Ich muss dir was sagen.* (Papa. I have something to tell you.)

He looks up. He has not heard her speak German to him since before the war. He does not look alarmed; he looks — she cannot say exactly what he looks. He says, in German: *Dann sag es.*  (Then say it.)

She says: she knows about him and Karl. About their conversations through the window. She knows Karl is from fifty kilometers from where he was born. She knows Karl is from home. She says: she speaks German now. Not just the farm words. She speaks it. She was afraid to say so to him, and she is not afraid anymore.

He is quiet for a long time. He says — in German, but more slowly than usual, as if weighing each word: *Du hast das von mir.* (You have this from me.) She says: *Ja.* He says — still in German: *Ich weiß das.* (I know that.) A pause. And then, very quietly, in English: "Hannah."

Just her name. In English, which he has not spoken to her — not once, not in passing — in more than two years. Her name, in the language he stopped using, in her first name, which he has not stopped knowing.

Magda does not look up from her darning. But her hands are still.

### Scene 4: After (what changed)
Hannah goes to bed. She does not know what her father's name in English means — or she knows too many things it might mean, which is not the same as knowing. She knows this: the English was real. He said her name. In English. To her. Which is the language of this country and of her and of the farm and of Lonzo Reed and the school and the harbor and everything that is not Bavaria, and he knows all of this, and he said it anyway.

**Close (resolved beat)**: She lies in the dark and thinks of the German word in the chapter's title: *zuhause*. At home. She has not been sure, for two years, what it means. Tonight it means the kitchen in January and her father saying her name. She goes to sleep.

## Outcome / Resolved Beat

Hannah's most significant arc moment: she speaks German to her father (initiating) and he receives it and answers and says her name in English. The resolved beat is exact and small — nothing dramatic, no speeches, no reconciliation scene. Her name in English is the resolution: he is still here, still her father, still in both languages even if only one of them is available to him right now. The chapter earns its resolution because of the accumulation of the previous thirteen chapters of Hannah's arc.

---

## New Question Raised

What does Albrecht's English mean going forward? Is Magda's stillness a beginning or a continuation? And Karl — what happens to Karl and the other prisoners as the war approaches its end?

## Continuity Notes

- Albrecht has not spoken English at home since the war started (continuity #8). This chapter's breaking of that: his saying Hannah's name in English is the first English he speaks to her in two-plus years.
- Magda's hands going still: she does not speak, but this is the first indication that her silence has a texture that changes. She does not speak in this chapter; she may not speak before the end of the book.
- *Ich muss dir was sagen* / *Dann sag es* / *Du hast das von mir* / *Ich weiß das* — these German phrases are untranslated in the text but their context makes them readable. Hannah's German is Bavarian, as is Albrecht's.
- *Zuhause*: "at home" in German. Hannah's interiority at the close uses this word naturally, as part of her accumulated German texture.
- The Battle of the Bulge (Dec 16, 1944 – Jan 25, 1945): the news that has hit Albrecht hardest of any war news. By January the Bulge is resolving in the Allies' favor, but the damage to Albrecht's interior has been done.
- Karl and the prisoners: minimal winter work, largely in the icehouse. Karl and Albrecht still speak through the window. Hannah and Karl have not had a significant exchange since late fall.
- This chapter is the resolution of Hannah's central father-relationship arc. It does not resolve everything about her (Karl's situation, the letter she has held since February 1944) but it resolves the most important thing.

## Voice Notes

Hannah's register: sensory and accumulative but with a quality of stillness in this chapter that matches the winter and what she's finally saying. The kitchen scene: the German exchange should be rendered with its actual words (see beats above), untranslated, with their meaning available from context. The English "Hannah" should land with the weight of everything that's been built — simply, without commentary. The close with *zuhause* should be brief: one word, her name, and sleep.
