# Chapter 06 — At the Harbormaster's

**One-line summary**: Marcus comes to the harbormaster's office to collect a book shipment from the mail boat; his first sustained encounter with Eira; asking obliquely about the body, he realizes she knows more than she says.

**POV**: Dr. Marcus Klein
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: The Coldwater Reach dock; the harbormaster's shack
**Time**: Early November 1943 (Friday, mail boat day)

**Chapter shape**: Single scene (dialogue-led)
**Ending mode**: Turn
**Word target**: ~2,600

---

## Goal / Obstacle

**Goal**: Marcus needs to collect a book order that arrived on the Friday mail boat from Rockland. The school ordered it; it requires the harbormaster's signature on the freight manifest because it came as cargo, not post.
**Obstacle**: The harbormaster is Eira Bowman, whom he has not spoken to before except by sight. He is curious about her relation to the body on the breakwater; he does not know how to ask; he is aware that asking directly is probably wrong.

---

## Action Beats

1. **The dock**: Marcus walks down to the harbor from the school. Early November, the dock smell: diesel, herring bait, cold salt. The mail boat *Heron* is tied up; two dockhands are moving freight. He is not dressed correctly for this — his overcoat is adequate for Cambridge and wrong for a Maine dock in November. He is aware of this.

2. **The shack**: He knocks on the harbormaster's shack door. Eira is at the desk with the log open. She looks up without surprise. He introduces himself — Dr. Klein, from the school — and states his errand. She knows who he is; the town is nine hundred people. She checks the freight manifest, finds the books, signs the slip. He signs his counter-signature. This takes perhaps four minutes.

3. **The conversation begins**: He thanks her. He does not leave. He asks — looking at the harbor through the window, not at her — whether there has been any word about the body from October. She tells him what the official record says: unknown individual, no identification, the investigation is in Portland's hands. She says this without emphasis. He hears the absence of emphasis.

4. **His oblique angle**: He asks (carefully, precisely) whether it was unusual for an unknown individual to come into the harbor at night. She says: people anchor in bad weather; the three-day blow before the twelfth had pushed a number of boats to seek shelter. He says: yes, of course. He hears that she has answered a slightly different question than the one he asked.

5. **The shack interior**: While they're speaking he takes in the room. The tide clock on the wall. The BC-1206 radio set. The log ledger open on the desk, and the visible quality of the handwriting — he can see, without reading it, that the handwriting changes at some point in the ledger; an older, looser hand at one point and a different, more compressed hand now. He does not comment on this.

6. **The turn**: As he picks up his books and turns to go, she says — informally, without inflection: "The manifest is the school's copy. You can keep it." He looks at the manifest in his hand. She has written, above the freight description, in her compressed hand: "Collected 7 November 1943, 14:10." He doesn't know why this matters. He tucks the manifest into his coat. Outside in the cold he walks up the hill with his books and the manifest and a clear sense that she knows exactly what she said and exactly what she left out, which means she is a better respondent than anyone he has interviewed in an academic context, and this is not a comfortable thing to think about a woman he has just spoken to for seven minutes.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The turn: Marcus understands that Eira knows more than she's told. Not from anything she said but from the way she didn't say it. His oblique question received an oblique non-answer, which is itself information. He leaves with his books and a new read on Eira that changes how he'll think about the whole mystery.

---

## New Question Raised

Will Marcus press further — and if so, how? What does he intend to do with what he suspects?

## Continuity Notes

- The mail boat runs Tuesdays and Fridays from Rockland; Marcus is there on a Friday.
- The harbormaster's shack: BC-1206 radio, tide clock, log ledger (world.md). The ledger's two handwriting periods are visible to Marcus without him commenting on their meaning.
- This is the first sustained exchange between Eira and Marcus. The brief nods and sightings before this don't count.
- Marcus's overcoat: he dresses carefully but wrong for the Maine climate (characters.md).
- The *Heron* is the mail boat (can be named here or left as "the mail boat").
- Eira does not warm to Marcus in this encounter — she remains wary and procedural — but she registers that he is precise, which she respects in the way she respects precision in things she doesn't quite trust yet.
- The manifest with her handwritten timestamp is a small object Marcus keeps. It does not recur directly, but it establishes his habit of keeping things.

## Voice Notes

Marcus's chapter: his precision is the vehicle for his curiosity, and his restraint is the vehicle for his intelligence. The scene should feel like two careful people feeling out the boundaries of what the other knows. Eira's dialogue is clipped; Marcus's is measured. The turn arrives not in dialogue but in Marcus's reading of the scene afterward, walking up the hill. Do not name what he concludes — render the clarity and the discomfort of it.
