# Chapter 04 — Downstream

**One-line summary:** The muster orders arrive; Asa walks from the sawmill to Plattsburgh and finds the defensive line already built and the scale of the British force larger than rumor suggested.

**POV character:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** The Beckwith sawmill on the Saranac → the river road downstream → Plattsburgh village → the south bank of the Saranac at the Lower Bridge site. September 3–5, 1814.

**Chapter shape:** Multi-scene  
**Ending mode:** Mid-action

**Target words:** ~2,500

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Get from the mill to his assigned position on the Saranac line.
- **Obstacle:** The chapter's obstacle is not physical but perceptual — the gap between the rumor of the British army and the reality of it. Each stage of the walk downstream brings Asa closer to the fact of what he has joined.

---

## Action Beats

1. **The orders.** They reach the mill in the morning — a rider on the road, or a neighbor who has already received his notice, coming to tell Elias. The Clinton County levy is called up. Asa is not surprised; he has known this was coming since the muster gossip at the mill two days ago. Elias takes it without ceremony. He has work and he will do it; Asa has work and will do his. A handshake. Asa takes his musket — already cleaned and primed — and his pack.

2. **The walk.** The river road follows the Saranac east toward the lake. Asa passes the places he has known his whole life: the bend where the current undercuts the clay bank, the crossing he has forded a hundred times, the Hazen farm, the flatter ground where the river opens before the village. Everything looks as it always has. The river is at its September level — low, moving, the stones on the bed visible through the shallows. He doesn't think of this as a last look; he thinks about the weight of his pack.

3. **Arrival at Plattsburgh.** The village is transformed. Earthworks on the bluffs above the south bank — Fort Brown visible above the Saranac, cannon pointed north. More men than Asa has seen in one place: regulars in their blue coats, other militia companies, laborers throwing up earthwork lines. The smell of an artillery park: iron, horse dung, wood smoke. Asa finds his company's assembly point and reports to his sergeant, a regular from one of Macomb's regiments.

4. **The ruined Lower Bridge.** Asa goes to the river to see. The bridge that crossed the Saranac in the middle of the village — gone. The stumps of the pilings in the water, the gap in the road. North of the river, the road runs straight toward Canada; the bank on that side has been cleared of trees, wide fields of fire opened. But there is no one on the north bank yet. Not yet.

5. **Beekmantown news arrives.** A group of soldiers from the 29th Infantry comes through — some on foot, a few on horses, some of them with wounds being dressed. They stopped the British advance long enough for the Plattsburgh line to be set. But the numbers they describe are staggering: six, eight, ten thousand men — Peninsular veterans, not the fencibles and militia the frontier has seen before. Captain Leonard's guns bought time. The delay worked. But the British are coming.

6. **End.** Asa stands at the ruined bridge and looks north across the Saranac. The north bank is cleared and empty now. The road that goes to Canada runs straight into the distance. He can see further than usual because the trees were cut. He knows this river, this bank. He knows where the fords are — this one, the Upper Bridge a mile and a half further, Pike's Ford three miles above. The British are not here yet. They will be here.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Asa is on the Saranac line. The British force is real, large, and close — not a border rumor. The defensive works are substantial. He has traded his ordinary life for this.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

He has not yet been to his assigned position — Pike's Ford, three miles upstream. What does the ford look like with British regulars on the north side?

## Continuity Notes

- The Lower Bridge in Plattsburgh village was **destroyed before the battle** and is already gone when Asa arrives. He sees the pilings in the water.
- The Upper Bridge (a mile and a half upstream) is also destroyed — Asa may hear this secondhand or know it already.
- Fort Brown is on the bluffs above the south bank, overlooking the Saranac. [cite:11]
- Tree-clearing for fields of fire on the north bank occurred before the battle. [cite:11]
- The Beekmantown engagement was September 6, 1814 — Major Wool with ~250 men of the 29th Infantry, Captain Leonard's artillery, delaying the British vanguard on the State Road. Estimates of British force ranged widely; what matters is the soldiers' sense of scale. [cite:10]

- Elias Beckwith stays at the mill. He does not serve.

## Prose Register Note

Asa's walk is the chapter's spine. The river road is not atmospheric background — it is the specific landscape of his life, the ground he knows, being traveled one-way for an indefinite time. The prose doesn't mark this as departure or loss; it renders the walk in the same observational register as the sawmill. The transformation of Plattsburgh into a military position should be concrete and dense: earthworks, cannon, men, the smell. The ruined bridge is a fact, not a symbol. It means the river has been chosen as the line.
