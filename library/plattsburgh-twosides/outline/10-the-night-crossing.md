# Chapter 10 — The Night Crossing

**One-line summary:** The standoff days continue; Captain McGlassin's company raids the British picket line on the night of September 9; Asa watches from the ford and sees the prisoners brought through in the morning.

**POV character:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** Pike's Ford and the south bank of the Saranac. September 8–10, 1814.

**Chapter shape:** Multi-scene (the standoff days, then the night of the raid, then the morning after)  
**Ending mode:** Resolved

**Target words:** ~2,800

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Hold the ford, stay sharp through the repetition of the standoff, and understand what the night raid means for the situation.
- **Obstacle:** The waiting. A standoff is harder than action for the men who are waiting for it. The days have a sameness that can make a company careless; Asa watches for it.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Standoff days, September 8–9.** The routine of the ford in the standoff: drill in the morning, watch rotation through the day, British picket fires visible downstream at night. Asa has begun to know the men in his company the way he knows a piece of timber — their grain, their tendency to bow in one direction. A few are genuinely skilled with a musket; more are not and won't be by the time it matters. The sergeant knows this too and doesn't say it.

2. **British across the water.** The north bank downstream is not quiet. British soldiers are visible in fragments during the day — red coats moving in the tree line, the sound of digging or construction (fortifying, perhaps, or just making camp). At the ford itself, the north bank is empty and forested. Asa watches it the way he watches the river for the sign of a log coming down: steadily, without expectation, ready to see whatever comes.

3. **The raid is announced.** September 9, late in the day: word reaches the ford company that Captain McGlassin is leading a regular company across the Saranac that night against the British picket line downstream. Not Asa's company — they are to stay at the ford. The purpose: disruption, intelligence, a few prisoners. Some of Asa's men are envious; some are relieved. The sergeant says: we stay.

4. **The night of September 9.** Asa's watch. The ford quiet. Downstream, past midnight, the sound of the raid: shouting, musket fire in ragged sequence (not volley fire, skirmish work), then silence. Then more shouting, brief, then nothing. Asa keeps his eyes on the north bank of the ford. The ford stays empty. He does not know what the sounds mean except that they are downstream and moving away.

5. **The morning after.** September 10. Before dawn, the raiders return — passing through or near the ford on their way south to Plattsburgh. Asa sees them: regulars, tired, a few with minor wounds. Three or four British prisoners with their hands bound, in the clothing of British regulars — the drafter may identify the regiment from research or leave it ambiguous. Asa looks at the prisoners. They look like men who had a bad night. One of them is young. He walks with his head up. The prisoners move past and are gone south toward Plattsburgh.

6. **Morning of September 10.** The ford is the same ford it was. The sergeant runs drill. The routine resumes. The day is quiet. Asa is at his position, the Saranac running past his feet, the north bank across the water, unchanged. End: resolved.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The night raid was a tactical success — a few prisoners taken, the British picket line disrupted. The ford remained untested. The morning of September 10 is quiet and ordinary.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

The British across the water have been here for four days now. The British fleet is somewhere on the lake to the north. When does this standoff end?

## Continuity Notes

- Captain McGlassin's September 9 night raid is a documented event. A company of American regulars raided British positions, took prisoners, and returned before dawn. [cite:11]
- The raid is downstream from Pike's Ford — Asa does not participate; he holds the ford.
- The prisoners from the raid are soldiers from the British force; which regiment is not specified in the sources consulted.
- The British regiments at Plattsburgh include Peninsular War veterans: 3rd, 5th, 27th, 39th, 76th, 88th Foot and others. [continuity #23]
- Brigadier-General Brisbane oversees the Saranac approach. Never POV.
- The standoff at the Saranac lasted from approximately September 7 to September 11.
- Black and Indigenous men may be present in the company without being centered. The valley's demographics in 1814 are relevant background; see `canon/research.md` section 18.

## Prose Register Note

The raid's sounds are the chapter's acoustic center — heard from Asa's position at the ford, they are directionless and ambiguous, exactly as Asa experiences them. He doesn't interpret the sounds as victory or defeat; he hears shooting and then silence and stays where he is. The prisoners seen in the morning are rendered with the flat specificity of Asa's observational register: they are men, they are tired, one is young. The chapter should not use them to deliver a "both sides are human" message — the prose does not editorialize. The observation lands because it is accurate, not because it teaches.
