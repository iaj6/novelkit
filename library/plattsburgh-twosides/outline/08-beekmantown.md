# Chapter 08 — Beekmantown

**One-line summary:** The Beekmantown engagement's aftermath reaches Asa at Pike's Ford through wounded soldiers of the 29th Infantry; British pickets appear in the tree line across the Saranac by evening.

**POV character:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** Pike's Ford and the south bank of the Saranac, three miles upstream of Plattsburgh. September 6–7, 1814.

**Chapter shape:** Multi-scene (at the ford, then the news and its physical evidence arriving, the day changing around Asa)  
**Ending mode:** Mid-action

**Target words:** ~2,800

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Hold his position at the ford; understand what the sounds from downstream mean.
- **Obstacle:** Asa is not at Beekmantown — he cannot see what happened. The battle reaches him as sound, then as human debris, then as the presence of British soldiers across the water. The obstacle is the gap between knowing something and seeing it.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Morning of September 6.** Drill in the early light. The sergeant runs it cleanly by now — the company knows the sequence. Asa takes his position at the ford overlap point; the ford is unchanged, the north bank empty. A normal morning. The sounds from downstream are road noise: horses, the odd shout, wagons. The army is on both sides of the river and the valley is full of its sounds.

2. **Gunfire, distant.** Early afternoon. Sounds from the north — not road noise, not hammering, but the flat crack of muskets in quantity, a sound that travels differently from anything Asa has heard before. Occasional boom that might be artillery. It is to the north and east, several miles. The sergeant tells the company: hold position. They hold.

3. **The survivors come through.** Late afternoon. A small group of soldiers of the 29th Infantry appears on the road on the south bank, coming from the direction of Beekmantown. Some walking, some being helped. They are not in formation; their dress is fouled. One man's arm is bound with a strip of linen. They stop at the ford to water themselves. Asa's company has water. Asa brings some.

4. **What the soldier says.** A brief exchange at the water's edge: one of the 29th Infantry soldiers, sitting in the shade, catching his breath. He answers Asa's question about what happened with the economy of a man who is very tired and doesn't want to be asked again. The British came down the State Road in force — far more than the scouts had said, Peninsular veterans, well-drilled, moving fast. Captain Leonard's guns bought time at Culver Hill. They held them long enough. But the British are in Beekmantown now, and their vanguard will be at the Saranac before dark. He gets up and joins his company moving south.

5. **The ford in the afternoon.** The company at the ford is quieter now. The sergeant says nothing directly but adjusts the watch rotation: all men on the bank during the remaining daylight, no one drawn back to cook or rest. Asa prepares his musket — not because he is afraid, but because the day has changed and the next thing is to have the piece right.

6. **First sight of British soldiers.** Near dusk, Asa is at the water's edge. Downstream, half a mile or a little more, movement in the tree line on the north bank. Not wind; deliberate. Red coats in fragments between the leaves: British soldiers establishing the picket line. He watches for several minutes without calling attention to himself, reading the movement the way he reads anything moving in the trees — direction, number, intent. Then he calls the sergeant and shows him.

7. **End.** Evening. The north bank of the Saranac holds British soldiers, and Asa can see their fires beginning downstream in the last of the light. New fact: the standoff has begun. The army has arrived.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The British army is on the north bank. The Saranac standoff is now a physical reality, not an anticipated event.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

When will they come for the ford? The Lower and Upper Bridges are already destroyed; the ford is the route they need.

---

## Continuity Notes

- Beekmantown engagement, September 6, 1814: Major Wool with approximately 250 men of the 29th Infantry; Captain Leonard's field guns; the British vanguard on the State Road; delaying action at Halsey's Corners and Culver Hill. Casualties approximately 45 American, 200–300 British (estimates vary). [cite:10]
- Asa is not at Beekmantown — he is at Pike's Ford throughout. The engagement reaches him through sound and through the men who survived it.
- After Beekmantown, the British army establishes picket lines on the north bank of the Saranac.
- The Lower Bridge and Upper Bridge across the Saranac had already been destroyed before the army arrived. [continuity #21]
- The British regiments at Plattsburgh include Peninsular War veterans: elements of the 3rd, 5th, 27th, 39th, 76th, 88th Foot and others. [continuity #23] Asa doesn't know the regiment names; the soldier he speaks to may not either.
- Brigadier-General Brisbane commands one of the British brigades and will direct the Saranac crossing attempts. He is never POV.
- The fires on the north bank are an historical detail of the standoff period.

## Prose Register Note

The battle arrives to Asa at second-hand — through sound, through exhausted men, through what he can see across the river. This is exactly how the brief describes his access to the larger war: "from below," without a commanding view. The chapter's movement is from the ordinary morning (drill, the ford, nothing unusual) through the arrival of evidence (sound, then soldiers, then fires) to the fact of the standoff. The drafter should resist the temptation to have Asa "understand" the significance of what he sees. He sees it, reads it practically, and reports to the sergeant. That is the whole of what the chapter claims.
