# Chapter 05 — Running Out

**One-line summary:** Two days of sailing south on Lake Champlain, the gun drills repeated; Thomas's assessment of his crew firms into something like knowledge.

**POV character:** Thomas Briggs  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** H.M.S. *Confiance* on Lake Champlain, heading south. September 6–7, 1814.

**Chapter shape:** Time-skip / montage (two days condensed — multiple drill sessions, the lake's changing geography, a specific incident that sharpens the chapter)

**Ending mode:** Resolved

**Target words:** ~2,800

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Work the gun crew into a functioning unit in the time available.
- **Obstacle:** The time is short, the men are strangers to one another's working habits, and the ongoing carpentry overhead is a reminder of the ship's incomplete state.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Second day on the lake.** Morning. Thomas takes his station before the drill is called. He has been here ten years longer than most of the men at his gun, and he has the economy of motion that comes from it — the specific way he handles the train tackle, checks the quoin, lays the rammer in its place. The gun crew assembles. He runs through the positions: Gun Captain, Second Captain, Sponger, Loader, side tacklemen, train tacklemen, powder boy. He is assigning names to each function and watching whether the names stick.

2. **Reading the crew.** The first cycle of the second day goes better. Two of the men — the able seaman from the Channel draft and a man who came from the Richelieu River gunboat service — have learned the sequence. Three others are consistent but slow. The soldier of the 39th has found the correct grip for the rammer. One man is still wrong in his timing, always half a beat behind the sponger. Thomas adjusts without announcement.

3. **Lake geography passing.** Between drills, the lake. The Vermont shore: wooded ridges, the mountains higher to the south. The New York shore: lower, the Adirondack foothills. A civilian schooner is sighted heading north — keeping well to the Vermont side, wanting no proximity to the British squadron. Thomas watches it until it's past. He thinks of the passage home from Malta in 1811, the feeling of a ship moving toward a destination that mattered. This is not that.

4. **A drill error and its resolution.** In the afternoon of the second day, a cartridge is torn when the rammer is driven in too hard — the powder boy's charge nicked by the edge of the bore. The vent must be cleared with the priming wire before the gun can be loaded again. Thomas does it, then demonstrates the correct technique for cartridge handling: gentler than the men expect, the ramrod driven home without force. He runs the load cycle again with the same crew. No error the second time.

5. **Thomas and Achilles.** A brief interior passage — not a declared memory, but Thomas working with his current crew and his knowledge arriving from a different ship, a different time. What a working gun crew sounds like when it's right: the noise of it has a rhythm, the individual motions fitting together, the silence when the gun captain raises his hand. He heard that rhythm from *Achilles*' gun deck at Trafalgar. His current crew doesn't have it yet. Some of them might get there. Some might not.

6. **End of the second day.** Evening. The ship anchors for the night, the southern lake visible ahead — the lake narrowing slightly, the mountains of Vermont visible to the south now as well as the east. Thomas is on the evening watch, the deck quiet, the water slate-colored. The Adirondacks to the west dark against the sky. He notes the wind: still from the south. The watch changes. He goes below. The routine is complete.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Two days of drilling. The crew is better than it was. Not good — Thomas has a standard from *Achilles* to measure against — but better. The ship is a day and a half from Cumberland Head. The routine is holding.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

When they see the American line at anchor, when the first broadside comes in, will the crew hold what the drills have built?

## Continuity Notes

- Thomas served at Trafalgar aboard H.M.S. *Achilles*, Collingwood's lee column. That ship and that gun deck are his frame of professional reference. [continuity #6]
- *Achilles* at Trafalgar: 74 guns, Captain Richard King, engaged *Montañés*, *Argonauta*, the French *Achille*, and *Berwick*. Casualties: 13 killed, 59 wounded. Thomas was twenty-three. [cite:03]
- The gun crew positions from research [cite:23]: Gun Captain, Second Captain, Sponger, Loader, side tacklemen (2), train tacklemen (2), handspikemen, powder boy. Twelve men plus one boy for a 24-pounder.
- The priming wire clears a fouled vent or a torn cartridge remnant from the breech end. This is standard procedure, not a crisis.
- Lake geography: Vermont mountains on the east (visible and rising to the south), New York / Adirondack foothills on the west.
- The wind from the south is still noted — a consistent fact in Thomas's chapters.

## Prose Register Note

This chapter can condense time openly — two days rendered in the accumulation of repeated actions rather than in scene-by-scene chronology. The montage form should feel earned, not rushed: the repetitions of drill become a texture in themselves. The interior passage about *Achilles* must arrive as free indirect discourse — Thomas's professional vocabulary and his eleven years in service infusing the narrator's observations — not as a declared flashback. The comparison is implicit, alive in his handling of the gun, not announced.
