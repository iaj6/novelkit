# Chapter 01 — Green Timber

**One-line summary:** Thomas completes a forenoon watch aboard *Confiance* at Île aux Noix — a ship still becoming what it will be, crewed by men who do not know each other.

**POV character:** Thomas Briggs  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** H.M.S. *Confiance*, at the Île aux Noix naval shipyard, Richelieu River, Lower Canada. September 3–4, 1814. The forenoon watch (0800–1200).

**Chapter shape:** Single scene  
**Ending mode:** Literary fade

**Target words:** ~2,000

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal (chapter-level):** Thomas does his watch correctly — a quiet, workmanlike ambition, not a dramatic one.
- **Obstacle:** The ship's unreadiness: incomplete gunlocks, strangers at every station, carpenters still working overhead.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Opening.** Thomas takes his station at the start of the forenoon watch. The ship: her main deck gun line, the smell of freshly worked timber and lamp-black, the sounds of the yard still audible from the bank. The crew assembling at their gun stations for the first full drill of the day — nine men who have been aboard less than a week. Thomas knows one by name: a man from the Channel draft who came inland with him (name optional for the drafter to supply).

2. **The drill.** "Cast Loose and Provide" — the tompion pulled, the covers off, the powder boy (a boy of fifteen or so) taking his place. The quoin adjusted. The side tackle uncoiled. One of the gun crew is a soldier of the 39th Foot serving as the secondary loader; he handles the rammer with the wrong grip. Thomas corrects him without comment — showing, not telling. The drill completes.

3. **Midday mess.** Thomas eats with his mess. The ordinary food. The conversation around the table drifts to the lake — how far south, how long. One man asks about the American fleet. Another says he heard they're already in the bay. Thomas says nothing about what he has thought watching the ship being built and fitted. He has thought it constantly; it has not become a sentence he says aloud.

4. **The gunlock problem.** After dinner, a gunlock from the armory is brought to the main deck gun line — one of the pieces that still lacks a functioning lock. Thomas examines it. The flint doesn't catch cleanly; the spring tension is off. He sets it aside and prepares the linstock instead. The fact is noted. Nothing more.

5. **The carpenters overhead.** In the afternoon, the sound of mallets from the upper deck — something still not finished. Thomas passes under the hatch and looks up. A French-Canadian carpenter from the Île aux Noix yard is working on a section of planking that has checked in the sun — the green timber shrinking as it dries. The carpenter doesn't know what the ship is here to do. He knows the plank needs to be right.

6. **End of watch.** Thomas on deck at the change of watch. The Richelieu opens to the north, and past its mouth he can see the lake — flat gray-green, the far shore of New York State low and dark. The June when he stepped her masts seems long ago. He notes the wind: light, from the south. The evening light on the water.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Nothing changes in the plot. The watch is completed. The ship is still incomplete, the crew still strangers. Thomas is still aboard.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

What will this crew be like when the guns are run out in earnest and the sequence must be completed under fire?

---

## Continuity Notes

- Thomas served eleven years in the Royal Navy; *Confiance* is not his first ship and not his first unready ship, but it is this one.
- Soldiers of the 39th Foot serve as marines aboard *Confiance*; some of them are also gun crew auxiliaries.
- Some of *Confiance*'s gunlocks are non-functional at this stage of fitting-out.
- The crew were "totally unknown to each other" as late as September 5 — these are the days just before that.
- Thomas carries Elizabeth's worn letter in his pocket. He does not reread it in this chapter — the fact of it is enough, the weight in the pocket.
- The carpenter detail is introduced here as physical texture, not foreshadowing; it does not return.
- Named historical officers (Downie) must not be given interiority; if he appears, it is as a figure seen across the deck or heard giving an order, nothing more.
- The lake is visible from the ship but Thomas is not yet on it.

---

## Prose Register Note

This chapter establishes Thomas's voice before any action. The register must be fully present from the first paragraph: spare, technical, the vocabulary of eleven years at sea used without explanation. His assessment of the ship and crew lives entirely in what he observes, not in what he announces. "He had noticed" is the failure mode; the observation itself, rendered in his vocabulary, is the lean-into. When the chapter registers unease, it does so through his attention to specific physical details — the wrong grip on the rammer, the flint that doesn't catch — not through declared states.
