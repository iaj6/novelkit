# Chapter 12 — Both Banks

**One-line summary:** The night of September 10–11: British movement on the north bank, a probe at the ford near midnight, and first light of September 11 with red coats visible in the tree line.

**POV character:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** Pike's Ford, Saranac River. The night of September 10 into the dawn of September 11, 1814.

**Chapter shape:** Single scene (one long night at the ford, running to dawn)  
**Ending mode:** Mid-action

**Target words:** ~2,800

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Hold the watch through the night; see what the morning brings.
- **Obstacle:** The darkness and the sounds from the north bank — organized, purposeful, and getting closer.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Asa's night watch begins.** He takes the watch at some point in the night of September 10 — his rotation, his hour. The ford: the familiar sound of the Saranac over the gravel bar, the dark north bank, the stars visible above the Vermont mountains in the east. September cold — he has his coat. The company is deployed in their positions; most are awake.

2. **Movement on the north bank.** Lights in the trees, further upstream than they've been before — not the established picket fires downstream but something new, closer to the ford. The sound of men moving through brush — not one man, not casual. Equipment sounds: the creak of leather, the faint ring of metal on metal. Asa wakes the sergeant.

3. **The probe.** Near midnight (or in the early hours of September 11), a small party of British soldiers enters the ford from the north bank. They wade in — maybe to the gravel bar, maybe halfway across. The sound of boots on the submerged gravel. Asa can hear them more than he can see them. The sergeant orders: hold fire. They hold. The British soldiers — three, perhaps five — stop in the water, listening. Then pull back. The ford is silent again. The company remains on the bank.

4. **The rest of the night.** The north bank is not quiet after the probe. More movement, more organized sound. It is clear to Asa, listening in the dark and reading what he hears the way he reads water, that there are more men on the north bank than there were yesterday. Many more. The full weight of what is being brought against this ford is audible before it is visible.

5. **Before first light.** The company is entirely on the bank, all rotation suspended. The sergeant moves along the line, checking positions. Asa is at his point — the position that covers both the gravel bar and the channel. He has been here in the dark so many times in the past week that he knows the ford by sound now: the bar, the channel, the eddy behind the downstream clay bank where the water moves differently.

6. **First light.** The eastern sky lightens — the Vermont mountains first, gray shapes becoming defined. The light comes incrementally across the water. The ford's details come back: the gravel bar, the channel, the north bank. In the growing light, Asa can see the north bank more clearly than he could all night. The tree line: British soldiers, in numbers, in the shadows just inside the edge of the trees. Red coats visible between leaves. More than he'd seen downstream during the standoff days. The sergeant's command runs down the line: two ranks, take position. The company moves. Asa takes his place.

7. **End.** The morning is here. Movement on the north bank. The ford between them. The chapter ends with both banks occupied and the space between them — the ford Asa has known his whole life — about to become something else.

---

## Outcome / Turn

September 11 has arrived. The British are massed on the north bank. The company is in position. The morning is here.

- The cannonade from the bay is NOT audible yet in this chapter — it begins in Chapter 14, once the naval battle starts around 8:00–8:30 AM. Chapter 12 ends at or just before dawn.

- The ford's specific geometry: gravel bar fifty yards out from the north bank, a channel between the bar and the south bank, chest-deep on an adult.
- Asa's position covers both bar and channel — established in Chapter 06.
- The note about parallel structure: Chapter 11 (Thomas) ended on the structural turn — the squadron moves at first light. Chapter 12 (Asa) ends on the physical arrival of that same morning at the ford. The two chapters are approximately simultaneous in narrative time.

## Prose Register Note

This chapter is Asa's closest approach to Thomas's "note the wind" mode — he reads the sounds of the night the way Thomas reads the wind. The darkness is the chapter's sensory medium; the ford's acoustic texture (the gravel bar, the water sounds, the specific quiet of a ford at night) is its landscape. The chapter must resist the temptation to be tense or atmospheric — it should be precise and watchful, which is what Asa is. The ending is the arrival of light, not a dramatic revelation: the red coats between the leaves are visible because the light increased, nothing more.
