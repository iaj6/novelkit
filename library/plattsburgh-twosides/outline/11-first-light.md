# Chapter 11 — First Light

**One-line summary:** The evening of September 10: Thomas prepares his gun for the last time; the carpenters go ashore in the jolly boat; the word passes that the squadron moves at first light.

**POV character:** Thomas Briggs  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** H.M.S. *Confiance*, at anchor off Cumberland Head. September 10, 1814, late afternoon through the night.

**Chapter shape:** Interiority-heavy (the final preparations, the quiet evening, the chapter lives in the gap between what is known and what is not said)  
**Ending mode:** Turn

**Target words:** ~2,500

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Have the gun ready. Have himself ready.
- **Obstacle:** The obstacle is not external here. It is the fact of tomorrow being different from today, held without being named.

---

## Action Beats

1. **The carpenters go ashore.** Late afternoon of September 10. A jolly boat is lowered; the carpenters from Île aux Noix load their tool bags and go over the side. Thomas watches from the deck. They have been part of the ship's daily sound for weeks — the mallets, the planes, the running argument between two of them about the planking. Now they go. The boat pulls toward shore. The ship becomes, in that moment, only a warship. No more civilian margin.

2. **Downie's inspection.** Downie moves along the main deck gun line, stopping at each piece, asking a question of the gun captain. Thomas is at his station when Downie reaches his gun. A single question — perhaps: is the lock true? Perhaps something about the crew's readiness, or the powder charge. Thomas answers accurately. Downie moves on. That is all Thomas has of him.

3. **Preparing the gun.** Thomas's gun inspection, unhurried. This is the specific physical work of an able seaman making a gun ready for action: the quoin positioned, the breeching checked for wear, the side tackle laid free, the train tackle run out and back. The powder boy briefed on where he stands and when he moves. The sponge and rammer placed. Thomas tests the lock again; it fires. He sets it. Then places the linstock in its notch as well — both options available. He takes no pleasure in this preparation, and no dread. He has done it many times; he does it now.

4. **Mess dinner, quiet.** The evening meal. The men eat with less talk than usual. Not silence exactly — someone says something, there is a brief exchange — but the dinner is different from the others. One of Thomas's named messmates makes a remark that is not quite a joke, something understated about the morning. Thomas eats. He does not respond. This is not because he has nothing to say; he has the reserve of a man who has been at a gun under fire before and knows that what he could say is not useful.

5. **On deck, the evening.** After dinner, Thomas stands on deck. The lake of September 10 is flat and dark, the sky clear, the air cold. The American fleet is invisible in the darkness across the bay, but its position is precise in his mind: the line of four ships, the gunboats, the springs on the cables, the Saratoga in the center with Macdonough's flag. The wind is still from the southwest. He notes it. He notes it.

6. **The word passes.** Before Thomas turns in, the word has moved through the ship by the usual channels: the squadron moves at first light. It is not a surprise. But it is now a fact, not a date on a piece of paper in the captain's cabin. Thomas goes below. In his hammock, the ship's sounds: water against the hull, the creak of the timber, the anchor cable running. He does not sleep immediately. He notes the wind through the ship's sounds: the particular way the stays sing at this direction. Same as it was.

## Outcome / Turn

This is the book's structural turn. Tomorrow will be different from today. The squadron moves at first light.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

This is the turn; there is no new question to hold the thread forward. The morning is the answer.

## Continuity Notes

- The carpenters were dropped off at Cumberland Head before the engagement — this is documented. [cite:14] This chapter stages that departure.
- Downie is never POV. He is seen passing along the gun line, asking one question, moving on. His interiority is inaccessible. [continuity #29]
- Thomas carries Elizabeth's letter. The fact of it is present; the letter is not opened or described. Its weight in the pocket is enough.
- The battle begins at approximately 8:00–8:30 AM on September 11. [continuity #13] The squadron would have moved before dawn to round Cumberland Head in position.
- The wind from the southwest is historically significant for the British approach; Thomas has noted it for three chapters.
- This is the only chapter assigned the "turn" ending mode. It is the closest the book comes to a cliffhanger, and it earns it through accumulated weight, not through revelation.

## Prose Register Note

This is the most quiet chapter in Thomas's arc, which is to say it is the most interior — but "interior" remains in his register: the specific acts of preparation, the weight of the familiar in the routine, the thing that is felt in the absence of what is usually talked about at dinner. The chapter does not announce that tomorrow Thomas will face something that has a reasonable chance of killing him; the prose does not need to say this. The reader knows. What the chapter offers is Thomas doing what he has always done, with the care he has always brought to it, on the last evening before everything changes. The ending — "same as it was" — is the turn without the announcement.
