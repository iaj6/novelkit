# Chapter 03 — The Lake Opens

**One-line summary:** *Confiance* departs Île aux Noix and enters Lake Champlain; Thomas runs his gun through its first drill on open water with a crew that barely knows the sequence.

**POV character:** Thomas Briggs  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** Île aux Noix → the Richelieu River → entering Lake Champlain. September 5–6, 1814. The day of departure and the first night on the open lake.

**Chapter shape:** Multi-scene  
**Ending mode:** Mid-action

**Target words:** ~2,500

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Get the ship out of the Richelieu and onto the lake in correct order; begin making the crew into a working gun unit.
- **Obstacle:** The remaining work still being done aboard; the crew's near-total unfamiliarity with each other and with the ship; and the wind, which is from the south — a headwind for the passage into the bay when that day comes.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Departure morning.** All hands on deck before sunrise. The squadron forming in the Richelieu: *Confiance* at the center, *Linnet*, *Chubb*, *Finch*, the twelve gunboats. The anchors hove short and then up. Lines let go from the shore. Thomas at his station on the main deck — his view is limited to the immediate deck and the water alongside, which is how it should be. He does not see the whole picture; he sees what is within his arc of observation.

2. **The Richelieu.** The river is narrow for a ship of *Confiance*'s size. The banks close. Canadian farmland on both sides, then the fortifications at Île aux Noix receding astern. Thomas watches the shore without sentiment. This is the third time in his career he has left a place he lived in for months. The habit of departure is an old one.

3. **Entering the lake.** The Richelieu widens. Then the lake opens: several miles of water, the Vermont mountains on the eastern shore rising in the morning haze, the New York shore to the west lower and wooded. The squadron spaces out. Thomas notes the wind: from the south-southwest. A headwind for anyone trying to enter the bay from the north.

4. **First open-water drill.** The gun crews called to stations. Thomas at his piece — the long gun amidships on the larboard side. He runs the crew through the sequence for the third time overall and the first time with the lake moving under them. "Cast Loose and Provide." The tompion out. "Sponge your guns." One man — a seaman from the Channel draft — does it cleanly. The soldier of the 39th who serves as secondary loader is faster this time than before but handles the rammer from the wrong shoulder. Thomas repositions him. "Load Cartridge."

5. **The foul tackle.** When the gun is run in for the second cycle, the train tackle jams on its block — rope kinked from being coiled incorrectly after the previous drill. It takes nearly a minute to clear. The crew watches Thomas work it loose. One man tries to help and is gently moved aside. The tackle clears; the drill resumes. "Run Out." The gun runs out through the port. The lake visible past the muzzle.

6. **End-in-action ending.** The command is given: "Fire as your guns bear." The gun captain (Thomas) raises his hand — the signal that the crew is ready. The linstock comes to the touchhole. The chapter ends here, in the moment before the hammer falls, the gun run out and aimed at open lake.

---

## Outcome / Turn

*Confiance* is on Lake Champlain. The first drill has identified the capable men and the gaps. The crew is neither ready nor hopeless — they are what they are: strangers learning.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

A week of sailing south and drilling every watch — will it be enough? And the wind is against them.

---

## Continuity Notes

- The squadron: *Confiance* (flag, Downie), *Linnet* (Pring), *Chubb*, *Finch*, twelve gunboats — all depart together.
- Carpenters from the Île aux Noix yard are still working aboard *Confiance* as she sails; they will be dropped off at Cumberland Head before the battle. Their presence is part of the background.
- Thomas's gun position: a long gun on the main deck. *Confiance* carried a mix of long guns and carronades; the specific caliber of Thomas's piece should be consistent with the ship's armament as documented in `canon/research.md`.
- Gun drill sequence from research: "Cast Loose and Provide," "Sponge your guns," "Load Cartridge," "Ram Cartridge," "Load Round," "Ram Round," "Run Out," "Fire as your guns bear."
- The wind from the south is a recurring fact in Thomas's chapters — it means the British approach to the bay will be a beat against the headwind.
- Downie is not POV. If he appears, he is a figure at the quarterdeck rail, heard giving a general order, nothing more.
- The lake's geography: Vermont mountains on the east, the Adirondacks on the west, the lake running roughly north-south.

## Prose Register Note

This chapter should feel like the moment a seaman's world changes scale — from the confined yard, the river, the half-finished ship, to the open lake. The prose can use the lake's breadth as a physical fact without making it symbolic. Thomas notes what it means for the approach (the wind); he does not feel awe at the mountains. His register is calibrated to practical observation.
