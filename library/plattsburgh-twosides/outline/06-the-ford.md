# Chapter 06 — The Ford

**One-line summary:** Assigned to Pike's Ford, Asa teaches the regular sergeant what he knows about the ground; the chapter closes on his first night watch, the river dark below.

**POV character:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** Pike's Ford, Saranac River, approximately three miles upstream of Plattsburgh's Lower Bridge. September 4–7, 1814.

**Chapter shape:** Bottle (one location, compressed days)  
**Ending mode:** Literary fade

**Target words:** ~2,400

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Establish himself and his company at the ford; make the sergeant believe that Asa's knowledge of the ground is tactically usable.
- **Obstacle:** The gap between knowing the ground as a millwright and knowing it as a defender — the sergeant's initial professional skepticism of a nineteen-year-old's claimed expertise; the company's uneven skill with a musket.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Arrival at the ford.** Asa and his company assigned to Pike's Ford and walking the three miles upstream from Plattsburgh. The ford at lower September flow: the gravel bar visible through the shallows, knee-to-waist deep across most of the crossing, the clay banks upstream and down. Asa reads it without announcing what he is doing: the channel between the bar and the south bank, where the footing softens from gravel to silt, where the current runs faster than it looks. This is not observation for the chapter's benefit — it is how he always looks at the river.

2. **The sergeant's drill.** The sergeant — a regular from Macomb's command, experienced, skeptical — sets up two-rank drill immediately. "Make ready — Present — Fire — Prime and load." The sequence runs. The men are a cross-section of the valley: two farmers from Clinton County, a blacksmith's assistant, a tavern keeper's son, several actual hunters, one man who has served before in the 1812 border skirmishes. The sergeant watches who is fast and who is not.

3. **Asa and the sergeant, talking ground.** In the afternoon, Asa approaches the sergeant with specific information: the gravel bar gives firm footing for fifty yards from the north bank, but the channel on the south side of the bar is chest-deep and the clay bottom is slick. A column of men trying to cross all the way would hit the channel at full extension. The sergeant listens with the neutral attention of a man checking his information. He asks: where would you stand to cover both the bar and the channel? Asa shows him — a position on the south bank, slightly elevated, that commands the bar's shallows and the channel mouth. The sergeant walks the position. He doesn't say Asa is right; he tells the company to keep a man there during watch.

4. **Wading the ford.** Asa crosses the ford at low water to demonstrate and verify. He wades it slowly — the gravel bar, the channel, the south bank — and comes back. The north bank is empty. The pines on the north side are dense; the forest comes to within thirty yards of the water. He notes this without comment, as he notes the depth of the channel and the firmness of the bar.

5. **The company settling in.** Days pass in the same pattern: drill in the morning, watch in rotation through the day and night. The men begin to know each other. The sergeant establishes a rotation; Asa is on the first dog watch and again at midnight. A false alarm one night — a deer crossing the shallows — three men bring their muskets up and one fires before the sergeant's hand comes down. No harm. The ford is still the ford.

6. **Closing: first full night watch.** Asa at the ford in the dark. The river black and moving, the sound of the current over the gravel bar constant under everything else. The Vermont mountains to the east are darker shapes against the stars. The north bank is invisible — not because anything hides there, but because the night is the night. The water sounds. He has heard this sound all his life.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Asa and his company are positioned at the ford. The sergeant trusts Asa's reading of the ground. The position is established.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

The British are on the north bank downstream — from what Asa heard at Plattsburgh, they're already establishing their line. How long before they test this ford?

## Prose Register Note

This is the chapter that establishes Asa's ground — the ford, the river, the specific terrain that will determine the action of Chapter 14. The drafter should render the ford as a place Asa reads the way a skilled person reads their domain, not as dramatic backdrop. The conversation with the sergeant is not a coming-of-age validation; it is a practical exchange between two men who know what they're doing in different registers. The ending should land on sound and darkness — the ford at night is the physical world Asa will be defending — without marking it as such.
