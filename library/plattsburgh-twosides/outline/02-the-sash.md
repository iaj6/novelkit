# Chapter 02 — The Sash

**One-line summary:** Asa runs a full day at his uncle's sash sawmill on the Saranac; the British muster is still rumor.

**POV character:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** Elias Beckwith's sash sawmill on the Saranac River, three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village, Clinton County, New York. September 2–3, 1814. A full working day, opening to closing.

**Chapter shape:** Single scene  
**Ending mode:** Resolved

**Target words:** ~2,000

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal (chapter-level):** Complete the day's cut. Get close to a thousand board feet.
- **Obstacle:** A log that comes in cracked, or the pitman arm making an irregular knock that must be diagnosed before the day can run properly. The workday has the texture of skilled labor with practical problems to solve — small obstacles, the ordinary kind.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Opening.** Asa opens the headgate before the sun is fully up. The water catches, the wheel turns, the pitman arm converts rotation into the sash's up-down stroke. The sound builds: the rasping of the blade, the building vibrating through the floor, the millpond dimpling from the wheel's overflow. The chapter establishes the specific vocabulary of the sash mill — pitman, log carriage, log dogs — as Asa uses them, not as terms being explained.

2. **First log.** A white pine log on the carriage, the dogs driven in. Asa reads the grain — a crook in the wood that means he'll need to reset the carriage after the first face cut. He adjusts without making much of it. The first cut: fresh pine scent, the saw's kerf widening as the carriage advances. Sawdust falling.

3. **Midday stop.** A neighbor or local man stops at the mill — news from the village. The British have come over the border into lower Canada, maybe further. The muster notices are going around. Elias is unworried; he's heard militia rumors every summer since 1812. Asa listens differently. His father served. He has been the household's working head since fifteen. He doesn't say what he thinks; he takes up the next log.

4. **Afternoon work.** More cutting. The competency of Asa's work shown through specific actions: reading which way a log wants to move as it advances through the cut, keeping the blade from binding by easing the carriage feed, checking the sash bolts at the halfway point of the day. The mill is his world; his mastery of it is quiet and complete.

5. **The ford, briefly.** At some point in the afternoon — perhaps when he goes to the millpond to clear a floating slabwood that's lodged against the dam — Asa is at the river. He can see the ford from here: the gravel bar where the water shallows, the color change where the channel deepens, the clay banks on the far side. He has crossed it hundreds of times. This is not a portent. It's just the ford he knows.

6. **End of day.** Elias tallies the cut at close of the afternoon: good, close to target. The headgate closed. The wheel slowing to stillness, the sash coming to rest. They eat beside the mill. The Saranac runs past in the dusk. The first September cool in the air. No orders have come. The day is done.

---

## Outcome / Turn

A day's work, fully rendered and complete in itself. The mill ran. The timber was cut. The men ate. Nothing changed.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

The muster talk is present but not urgent. The question is implied by what the reader knows: how long before rumor becomes orders?

---

## Continuity Notes

- Elias Beckwith runs a **sash sawmill** — reciprocating blade in a wooden sash frame, pitman arm from the waterwheel, log carriage with log dogs. Not a circular saw; not a frame saw. Specific mill type.
- The mill is three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village, on the Saranac River.
- Asa's father Daniel Beckwith is incapacitated (fever from Sackett's Harbor). Asa has been the household's working man since he was fifteen. This fact should be present in the chapter's texture without being announced.
- September in the Champlain Valley: "nights already cold enough for a coat; days in the low sixties." First maple color at the highest elevations but the ridges still mostly green.
- The Saranac at lower September flow: tea-colored over limestone where it shallows over gravel bars.
- The muster is rumor at this point — not orders. Asa has not yet been called up.
- Elias Beckwith does not go to war. He holds the mill through the crisis.

---

## Prose Register Note

Asa's chapter opens the American register: closer to the surface than Thomas, paratactic, the world delivered in sequence. He thinks in the river — its flow, the reading of water in motion — and the sawmill is an extension of that same observational intelligence. Shorter sentences on average than Thomas's chapters. The register is contemporary-feeling in idiom but not anachronistic; Champlain Valley vernacular of 1814, not 2024 colloquial. The chapter must feel genuinely ordinary — not weighted as "the last ordinary day." The prose does not know what day this is in the arc of the story.
