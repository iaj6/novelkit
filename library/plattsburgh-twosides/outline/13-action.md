# Chapter 13 — Action

**One-line summary:** September 11, 8:00 AM to noon: *Confiance* enters Plattsburgh Bay, Downie dies within fifteen minutes, and Thomas works his gun until the colors come down.

**POV character:** Thomas Briggs  
**Tense:** Third-person limited, past tense  
**Setting/time:** H.M.S. *Confiance*, Plattsburgh Bay, Lake Champlain. September 11, 1814, approximately 8:00 AM to noon.

**Chapter shape:** Action-heavy  
**Ending mode:** Resolved

**Target words:** ~4,000

---

## Goal / Obstacle

- **Goal:** Serve the gun correctly, through the full action, without breaking the sequence.
- **Obstacle:** Everything: the American broadside, the ship's unreadiness, the wind delaying the approach, Downie's death, the diminishing crew, the chaos of three hours under fire.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Before dawn, the squadron moves.** *Confiance* weighs anchor off Cumberland Head and begins the approach, the squadron falling into position. Thomas at his station below the deck level of the officers — he cannot see the full approach but he knows its shape from the sounds: the anchors up, the sails filling, the change in the ship's motion as she clears the head and the bay opens around her.

2. **Rounding Cumberland Head.** The approach into the bay. The wind is from the southwest — a headwind — which means *Confiance* must beat up against it, tacking across the bay's mouth to gain position. This slows the approach; the ship is not in her designated position when the first shots are fired. Thomas can hear the commands on the quarterdeck, the activity of the topmen overhead, but his world is his gun and the men around it.

3. **Before the opening.** The waiting at the gun station. The American line visible through the larboard gun ports as *Confiance* angles into the bay. Thomas can see *Saratoga*'s hull and the white line of her gun ports across the water. He sees the springs on her cables. He checks his crew one last time: positions, faces, the powder boy crouched below the level of the gun in his place. He notes the wind through the gun port.

4. **The opening broadside.** *Saratoga* opens fire — a full broadside from the American flagship. The concussion is different from practice: the sound, the pressure wave in the air, the impact on the hull. *Confiance* has not yet come to anchor in her designated position; she is caught partly across the line, taking the broadside from a bad angle. The deck. The sound of the first shot striking timber. Smoke beginning to drift. Thomas runs his gun on the first cycle. The sequence is the chapter's rhythm from here: sponge, load, ram, run out, fire.

5. **Downie falls.** Thomas does not see it. He is at his gun, on the starboard quarter of the main deck, and the captain falls on the quarterdeck. The word comes in the way battle news travels — not as an announcement but as a change in the quality of the orders coming down from above, and then the word itself passing from man to man: *the captain is down*. Within fifteen minutes of the action's opening. Thomas receives it, processes it, and runs the gun again.

6. **The action, sustained.** The ship pounds and is pounded. Specific, observable details: a spar comes down on the starboard side; the wreckage has to be cleared from around a gun before it can fire again. A man in Thomas's crew is hit on the third or fourth cycle — not killed, his arm useless — and Thomas moves to take both positions, adjusting the sequence. The gun crew is now one short and running a modified sequence that Thomas improvises from practice. The gun keeps firing.

7. **Mid-battle: the ship wounded.** *Confiance*'s spring cables are shot away by American fire; she swings at anchor, her broadside no longer bearing on *Saratoga*. For a period the larboard guns are firing at a less effective angle; then the spring is replaced or improvised. The ship takes damage below the waterline — the pumps are heard working constantly. The deck is different from what it was at the start of the day.

8. **Thomas's frame of reference: Trafalgar.** A brief interior passage — not a declared memory, but a professional comparison arriving through the specific sounds and sensations of action. At Trafalgar, aboard *Achilles*, the gun deck had a rhythm: the coordinated crews, the familiarity, the particular sound of a working battery. That sound is partly here now — his crew has it, in fragments, because they drilled — and partly not, because they don't have it all. He runs the gun again.

9. **The final hour.** Thomas's gun is one of the few still operational on the main deck. His powder boy is still in place — the boy has been in his position for three hours, moving from below to the gun and back, through the smoke and the noise. Thomas runs the gun again.

10. **The surrender.** An order from above — a voice Thomas doesn't recognize, not Downie's (Downie is dead) but someone's. The colors are to be struck. Thomas does not know for certain who gives the order; he knows what it means. He hears the cry pass. The guns fall silent — his, and then the others that remain. The colors come down from the mizzen.

11. **The silence.** The particular silence after a long action: the guns stopped, the smoke still present, the ship still moving on the water. Thomas's ears are damaged from the guns — sounds come through a ringing and a muffling. He looks at what is left: the deck, the crew, the gun still in its place. The window frames are gone from the light. The ship moves with a different heaviness in the water than she had at dawn.

---

## Outcome / Turn

*Confiance* has struck her colors. The action is over. Thomas is alive. His gun was at the ship to the end.

## Continuity Notes

- The action opened approximately 8:00–8:30 AM on September 11, 1814. [continuity #13]
- Downie was killed within the first fifteen minutes. Cause: a gun knocked from its carriage by American fire crushed him against the quarterdeck rail. His pocket watch stopped at the moment of impact; there was no external wound. [continuity #16]
- Downie is NEVER POV. His death is received by Thomas from below — through the chain of report that moves down a lower deck. [continuity #29]
- After Downie's death, command passed to the surviving officers aboard *Confiance*. The historical figure is Lieutenant Robertson, who became subject of a court martial for the surrender. He is not a named presence in Thomas's chapter — Thomas doesn't know or see who gave the final order.
- *Confiance* struck her colors approximately noon. [continuity #13]
- *Saratoga* wound 180° during the action using kedge anchors, presenting her undamaged broadside. This is visible to Thomas during the action if his gun position faces it.
- The springs on *Saratoga*'s cables: the American ships could rotate at anchor to present fresh broadsides. [continuity #18]
- *Confiance*'s armament: 37 guns, mix of long guns and carronades. Details in `canon/research.md`.
- *Chubb* was captured early in the action; *Finch* ran aground on Crab Island shoals. Thomas may be aware of this through the sounds of the action.
- Thomas's reference frame: *Achilles* at Trafalgar, October 21, 1805. [continuity #6]
- The chapter contains the only direct use of Thomas's Trafalgar experience as comparison — not as nostalgia, but as professional calibration.
- The powder boy is not named; the drafter may give him a name and a brief characterization established in an earlier chapter.

## Prose Register Note

This is the longest chapter in the book and its climax. The prose in action shortens: sentence lengths contract, details become the unit of perception, subordinate clauses earn their place only when earned. The gun drill sequence — sponge, load, ram, run out, fire — is the chapter's structural rhythm; it runs and runs and is modified as men fall and positions shift. Thomas's awareness in battle is perceptual, not interpretive: he knows what his gun is doing, what the men around him are doing, what he can see through the gun port. He does not know the larger shape of the battle; the reader does, because the reader has read the brief. What Thomas has is his gun station, his crew, and the relentless machinery of the sequence. The chapter ends not on a feeling but on a fact: the guns are silent, the colors are down.
