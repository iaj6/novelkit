# Chapter Map — *Two Shores*

## Overview

**Total chapters:** 16  
**Target manuscript length:** ~40,000 words  
**Average words per chapter:** ~2,500 (range: 1,800–4,500)  
**POV structure:** Two POVs, strict alternation throughout — Thomas opens (Chapter 1); Asa closes (Chapter 16)  
**Temporal span:** September 2–3 (Asa's day-before) through September 12, 1814 (both day-after chapters)

---

## POV Decision: Thomas Opens

Thomas Briggs opens the book. His world is geographically remote from the reader's likely orientation — a British ship being built in Canada, a crew of strangers, a lake the reader sees first from the water. Opening with the more dislocating perspective gives both threads equal weight by establishing the foreign register first; Asa's home ground then reads as a counterweight, not a default.

## Arc Shape

| Phase | Chapters | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| **Setup** | 1–4 | Day-before chapters (ordinary life) + first approach movements. The world as it was before the collision. |
| **Escalation** | 5–10 | Approach-and-standoff. Both characters move toward September 11. Each chapter builds from routine toward tightening, without manufactured tension. |
| **Turn** | 11 | Thomas's final night. The structural turn of the book: the squadron moves at first light. The last chapter before the axis. |
| **Pre-axis tension** | 12 | Asa's final night. First light of September 11, British soldiers at the ford. |
| **Climax** | 13–14 | September 11. The naval battle (Thomas); the ford battle (Asa). The two longest chapters in the book. Simultaneous — the naval cannonade audible from the ford in Chapter 14. |
| **Coda** | 15–16 | September 12. The prize tender (Thomas); the south bank (Asa). What remains. |

## Notes for the Drafter

- **Thomas and Asa never meet, never see each other, never know the other exists.** This is structural and non-negotiable.
- **The only sensory crossover** between threads is the naval cannonade audible from Pike's Ford in Chapter 14. It enters Asa's chapter as rolling percussion — weather, not narration.
- **Named historical officers are never POV.** Downie, Pring, Macdonough, Mooers, Macomb, Prevost, Brisbane — all seen from below only.
- **The day-before chapters do not foreshadow.** No ominous register. The prose observes.
- **The day-after chapters do not philosophize.** No weighing of the battle's significance.
- **Historical accuracy** is non-negotiable. All specifics in `canon/research.md`.
