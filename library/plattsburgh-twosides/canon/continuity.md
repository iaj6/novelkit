# Continuity — *Two Shores*

Load-bearing facts that must not break across any chapter, phase, or revision. Numbered for cross-reference.

---

### Characters

1. **Thomas Briggs** is an entirely invented character. He is 32 years old in September 1814. He was born in Plymouth, Devonshire, and raised in Devonport. He is an able seaman in the Royal Navy.

2. **Asa Beckwith** is an entirely invented character. He is 19 years old in September 1814. He is a Clinton County, New York militiaman, serving in Major-General Mooers's levy.

3. **Thomas's wife** is Elizabeth Briggs, resident of Devonport. Thomas has a son born after he sailed; he has never seen the child. He carries a worn letter from Elizabeth.

4. **Asa's father**, Daniel Beckwith, served at Sackett's Harbor and returned with a fever that did not leave. Asa has been the working head of the household since he was fifteen.

5. **Asa's uncle**, Elias Beckwith (invented), runs the sash sawmill on the Saranac River, three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village. The sawmill is Asa's entire working world before the muster.

6. **Thomas served at the Battle of Trafalgar, October 21, 1805**, aboard H.M.S. *Achilles*. He was twenty-three. *Note for drafters: the research dossier (canon/research.md) covers HMS Achille (1798) — a different vessel also in Collingwood's column. The brief specifies Achilles; downstream agents should use Achilles and cross-check specific ship details with care.*

---

### The Two POVs: Structural Constraints

7. **The two protagonists never meet, never see each other, never know the other exists.** This is non-negotiable throughout all 16 chapters.

8. **The only direct sensory crossover between the two threads** is the sound of the naval cannonade heard from the upper Saranac fords on September 11. No other sensory or informational crossover.

9. **Chapters strictly alternate POV** — Thomas, Asa, Thomas, Asa (or the reverse if the plotter opens with Asa) — throughout all 16 chapters, no exceptions.

10. **Each character has exactly eight chapters**, structured as: 1 day-before + 5 approach-and-standoff + 1 climactic action + 1 day-after.

---

### Timeline

11. The book spans approximately nine days, from roughly September 3–4 (day-before chapters) to the evening of **September 12, 1814** (day-after chapters). It does not extend beyond September 12.

12. **The day-before chapters** take place in ordinary time — Thomas's in the last days of *Confiance*'s fitting-out or on the southward passage; Asa's at his uncle's sawmill before the muster call. The prose observes; it does not foreshadow. No ominous register.

13. **The climactic action chapters** are September 11, 1814. The naval engagement begins approximately 8:00–8:30 AM and is resolved by noon. The British army's retreat begins the evening of September 11.

14. **The day-after chapters** are September 12, 1814. Thomas is on an American prize tender on Lake Champlain; Asa is on the south bank of the Saranac with the British retreat road visible to the north. The prose observes what has changed; it does not philosophize.

### Historical Facts: The Naval Battle

15. **H.M.S. *Confiance***: 37 guns; fifth-rate corvette; built at Île aux Noix naval shipyard on the Richelieu River; launched August 1814; fitted out while already under way south; crew of approximately 320 men, "totally unknown to each other" as late as September 5. Soldiers of the **39th Foot** served as her marines.

16. **Captain George Downie**: born January 19, 1778, Isle of Lewis, Scotland; age **36** at death; commanding officer of *Confiance* and British flagship. Killed within the first fifteen minutes of the September 11 engagement — a gun knocked from its carriage by American fire crushed him against the quarterdeck rail. His pocket watch stopped at the moment of impact; there was no external wound. Buried at Plattsburgh.

17. **Commander Daniel Pring**: commanding officer, H.M.S. *Linnet*, 16-gun brig, 99 crew, 16 × 12-pounders. Senior surviving British naval officer after Downie's death.

18. **The American squadron** anchored in a north-to-south line in Plattsburgh Bay, approximately 1,000 yards from shore: *Eagle* (north), *Saratoga* (flag, Macdonough), *Ticonderoga*, *Preble*, with ten gunboats. *Saratoga*: 36 guns, crew 212. Ships fitted with springs on cables. *Saratoga* wound 180° mid-action using kedge anchors.

19. **Master Commandant Thomas Macdonough**: born December 31, 1783; age 30 at the battle. Never POV.

---

### Historical Facts: The Land Battle

20. **Pike's Ford**: approximately three miles upstream from the Lower Bridge in Plattsburgh village (also described as "a mile and a half above the Upper Bridge"). This is where Asa stands on September 11. The British infantry attempted three Saranac crossings on September 11: at the Lower Bridge site, the Upper Bridge site, and Pike's Ford.

21. **The Lower Bridge and Upper Bridge** across the Saranac were destroyed by the Americans before the battle.

22. **Major-General Benjamin Mooers** commands the Northern New York Militia, including the Clinton County levy. He is never POV.

23. **The British land force** includes Peninsula War veteran regiments — among them elements of the 3rd, 5th, 27th, 39th, 76th, and 88th Foot. Never POV.

24. **Brigadier-General Alexander Macomb** commands the American regulars at Plattsburgh. Never POV.

25. **Sir George Prevost** commands the British invasion force and orders the retreat on September 11 following the naval defeat. The controversy around his decision is rendered through what characters observe, not editorially. Never POV. (He died January 5, 1816, before his scheduled court martial could be held.)

---

### Narrative Prohibitions (structural)

26. **No fate-of-the-nation narration.** No sentence may carry historical retrospect — no reference to Ghent, the treaty, the end of British invasion plans, or the battle's larger significance. Neither character, nor the narrator, knows what September 11 will come to mean.

27. **No invented intelligence subplot**, spy narrative, secret messages, or coded signals.

28. **No coda beyond September 12, 1814.**

29. **Named historical officers are never POV.** Downie, Pring, Macdonough, Mooers, Macomb, Prevost, Brisbane — all appear from the outside only.

30. **Race and class are facts of the period, not subject lectures.** Black and Indigenous people are present in the Champlain Valley in 1814 (and present in the Royal Navy); they should not be invisible, but their treatment avoids both erasure and centering. Approximately 15–20% of the US Navy's sailors during the War of 1812 were Black; the proportion at Lake Erie was approximately 25%.
