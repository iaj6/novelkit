# Glossary — *Two Shores*

Alphabetical. Names of real places, ships, persons, and period terms. Later phases may append entries; do not delete or alter existing entries without reconciling continuity.

---

**Able seaman** — Royal Navy rating below petty officer; a man certified competent in the full range of a seaman's duties. Thomas's rating aboard *Confiance*. Pay in the period: approximately £1 13s 6d per month.

**Beekmantown** — Village north of Plattsburgh on the road from Canada. Site of the British advance and the American delaying action (Major Wool with elements of the 29th Infantry) on September 6, 1814.

**Breeching** — Heavy rope securing the breech of a naval gun to the ship's side, absorbing recoil.

**Brisbane, Brigadier-General** — British brigade commander on the Saranac approach, September 11, 1814. Never POV.

**Carronade** — Short-barreled naval gun of large bore, effective at close range; lighter than a long gun of equivalent caliber. *Saratoga* carried both long guns and carronades.

**Cast Loose and Provide** — The first command in the RN gun drill sequence, ordering the gun crew to remove the tompion, remove protective covers, and prepare the piece for action.

**Champlain, Lake** — The lake forming the strategic corridor between New York and Canada. Roughly 120 miles long, widest in its northern third (Plattsburgh Bay). Drains north via the Richelieu River into the St. Lawrence.

**Clinton County** — New York county immediately west and south of Plattsburgh; the origin of Asa's militia unit.

***Chubb*** — British sloop in the Lake Champlain squadron, September 1814. Captured early in the naval action.

***Confiance*, H.M.S.** — British flagship, Lake Champlain, September 1814. Thirty-seven-gun fifth-rate corvette. Built at Île aux Noix, launched August 1814. Captain George Downie commanding. The largest warship ever launched on Lake Champlain.

**Cumberland Head** — Peninsula forming the northern and eastern boundary of Plattsburgh Bay. The British squadron rounded it at the start of the naval battle.

***Eagle*, U.S.S.** — American brig, northernmost ship in the Plattsburgh Bay line. Anchored at the north end of the American line.

**Elias Beckwith** — Asa's uncle (invented). Operator of the sash sawmill on the Saranac River, three miles upstream of Plattsburgh. Never POV; appears in day-before and day-after chapters.

***Finch*** — British sloop in the Lake Champlain squadron. Ran aground on Crab Island shoals during the battle.

**Forenoon watch** — Royal Navy watch, 0800–1200. One of seven daily watches.

**Fort Brown** — American earthwork fortification on the bluffs above the south bank of the Saranac at Plattsburgh, overlooking the river line.

**Gunlock** — Flintlock firing mechanism fitted to a naval gun in lieu of a slow match and linstock; *Confiance* had incomplete or absent gunlocks on some of her pieces at the time of the action.

**Handspike** — Iron-shod wooden lever used to traverse and elevate a naval gun. A primary tool of the gun crew.

**Île aux Noix** — Fortified island in the Richelieu River, a few miles north of Lake Champlain; site of the Royal Navy shipyard (1812–1834) where *Confiance* was built. Present-day Quebec, Canada.

**Larboard** — Left side of a vessel as seen from aft (i.e., facing forward). The period Royal Navy term; "port" was in transition but "larboard" remains correct for Thomas's register.

***Linnet*, H.M.S.** — British brig in the Lake Champlain squadron. Sixteen guns (12-pounders), 99 crew. Commander Daniel Pring commanding. Surrendered after *Confiance* struck her colors.

**Log carriage** — The sliding platform in a sash sawmill that holds the log and advances it through the blade.

**Log dogs** — Iron spikes or clamps fastening the log to the log carriage in a sawmill.

**Lower Bridge** — The bridge across the Saranac River within Plattsburgh village, on the main road. Destroyed by the Americans before the battle.

**Macdonough, Master Commandant Thomas** — American flagship commander, U.S.S. *Saratoga*. Born December 31, 1783. Never POV. His after-action letter to Secretary of the Navy Jones opens: "The Almighty has been pleased to grant us a signal victory on Lake Champlain."

**Macomb, Brigadier-General Alexander** — American land commander at Plattsburgh. Never POV.

**Messmate** — A man who eats and lives with a fixed group of shipmates in a shared mess on the lower deck. The mess is the primary social unit on a Royal Navy lower deck.

**Millpond** — The upstream reservoir formed by the milldam; the head of water that drives the waterwheel.

**Mooers, Major-General Benjamin** — Commander of the Northern New York Militia. Asa's chain of command. Never POV.

**Pitman arm** — The connecting rod in a sash sawmill's drive mechanism, converting the rotary motion of the waterwheel into the reciprocating vertical motion of the saw blade.

**Pike's Ford** — A crossing point on the Saranac River approximately three miles upstream of the Lower Bridge (also described as about a mile and a half above the Upper Bridge). The site of the British crossing attempt and Asa's position on September 11, 1814.

***Preble*, U.S.S.** — American sloop, southernmost ship in the Plattsburgh Bay line.

**Prevost, Sir George** — Governor-General of British North America; commanding general of the British invasion force, 1814. Ordered the retreat on September 11 following the naval defeat. Died January 5, 1816, before his court martial could be held. Never POV.

**Pring, Commander Daniel** — Commanding officer, H.M.S. *Linnet*. Senior surviving British naval officer after Downie's death. Praised Macdonough's humane treatment of prisoners.

**Quoin** — A wedge-shaped block inserted under the breech of a naval gun to control the angle of elevation.

**Saranac River** — River running east from the Adirondack foothills into Lake Champlain at Plattsburgh. The American defensive line runs along its south bank. Three crossing points: Lower Bridge (destroyed), Upper Bridge (destroyed), Pike's Ford (three miles upstream).

***Saratoga*, U.S.S.** — American flagship, Lake Champlain. Thirty-six guns; crew 212. Master Commandant Thomas Macdonough commanding. Built at Vergennes, Vermont. Fitted with springs on anchor cables; executed a 180° turn mid-action using kedge anchors.

**Sash sawmill** — A water-powered sawmill using a reciprocating (up-and-down) saw blade held in a wooden sash frame driven by a pitman arm. The standard mill type on the Saranac in 1814.

**Side tackle / train tackle** — Rope rigs used to work a naval gun laterally (side tackle) and fore-and-aft (train tackle) for aiming.

***Ticonderoga*, U.S.S.** — American schooner in the Plattsburgh Bay line.

**Tompion** — Wooden plug sealing the muzzle of a naval gun when not in use; removed at the start of gun drill.

**Upper Bridge** — The bridge across the Saranac approximately a mile and a half upstream of the Lower Bridge. Destroyed by the Americans before the battle.

**Watch** — Royal Navy shipboard time division. Seven watches in twenty-four hours: First Watch (2000–midnight), Middle Watch (midnight–0400), Morning Watch (0400–0800), Forenoon Watch (0800–1200), Afternoon Watch (1200–1600), First Dog Watch (1600–1800), Last Dog Watch (1800–2000). Each watch is divided into half-hour increments marked by the ship's bell.

**39th Foot** — British infantry regiment whose soldiers served as marines aboard H.M.S. *Confiance*.

**Corbett** — Able seaman aboard H.M.S. Confiance; from the Channel draft; made the inland passage with Thomas from Chambly to Île aux Noix. Thomas's primary messmate. A man of patient economy, given to few words.

**Rowan** — Soldier of the 39th Foot serving as secondary loader at the larboard number three gun aboard Confiance. A large man, competent with a firearm but unfamiliar with naval gun-crew procedure on first drill.

**Weir** — Powder boy at the larboard number three gun aboard Confiance. Fair-haired, approximately fifteen years old.

**Hamlyn** — Able seaman, Portsmouth draft; one of Thomas's six messmates aboard Confiance. Speaks rarely.

**Fenn** — Able seaman, Portsmouth draft; one of Thomas's six messmates aboard Confiance. Speaks at meals without particular direction.

**Spry** — Able seaman, Portsmouth draft; one of Thomas's six messmates aboard Confiance.

**Curtin** — Able seaman, Portsmouth draft; member of both Thomas's gun crew (larboard number three) and his mess aboard Confiance. Initially works the handspike at the wrong angle.

**Linstock** — A length of hardwood fitted with a slow match at one end, used to fire a naval gun when the gunlock is absent or non-functional. Thomas prepares the linstock as a backup for number three's defective gunlock.

**Frizzen** — The steel plate in a flintlock mechanism against which the flint strikes to produce the spark that fires the priming charge. Part of the naval gunlock.

**Mainspring** — The primary spring in a flintlock gunlock mechanism, providing the driving force for the hammer's fall against the frizzen.

**Chambly** — Town on the Richelieu River, upstream (north) of Île aux Noix. Thomas and Corbett made the inland passage south from Chambly to join the Lake Champlain squadron at Île aux Noix.

**Slow match** — A length of treated cord that burns slowly and steadily; coiled in a bucket and kept alongside a linstock to provide fire for igniting a naval gun in the absence of a functioning gunlock.

**sash sawmill** — A water-powered sawmill using a reciprocating blade mounted in a wooden sash frame, driven by a pitman arm connected to the waterwheel. Distinguished from circular-saw and frame-saw mills. Elias Beckwith's mill on the Saranac is this type.

**pitman arm** — The connecting rod that converts the waterwheel's rotation into the up-and-down reciprocating stroke of the sash frame and blade.

**log carriage** — The wheeled platform on a horizontal track that holds the log and is advanced into the blade cut by cut.

**log dogs** — Spiked iron clamps driven into the log to hold it firmly against the log carriage during the cut.

**feed dog / feed ratchet** — The ratchet mechanism that controls the carriage's advance into the blade; releasing it (lifting the feed dog) allows the carriage to be run back for the next pass.

**kerf** — The slot or groove cut by the sawblade as it passes through the wood.

**sash bolts** — The bolts securing the lower corners of the sash frame; they work loose from the vibration of a running mill and must be checked and tightened regularly during the day.

**headgate** — The sluice gate at the millpond that, when lifted, admits water to the wheel race and sets the waterwheel in motion. Closing it stops the mill.

**Pell** — A neighbor who holds a small farm approximately half a mile down the road toward Plattsburgh village from the Beckwith sawmill. Occasional customer for dressed boards. Brings news of British movements at noon on September 3, 1814.

**Daniel Beckwith** — Asa's father. Mustered for Sackett's Harbor in 1812 (the twelfth year). Returned that autumn with a fever that has not left him; he is incapacitated by 1814. Referred to as 'the man in the chair at the end of the kitchen table.'

**leadsman** — A sailor stationed at the bow (or the chains) who casts the lead line to call water depths during navigation through uncertain or shallow water. His cadence of depth calls is background sound aboard *Confiance* during the Richelieu passage.

**Cumberland Head** — A headland on the western shore of Lake Champlain, near Plattsburgh village, where the Île aux Noix yard workers still aboard *Confiance* were to be put ashore before the squadron entered Plattsburgh Bay. Established canon: the yard workers (including the French-Canadian carpenter in the checked cap) depart the ship there.

**long twenty-four** — Period shorthand for a long-barrelled 24-pound cannon — the type of long gun carried on *Confiance*'s main deck. Thomas's station is larboard number three, a long twenty-four amidships.

**Fenn** — One of the two Portsmouth-draft seamen in Thomas's gun crew and mess, the younger of the pair (the other is Curtin). He attempted to help clear the kinked train tackle in the first open-water drill and was waved off by Thomas.

**Hazen farm** — Farm on the river road between the Beckwith sawmill and Plattsburgh village, downstream of the clay bank. Near meadow mowed twice by September; far field in late grass; farmhouse behind a windbreak of planted spruce. An invented landmark along Asa's walk route.

**Sergeant Dunbar** — Sergeant from Macomb's regulars, stationed on the Plattsburgh line. Compact, close-shaved, coat repaired at the left elbow with a lighter-cloth patch. Keeps a ledger book of militia reporting in. Establishes Asa's river knowledge as a military asset in Chapter 4. Invented character.

**Lower Bridge** — Bridge crossing the Saranac River in the middle of Plattsburgh village, on the main road to Canada. Destroyed before the battle; only cut pilings remain in the water when Asa arrives. The stone abutments on both banks still stand.

**Upper Bridge** — Bridge crossing the Saranac approximately one mile and a half upstream of the Lower Bridge. Also destroyed before the battle, taken out the same day as the Lower Bridge.

**Fort Brown** — American earthwork fortification on the bluffs above the south bank of the Saranac River at Plattsburgh village. Cannon pointed north. Constructed before the British army arrived.

**Clinton County levy** — The New York State militia levy from Clinton County, called up under Major-General Benjamin Mooers in late August/early September 1814. Asa Beckwith is a member; Pell is also a member.

**cant hook** — A wooden-handled tool with a hinged metal hook used in sawmill operations for rolling and maneuvering logs. Asa is sharpening one in the lean-to when Pell arrives with the muster notice.

**Hamlyn** — Invented character. Able seaman in Thomas Briggs's gun crew at larboard number three, H.M.S. Confiance. Wiry, quick-handed. Spent two seasons running small gunboats on the Richelieu River before posting to Confiance; his facility with machinery in motion translated to the gun crew sequence more readily than the soldiers'. Secondary sponger/loader position. First appears ch05-running-out.

**H.M.S. Revenge** — Royal Navy vessel aboard which Thomas Briggs served in 1811, returning north from Grand Harbour (Malta) after a detail with the Adriatic garrison. Part of Thomas's Mediterranean service history, 1805–1814. Referenced only in passing memory in ch05-running-out. Not part of the Lake Champlain action.

**priming wire** — Narrow iron implement, just wide enough to pass through the vent of a cannon into the powder chamber. Kept bracketed to the carriage side of the gun. Used to clear a fouled vent or broken cartridge remnant before reloading. Standard Royal Navy gun crew equipment. Procedure shown by Thomas in ch05-running-out.

**larboard number twelve** — Thomas Briggs's gun station as gun captain aboard H.M.S. Achilles at the Battle of Trafalgar, October 21, 1805. The professional benchmark against which he measures his crew aboard Confiance (his station aboard Confiance is larboard number three). Established ch05-running-out.

**Worth** — A Black farmer from near the Beekmantown road; approximately thirty years old; serving in Asa's company at Pike's Ford. Most composed drill form in the company. Dunbar positioned him at the dress end of the front rank of the two-rank formation.

**Holt** — Member of Asa's company at Pike's Ford. Veteran of the 1812 border skirmishes on the northern border. Has a scar below his left cheekbone he never mentions. Fastest at the two-rank drill — the sequence has been in his body since the twelfth year. Ran the rear rank through reloading drill under Dunbar's supervision.

**Taber** — Member of Asa's company at Pike's Ford. Fired the false-alarm shot at a deer crossing the ford at approximately 4 a.m. on the third night (approximately September 7, 1814). No harm resulted.

**Covell** — Member of Asa's company at Pike's Ford. A hunter with the patience, Asa observes, of a man who works a trap line in winter. Assigned to the far end of the watch rotation. Asa relieved him at midnight on September 7, 1814.

**Vail** — One of two Peru township farm brothers in Asa's company. Dunbar corrected his elbow position during the two-rank drill on the first afternoon at the ford.

**The rise (Pike's Ford position)** — An elevated position on the south bank approximately eight to ten feet above the ford waterline, slightly upstream of the channel mouth. Gives a clear sight line over the gravel bar's full length and commands the chest-deep channel. Established by Sergeant Dunbar as a day-watch and night-watch post on Asa's recommendation, approximately September 6, 1814.

**spring (naval)** — A line run aft from a ship's anchor cable through a port or hawsehole in the ship's quarter to the capstan. By hauling on the spring with the capstan, the ship can be rotated on its anchor to present a fresh broadside without making sail or moving from position. Macdonough rigged springs on all four principal American vessels at Plattsburgh Bay.

**Cumberland Head** — A long peninsula on the western shore of Lake Champlain that forms the northern and eastern boundary of Plattsburgh Bay. The British squadron anchored off the head (in twelve fathoms, outside the bay) on approximately September 9, 1814, before the engagement. The yard carpenters from Ile aux Noix were put ashore here.

**USS Eagle** — A brig, northernmost vessel in the American line at Plattsburgh Bay. Approximately 18 guns per lower-deck reports on Confiance. Positioned at the north end of the American line.

**USS Ticonderoga** — Sloop-rigged, flush-decked. Third vessel in the American line at Plattsburgh Bay (south of Saratoga).

**USS Preble** — Smallest of the four principal American vessels. Positioned at the south end of the American line at Plattsburgh Bay.

**Curtin** — One of Thomas's messmates, from the Portsmouth draft. Also in Thomas's gun crew at larboard number three. Reported at supper (Ch07) that Prevost's dispatch was the second pressing — that Downie had previously requested more time before engaging.

**Halsey's Corners** — A location on the road north of Plattsburgh where the British vanguard first engaged Wool's delaying force on September 6, 1814. Asa learns this name from the 29th Infantry private at the ford.

**Culver Hill** — A position on the retirement route from Beekmantown where Captain Leonard's field guns held one of their covering positions during Wool's delaying action, September 6, 1814.

**29th Infantry** — U.S. regular infantry regiment present at the Beekmantown delaying action on September 6, 1814. Fourteen of their soldiers pass through Pike's Ford that afternoon, retreating south toward Plattsburgh.

**picket fires** — Watch fires spread at intervals along a defensive line for sight lines and communication, not bunched for warmth. Asa identifies the British fires on the north bank on the evening of September 6 as picket fires by their spacing.

**mainspring vise** — A bent-iron tool with a captive clamp used to compress and hold the mainspring of a flintlock while the lock is disassembled or reassembled. One of three tools Thomas carries in his personal tool roll for gunlock maintenance.

**turnscrew** — Period term for a screwdriver. Thomas's has a worn wooden handle wrapped twice in tarred twine.

**bridle (gunlock)** — A plate within a flintlock mechanism that retains the tumbler and other components. The bridle screw can be adjusted to alter the tension and behavior of the cock's fall against the frizzen.

**linstock** — A forked wooden staff used to hold a length of slowmatch — burning cord — which is applied directly to the touch-hole to fire a cannon. Used as a backup to the flintlock ignition system; reliable under conditions (concussion, damp) that may disable a lock.

**slowmatch** — A slow-burning cord impregnated with saltpeter, used with the linstock to fire artillery. Thomas hangs a fresh length coiled over the linstock fork as a backup to the repaired gunlock.

**McGlassin, Captain** — American regular army officer who commanded the September 9, 1814 night raid across the Saranac River against the British picket line downstream of Pike's Ford. Not POV. His company returned before first light on September 10 with four British prisoners.

**breeching rope** — The heavy rope that limits the recoil of a naval gun after firing, running from the cascabel band through the cascabel ring to an eye-bolt set in the ship’s side. At larboard number three on Confiance, the breeching rope has a softening in the outer weave at the near ring by September 10, 1814.

**apeak (anchor apeak)** — Naval: an anchor brought apeak is hauled to a position directly below the hawse pipe, the cable vertical, in the moment before the anchor breaks clear of the bottom. Used in Ch13 to describe the anchor-weighing sequence as Confiance gets underway before dawn on September 11.

**mizzen peak** — The upper point of the mizzenmast's gaff, from which the ensign was flown aboard Confiance. The red ensign came down from the mizzen peak at the surrender, approximately noon on September 11, 1814.

**cartridge apron** — A leather or heavy-cloth protective apron worn by powder boys to reduce the risk of ignition when carrying powder charges from the magazine passage to the gun. Weir wears one in Ch13.

**Robertson, Lieutenant (HMS Confiance)** — Historical figure. Senior surviving officer aboard Confiance after Downie's death; gave the order to surrender. Subject of subsequent court martial for the surrender. Never named or given interior access in Thomas's chapters — Thomas does not know or identify who gave the final order.

**Solomon Marsh** — Clinton County militiaman in Asa's company at Pike's Ford. His farm is approximately two farms downstream of the Beckwith sawmill on the Saranac. Approximately four years older than Asa; his older brother is Elkanah Marsh. Wounded in the right shoulder during the third British assault on Pike's Ford, September 11, 1814. Alive at the chapter's close.

**Elkanah Marsh** — Solomon Marsh's older brother. Asa worked two seasons clearing slabwood from the millpond outlet alongside Elkanah Marsh. He does not appear directly in the narrative but is referenced by Asa's memory of the Marsh family in Ch14.

**upstream second bend** — A stretch of the Saranac River approximately 250-300 yards upstream of Pike's Ford, where the channel runs chest-deep on both sides in September low water with clay bottom and no gravel bar. Attempted twice by a British section on September 11, 1814 and failed both times. Asa's local knowledge of this stretch was the basis for correctly advising Dunbar to cover it.

**Prize tender** — A small vessel (schooner or sloop) repurposed to transport captured prisoners after a naval action. The American prize tender in Ch15 holds prisoners from HMS Confiance and the British gunboats in Plattsburgh Bay on September 12, 1814. Prisoners are paroled aboard rather than placed in irons.

**Dartmoor (prisoner-of-war context)** — British prison depot in Devon used for prisoners taken in deep-water (ocean) naval engagements. The prisoners aboard the tender debate whether Dartmoor applies to inland lake prisoners; Fenn argues it does not. Inland lake captured men were expected to be paroled to American interior towns. The book does not resolve where the Lake Champlain prisoners were actually sent.

**Pittsfield** — Town in western Massachusetts named by one of the gunboat crew prisoners as a possible inland parole destination for the British Lake Champlain prisoners. Unconfirmed in the book's timeframe.

**Burlington** — Town in Vermont named by another prisoner as a possible inland parole destination on September 12, 1814. Unconfirmed in the book's timeframe.
