# Pitch — *Two Shores*

Lake Champlain, the first week of September 1814. Two invented men — one on each side of a war neither chose — move toward the same Sunday morning from opposite shores of Plattsburgh Bay. Thomas Briggs, thirty-two, is an able seaman aboard H.M.S. *Confiance*, Captain Downie's thirty-seven-gun flagship and the largest warship ever to sail this lake — a ship he has known from the keel up was not ready. Asa Beckwith, nineteen, is a Clinton County militiaman standing at Pike's Ford on the Saranac River, three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village, watching a tree line for British regiments he can smell before he sees. They will never meet, never share a word, never know the other exists. On September 11, 1814, between half past eight in the morning and noon, Captain Downie is killed in the first fifteen minutes, *Confiance* surrenders by noon, and the British army retreats back up the road to Canada. By the evening of September 12, Thomas is alive on an American prize tender and Asa is on the south bank of the Saranac with the smoke long since cleared. The book asks neither man to understand what the day meant. It only asks what it cost.
