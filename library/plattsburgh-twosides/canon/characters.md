# Characters — *Two Shores*

## POV Characters

---

### Thomas Briggs (POV — British chapters)

**Age:** 32 (September 1814)  
**Role:** Able seaman, H.M.S. *Confiance*, British flagship, Lake Champlain  
**Status:** Invented character  
**Born:** Plymouth, Devonshire; raised in Devonport's dockyard community

**Background:** Thomas enlisted young and had been eleven years in the Royal Navy by 1814. In October 1805, at twenty-three, he was aboard H.M.S. *Achilles* at the Battle of Trafalgar — Collingwood's lee column. He served the Channel and the Mediterranean for nearly a decade after that. In early summer 1814 he was pulled from a ship of the line and reassigned inland, to the Lake Champlain squadron, arriving at Île aux Noix in time to watch *Confiance* finished and commissioned. He helped step her masts. He ran drills on her main deck with men who did not know each other's names. He has never said aloud what he thinks of the ship's readiness. He has thought it constantly.

**Family:** His wife Elizabeth lives in Devonport. She has borne a son — born after Thomas sailed — whose face he does not know. He carries one of her letters, folded and read so many times the ink is worn along the creases. He does not reread it in front of others.

**Voice and interiority:** Spare. Technical. His vocabulary is that of eleven years at sea — not jargon as decoration but precise words for real things: the forenoon watch, the larboard bow, the breeching rope, the quoin under the gun's breech. His emotions do not surface as declared states. When he is afraid he notes the wind. His inner life moves through what he does not say: a professional's habit of bearing discomfort without narrating it. His Englishness lives in word choice and sentence rhythm — never in phonetic dialect. He thinks about his wife by not thinking about her, the way a man with a sore tooth avoids a certain side of his mouth.

**Drives:**  
- A professional obligation: he will do his job correctly even on a ship he knows is wrong  
- A suppressed, unspoken grief: three years from home, a son whose face he does not know  
- An old seaman's stubbornness — he has outlasted worse ships and worse odds; Trafalgar demonstrated that possibility  

**Relationships:** No named friend aboard *Confiance* is established in the brief; the drafter may invent one or two messmates. Thomas's relationship with the ship itself is the dominant one: the man who knows from the keel up that the ship is not ready, yet stands his post.

### Asa Beckwith (POV — American chapters)

**Age:** 19 (September 1814)  
**Role:** Militiaman, Clinton County levy, under Major-General Benjamin Mooers  
**Status:** Invented character  
**Born:** Clinton County, New York, Champlain Valley

**Background:** Millwright's apprentice at his uncle's sash sawmill on the Saranac River, three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village. He has worked the river since he was old enough to be useful — knows its fords, its September currents, where the clay banks undercut and where there is firm footing on the gravel. In the last week of August 1814, the militia muster that had been rumor became orders, and he shouldered his musket and walked downstream to the line. His father Daniel Beckwith served at Sackett's Harbor and returned home with a fever that never left; Asa has been the working head of his household since he was fifteen.

**Family:** Father Daniel Beckwith, incapacitated. His mother's name is not established in the brief; the drafter may supply it. An uncle (see below) whose sawmill is Asa's whole working world before the muster.

**Voice and interiority:** Closer to the surface than Thomas — the nineteen-year-old consciousness is less armored. He thinks in the river: flow, fall, shoals, the reading of water in motion. He is not bookish but he is not stupid; he has the observational precision of a person who has spent years reading water and timber for a living. The sound of the naval cannonade from the bay enters his chapters as weather — directionless percussion, not narration. His Americanness is in idiom and tempo — the valley's vernacular, not dialect spelling.

**Drives:**  
- Obligation to what he knows: the ford, the bank, the tree line he can read better than anyone here  
- Obligation to the household at the sawmill that has been waiting for his return  
- A fear he names as caution, not cowardice — a nineteen-year-old's honesty about his own limits  
- The absence of his father: not a ghost story, just a fact about who is not here

## Historical Figures (never POV — seen from below)

These persons appear in the narrative but are **never given interior access**. They are glimpsed across a deck or a tree line, heard giving an order, seen in the consequence of their decisions. The story is told at the deck-and-trench level, not the command level.

---

**Captain George Downie** — Commanding officer, H.M.S. *Confiance*. Born January 19, 1778, Isle of Lewis, Scotland. Age 36 at death. Veteran of Camperdown (1797); commanded smaller vessels on the Great Lakes before *Confiance*. Received the Lake Champlain command on short notice and inherited a building schedule he could not alter. A careful officer pressed into impossible circumstances. Killed in the first fifteen minutes of the engagement — a gun knocked from its carriage by American fire crushed him against the quarterdeck rail; his pocket watch stopped at the moment of impact. No external wound. Buried at Plattsburgh.

**Commander Daniel Pring** — Commanding officer, H.M.S. *Linnet* (16-gun brig, 99 crew, 16 × 12-pounders). Senior British naval officer surviving the action after Downie's death. Praised Macdonough's humane treatment of prisoners in his testimony.

**Master Commandant Thomas Macdonough** — American flagship commander, U.S.S. *Saratoga* (36-gun ship, crew 212). Born December 31, 1783; age 30 at the battle. Prepared the bay anchorage with springs and kedge anchors that allowed *Saratoga* to wind 180° during the action.

**Major-General Benjamin Mooers** — Commander, Northern New York Militia. Commands the Clinton County levy that includes Asa. Large landholdings in the region (the Canadian and Nova Scotia Refugee Tract).

**Brigadier-General Alexander Macomb** — American land commander at Plattsburgh. His regulars hold the earthworks on the south bank.

**Sir George Prevost** — Governor-General of British North America; commanding general of the invasion force. His decision to retreat after the naval defeat — and the controversy surrounding it — shapes everything north of the Saranac line.

**Brigadier-General Brisbane** — Commands one of the British brigades on the Saranac approach.

## The Uncle: Elias Beckwith (minor, invented)

Asa's uncle and employer. Runs the sash sawmill on the Saranac, three miles upstream of Plattsburgh. Not a soldier; he holds the mill through the crisis. The sawmill and its routines define Asa's day-before chapter.
