# Bibliography

Sources are listed by cite-key in the order they were first used in `research.md`. Each entry notes the URL, author/publisher where known, and what the source was used for.

---

- `[cite:01]` — "Battle of Plattsburgh." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Plattsburgh. Used for: British brigade and regimental composition (Robinson, Brisbane, Power), the three Saranac crossing points, September 6–12 timeline, Prevost court martial controversy, scaling-the-guns signal, casualty figures, British retreat and desertion figures, squadron positions and battle sequence.

- `[cite:02]` — "HMS Confiance (1814)." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Confiance_(1814). Used for: ship specifications (dimensions, tonnage, armament), construction timeline at Île aux Noix, commissioning deficiencies (incomplete magazine, missing gunlocks, substandard timber), 39th Foot serving as marines, crew readiness crisis, opening broadside effect on Saratoga, Downie's death circumstances, surrender and prize aftermath, ship's final fate.

- `[cite:03]` — "HMS Achille (1798)." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Achille_(1798). Used for: ship class and guns (74-gun, Pompée class), Captain Richard King at Trafalgar, position in Collingwood's column (7th), time of first engagement (12:15 PM), ships engaged (Montañés, Argonauta, French Achille, Berwick), casualties (13 killed, 59 wounded), post-Trafalgar service.

- `[cite:04]` — "George Downie." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Downie. Used for: complete career chronology (Camperdown, Seahorse, Royalist, Montreal), birthdate and birthplace (January 19, 1778, Isle of Lewis), age at death (36), promotion dates, crew composition of Confiance ("totally unknown to each other"), exact description of Downie's death (watch stopped at moment of impact, no external wound), burial at Plattsburgh.

- `[cite:05]` — Search result compilation from multiple overlapping sources. Used for: British naval squadron total crew (~930), HMS Linnet armament and crew (16 × 12-pounders, 99 crew). Primary underlying sources: HMS Linnet (1814) Wikipedia article at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Linnet_(1814); supplemented by search results from NHHC and warandsecurity.com.

- `[cite:05a]` — "HMS Linnet (1814)." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Linnet_(1814). Used for: Linnet specifications (16-gun brig, 350 tons, 82 ft 6 in, 99 crew, 16 × 12-pounders), Commander Daniel Pring's identity and role, battle engagement against Eagle, casualty figures (10 killed, 15 wounded), post-battle incorporation into US Navy and 1825 sale.

- `[cite:06]` — "USS Saratoga (1814)." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Saratoga_(1814). Used for: ship specifications (734 tons, 143 ft, 36 ft 6 in beam, 8 × 24-pdr + 6 × 42-pdr carronade + 12 × 32-pdr carronade, crew 212), construction at Vergennes VT by Adam and Noah Brown, 35-day keel-to-launch timeline, anchor and spring arrangement, winding maneuver (bow cable cut, kedge lines hauled, ship swings 180°), post-war sale at Whitehall 1825.

- `[cite:07]` — Macdonough, Thomas, letter to Secretary of the Navy William Jones, September 11, 1814. Text preserved and quoted in multiple secondary sources. Full text at historiclakes.org/Plattsburg/macdonough_letter.html (connection refused during this research; content accessed via search result summaries at mywarof1812.com and ussconstitutionmuseum.org). Used for: Macdonough's famous opening sentence ("The Almighty has been pleased..."), description of the battered state of both squadrons' rigging, Jones's response quote.

- `[cite:08]` — "Benjamin Mooers." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Mooers. Used for: Mooers's appointment as Major General of Northern New York Militia (1811), geographic scope of his command (Franklin, Clinton, Essex, Warren, Washington, Rensselaer, Columbia counties), his landholdings in the Clinton County area (49,400 acres of the Canadian and Nova Scotia Refugee Tract).

- `[cite:09]` — Search result compilation for Pike's Ford location. Primary underlying source: Battle of Plattsburgh Wikipedia article [cite:01] and newyorkalmanack.com September 11 article. Used for: Pike's Ford distance from Macomb's defenses (3 miles / 4.8 km), and the "mile and a half above the upper bridge" figure from the New York Almanack source. URL for Almanack article: https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2012/09/battle-of-plattsburgh-countdown-to-invasion-sept-11/

- `[cite:10]` — New York History Blog, "Battle of Plattsburgh: Countdown to Invasion (Sept 6)." https://www.newyorkhistoryblog.com/2012/09/battle-of-plattsburgh-countdown-to-invasion-sept-6.html. Used for: Beekmantown engagement on September 6 — sequence of events (militia disarray, Culver Hill, Halsey's Corners), Major Wool's role with 250 regulars of the 29th Infantry, Captain Leonard's field guns, British left wing approach on the State Road, estimated casualties (45 American, 200–300 British).

- `[cite:11]` — Lake Champlain Maritime Museum, "Imagining the Common Soldier's Experience in the Battle of Plattsburgh – Part 1." https://www.lcmm.org/imagining-the-common-soldiers-experience-in-the-battle-of-plattsburgh-part-1/. Used for: Captain McGlassin's September 9 night raid, William Apess (Pequot) account, African American labor parties building forts, racial tensions in the army, Dr. James Mann's hospital records (500 sick moved to Crab Island; 30+ amputations), Simeon Doty's burial account ("dead buried in trenches without coffins"), Fort Brown's location overlooking the Saranac, clear-cutting for fields of fire. Primary sources cited in this article: Lossing's Field-Book, Dr. Mann's hospital correspondence, General Izard's letters, Plattsburgh Republican newspaper.

- `[cite:12]` — "Prevost, George." Dictionary of Canadian Biography, vol. 5. University of Toronto/Université Laval. https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/prevost_george_5E.html. Used for: Prevost's decision to attack "impatiently and precipitately," his failure to provide promised land-force support, his retreat justification (army preserved for provincial defense), the 1815 naval court martial finding placing blame on Prevost, his scheduled court martial (January 12, 1816, postponed to February 5), his death from dropsy at age 48 on January 5, 1816, one month before the hearing.

- `[cite:13]` — The Canadian Encyclopedia, "George Downie." https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/george-downie. Access returned HTTP 403 during this research; content available via search result summaries. Used for: confirmation of Downie's biography, his arrival at Île aux Noix.

- `[cite:14]` — Search result compilation for HMS Confiance construction details (search query: "Île aux Noix shipyard HMS Confiance 1814 '39th foot' marines construction crew"). Underlying sources include Parks Canada (Fort Lennox NHS) at https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/qc/lennox/culture/site/1812 and VTDigger at https://vtdigger.org/2021/09/12/then-again-shipwrights-in-vergennes-were-the-key-to-a-critical-1814-battle/. Used for: confirmation that the crew embarked as late as September 5, crew described as "totally unknown" in British post-action reports, carpenters dropped off at Cumberland Head before battle.

- `[cite:15]` — "Île aux Noix." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele_aux_Noix. Used for: Île aux Noix Naval Shipyard dates of operation (1812–1834), establishment as Royal Navy facility, HMS Confiance as largest vessel built there.

- `[cite:16]` — "Plattsburgh Bay." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plattsburgh_Bay. Used for: bay geography (western shore of Lake Champlain, northern third of the lake), Cumberland Head as northern/eastern boundary, American squadron line arrangement (~1,000 yards offshore, north-to-south: Eagle, Saratoga, Ticonderoga, Preble).

- `[cite:17]` — City of Plattsburgh LWRP (Local Waterfront Revitalization Program), 2020 Draft, Introduction section. https://cityofplattsburgh.com/sites/cityofplattsburgh.com/files/community-development/LWRP%20Comp%20Plan/2020%20Plattsburgh%20Updated%20Draft%20LWRP.html. Also: Historic Saranac Lake LocalWiki. Used for: sawmill history on the Saranac — Count de Fredenburg's 1768 mill "three miles upriver," Charles Platt's 1785 settlement party and sawmill construction, Saranac River as the economic spine of the Champlain Valley timber trade, sawmills and gristmills as primary industrial users of the Saranac.

- `[cite:18]` — "Île aux Noix." Wikipedia [see cite:15]; search result summary from Parks Canada Fort Lennox page. Used for: shipyard operational dates (1812 established, 1834 closed), construction of HMS Confiance as largest vessel built there.

- `[cite:19]` — Thomas Macdonough biography, compiled from search result summaries citing: Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Macdonough), Britannica, and the USS Constitution Museum biography. Used for: birthdate (December 31, 1783), family background (orphaned at 12), naval career milestones (midshipman 1800, Quasi-War, Barbary War, promotion to lieutenant 1807, Lake Champlain command October 1812), limited pre-1814 combat experience.

- `[cite:20]` — "Samuel Strong (Vermont politician)." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Strong_(Vermont_politician). Used for: Strong's role commanding Vermont volunteers at Plattsburgh (approximately 2,000–2,500 men), his unanimous selection at Burlington, Vermont militia's contribution to the defense.

- `[cite:21]` — William Thorpe, HMS *Minotaur*, account of the Battle of Trafalgar, 1805. Royal Museums Greenwich, NMMN accession no. rmgc-object-505823. https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-505823. Used for: existence of lower-deck Trafalgar accounts; provides evidence that ordinary seamen produced written records of the battle.

- `[cite:22]` — Search result compilation for Royal Navy lower-deck memoirs. Primary sources identified but not retrieved in full during this research: (a) Robert Hay, *Landsman Hay* [also titled *Memoirs of Robert Hay*], covering 1803–1811 service; described at https://www.lindacollison.com/memoirs-of-robert-hay/; (b) Samuel Leech, *A Voice from the Main Deck* [also *Thirty Years from Home*], 1844, available at Google Books: https://books.google.com/books/about/A_Voice_from_the_Main_Deck.html?id=tSh_AAAAMAAJ. Used for: lower-deck life as social/commercial space, messmate culture, "liberty" terminology, pressed vs. volunteered percentages, able seaman vs. ordinary seaman distinction, men serving with wives and families aboard in port.

- `[cite:23]` — Navy & Marine Living History Association, "1800 Gun Drill." https://www.navyandmarine.org/ondeck/1800gundrill.htm. Used for: complete gun crew positions (Gun Captain, Second Captain, Sponger, Loader, side tacklemen, train tacklemen, handspikemen, powder boy), crew size per gun (12 + 1 boy for a 24-pounder), complete firing command sequence in period language ("Cast Loose and Provide," "Sponge your guns," "Load Cartridge," "Ram Cartridge," "Load Round," "Ram Round," "Run Out," "Fire as your guns bear"), all period technical terms (tompion, quoin, breeching, passing box, priming wire, touchhole, handspike, train tackle, side tackle).

- `[cite:24]` — Historic Naval Fiction, "The Watch System." https://www.historicnavalfiction.com/general-hnf-info/naval-facts/the-watch-system. Used for: names and hours of all seven watches (First Watch, Middle Watch, Morning Watch, Forenoon Watch, Afternoon Watch, First Dog Watch, Last Dog Watch), two-watch division (larboard and starboard), bell system (every 30 minutes), able seaman pay (£1 13s. 6d. per month in 1815).

- `[cite:25]` — Ledyard Sawmill website, "What is a Sash Sawmill?" https://www.ledyardsawmill.org/sash-sawmills/what-is-a-sash-sawmill. Used for: core sawmill components (sash, pitman, millsaw, log carriage, log dogs, crank), the cutting cycle (cuts on downstroke; carriage advances on upstroke), waterwheel types, one-operator efficiency versus two-man pitsaw teams, "sawmill irons" as the metal components requiring a blacksmith, millwright vs. sawyer roles.

- `[cite:26]` — Ledyard Sawmill website, "The Up-Down Sawmill" exhibit page. https://www.ledyardsawmill.org/exhibits/the-up-down-sawmill. Used for: Thomas Ellicott's 1795 productivity quote ("One mill, attended by one good man...will saw more than 20 men"), sensory description ("rasping of the saw echoing across the millpond," "building vibrating in rhythm"), the 1,000 board feet per day target metric.

- `[cite:27]` — Search result compilation for American infantry drill 1814. Primary underlying sources: Scott's *Rules and Regulations for the Field Exercise and Manoeuvres of Infantry* (December 1814 / adopted February 1815), available at Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/rulesregulat00unit; and NPS article on the Battle of Chippawa (https://www.nps.gov/articles/battle-of-chippawa.htm). Used for: Scott's Buffalo camp of instruction (April 1814), the December 1814 formal codification date, two-rank firing commands (make ready, present, fire, prime and load), the French-derived tactical tradition.

- `[cite:28]` — Search result compilation for War of 1812 prisoner-of-war procedures. Primary underlying sources: Provisional Agreement for the Exchange of Prisoners, November 28, 1812, Avalon Project (https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/cartpre.asp); 1812privateers.org prisoner index (https://1812privateers.org/Great_Britain/prisoners.html); USS Constitution Museum, "British and American Naval Prisoners of War During the War of 1812" (https://ussconstitutionmuseum.org/publications/british-and-american-naval-prisoners-of-war-during-the-war-of-1812/). Used for: parole subsistence rates (officers 3s./day, others 1s. 6d./day), inland parole town system, Macdonough's humane treatment of prisoners per Pring's testimony.

- `[cite:29]` — USS Constitution Museum, "Black Sailors During the War of 1812." https://ussconstitutionmuseum.org/2020/02/21/black-sailors-during-the-war-of-1812/. Also: search result compilation from additional sources including New York State Military Museum (https://museum.dmna.ny.gov/unit-history/conflict/war-1812/black-americans-us-military-american-revolution-korean-war-war-1812) and the USS Constitution Museum downloadable publication. Used for: 15–20% Black sailor proportion in US Navy during War of 1812, 1798 ban and 1813 reversal, Battle of Lake Erie ~25% Black proportion, ~1,000 Black men identified in Dartmoor Prison records.
