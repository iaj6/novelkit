# Threads — *Two Shores*

Five threads braid the sixteen chapters. Three live in Thomas's arc, two in Asa's, and one (Thread 5) resolves across both climax chapters simultaneously. The alternating-POV structure means Thomas's threads are invisible to Asa and vice versa; the reader holds all five across the divide.

---

## Thread 1: The Unready Ship

- **Question**: Will *Confiance*'s known deficiencies — green-timber planks, a crew of strangers, defective gunlocks, carpenters still working as she sails — be the difference when the guns are run out in earnest?
- **Enters**: `01-green-timber` — Thomas corrects a soldier's grip on the rammer, notes a gunlock that won't catch cleanly, watches a carpenter caulk a plank that has checked in the sun. The ship's unreadiness is present in every specific detail, never announced.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `03-the-lake-opens` — first open-water drill; the foul train tackle; the crew who barely know the sequence; Thomas noting the headwind that will slow any approach to the bay.
  - `05-running-out` — two days of montaged drilling; the crew improving but still not the rhythm Thomas heard from *Achilles*' gun deck at Trafalgar; the Trafalgar comparison that holds the gap in professional silence.
  - `07-the-dispatch` — Thomas sees the American fleet at anchor: springs on the cables, compact line, prepared position. He does not say what he thinks. He sets down his spoon.
  - `09-riding-at-anchor` — the gunlock repaired, then the linstock kept alongside it anyway. The ship as ready as she will be. Prevost wants her readier by tomorrow.
  - `11-first-light` — the carpenters go ashore into the jolly boat; the ship becomes "only a warship"; Thomas prepares his gun with the full care of eleven years' practice on a ship he has always known was wrong.
- **Resolves in**: `13-action` — *Confiance* surrenders shortly before noon. The unreadiness was determinative: the approach delayed by headwind, the coordination with the land assault broken, the ship eventually pounded into striking her colors. Thomas's gun fired to the end. The thread closes not as vindication of Thomas's judgment but as fact.
- **What it's about underneath**: This thread is the book's argument about institutional failure experienced at the lowest level. Thomas has known since he watched *Confiance*'s keel laid that she was not ready; the knowledge has never become a sentence he says aloud; nothing he could say would change it; his professionalism is precisely the willingness to serve a ship he cannot make ready. The thread pressurizes the question of what obligation means when the institution has already failed — and answers it by showing Thomas at his gun to the last.

## Thread 2: The Unreachable Home

- **Question**: Can Thomas maintain his connection to Elizabeth and to the son whose face he has never seen across three years of separation, an inland war he did not choose, and the violence of a September morning?
- **Enters**: `01-green-timber` — the letter is in Thomas's pocket; it is not taken out; its weight is the whole of what the chapter offers. He has read it so many times the ink is worn along the folds. He does not reread it in front of others.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `03-the-lake-opens` — the lake passing on both sides; Thomas notes a civilian schooner heading north and thinks, briefly, of the passage home from Malta in 1811 — the feeling of a ship moving toward a destination that mattered. This is not that.
  - `05-running-out` — the Trafalgar memory arrives not as nostalgia but as professional calibration; the comparison to *Achilles*' working battery is also a comparison to a time when the direction of travel held Elizabeth at the end of it.
  - `09-riding-at-anchor` — an hour off watch; the letter in the pocket; he does not take it out. Her room in Devonport is a specific place he does not let himself think about for more than a moment.
  - `11-first-light` — the night before battle; the letter present but not opened; the specific room he does not think about.
  - `15-prize` — Thomas's hand goes to his pocket. The letter is there. The paper is damp. He returns it to his pocket without opening it.
- **Resolves in**: Deliberately unresolved. Thomas ends the book alive on a prize tender on Lake Champlain, no closer to Devonport than he was at *Confiance*'s keel, the son still a stranger, the letter unopened in a damp pocket. The war continues; the peace negotiations at Ghent are invisible to him; the date of his return is unknown. The thread does not fail to resolve because the author forgot — it refuses resolution because the situation does not offer one. Thomas is alive and watching a lake. That is what the day left him.
- **What it's about underneath**: This is the book's most interior thread, and it lives almost entirely in omission — in what Thomas does not say, does not open, does not let himself think. It pressurizes the question of what is kept intact through violence and distance, and it proposes that the keeping-intact is not resolution: the letter is the same letter at the end as at the beginning, the connection the same connection, the gap the same gap. The thread matters because the book refuses to make captivity into a reunion fantasy or a grief performance; Thomas is simply still in it.

## Thread 3: The Household Man

- **Question**: Can Asa carry both the obligation he walked downstream to fulfill — the militia duty, the ford, the company depending on his knowledge of the ground — and the obligation that was waiting for him before the war: the incapacitated father, the mill upstream, the household he has run since he was fifteen?
- **Enters**: `02-the-sash` — a full working day at the mill; the father's incapacity is texture, not scene; Asa is the skilled man here, reading timber the way he reads water, doing the work that keeps the household going. The muster is rumor at the end of the day. Nothing has changed yet.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `04-downstream` — Asa walks away from the mill. He does not dramatize the departure; he shoulders his musket and goes. The mill recedes behind him on the river road.
  - `06-the-ford` — Asa holds his expertise against the regular sergeant's professional skepticism; his knowledge of the ford is his credential, won from years of milling on the same river. The millwright's apprentice asserting what he knows is the household discipline in military form.
  - `08-beekmantown` — the British are across the water; Asa is committed; the mill is three miles upstream and receding further. Both obligations now in tension.
  - `10-the-night-crossing` — Asa holds the ford while the raid goes out without him. He stays at his post. The household discipline holds in a military situation that rewards it.
  - `14-pikes-ford` — Asa stands three assaults; a man beside him he has known since childhood; his company still on the bank. He holds because he knows the ford, because he was drilled, because he is the kind of person who stays at a job.
- **Resolves in**: `16-the-south-bank` — the afternoon of September 12; the retreat road emptying to the north; Asa sitting on the south bank of the Saranac, thinking — without saying — about the mill three miles upstream, the pitman arm, the log carriage, the smell of fresh pine. He does not announce that he will go back. But both obligations survived the nine days. The mill is still there. He is still here. The thread resolves in what Asa allows himself to think about now that the day is over.
- **What it's about underneath**: The household-man thread is about the weight that precedes war — the fact that Asa was already carrying something before the muster orders arrived — and about whether that weight is a burden or the source of his steadiness at the ford. The thread proposes that the same qualities that made him the man of the house at fifteen (precision, patience, the willingness to stay at the job) are what hold the ford. The resolution is quiet because the thread was always quiet: Asa goes back to where he was needed before the war needed him.

## Thread 4: Knowledge of Ground as Weapon

- **Question**: Will a millwright's apprentice's intimate knowledge of a specific stretch of river — the gravel bar's depth gradient, the chest-deep channel between bar and south bank, the clay bottom that pulls at boots — actually be enough to hold a ford against Peninsular War veterans?
- **Enters**: `02-the-sash` — in the afternoon, Asa goes to clear a slabwood from the millpond and is at the river; the ford is visible from there, its shallows and channel readable at a glance. He has crossed it hundreds of times. This is not a portent. It's just the ford he knows.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `04-downstream` — the ruined Lower and Upper Bridges; the ford three miles upstream is now the only crossing not destroyed; the ground Asa knows has become the ground that matters.
  - `06-the-ford` — Asa teaches the sergeant: the gravel bar gives firm footing fifty yards from the north bank; the channel on the south side runs chest-deep over clay; a column pressing all the way across will lose its footing at full extension. The sergeant walks the position. He doesn't say Asa is right. He puts a man there on watch.
  - `08-beekmantown` — British soldiers arrive on the north bank. The ford is no longer hypothetical.
  - `10-the-night-crossing` — four days of standoff; the ford held; the knowledge untested but Asa watching the north bank steadily, reading what's there the way he reads water in motion.
  - `12-both-banks` — the night probe: soldiers enter the ford from the north, reach the gravel bar, and pull back. The sergeant holds fire. The channel has not been found yet. In the dark before dawn, Asa hears the British force assembling on the north bank — far more than before.
- **Resolves in**: `14-pikes-ford` — the channel breaks both main crossing attempts. The British regulars advance across the gravel bar in formation and hit the chest-deep current over clay; the column breaks; they pull back. A second attempt upstream fails the same way — Asa tells the sergeant the bend is worse; the sergeant sends men to cover it anyway; the channel holds them. The ford is defended exactly as Asa said it would be, by the water. What Asa knew turned out to be enough.
- **What it's about underneath**: This thread is the book's argument that local knowledge is a form of force — that a nineteen-year-old's ten years of fording the same river constitutes a kind of expertise no amount of European campaigning provides. The thread does not make Asa heroic; it makes him correct. The Peninsular veterans are not defeated because they are inferior soldiers; they are defeated because the river does not care about their credentials. The thread pressurizes the question of what counts as preparation for violence — and gives a quiet, hydraulic answer.

## Thread 5: Prevost's Clock

- **Question**: When Sir George Prevost's repeated dispatches force *Confiance* into action on a timeline the ship cannot meet — before the crew is ready, before the gunlocks are all true, while the carpenters are still aboard — will the forced coordination between naval and land assault determine the battle's outcome?
- **Enters**: `07-the-dispatch` — a dispatch boat from Prevost's shore headquarters comes alongside. The letter is taken aft; Downie receives it; by evening the word has moved through the ship in the way it always moves on a lower deck: there is a date, there is a plan, the two forces are to move together. Thomas sets down his spoon and finishes his meal.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `09-riding-at-anchor` — a second dispatch. The word: the army is ready and wants the fleet to move. The date was supposed to be yesterday. Now it is the day after tomorrow. Or possibly tomorrow. The uncertainty is consistent: Prevost wants action; the ship is not yet the ship he wants. Thomas fixes one gunlock. He keeps the linstock.
  - `11-first-light` — the carpenters go ashore. The date is no longer a piece of paper in the captain's cabin; it is a fact moving through the ship. Thomas on deck after dinner: the word has passed. The squadron moves at first light.
- **Resolves in**: `13-action` and `14-pikes-ford` — the forced timeline plays out on both sides simultaneously. *Confiance* approaches the bay against a headwind that delays her into a bad tactical position; the coordinated timing with Brisbane's land assault breaks down; she is pounded at anchor for four hours and strikes her colors before noon. In `14-pikes-ford`, the British assault at Pike's Ford — the land side of Prevost's plan — fails at the channel; when the news of the naval defeat reaches the north bank, the army begins to withdraw. Asa watches the British soldiers on the road moving north. He does not know why. The reader does.
- **What it's about underneath**: The Prevost's Clock thread is the book's argument from above — or rather, the argument that reaches down from above and lands on Thomas and on the men at the ford without either of them knowing it. Prevost's dispatches are never seen by the reader; they pass through Downie and arrive on the lower deck as rumor. What matters is the pressure they generate and the outcome it shapes. The thread pressurizes the question of what command decisions cost at the deck level: the men who set the clock are not the men who stand at the gun when it runs out.
