## Compared sample against brief anchors

**Exemplars checked:** Shaara (*The Killer Angels*), O'Brian (*Master and Commander*), Frazier (*Cold Mountain*) — with O'Brian as the primary anchor for this Thomas chapter.

**Opening convention:** Guidance requires Thomas chapters to open on the ship and the work, O'Brian-style — "in the middle of something already happening." The sample opens with "The forenoon watch began with the smell of new timber." Exact compliance. The first paragraph anchors in physical sensation aboard *Confiance*, deploys naval vocabulary without gloss (forenoon watch, ropewalk, gun tackles), and withholds Thomas's unspoken assessment of the ship's readiness while making it fully present through the inventory of deficiencies. This is O'Brian cadence executed correctly.

**Sentence length — Thomas target 14–20 words average:** The sample's sentence lengths span a wide range (from 2-word fragments like "The grip." and "The tompion came out." to compound-appositive sentences in the 38–49-word range). Rough average across 22 sentences lands around 18–20 words, which is within or just at the top of the specified Thomas window. The short declaratives in the drill sequence ("He watched the rammer." "The drill finished in reasonable time." "The grip.") balance the longer introductory sentences. The distribution feels correct for the register.

**Dialogue density:** One line: "'Cast loose and provide.'" — unexplained, operational, in stride. Precisely what the guidance specified.

**Free indirect discourse:** Present and correctly executed. Thomas's professional assessment of the crew's inadequacy arrives entirely through what he *does not say*: "Thomas did not say that it would not have finished in reasonable time under fire." His interior renders through action and attention ("He did not look at Rowan directly when Rowan took up the rammer. He watched the rammer."), not through declared cognition.

**Register check against Shaara:** Like Shaara's chapter-opening interiority, the sample is filtered entirely through Thomas's professional consciousness — we see the crew through his expert eye, not through a narrator's overview. The prose has no authorial thumb. Like Shaara, neither approbation nor condemnation is stated; professional assessment does all the work.

## What the sample does well

- **Naval vocabulary in stride, no italics, no glossing.** Forenoon watch, tompion, breeching rope, quoin, ropewalk, larboard — all used as a working seaman uses them. The reader is trusted to follow from context. This is the O'Brian technique executed cleanly.
- **Interior rendered through omission, not announcement.** "He did not say" is the structural vessel for Thomas's suppressed assessment throughout the closing paragraph, and the technique is earned rather than mechanical. The tautological formulation ("nine men who had run the sequence four times together were nine men who had run the sequence four times together") is a quietly excellent piece of professional understatement.
- **Ending on image without gloss.** "Overhead, from somewhere above the hatch, came the sound of a mallet." The mallet — a construction sound, indexing *Confiance*'s unfinished state — is left to do its work alone. No second sentence explains it. This is exactly what the anti-default against image-glossing requires.
- **Character voice audible from the first sentence.** A reader handed this paragraph mid-book would identify Thomas within two sentences, via the vocabulary, the professional precision of the observation, and the dry register. The guidance's distinguishability test is clearly passed.
- **No foreshadowing in a day-before chapter.** The ship is ordinary. The drill is ordinary. The mallet overhead is ordinary. The prose does not italicize any detail for weight. The brief's constraint is honored.

## What the sample drifts on

- **Several sentences in the crew-introduction paragraph exceed the 35-word audit threshold.** Sentences 5, 6, and 7 (introducing the assembled crew, Corbett, and Rowan) run 38, 44, and 46 words respectively — all above the stated maximum. These are O'Brian-style appositive enumeration ("the man from the Channel draft, the one who had made the inland passage with Thomas from Chambly; the others were men the Île aux Noix establishment had scraped together from various stations and drafts, some willing and some not") and structurally they work for the register. But the cluster of three above-threshold sentences in a row should be flagged as a drift pattern to watch across chapters: if character introductions consistently produce sentence clusters in the 38–48 word range, the average will creep above the Thomas target. This is minor — a single paragraph — not a register failure.
- **Sentence 3 (*Confiance* inventory)** runs approximately 49 words as a post-colon list. The enumeration technique is exactly right for this kind of ship-condition inventory, but the raw word count should be noted.

Neither drift item breaks the register or the POV strategy. They are patterning items for the drafter to monitor in subsequent chapters.
