# Editor — Pacing Pass
## Macro arc assessment

### Overall shape

*Two Shores* has a clean formal skeleton: eight Thomas chapters and eight Asa chapters in strict alternation, bracketed by matching day-before and day-after pairs, with a paired climax at the axis. The thread system is tight — all sixteen chapters advance at least one thread, and the ending-mode distribution is deliberately varied (four literary fades, five resolved beats, six mid-action cuts, one structural turn). The design is architecturally sound.

The arc divides roughly as:
- **Setup (Chs 1–4):** ~25% of the book
- **Escalation / approach-and-standoff (Chs 5–10):** ~37.5%
- **Turn + pre-axis (Chs 11–12):** ~12.5%
- **Climax (Chs 13–14):** ~12.5%
- **Coda (Chs 15–16):** ~12.5%

The single structural concern the design cannot hide: the escalation phase is the longest third of the book, and it is also the zone where both characters are most passive. Neither Thomas nor Asa takes meaningful external action in Chs 5–10; they drill, wait, hold their positions, and watch others act (Beekmantown, McGlassin's raid). This is the book's midbook sag zone, and the question is whether the internal pressure on the characters genuinely rises across these chapters — or whether they repeat the same emotional note (professional suppression, patient vigilance) with new surface details.

### Midbook sag

The structural turn (Ch 11) arrives at chapter 11 of 16 — a late 69% mark. The "waiting" middle occupies the widest band in the book. The external circumstances do escalate across Chs 5–10 (Prevost's first dispatch in Ch 7; British pickets visible in Ch 8; second dispatch in Ch 9; McGlassin's raid in Ch 10), so the design is not static on the surface. The risk is felt-pressure: if Thomas's interiority in Ch 9 is emotionally indistinguishable from Ch 5 — both chapters show him drilling, noting the gap between *Confiance* and *Achilles*, keeping the letter in his pocket — the thread advances on paper but the reader experiences stasis. The per-chapter passes should test Chs 5, 9, and 10 specifically for whether each successive occurrence of the waiting-beat adds new internal pressure or merely restates the established one.

### Repeated beats

Thread 2 (Unreachable Home) uses the same gesture in five chapters: the letter is in Thomas's pocket and is not opened. The story-arc log shows this in Chs 1, 3 (implied, via Malta memory), 5, 9, 11, and 15. Six occurrences across eight Thomas chapters. If each appearance reads identically — the hand to the pocket, the decision not to open it — this will feel like a tic rather than an accumulating thread. The final appearance in Ch 15 needs to carry a qualitatively different weight than the appearances in Chs 1 and 5. The per-chapter pass on Ch 15 should check this.

The craft log also records `did not say that` and `said nothing` as recurring constructions in Ch 1. If these run through all Thomas chapters (as the professional-suppression register invites), they risk becoming the book's most visible stylistic tic. This is a voice-pass issue primarily, but it has pacing consequences: a reader who starts predicting Thomas's suppressions before they arrive will feel the middle chapters as slower than they are.

### Consecutive climax chapters and the dramatic-irony problem

Ch 13 (Thomas) ends **Resolved** — *Confiance* strikes her colors around noon. Ch 14 (Asa) follows immediately. The reader enters Asa's climax already knowing the naval result, and the British withdrawal is therefore telegraphed before Asa experiences it. Thread 4 (Knowledge of Ground) resolves on the hydraulic reality of the channel, not on suspense about the outcome — which is the right design choice — but Ch 14's ending mode is logged as **Mid-action**, which is puzzling if the British withdrawal is visible by the chapter's close. The per-chapter pass on Ch 14 should verify whether the "mid-action" ending actually earns its label or whether the chapter lands closer to resolved once the British begin to retreat.

### Ch 12 and the single-thread problem

Ch 12 advances only Thread 4 at 2,800 words. As the final chapter before the paired climax, it carries significant structural weight. Its story-arc content is strong (midnight probe at the gravel bar; British force massing by first light), but it shares its basic texture with Ch 10: Asa watching the ford at night. The differentiation is real — probe contact and the revelation of assembled force are new — but if the prose registers of Chs 10 and 12 are too similar (same darkness, same vigil, same river sounds), the book's penultimate escalation beat will feel like another extension of the standoff rather than a final tightening. The per-chapter pass on Ch 12 should check whether it is sufficiently distinct from Ch 10 in felt register.

### Climax / coda balance

At 4,000 words each, the climax chapters are the book's longest. The coda is 2,000 words each — half the climax chapter length, matching the day-before chapters. The symmetry (day-before = day-after in length) is structurally elegant and consistent with the brief's emphasis on ordinary continuity. The coda does not feel oversized relative to the brief's constraints. No concern here.

### Summary of items flagged for per-chapter passes

1. **Ch 5 and Ch 9 (Thomas escalation chapters):** Test whether internal pressure genuinely rises across these chapters or whether both land on the same emotional note.
2. **Ch 10 and Ch 12 (Asa standoff/pre-climax):** Test whether Ch 12 is sufficiently distinct in register from Ch 10 to earn its pre-climax position.
3. **Ch 14 (Asa climax):** Verify that the "mid-action" ending label is earned, and assess whether the dramatic irony from Ch 13's resolution undercuts the ford's tension.
4. **Ch 15 (Thomas coda):** Check whether the final letter-in-pocket beat carries qualitatively different weight from the same beat in Chs 1 and 9.
5. **Cross-chapter stylistic tic (Thomas chapters):** Monitor `did not say / said nothing` for frequency across the full arc — a voice-pass primary concern with pacing consequences.


## 03-the-lake-opens

After the summary sentence, these steps restated what "clean" already communicated; they added checklist weight between a meaningful character note (Rowan at the right shoulder without correction) and a meaningful callback (Curtin on the train tackle without a tangle). **Larger issue — Thread 2 beat absent from the chapter:** `threads.md` credits Ch 3 with advancing Thread 2 (Unreachable Home) via Thomas noticing a civilian schooner heading north and thinking briefly of the passage home from Malta in 1811 — "the feeling of a ship moving toward a destination that mattered. This is not that." This beat does not appear in the draft. The closest substitute is the departure list (Plymouth 1803, Minorca 1807, Île aux Noix 1814), which reads as professional self-cataloguing rather than as an emotional register of home-direction. Thread 2's arc across Chs 1, 3, 5, 9, 11, 15 loses a stepping stone: the reader goes from the letter-in-pocket of Ch 1 directly to the Trafalgar/Achilles comparison of Ch 5 without the intermediate emotional calibration this chapter was designed to provide. The fix is an addition (probably a single paragraph in the transit section, where a boat passing north gives Thomas one moment of "that is the direction this ship is not going"), which is outside the pacing pass's scope but must be addressed before the chapter is finalized.

## 11-first-light.md

**Small fix applied: mess section compressed.**

The chapter's four strong sections (carpenters-gone opening, Downie inspection, gun preparation, deck/hammock close) were separated by a fifth section — the mess dinner — that created a speed-bump lull without delivering new thread content. The original seven-sentence mess section opened with crew banter (Corbett's biscuit joke, Hamlyn receiving it "at the correct interval," Fenn examining both halves of his biscuit, Spry's low remark), introduced four crew names without individualizing them, and then delivered its actual payload — Thomas's suppression and "the quietest of the three" — in two sentences at the end. **Macro arc concern about Ch 9's ending mode: resolved.**

The differentiation is real and sufficient: (a) Thomas goes *below* — the chapter ends in the hammock, not at the rail; (b) the Thread 2 beat escalates — Elizabeth's room is rendered more specifically here (east-facing, the morning light on the floorboards, before the rest of the house had woken) than in Ch 9's version, before "Then he stopped," suggesting he permitted slightly more before pulling back — a genuine internal escalation; (c) the final note — "The wind in the stays was southwest. The accumulation of the three Thomas closes (Ch 5: Trafalgar comparison; Ch 9: moon-watch; Ch 11: hammock in the dark) reads as a deliberate, appropriately tightening arc rather than repetition.

**Chapter otherwise clean.** The carpenters-gone opening is the chapter's best writing. The Downie inspection is precise and efficient. The convergence of all three threads (Thread 2 letter, Thread 1 breeching rope, Thread 5 word through the ship) in the deck section is the chapter's structural climax and is well-paced. The hammock ending is new ground. No thread-drift, no repeated emotional beat with Ch 9 after the fix.

## 12-both-banks.md

**Small fix applied: bar-and-channel restatement cut from Section 5.**

The chapter's probe section (Section 3) already establishes the ford's tactical geometry in motion: *"knee-deep now… solid limestone, firm footing… Another twenty yards of bar remained on their south side before the gravel ended… The channel lay there: five feet of water over clay, thirty yards of it between the bar's south edge and the south bank."* Section 5 — the beat of strategic recognition between probe and dawn — then opened with two sentences restating the same geometry in summarizing form: *"The bar: fifty yards of limestone gravel from the north bank to its south edge… The channel: thirty yards from the bar's south edge to the south bank, five feet of water over a clay bottom…"* These two sentences were cut. **Chapter otherwise clean.** The single-thread focus (Thread 4 only) is appropriate and noted in `threads.md`. The probe sequence earns its length — each phase (distant movement → approach stops → water entry → midbar → stillness → retreat) is a distinct beat without checklist quality. The millpond-intake metaphor (reading aggregate sounds the way he reads the headgate in high water) is the chapter's most precise Thread 4 move and arrives at exactly the right structural moment after the probe. The structural triple-look is preserved; the reader carries the specific detail (Rowan's arm, Weir's cartridge) from the silence section; the final paragraph's "the men had held their sequence" has its named antecedent without the repeated portrait. **Chapter otherwise clean.** The Weir section ("Every other fact had changed") is the chapter's emotional high point and earns its weight. The Downie section is correctly brief. The montage device ("Third cycle, or the fourth... Fifth or sixth...") compresses the battle's middle hours without checklist quality.
