Notes from per-chapter review. Chapters with no entry below had no continuity issues.

## 07-the-dispatch

- **Issue:** American gunboat count in the chapter (~12) diverges from canon.
- **Conflict:** Canon fact #18 specifies "ten gunboats" in the American squadron. Chapter 07 has Thomas count "twelve of them, or close to it; the southern end of the line was partly obscured by the *Ticonderoga*'s hull and he could not be certain of the last." The explicit uncertainty language in the prose covers Thomas's imperfect observation and no fix is needed in the text itself. However, the ch07 continuity log (logs/continuity.md, entry ~2026-05-28T18:35:42.366Z) recorded "approximately twelve gunboats" as if it were a settled fact about the American fleet. Future chapters should use ten (canon) rather than twelve if the gunboat count needs to be cited as narrator fact rather than character estimate.
- **Suggested resolution:** No text change required. Drafters of subsequent chapters should treat the American gunboat count as ten (canon fact #18), not twelve. If the ch07 log entry causes downstream confusion, note that Thomas's count was explicitly uncertain and the authoritative figure is ten.

- **Issue:** "Forty-six ships" for the Combined Fleet at Trafalgar is historically inaccurate.
- **Conflict:** The chapter states, in the narrator's voice, that the Combined Fleet at Trafalgar was "stretched across two miles of horizon — forty-six ships." No canon or log fact specifies this number, so this is not a canon continuity violation. However, the historical Combined Fleet (French and Spanish) comprised 33 ships of the line plus approximately 8 frigates and brigs — approximately 41 total vessels; no count reaches 46. The statement is presented as authorial fact rather than Thomas's uncertain count. In a novel where period accuracy underlies the prose's credibility, this is a material error.
- **Suggested resolution:** This requires a historical accuracy review rather than a continuity repair. The correct count for the Combined Fleet's ships of the line is 33; total vessels including frigates and brigs approximately 41. The passage could read "thirty-three" (ships of the line only) or "forty-one or more" (all vessels). A separate historical-accuracy pass should determine the intended meaning and correct the figure.

## 09-riding-at-anchor

- **Issue:** Elizabeth's letter dated "May" and characterized twice as "four months old," conflicting with the established late-June arrival date.
- **Conflict:** `logs/continuity.md` (entry 2026-05-28T21:14:57.054Z, flagged as "established in Ch15") records: *"The letter from Elizabeth reached Thomas in the packet at Île aux Noix in late June 1814."* The chapter contained three errors arising from the wrong month: (1) "He had read it last in May" — impossible if the letter arrived in late June; (2) "as he had carried it for four months" — late June to September 10 is approximately two months, not four; (3) "a single line of a letter four months old" — same arithmetic conflict.
- **Fix applied:** All three instances corrected in the draft: "in May" → "in late June"; both "four months" → "two months". No other prose was altered.

## 15-prize

- **Issue:** Second direct Trafalgar comparison contradicts the ch13 continuity-log constraint.
- **Conflict:** The ch13 continuity log (2026-05-28T19:43:51.557Z) explicitly records "Thomas's Trafalgar comparison (first and only direct use in the book) arrived mid-action as professional calibration…" — establishing a one-use-only constraint. Chapter 15 contains a second direct, named invocation: *"He had seen Trafalgar's aftermath from the lower deck of Achilles, the damaged ships on both sides lying in a confused and rolling sea, the French prizes being worked across the water in a rising gale."* This is a full narrative Trafalgar comparison by name, with aftermath detail — clearly a second direct use.
- **Suggested resolution:** Either (a) remove the Trafalgar-named comparison in ch15 and replace it with an unmarked reference to "a battle's aftermath he had seen once before" (preserving the contrast without naming it), or (b) revise the ch13 Trafalgar moment so that it is the professional-calibration use only, and ch15's is the retrospective / elegiac use — establishing two differently-purposed uses rather than one. A human drafter must decide which moment carries the weight.
