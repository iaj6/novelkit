# Plotter guidance — The Speaking Image

## POV count and rotation

This book has no POV characters in the traditional sense. It is a compiled file — a dossier of 30 sections. The plotter designs a **chapter map** specifying for each section:
1. Dominant register group (A/B/C/D and sub-mode)
2. Which documents appear (type, issuer, date, title if any)
3. Which crack (if any) is planted or completed
4. Current ingestion count and what was newly confirmed
5. Launch clock position (T-minus or count-up)
6. New foreclosure this chapter enacts, and which prior chapter it closes

Three anchor voices provide reader continuity: apparatus (A, compiler, all chapters), Reyes (C, recurring), Sefton (B, recurring). Rotation: roughly B → C → D with A throughout, at plotter's discretion within the fixed act boundaries. No more than 3 consecutive chapters in the same dominant register group.

## Chapter-shape preferences

Chapters are dossier shapes, not dramatic scenes. Preferred shapes:

- **Dossier (dominant):** Multiple documents from one date range, unified by the compiler's apparatus. The most common shape. 3–5 documents per chapter typically.
- **Single-document-center:** One long document (a Sefton script, a long Reyes log, the press release) framed by apparatus and supplemented by supporting documents to reach the 1,900-word floor.
- **Parallel-documents:** Two or more documents illuminating the same event from different registers (the "Not yet" incident in §19–§20 seen through incident ticket + pamphlet + forum thread).
- **Escalation-stack:** A series of documents whose register shifts as the chapter progresses, ending in a register gap that shows what the event meant across communities.

No chapter is pure interiority. No chapter contains free-standing prose dialogue between characters. All chapters are document assemblages.

## Ending-mode distribution

Specify the ending document for each chapter in the chapter map. Avoid runs of three or more chapters with the same ending mode.

Target across §1–§29 (§30 is structural, not classified):
- **Cliffhanger / hard-cut** (log entry breaking off, forum thread with no reply yet, §4 Casperson break): 8–10 chapters
- **Document-end** (press release last line, incident ticket resolution field, script outro): 8–10 chapters
- **Literary-fade** (Sefton private note, short apparatus footnote, Magellan output's last addressed line): 5–8 chapters

Deliberate distribution: Act I skews toward hard-cut and document-end (urgency of the intake); Act II mixes (the procedural middle has more institutional document-ends); Act III escalates toward the last three structural endings (§28 log-end, §29 script-outro-wrong, §30 structural join).

## Chapter target words

- **Floor:** 1,900 words. Hard gate. Every chapter must clear it.
- **Target range:** 1,900–2,400 words. Some chapters (§18, §30) may be longer by necessity.
- **Planning assumption:** Every chapter needs 3–5 documents to clear the floor. Single-document chapters will be under floor. The plotter's chapter map should note the minimum document count per chapter.
- **Total target:** ~62,000 words across 30 chapters.
- **Under-writing reflex:** The documentary format is at particular risk because transcripts and tickets run short. The chapter map must plan for full dossiers, not single-document sections.

## Genre-specific structural conventions

**The two compounding ladders — mandatory in the chapter map row for every chapter §3–§29:**

Column 1: `ingestion_count` — what is the count now, and what was newly confirmed in this chapter.
Column 2: `clock_position` — T-minus [X] (pre-§18) or Day [N], users [N] (post-§18).
Column 3: `new_foreclosure` — what became impossible in this chapter; which prior chapter it closes.

**The foreclosure logic — no re-measurements:**

Each chapter's new foreclosure must be a new foreclosure — not a re-confirmation of something already established. When the chapter map is complete, each Act III row should cite a specific §X as the chapter it is closing. If two adjacent rows cite the same prior chapter as foreclosed, one of them is a re-measurement and must be revised.

**The register ladder:**

Documents get shorter and more urgent as the book progresses. Act I: longest individual documents (full memos, extended Casperson reports, long Sefton scripts). Act II: medium-length documents. Act III: shorter, more fragmented (private notes, truncated threads, partial logs). The compression is in the documents; the apparatus stays level throughout.

**The chorus arc:**

The chorus (D3) appears at least once per act. Comic register in Act I (§5 forum, §7 script audience reactions); accurate in Act III (§20 pamphlet is Act II's most accurate document; the pattern continues). The plotter must place at least one D3 chapter per act and must increase the chorus's accuracy without changing its register or condescending to it.

**The join (§30 structural requirement):**

§30 has no chapter header with a date range, no apparatus, no headnotes, no section header beyond "§30." It is the only chapter in the book that carries none of these. The plotter must flag §30 in the chapter map as apparatus-free. The plotter must ensure that by §28 all five cracks are planted or completed, and that `fragment-hermopolis` and `preface` are already registered as records.

## The anti-sag spine (mid-book §10–§17)

The highest sag-risk zone is §10–§17 — the procedural middle of Act II, after the fragment has printed and before the launch. Every chapter in this zone must have:
- A new ingestion count confirmation (specific material named)
- A new T-minus milestone or institutional development (not just "launch is approaching")
- A new foreclosure or capability-loss event (however small)
- A register that has not dominated the previous two chapters

The escalation ladder replaces the interiority that a prose novel would use to carry the midbook. Without the ladder, these chapters are documents in a row. With it, they are a tightening vice.
