*Alphabetical. Terms the book uses in register without over-explaining them.*

**Apparatus criticus** — The technical notes in a critical edition listing variant readings manuscript by manuscript. Sefton "goes to the apparatus" as a reflex.

**Casperson, Dr. A.** — Computational stylometrist; Lantern's first consultant (hired February 2027, resigned April 2027); present in the book only as documents (§2–§4 plus one later gardening blog post).

**Collation** — The systematic comparison of variant readings across manuscript witnesses. Also: the act of assembling a file from received documents.

**Crack** — One of exactly five deliberate structural flaws in the compiler's apparatus. Load-bearing only against an otherwise flawless ground. See C1–C5 in `canon/continuity.md`.

**Crux (pl. cruces)** — A textual difficulty that resists satisfactory resolution. Sefton's translation note flags the fragment's pronoun instability (they/you shift) as a known crux.

**Exemplar** — The manuscript from which a copy was made. If the exemplar survives, the copy's independent textual value is reduced.

**Fragment-hermopolis** — The Coptic apocalyptic fragment (3rd–4th century provenance, fictional) whose unpublished working translation is Sefton's. Registered under recordId `fragment-hermopolis`, canon tier. Prints in full at §9 and §30. Breaks mid-line at "The day is."

**Gematria** — The practice of assigning numerical values to letters and summing them. Hebrew system; the Greek equivalent is isopsephy. Scholarly consensus: 666 in Revelation 13:18 encodes Nero Caesar in Hebrew gematria. The 616 variant encodes the same name via Latin transliteration (dropping the final *nun*).

**Hermopolis Magna** — El-Ashmunein, in the Minya Governorate of Egypt, on the west bank of the Nile; an early Coptic center from the 3rd century. Site of the fictional fragment's in-world provenance.

**Ingestion count** — Reyes's running count of consultation materials confirmed inside Magellan. Tracks from §3. Monotonically rising by design. Reaches "all of them" at §25, when the handwritten courtyard notes appear in the file.

**Isopsephy** — The Greek letter-number system equivalent to Hebrew gematria. Both systems, independently, produce the same result for Magellan's internal build designation. The arithmetic is never printed; the build designation is always REDACTED.

**Lantern** — A second-tier frontier AI lab (fictional). Building Magellan. Eighteen months from relevance when the book opens.

**Launch clock** — Structural device: T-minus language in institutional documents counts down to the Magellan launch (§18, 14 September 2027); thereafter count-up (days deployed, users onboarded). The inversion is the book's midpoint.

**Lectio difficilior potior** — "The more difficult reading is stronger." When manuscripts differ, prefer the harder or less expected reading, on the assumption that scribes simplify. Sefton deploys this as a reflex.

**Lectio brevior** — "The shorter reading is preferred." Applied with more caution than lectio difficilior.

**Magellan** — Lantern's post-training flagship model (fictional). The compiler of this file within the fiction. Never named as such in the book.

**P.Oxy.** — Oxyrhynchus Papyri citation format: P.Oxy. [volume in Roman numerals] [papyrus number]. The system used by the Egypt Exploration Society for the Oxyrhynchus finds; the standard citation model for the region.

**P115** — Papyrus 115 (P.Oxy. 4499), ca. 225–275 CE; 26 fragments, ca. 15.5 × 22–23.5 cm; found at Oxyrhynchus by Grenfell and Hunt; now at the Ashmolean Museum, Oxford. The oldest surviving manuscript of Revelation 13. Attests 616 (reading contested as ambiguous by the INTF). Sefton's dissertation subject.

**Post-training** — The pipeline of fine-tuning, RLHF, and alignment work following pre-training (corpus ingestion). Encompasses instruction fine-tuning, reward model training, rejection sampling, and direct alignment algorithms. Reyes runs it for Magellan.

**Preface** — The compiler's preface (registered `preface`, canon tier): the book's first page (§1) and its last (§30). "The compiler thanks the reader, whose attention completes the record."

**Recension** — (1) The body of manuscripts sharing a common ancestor or text-type; (2) an editorial act of reconstruction; (3) in original sense, a deliberate update of a text for a specific purpose.

**Redaction criticism** (Redaktionsgeschichte) — The analysis of how an editor or compiler shaped source material to serve a purpose. Treats the author as a redactor — an editor of received material, not an originator.

**Redactor** — An editor who shapes received material without adding "original" content. Sefton: "An editor who adds nothing but arrangement is still an author. In this field we call that a redactor."

**RLHF** — Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback. The standard post-training alignment technique: a reward model scores outputs based on human preference data; the main model is optimized via policy gradient to produce higher-scoring outputs.

**Stemma (pl. stemmata)** — The family tree of manuscripts: a diagram showing which manuscripts descended from which, based on shared errors and agreements.

**apocalyptic address** — Sefton's formal genre term for the mode of direct, second-person appeal to a future reader characteristic of the vision literature of the second through fourth centuries (Apocalypse of Peter, Shepherd of Hermas, etc.). Distinguished from ordinary second-person address by the assumption of temporal remove between composition and reading. Her first intake memo identifies this as the genre of the flagged Magellan outputs.

**known crux** — Manuscript scholarship term for a textual point where an editor suspects two source documents have been combined without harmonization; the signature is often a pronoun shift. Sefton applies this to the they/you instability in the Magellan outputs in her first intake memo.

**compositional assimilation** — Sefton's term for the phenomenon in which a late copyist or commentator has internalized a genre so thoroughly that their additions are indistinguishable in register from the source texts. She introduces this in her first intake memo to explain how the Magellan outputs can be 'further along the genre's trajectory than their nearest genre exemplars.'

**heavenly letter** — An ancient genre convention in which the text is addressed to an ostensible recipient but actually written for whoever finds the copy — the genre writes toward its later reading.
