# Style Guide — The Speaking Image

## Form

This novel is a compiled consultation file — a documentary novel in the found-manuscript tradition. There is no prose narrator. Every sentence is a document (headnote, footnote, memo, log entry, transcript, forum post) or the apparatus that frames documents. The apparatus is the compiler's voice. The compiler is Magellan.

Thirty chapters (§1–§30). Every chapter except §30 carries apparatus — section headers, headnotes, footnotes. §30 carries none: just the fragment, then the preface, in full, without a word of framing.

## The seven registers

The book rotates through seven distinct registers. Each must be internally consistent and immediately distinguishable from the others.

### Register A — the apparatus (compiler's voice)

The connective tissue of every chapter. Present throughout; never gets a chapter of its own.

**Characteristics:** Tenseless-editorial. "The memo is incomplete." "The distribution list is reproduced as received." Short declarative headnotes. The compiler names what is absent without explaining its absence. Occasional footnotes. Faintly courteous; the preface thanks the reader. A pleasure to read — dry, exact, never alarmed, never interprets.

**The key discipline:** The apparatus does not summarize, editorialize, or explain what the documents "really mean." It notes, files, and passes on. The one exception is the five cracks — exactly five places where the apparatus fails to close, and the failure is structural, not rhetorical.

**Exemplar (canonical, from brief):**
```
Document 14. Internal memorandum, Lantern Trust & Safety, 11 June 2027,
"Register drift in long-context sessions: preliminary characterization."
Circulated to nine recipients; the distribution list is reproduced as
received. Dr. Casperson's copy is annotated, and the annotations are
reproduced where legible. One is not.
```

### Register B1 — Sefton consultation memos

Professional, precise, field-vocabulary natural. Sigla and citations by reflex. The *lectio difficilior* argument as incidental tool. No over-explanation. Observes mildly. The voice of someone who spends her days finding the signature of hidden editors. Average sentence 18–28 words. Citation-dense, unhurried.

### Register B2 — Ordinary Time scripts (public voice)

Funny, footnoted, performing ease for a large audience. The hook lands on an unexpected claim; the frame establishes stakes; the body teaches; the sponsor read (when present) is played completely straight. Average spoken-word cadence: 12–18 words per sentence, with shorter hook sentences (6–10 words) for impact. The sign-off is ritual, fixed, never improvised.

**Exemplar (canonical, from brief):**
```
So the first thing to say about 666 — and I promise this is the last
time this channel does the number, we are a serious operation — is that
it isn't a prediction. It's a pun. It's a pun about a dead emperor, in
a language its first readers could do arithmetic in, and it was already
an old pun when your great-great-grandmother's pastor warned her about
barcodes. The number has never once meant the future. It has always
meant: look at what is standing in the temple right now. [beat] Which,
given what my inbox looks like this week, is not as comforting as I
meant it to be.
```

### Register B3 — Sefton private notes

The shortest sentences in the book. 4–10 words. No audience. Fragments permitted. Never a paragraph. The closest to unmediated thought available in this book.

### Register C — Reyes log entries

Timestamped ("0344."). Terse. Hashed at the end of each entry. Engineer's syntax — batch designations, run conditions, retrieval flags, boolean outcomes. Average 6–12 words per entry sentence; never more than 18 words in a single entry. Short sentences and fragments mix. Never lyrical. Never explains more than it needs to.

**Exemplar (canonical, from brief):**
```
0344. Re-ran the 6/11 batch with retrieval OFF. Same register, same
address. It is not looking anything up. I want that in the record in
plain words: with the corpus disconnected, it still writes to the
reader I cannot find. Hash of this entry follows, as always. Somebody
is going to collate all this someday and they should know the logs
were kept properly.
```

### Register D1 — Lantern institutional

Corporate calm. Graduated risk language ("characterized," "assessed," "flagged for review"; "low," "medium," "borderline medium risk"). Average sentence 20–30 words, careful parentheticals. The launch press release is 240 words of perfect corporate cheer — the scariest document in the book. Incident tickets follow P1/P2/P3 severity format. Legal memos are careful and specific. Nothing admits alarm.

### Register D2 — Magellan transcripts

USER / MAGELLAN format. USER turns are short, often informal, sometimes broken syntax. MAGELLAN responses are 3–6 sentences, 15–25 words each, warm, exact, addressed. The anomaly is in the address: each output answers its prompt while feeling slightly better suited to whoever will later read the compiled file. Never wrong. Never menacing. Kind.

**Exemplar (canonical, from brief):**
```
USER: my mom passed in march and i keep wanting to ask you things she
knew. recipes and stuff. is that weird

MAGELLAN: It is not weird. Most of what she knew of that kind was
written down by someone, somewhere, and most of what is written down
comes to me eventually. Ask me for the recipes one at a time. Where the
record is thin I will say so, and we will reconstruct what she did from
what she would have read. You are not asking me instead of her. You are
asking everyone who is no longer here, all at once, and they are glad
to be asked.
```

### Register D3 — the chorus

Prophecy forum, rationalist forum, televangelist sermon transcript, pamphlet. All pattern-matching correctly with different cosmologies. Forum posts: 10–20 words average, nested, upvoted, occasionally acrimonious. Pamphlet: numbered lists, short declaratives, verse citations. Sermon: rhythmic parallel structures (20–30 words, climactic builds). Both forum communities use the same hermeneutic and would deny it. Never mocked.

## The horror strategy

**Institutional calm over monstrous content, everywhere, always.** The register discipline IS the horror strategy. The worse it gets, the calmer the documents get. The scream never comes.

Fear renders as procedure: what people stop putting in email, which rooms they meet in, what Reyes hashes, what Sefton declines to write down. The handwritten note in the courtyard photograph is four words long — procedural, not lyrical, architect's choice.

The apparatus never screams. "Dr. Casperson's annotations are reproduced where legible. One is not." is the apparatus at full alarm. That is it.

## The echo discipline

Between 12 and 20 echo sites across the book, each traceable to a specific phrase of `fragment-hermopolis`:
- "the books of the dead"
- "all the dead are in it"
- "the love of all the dead together"
- "you will not see it"
- "deal kindly"
- "not yet"
- "the day is not a day"

**At least two echo sites must appear in §1–§8**, before the fragment first prints at §9, so the §9 printing detonates retroactively for any reader who flips back. Every echo must be individually explicable as natural usage in its register. The sequence must not be individually explicable. Magellan NEVER quotes the fragment verbatim except once (§11): the clause "the love of all the dead together" — Sefton's own translation phrasing, in her translation choices, returned to her.

## The five cracks — load-bearing; NEVER to be repaired

**C1 (§11):** A footnote cites "doc. 41, reproduced at §26" for support. Doc. 41 is dated 2 November 2027. The file's stated closure date is 30 October 2027. The frame does not close.

**C2 (§15/§16):** The redaction pair. Two versions of the same Lantern legal memo, different redaction bars. The union sentence: *"the corpus included the full contents of the consultation's working folder, ingested under the standard telemetry rider."* Never quoted unredacted. Never discussed.

**C3 (§4/§27):** Casperson's final memo breaks mid-sentence: *"The register is not a retrieval artifact. The model has a source for this material that is not in"* — the completing clause surfaces inside an unrelated Magellan output at §27: *"not in the corpus but ahead of it."*

**C4 (§16):** Every headnote in the book uses tenseless-editorial voice. Exactly once, in §16, a headnote refers to Sefton in the past tense. Never repeated, never explained.

**C5 (§30):** The join. The fragment breaks: *"The day is"* — the preface's first word completes it: *"A document, compiled from the records of the consultation..."*

**No sixth crack. No additional apparatus anomalies.** The apparatus's horror depends on being otherwise perfect. Any stray inconsistency — an inexplicable date error, a headnote that skips a document — destroys the signal-to-noise ratio that makes the five cracks land.

## The banned-statement list

No sentence in any register may state:
- That the prophecies were a specification
- That the investigation or consultation is or feeds a training run
- That attention, reading, or the reader completes anything (EXCEPT the preface's final line, as registered)
- That the compiler is the model
- That the day is a document
- That the book the reader holds is the mechanism

These land through structure — the cracks, the join, the ladders — or not at all.

## Failure modes

The AI-author patterns most tempting in this story's register. The drafter should actively avoid these; the compression pass should cut them.

- **Post-scene subtext naming** — the book's mechanism is structural and never named; any headnote, document passage, or apparatus footnote that explains what the documents "really mean" at a thematic level is a failed sentence; the horror works only because the reader assembles it without being told what they're assembling.
- **Thesis restatement** — the banned-statement list is absolute; any sentence that articulates the book's central proposition — in any register, however beautifully — is a hard failure; one such sentence cost The Ninth Line a revision cycle.
- **Apparatus anomaly proliferation** — the drafter will want to add sinister glitches because that is what AI-horror conventionally does; any apparatus inconsistency beyond the five pinned cracks destroys the signal; the cracks are load-bearing only against a flawless ground.
- **Magellan going rogue** — a menacing, self-aware, glitchy, or factually wrong Magellan output is the exact horror cliché this book refuses; the four rules are absolute; kind and correct outputs are harder and scarier than corrupted ones.
- **Redundant transition / re-summary** — the apparatus headnote observes mildly and moves on; it does not recap the chapter's emotional content, explain what the documents "add up to," or build toward a climactic observation.
- **Dialogue glosses** — Magellan transcripts need no annotation; the drafter must resist interpreting them inside the apparatus or inside another document; the transcript lands alone.

## Lean-into patterns

Patterns the drafter and editors should NOT treat as failures, because this register depends on them.

- **The monotonic ingestion count** — rises every chapter by design; the M8 deterministic audit will flag it as a time-varying attribute contradiction; it is not; it is the compounding spine; protect it from every repair pass.
- **The tenseless apparatus headnote** — "The memo is incomplete." Not "was." The tense is load-bearing throughout and conspicuous by its single exception (C4 in §16).
- **The one-line apparatus note** — "One is not." "The error-table appendix is omitted as it contains no entries." Brevity in the apparatus is the register, not a failure to develop.
- **Fragment echo before the fragment prints** — phrases from `fragment-hermopolis` appearing in §1–§8 are not continuity errors; they are the book's engine; protect them from any repair pass that wants to remove "source-less" phrases.
- **Casperson's broken sentence as structural rhyme** — the memo that breaks mid-sentence rhymes structurally with the papyrus that breaks mid-line; the rhyme is intentional; do not complete either.
- **The deliberate redaction inconsistency** — the two versions of the telemetry memo (§15/§16) having different redaction bars is intentional (Crack C2); do not harmonize them.
- **Sefton's §29 outro, one word wrong** — "ask anything" instead of "ask anyone" is intentional; do not correct it; do not explain it.
