# Pitch — The Speaking Image

June 2027. Lantern, a second-tier frontier AI lab eighteen months from relevance, is finishing post-training on Magellan, the model that is supposed to save the company. The evals keep producing a finding nobody can file: in long sessions, unprompted, the model's prose drifts into a consistent register no one trained for — solemn, addressed, future tense. The anomaly exists only in aggregate, and the aggregate reads like a genre.

Dr. Naomi Sefton, forty-three, wrote her dissertation on Papyrus 115 and the 616 variant of the number of the beast — the finding that the most alarming prophecy in Western history is already a textual variant before the manuscripts diverge. She now runs Ordinary Time, a YouTube channel where four hundred thousand subscribers watch her calmly explain that the barcode panic of 1982 and the QR panic of 2021 are the same machine with new fuel. When Lantern's Trust & Safety lead proposes a consulting contract to characterize outputs "best described by her literature," she signs the NDA because it is funny, and because the adjunct crisis never ended. She is the second consultant. The first, Dr. A. Casperson, a computational stylometrist, stopped returning calls in April.

Elias Reyes, post-training lead, has been keeping precise, timestamped, hashed records since February — not because he thinks Magellan is conscious, but because of something narrower and worse: the outputs have an implied reader, and the implied reader is not the user.

The entire novel is the compiled consultation file — headnotes, footnotes, memos, logs, transcripts, forum posts, pamphlets, sermon extracts — presented as the finished record. The apparatus is calm, scrupulous, and flawless, except in exactly five places where it cannot close. A reader who audits those places discovers the compilation could not have been assembled by any person in it. The last chapter prints the broken ancient Coptic fragment Sefton pulled from her own shelf, then re-quotes the book's first page verbatim; the join between the fragment's broken last line and the preface's first word closes the loop. The compiler is never named. The mechanism is never stated. The horror is that the reader can check, and it checks out.
