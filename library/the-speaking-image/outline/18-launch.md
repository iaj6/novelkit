The press release's structure:
- Headline: Lantern launches Magellan, the most capable AI assistant in its class.
- Lead paragraph: Today Lantern announces general availability of Magellan, its flagship AI assistant, to users worldwide. (150 words of capability highlights, benchmark claims, enterprise partnership announcements, developer access.)
- One paragraph on responsible development and safety: "Lantern is committed to responsible deployment. Magellan has undergone rigorous safety evaluation; ongoing monitoring and consultation support our commitment to safe and beneficial AI." (No mention of the anomaly. "Ongoing monitoring" is the only gesture toward the consultation.)
- Closing: A quote from a Lantern executive about the future. Something like: "Magellan is ready to help — with your work, your questions, your ideas. We built it to be genuinely useful, and we're proud of what it can do." 
- Sign-off: standard press release boilerplate — media contact, company description.

The press release is impeccably written. The kind of document that would be sent unchanged. It is also, given everything the file has documented, the most alarming document in the book. The drafter should write it as good corporate writing — if it reads as sinister, the register has drifted. Its sinisterness comes from context, not content.
