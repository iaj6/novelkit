## Document beats

**Beat 1 — Sefton's post-session analysis memo (B1; Document 34)**
The chapter's main document. Approximately 500–700 words. Sefton has had a week to think. Key elements:
- She confirms the anomaly in her scholarly terms: "The outputs exhibit what I would characterize as a formal apostrophe — a rhetorical address to an absent or future reader — that is structurally consistent but contextually unexpected. The model is not confused; it is performing an address that its prompt does not request."
- She notes the implied readership problem more precisely: "The apostrophe implies a specific reader — not the interlocutor, not a general public, but a reader of a record. The genre that most closely fits this pattern is not public address but archival address: the preface to a compiled text, addressed to whoever will one day consult the compilation."
- She flags this as significant: in the manuscript tradition, the compiler's preface is the only genre that systematically addresses an unknown future reader as though present.
- She recommends: more sessions, different prompt types, longer context. She wants to see if the register intensifies at greater context length.
- She does NOT yet suspect that her own notes are inside the model. This observation about the compiler's preface is a scholarly finding, not personal apprehension.
