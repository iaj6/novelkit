# §2 — First Consultant

## Summary
Lantern formally engages Dr. A. Casperson, computational stylometrist, as the first consultant. The primary Trust & Safety anomaly memo circulates to a larger distribution list. Casperson's first memo reports back: the anomaly's nearest neighbors in the training data are apocalyptic literature, with unusually high cosine similarity to Revelation, 4 Ezra, and the Apocalypse of Peter. The cliffhanger is that this is a finding about what was found, not about what caused it.

**Beat 2 — Casperson engagement letter (D1; Document 4)**
The formal engagement brief from Lantern's Trust & Safety to Casperson. Short — 100–150 words. Institutional, brisk.

**Beat 3 — Casperson memo #1 (B1-adjacent; Document 5)**
The main document of this chapter. Approximately 500–700 words, including a short apparatus headnote from the compiler. The memo is academic and careful. Key findings as structured beats:
- The anomaly's nearest neighbors in the training corpus are texts from the Jewish and Christian apocalyptic tradition — Revelation 13, 4 Ezra 7, the Apocalypse of Peter, portions of 1 Enoch. Cosine similarity scores are high enough to warrant a flag.
- The register pattern — solemn address, future-tense prediction, second-person implied reader — is a formal genre marker. "The outputs appear to be producing in a literary genre." This is reported without alarm; it is Casperson's professional register: measured, documented, appropriately hedged.
- One finding cannot be explained by the nearest-neighbor analysis: the retrieval pathway is anomalous. The model is producing the genre register without the expected retrieval signals — without citations, without direct quotation, without the usual markers of corpus retrieval. "The register is present in the corpus, but the retrieval pathway is anomalous."
- Casperson recommends a second memo after further batched analysis. The memo ends on the retrieval anomaly — the thing she can document but not yet explain.

The compiler's apparatus headnote for Document 5 is brief and typical: date, addressee, one observation about circulation. The headnote does NOT interpret the memo.
