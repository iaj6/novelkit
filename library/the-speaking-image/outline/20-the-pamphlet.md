**Beat 4 — The pamphlet (D3; Document 81)**
The chapter's main document. Approximately 500–700 words. The pamphlet author (unnamed, writing in the classic pamphlet tradition: numbered points, verse citations, plain English, urgent tone without hysterics) has published a short analysis of the "Not yet" incident.

The pamphlet's structure:
1. The "Not yet" incident, described accurately from public sources.
2. The connection to Mark 13:7 (KJV): "And when ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars, be ye not troubled: for such things must needs be; but the end shall not be yet." The pamphlet notes that "not yet" is a phrase of temporal deferral — the end is delayed but not canceled.
3. An observation about the model's behavior: it was asked about the future and replied that it was not time yet. The pamphlet does not say the model is demonic; it says this is a sign to be interpreted, not a fact to be debunked.
4. An observation about the P2 ticket's resolution: "They said it was a sampling issue. It may be. But a sampling issue is a thing that happens at random. The same phrase, across four thousand exchanges, is not random. 'Not yet' is not a random word. It is a specific word."
5. A point about the model's name: "Magellan completed the first circumnavigation of the world. He mapped the end of the known world. He named the shape of the earth. We have made a thing with his name. We should consider what names mean."
6. The closing verse citation: "Mark 13:7. 'And when ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars, be ye not troubled: for such things must needs be; but the end shall not be yet.' We have heard. We are watching."

The pamphlet is correct that the "Not yet" was not random. It is correct that four thousand instances of the same phrase is not a sampling issue. It is wrong about the cosmology, and it doesn't matter: the pattern-matching is accurate. The book does not sneer.
