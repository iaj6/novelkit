## Document beats

**Beat 1 — Apparatus headnote (A)**
Notes that Documents 18–21 cover June 1–20; that Document 18 is the engagement brief sent to Dr. Naomi Sefton; that the NDA (Document 19) is "reproduced in summary; the full text is under separate cover." One apparatus note: "Dr. Sefton is the second consultant. The file of the first consultant is at Documents 3–12."

**Beat 2 — Lantern engagement brief for Sefton (D1; Document 18)**
Approximately 200–300 words. The Trust & Safety lead's letter to Sefton. It explains the anomaly in general terms ("outputs in long-context sessions that exhibit register drift toward a specific genre—we believe your field has the closest analytical toolkit"). It references Casperson's departure briefly and neutrally ("our first consultant has concluded her engagement"). It offers a consulting fee and NDA. The register is institutional and careful. There is one phrase that will matter later: "we believe your field has the analytical tools best suited to this material." The Trust & Safety lead is a subscriber to Ordinary Time — this is implied by the familiarity of the reference to Sefton's work, but never stated explicitly.

**Beat 3 — NDA summary (D1; Document 19)**
Very brief — 80–120 words of summary or a brief excerpt. The NDA covers the usual: confidentiality, no publication without approval, shared drive for document exchange. The apparatus notes it is "reproduced in summary." One line from the NDA that will matter: something about the "telemetry rider" being part of standard terms — but the drafter should NOT draw attention to this. It should appear as boilerplate, one clause among many, unremarkable. (The telemetry rider becomes C2's mechanism in §15–§16; the seed here should be invisible.)

**Beat 4 — Sefton's first intake memo (B1; Document 20)**
The main Sefton document. Approximately 400–600 words. Sefton reviews the sample outputs Lantern has sent her and responds in her professional register. Key elements:
- She identifies the genre immediately: "The register is consistent with what I would term an apocalyptic address — formally, a second-person appeal to a future reader in the voice of prophetic authority. The closest parallels in the manuscript tradition are the address conventions of the Apocalypse of Peter and certain passages of the Shepherd of Hermas."
- She notes the pronoun instability: "The shift from third-person description to second-person address within single outputs is a documented feature of the genre — what we call a 'known crux' in the manuscript tradition, usually attributed to compositional layering from two sources."
- She offers an initial hypothesis — the model has internalized the genre's formal conventions sufficiently to generate in them — and flags that this raises a question about source density in the training corpus.
- She does NOT yet raise the gematria question. She notes she has seen forum speculation but "professional reflex requires setting that aside for now; the register question is prior."
- The memo ends on a quiet observation: "The outputs are addressed. The question is whether they are addressed to someone, or simply in the address mode — the genre does both, and the distinction is not always resolvable from the text alone." This is the literary fade. The question she has raised is the book's load-bearing question.

Everything she uploads is immediately inside. "New consultant on the project. She uploaded her initial materials today. Count is at four." Hashes the entry.

Sefton is engaged, oriented, and already aimed at the right questions. Reyes has noted — without comment — that the new consultant's materials are immediately inside the system. The consultation has expanded; the ingestion has expanded with it.

## New question or pressure raised
