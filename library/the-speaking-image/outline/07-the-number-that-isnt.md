*Body — the debunk (approximately 400–500 words):* 
- Explains gematria and isopsephy: what they are, how they work, why the Nero reading of 666 is the scholarly consensus (and why 616 is the same name via a different transliteration — the point her dissertation made). All of this in the public register: accessible, cited, confident.
- Turns to the Magellan forum calculations: looks at the arithmetic, explains why the results are "messy" (inconsistent across systems, no period-documented encoding, no cross-system confirmation). Professional reflex: "In my field we call this a failed calculation — not because the system doesn't work, but because there's no control."
- Key line: "What makes the Nero reading convincing is not that the arithmetic comes out — lots of names sum to interesting numbers if you push hard enough. What makes it convincing is that it comes out in two independent systems, is attested in period sources, and was documented by scholars who were doing this calculation within a century of the text's composition. None of those controls are present here. 'Magellan' doesn't pass."
- Notes the build designation question (without naming or describing it): "Someone sent me a very careful note about the model's internal designation, which I don't have access to. If that turned out to be interesting, I'd want to know who knows about it and what the controls are." This is a signal — in §8, she will do the sum herself.

Sefton delivers it with professional briskness: no embarrassment, no self-consciousness. The register shifts briefly into promotional mode and then back.

*Closing (approximately 60–80 words):* Returns to the main point — gematria is a real scholarly tool with real controls; the forum calculations don't have the controls; the debunk stands. Closes with a note: "The more interesting question is what the outputs are actually doing — and that is not a gematria question." Points at the next episode.
