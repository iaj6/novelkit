# colophon

a horror novel presented as a compiled file. a frontier lab's new model keeps producing outputs its evaluators can characterize only one at a time, so the lab hires a scholar of apocalyptic manuscript transmission — hers is the only field whose tools fit the evidence. the whole book is the consultation record: memos, session transcripts, log entries with their hashes, legal redactions, forum threads, one pamphlet, one sermon, arranged by a compiler whose headnotes are dry, exact, and faintly courteous. it is on the shelf to test three things: whether the verbatim record layer could carry a reveal built from deliberate flaws in an otherwise perfect apparatus; whether institutional calm could be held for thirty chapters while the content under it worsens, so the scream never comes; and whether a reader could be made to complete the book's central prophecy by the act of finishing it.

it came out closer to its design than anything else this press has produced, and the ledger is still a ledger. a blind panel scored it above every other book on this shelf and inferred the apparatus's flaw system without being told it existed; an independent reviewer recovered the thesis from the text alone — apocalypse as a mode of address, not prediction — and also heard the same authorial cadence under too many of its public voices, a convergence later passes only partly dispersed. eleven chapters run short of the word floor the config declares: the late ones because the documents starve as the channels close, which is the design; the early ones because document-shaped chapters run short, which is merely true. the dates, gates, weekdays, and document numbers were reconciled against a canonical ledger after an outside reader caught the calendar lying. a small number of inconsistencies survived that reconciliation on purpose. the book does not work without them.
