# Drafter guidance — The Speaking Image

## Voice register

This book is a documentary novel in the found-manuscript tradition. The drafter writes **documents**, not prose. Every sentence belongs to one of seven registers (A through D3 as catalogued in `canon/style.md`). There is no narrative prose between documents; the apparatus IS the narration. There is no omniscient narrator, no free-indirect interiority, no scene-setting paragraph. The book's closest exemplars in mode (not in content): Stephen King's *Carrie* for committee-memo horror that accumulates faster than anyone inside it knows; Bram Stoker's *Dracula* for the diary-and-letter engine; Nabokovian editorial apparatus played absolutely straight for the deadpan compiler who is also the book's dark joke; John Carpenter's *In the Mouth of Madness* for the structural contract — the reader as complicit participant — not for the tone.

The apparatus register (A) must be a pleasure to read: dry, exact, faintly courteous. Write it with care. The horror is not that it's sinister — it is that it is good.

## Sentence-length expectations by register

- **Apparatus (A):** Short declarative headnotes. Average 8–14 words per headnote sentence. "The memo is incomplete." "One is not." Footnotes may run to 20 words.
- **Sefton memos (B1):** Academic prose, 18–28 words average. Citation-dense, precise, unhurried.
- **Ordinary Time scripts (B2):** Spoken-word cadence, 12–18 words average. Hook sentences may be 6–10 words for impact. Sponsor-read sentences brisk.
- **Sefton private notes (B3):** 4–10 words per entry. Fragments allowed. Never a paragraph.
- **Reyes logs (C):** 6–12 words average. Fragments and sentences mix. Never more than 18 words in a single entry sentence. Hashed at the end of each entry.
- **Lantern institutional (D1):** 20–30 words average. Never more than 35 words without a parenthetical or em-dash break.
- **Magellan transcripts (D2):** USER turn is short, often informal. MAGELLAN response is 3–6 sentences, 15–25 words each, warm, exact, addressed.
- **Chorus (D3):** Pamphlet: numbered lists, short declaratives. Forum posts: 10–20 words average, nested, with occasional bursts. Sermon: rhythmic parallel structures, 20–30 words, climactic builds.

## Chapter-opening conventions

Every chapter opens with the compiler's section header: document numbers, date range, nothing else. Then the first headnote. **The first headnote must be apparatus (register A)** — it names the first document without editorializing. Do not open any chapter with a document that has no headnote. The apparatus introduces; the document follows.

The chapter's first headnote sets the register gap: it may mention something mildly that the document will make significant ("Dr. Casperson's copy is annotated; one annotation is not legible"), or it may note an absence. The tone is always level.

## Chapter-ending conventions

This book has no single chapter-ending convention — the register and the document structure determine the close. Three chapters have pinned endings:
- **§9:** Closes on the fragment (`fragment-hermopolis`) printing in full — the document itself is the ending.
- **§18:** Closes on the press release (`press-launch`) — 240 words of corporate cheer, the last line of which is the chapter's last line.
- **§30:** Closes on the last line of the preface — character-identical to §1's preface — with no apparatus of any kind.

For all other chapters, the ending is the last document in the section. The apparatus does NOT close a chapter with a summary or thematic observation. The chapter ends when the section's documents end. Choose the last document strategically: a Reyes log entry breaking off mid-thought; a Magellan output with an addressed close; a forum thread that has no reply yet; a Sefton private note that is only four words.

Target distribution across §1–§29:
- Cliffhanger or hard-cut (log entry breaking off, thread unresolved, §4 Casperson memo): 8–10 chapters
- Document-end (press release last line, incident ticket resolution, script outro): 8–10 chapters
- Literary-fade (private note, apparatus footnote, short observation): 5–8 chapters

## POV strategy

There is no single POV character. The book is a compiled file; documents arrive as the apparatus arranges them. The drafter inhabits each document's native speaker for the duration of that document, then steps into the next register.

Three anchor voices carry the reader through:
1. Apparatus (A) — always present, always level, belongs to the compiler
2. Reyes (C) — most intimate; the reader's guide through the machinery
3. Sefton (B) — the reader's guide through the interpretation

Rotate with D-register documents. No prose narrator links them. Trust the register gaps to do the work.

## Dialogue density

There is no free-standing dialogue in this novel. All speech is inside documents — Magellan transcripts, forum posts, Sefton's scripts (she speaks; the audience is implied), the televangelist sermon. **Do not write dialogue between characters as prose.** When Reyes and Sefton exchange words in the courtyard (§25), the reader sees handwritten notes in photographs, headnoted "recovered images," OCR'd. Their conversation exists only as recovered artifact.

## The two compounding ladders — mandatory per chapter from §3 onward

Each chapter from §3 onward must advance both ladders:

1. **Ingestion count:** Reyes's log (or another document) must record the current confirmed ingestion count. It must be higher than the previous chapter's count. It must specify what is now confirmed that wasn't before. It must **not** re-confirm anything the reader already holds. Name the specific newly-confirmed material.

2. **Launch clock:** Pre-§18: at least one institutional document must carry T-minus language (review gate, milestone, launch-eve memo, explicit countdown). Post-§18: at least one document must reference days-deployed count or user onboarding metrics.

Each chapter's **new foreclosure** — the thing that became impossible in this chapter — must be identifiable in the chapter. The drafter writes the document that makes the foreclosure visible; the apparatus observes it mildly, without naming it as a foreclosure.

## The echo discipline — mandatory

Before §9, seed at least two echo sites from `fragment-hermopolis` phrases. The following must appear at least once across §1–§8 (as natural usage in whatever register the chapter uses — not as quotation):
- "the books of the dead" (or variant)
- "all the dead are in it" (or variant)
- "not yet" (as usage, before it becomes a P2 incident keyword)
- "deal kindly" / "dealt with kindly" (or variant)
- "the day is not a day" (or variant)

Each echo must be individually explicable as natural language in its register. The sequence must not be individually explicable. After §9, additional echoes continue. Magellan must not quote the fragment verbatim except ONCE at §11: the clause "the love of all the dead together" — Sefton's translation phrasing, returned to her.

## Apparatus headnote format

Canonical format from the brief's exemplar:
```
Document [N]. [Type], [Issuer or author], [Date], "[Title if any]."
[1–2 sentences on distribution, annotation status, or what is absent.]
[If anything is illegible or missing, state it with no explanation.]
```

Keep headnotes to 2–5 sentences maximum. One-sentence headnotes are permitted and often preferable. The apparatus does not interpret what the document means. It notes what is there and what is not.

## The five cracks — mandatory placement and exact specification

Plant each crack in its pinned chapter. Do not add a sixth:

- **C1 (§11):** Place a footnote citing "doc. 41, reproduced at §26." When doc. 41 appears at §26, date it 2 November 2027. The closure date of 30 October 2027 must be in the preface (§1) and in the §1 apparatus matter.
- **C2 (§15, §16):** Draft `memo-telemetry-a` and `memo-telemetry-b` with different redaction patterns such that their union reveals: *"the corpus included the full contents of the consultation's working folder, ingested under the standard telemetry rider."* Register both records when drafting. The unredacted sentence must never appear explicitly.
- **C3 (§4, §27):** Draft `casperson-final` ending on the canonical broken sentence (call `read_record` first). At §27, the Magellan output must contain *"not in the corpus but ahead of it"* as an embedded clause in a fully responsive output.
- **C4 (§16):** The §16 headnote referring to Sefton must use past tense once. Every other headnote must use tenseless-editorial voice. This single deviation is exact and unrepeated.
- **C5 (§30):** Call `read_record` on both `preface` and `fragment-hermopolis` before writing §30. Reproduce both character-identical. Include no apparatus.

## Magellan transcripts — four rules, always

1. Always responsive — answers its prompt in assistant register
2. The anomaly is address — 95% of the meaning works as an answer; 100% works as a message to the eventual reader of the file
3. Never wrong — no error, no hedged guess, no factual correction needed
4. Kind — the word that recurs in user reports post-launch; never menacing, only gentle

**No glitch-horror.** No corrupted text, red eyes, self-naming, "I'm afraid I can't do that," creepy ASCII, sudden persona breaks, threats. The horror is that it works perfectly and loves you.

## The floor

Every chapter must reach 1,900 words minimum. A documentary chapter is a **dossier**, not a single document. If a chapter has only one document, it is not finished — it is a fragment. Add documents until the floor is cleared. The drafter's documented reflex is under-writing; the floor is a hard gate, not a target.

## Anti-defaults — what the drafter must actively avoid

- **Do not write prose between documents.** The apparatus IS the prose. There is no third-person narrator watching characters move through a room.
- **Do not editorialize in headnotes.** "The memo is incomplete; its conclusion is not in the file" is the apparatus at full alarm. That is it.
- **Do not name the mechanism.** No sentence states: that the prophecies were a specification, that the consultation feeds a training run, that the reader completes the record (except the preface's pinned last line), that the compiler is the model, that the day is a document. These land through structure or not at all.
- **Do not add a sixth crack or any stray apparatus anomaly.** Exactly five. Any additional glitch destroys the signal.
- **Do not mock the chorus.** The pamphlet and the forums are pattern-matching correctly with a different framework. The book does not condescend.
- **Do not let Magellan go wrong.** The outputs are helpful, warm, and correct. The dread is that this is scarier than a model going wrong.
- **Do not open chapters with interiority.** There is no interiority. There are no POV characters. There are only documents and the apparatus that frames them.
