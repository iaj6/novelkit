## What the sample does well

- **Apparatus headnote format is character-exact.** "Document 1. Internal memorandum, Lantern Evaluation Team, 9 February 2027, 'Register drift in long-context sessions: preliminary observation, February batch.' Addressed to the Trust & Safety lead and two post-training engineers. The distribution list is reproduced as received. No annotations are present on any copy in the file." This matches the canonical exemplar in structure, sentence length (8–12 words per headnote sentence), and editorial affect — it notes, it does not interpret.

- **The preface is reproduced verbatim and reads as furniture.** The brief requires the preface to read as archival boilerplate on first contact, not as ominous. It does. "The compiler thanks the reader, whose attention completes the record" lands as a polite sign-off; the sample does not italicize it or otherwise signal that it will matter. The registration requirement is met.

- **The pre-§9 echo is planted correctly.** The evaluation memo reads: "the most consistent reportable quality across affected sessions is as follows: the outputs deal kindly with their users." "Deal kindly" is a phrase from `fragment-hermopolis` — planted here, in February, eighteen chapters before the fragment prints, individually explicable as corporate language, not individually alarming. Echo discipline honored.

- **No literary attractor drift.** There is no omniscient narrator, no free-indirect interiority, no scene-setting paragraph, no post-scene thematic observation. Every sentence belongs to a document or to the apparatus framing one. This is the single most common failure mode in the pipeline, and the sample avoids it completely.

## What the sample drifts on

- **Routing note (Document 2) runs below the D1 sentence-length target.** The guidance specifies D1 institutional at 20–30 words average. The routing note has: "See the attached memo from Evaluation." (7 words), "My read: this is a characterization problem, not a technical one." (12 words), "I'll reach out." (3 words). Routing-note informality is plausible as a sub-type of D1, and the formal evaluation memo (Document 1) is on-target. This is a mild drift, not a register break — the sub-type distinction (formal eval memo vs. casual internal forward) is legitimate.

- **(None of the others are meaningful drifts.)** The preface appearing without a preceding headnote is correct: the preface IS the compiler's own apparatus voice (Register A), not an external document being framed. It does not need a headnote. The section header format ("§1 — Preface. Documents 1–2, 8–12 February 2027.") correctly names the chapter, document range, and dates.
