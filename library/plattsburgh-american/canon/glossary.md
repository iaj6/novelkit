# Glossary — *A Line of Smoke*

Alphabetical. Terms are added as established in later phases.

---

## A

- **Asa Beckwith** — fictional; 19, Clinton County militiaman and millwright's apprentice; POV character. Assigned to Pike's Ford on the Saranac River.

## B

- **bail dogs** — iron clamps on the log carriage of a sash sawmill that grip and hold the log as it advances through the saw blade.
- **Beekmantown** — hamlet on the road from the Canadian border to Plattsburgh, approximately 8–10 miles north of the village. Site of Major Wool's September 6, 1814 delaying action.
- **bower** — the main anchor of a sailing vessel, used for routine anchorage. In Macdonough's winding maneuver, the bower cable was cut.
- **Brisbane, Brigadier-General** — British brigade commander who directed the three Saranac crossing attempts on September 11, 1814. Not a POV character.

## C

- **cant hook** — a log-handling tool: a wooden handle with a pivoting iron hook near the tip; used to roll and position logs at the sawmill or on the riverbank.
- **carronade** — short-barreled smoothbore naval gun that fires a large shot at relatively short range; devastating at close action. *Saratoga* carried twelve 42-pounder and six 18-pounder carronades.
- **Chubb, HMS** — British sloop; part of Downie's squadron; captured early in the September 11 battle.
- **Clinton County** — the northernmost county of New York State, containing Plattsburgh. Home county of the militia under Mooers's command.
- **Confiance, HMS** — British 36-gun frigate (largest vessel in either squadron); Downie's flagship; surrendered after the September 11 engagement.
- **Crab Island** — small island in Plattsburgh Bay; used as an American hospital during and after the battle; burial trenches dug on it afterward.
- **Cumberland Head** — the long peninsula forming the northeastern boundary of Plattsburgh Bay; Macdonough anchored behind it to force a narrow entry.

## D

- **Downie, Captain George (RN)** — commanding officer, HMS *Confiance* and the British naval squadron; killed early in the September 11 engagement. Not a POV character.

## E

- **Eagle, USS** — American brig; second-largest vessel in Macdonough's squadron; part of the line at anchor in Plattsburgh Bay.

## F

- **Finch, HMS** — British sloop; part of Downie's squadron; ran aground on Crab Island shoals during the September 11 battle.
- **flannel cartridge** — a measured charge of gunpowder sewn into a flannel bag; loaded into the cannon before the shot and wad.
- **flutter wheel** — a simple horizontal water wheel; used in smaller mills.
- **ford** — a shallow river crossing passable on foot; see *Pike's Ford*.

## G

- **garland** — a rack or cradle bolted to the gun deck that holds roundshot in rows, ready for loading.
- **gigging back** — returning the log carriage of a sash sawmill to its starting position after completing a cut.
- **gun captain** — the senior member of a gun crew; directly responsible for aiming and firing the piece; works under the quarter-gunner.

## H

- **head race** — the channel conducting water from the river or dam to the millwheel; also called the millrace.
- **Halsey's Corners** — the final position of Wool's fighting withdrawal on September 6; where the delaying action effectively ended.

## K

- **kedge** — a light anchor used for warping (moving the ship by hauling on the anchor cable); central to Macdonough's winding maneuver.
- **kerf** — the slot cut by a saw blade passing through a log.

## L

- **Linnet, HMS** — British brig; Commander Daniel Pring; became the effective British flagship after Downie's death.
- **log carriage** — the sliding bed of a sash sawmill that holds the log and advances it incrementally through the saw blade on each upstroke.
- **long gun** — a naval cannon with a longer barrel than a carronade; fires a smaller shot to greater range. *Saratoga*'s main battery: eight long 24-pounders.

## M

- **Macdonough, Master Commandant Thomas** — commanding officer, USS *Saratoga* and the American squadron on Lake Champlain; age 30 at the battle (born December 23, 1783). Not a POV character.
- **Macomb, Brigadier General Alexander** — commanding officer, American land forces at Plattsburgh. Not a POV character.
- **millrace** — the channel directing water from the river to the mill wheel; also called the head race.
- **Mooers, Major-General Benjamin** — commander, New York State Militia, Northern Division, Clinton County. Not a POV character.

## P

- **peavey** — a log-handling tool with an iron hook and a pointed tip; similar to a cant hook but used for different leverage.
- **Pike's Ford** — a ford on the Saranac River, approximately one to three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village; one of three Saranac crossing points the British attempted on September 11, 1814. Asa Beckwith's assigned position.
- **pitman arm** — the connecting rod that converts the rotary motion of the millwheel into the reciprocating (up-down) motion of the sash saw blade.
- **Plattsburgh Bay** — the sheltered body of water on Lake Champlain's western shore where Macdonough anchored his squadron before the battle.
- **powder monkey** — a ship's boy responsible for carrying flannel powder cartridges from the magazine to the guns during action; typically 10–14 years old.
- **Preble, USS** — American sloop; smallest of the four principal vessels in Macdonough's squadron.
- **Prevost, Sir George** — Governor-General of British North America; commander-in-chief of the British land force (~10,000–14,000 effectives). Not a POV character.
- **prime** — to place powder or a primer in the vent (touchhole) of a cannon in preparation for firing.
- **Pring, Commander Daniel (RN)** — commanding officer, HMS *Linnet*; effectively commanded the British squadron after Downie's death. Not a POV character.

## Q

- **quarter-gunner** — naval petty officer, one per four guns, under the gunner's mate; responsible for the maintenance, readiness, and action performance of his section of guns and their crews.

## R

- **ragwheel** — the toothed wheel on a sash sawmill that engages a pawl on the log carriage to advance the log a fraction of an inch with each upstroke of the saw blade.
- **roundshot** — solid iron cannonball; standard ammunition for naval long guns.

## S

- **Saranac River** — flows west into Lake Champlain at Plattsburgh village; the south bank was the American defensive line.
- **Saratoga, USS** — American corvette; Macdonough's flagship; 143 feet; 8 long 24-pounders + 12 42-pounder carronades + 6 18-pounder carronades; complement approximately 240 men; built at Vergennes, Vermont in approximately 40 days (spring 1814).
- **sash mill** — a water-powered sawmill using a reciprocating blade mounted in a wooden frame (the sash); standard small sawmill of early 19th-century New England and upstate New York.
- **Silas Ware** — fictional; 22, quarter-gunner, USS *Saratoga*, starboard battery; Maine-born; POV character.
- **slow match** — a slow-burning cord used as a fallback ignition source for cannon; kept smoldering during action.
- **spring line** — a rope or cable run from the ship's hull to an anchor set at an angle; by hauling on the spring, a ship at anchor can be swung to present either broadside. Macdonough's spring-line arrangement enabled the winding maneuver.
- **sponge** — the tool (a cylindrical swab on a long handle) used to clear the cannon bore between shots; also, the action of using it. First step of the reload sequence.

## T

- **tail race** — the channel returning water from the millwheel back to the river after it has done its work.
- **Ticonderoga, USS** — American schooner; part of Macdonough's squadron; converted from a steamboat hull.

## V

- **Vergennes, Vermont** — site of the American naval shipyard on Otter Creek; where Macdonough's squadron was built by Noah Brown's shipwrights in the winter and spring of 1813–14.

## W

- **winding** — in naval usage, turning a ship 180° using anchor cables and kedge anchors rather than under sail; specifically, Macdonough's maneuver on September 11 to bring *Saratoga*'s intact port battery to bear.
- **Wool, Major John E.** — commanding officer, 29th U.S. Infantry; led the Beekmantown delaying action on September 6, 1814. Not a POV character.

**Cyrus** — Powder monkey serving Silas Ware's section of the starboard battery, USS Saratoga. Approximately 12 years old; Black. His job is to carry flannel cartridge cases from the magazine hatch to the guns at a dead run during drill and action. Introduced and named in chapter 1.

**Eldridge** — Gunner's mate aboard USS Saratoga; Silas Ware's immediate superior in the battery chain of command. New Hampshire man, thin, deliberate. Carries the magazine record; responsible for the powder and shot tallies.

**Hals** — Swedish sailor; crew member of one of the American gunboats on Lake Champlain. Briefly aboard USS Saratoga in chapter 1; acquainted with Pinney.

**Jonas Fry** — Gun captain of gun #1, starboard battery, USS Saratoga. Connecticut man, age 26. Knows the loading sequence reliably. Works under Silas Ware.

**Pinney** — Crew member on gun #1, starboard battery, USS Saratoga. Came aboard from USS Preble approximately six weeks before the narrative's action (mid-July 1814). Plays jaw harp.

**Tanner** — Crew member on gun #3, starboard battery, USS Saratoga. Vermont farmer who enlisted in Burlington approximately three months before August 1814. Faulty sponge stroke: arm stopping short of the base of the bore. Observed but not yet corrected by Silas as of chapter 1.

**Webb** — Crew member on gun #1, starboard battery, USS Saratoga. Works the starboard tackle fall.

**Eli Beckwith** — Asa's maternal uncle; millwright-owner of the sash sawmill on the Saranac River approximately three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village. Approximately 50 years old, blocky and round-shouldered, laconic, teaches by demonstration. Not called up with the militia (age or the mill's designation as war-essential supply). Appears in all Asa chapters as a secondary character.

**head race** — The channel that carries water from the millpond/river to the top of the millwheel. At Eli Beckwith's mill, filled via the sluice gate. Asa gauges its level every morning against the nick in the flat rock at the millpond outlet.

**tail race** — The channel that carries water away from the millwheel after it passes through. At Eli Beckwith's mill, the still water here collects sawdust.

**sluice gate** — The gate controlling water flow into the head race (distinct from the wheel gate). Opened by a gate wheel and bolt mechanism.

**wheel gate** — The gate controlling water flow from the head race onto the millwheel's bucket boards. Opened by an iron handle. Distinct from the sluice gate.

**pitman arm** — The wooden connecting arm that converts the rotary motion of the millwheel shaft into the reciprocating (up-and-down) motion of the sash blade frame. It makes a characteristic groan on each downstroke.

**sash (mill sash)** — The wooden frame that holds the reciprocating saw blade; it moves up and down, cutting on the downstroke. 'Sash mill' refers to this type of reciprocating-blade sawmill, as opposed to a circular-saw mill.

**ragwheel** — A one-direction ratchet wheel that advances the log carriage by one tooth per upstroke of the sash, moving the log forward into the blade by the thickness of one board per cycle. Asa appreciates its automatic regularity. Becomes an analogy for drill procedure in Chapter 8.

**bail dogs** — Iron gripping dogs (spikes) that bite into a log's face to hold it firm against the log carriage frame during sawing.

**cant hook** — A log-handling tool with an iron hook on a long wooden handle, used to roll logs onto the carriage. The iron throat finds the log's curve; the handle provides leverage.

**log carriage** — The sliding platform that holds the log and advances it into the saw blade. Advanced by the ragwheel mechanism one board's-width per upstroke.

**drying shed** — The structure beside Eli Beckwith's mill where finished boards are stacked after coming off the carriage.

**log yard** — The area beside the mill where logs are stacked before being sawn. At Eli Beckwith's mill, managed by the unnamed Abenaki seasonal worker.

**nick (flat rock)** — A mark cut by Asa with a cold chisel approximately two summers before the narrative, on a flat rock at the millpond outlet, recording the August working water level the millwheel requires. Asa checks it every morning the mill is running.

**Abenaki laborer (unnamed)** — A man of mixed Abenaki descent who works the log yard seasonally at Eli Beckwith's mill; present at least 5 years by the time of the narrative. He is never named in the prose. His presence reflects the demographics of the Champlain Valley in 1814.

**Fales** — Ship's carpenter aboard USS Saratoga. Quiet, deliberate, systematic; keeps the hull's structural condition in his head as a running inventory. First appears September 5, 1814, working on the after bitt with his mate, rigging the new stern anchor arrangement that Silas observes but does not ask about.

**bower cable** — The main anchor cable aboard USS Saratoga, running from the hawse at the bow to the primary ground anchor directly below. Distinct from the mooring spring and from the new stern arrangement Fales rigs in early September.

**mooring spring** — A line running from amidships on USS Saratoga out and down to the bower chain, used to adjust the ship's heading while at anchor. Not to be confused with the new set of falls Fales installs at the after bitt in early September.

**action-rate drill** — Gun drill on USS Saratoga run at full battle speed rather than maintenance speed — the measure not of sequence correctness but of how fast a correct sequence can be sustained under pressure. Ordered by Macdonough and relayed via Eldridge approximately September 2–3, 1814.

**Nathan Beckwith** — Asa Beckwith's father. Named for the first time in chapter 4. Served at Sackett's Harbor in 1813; came home with a chronic fever that has not lifted. He is in the house at the edge of the woodlot three miles upstream of the mill. His powder horn (initials N.B. on the stopper) and shot pouch were left at the mill in Eli's keeping and given to Asa on departure. His lucid-hour instruction to Asa: 'You know the river better than any man Mooers's got. Tell them.' Not a POV character.

**Rufus Holt** — Clinton County militiaman; approximately 35; farmer from the north of the county. Carries a long rifle used for hunting every October for fifteen years. Knows Asa through Eli Beckwith (Eli planked the floor of Holt's hay barn two winters before the narrative). Deliberate, even-paced, not a talker. One of Asa's four tent mates at the Plattsburgh camp. Appears at the ford in chs 8, 10, and 14.

**Isaac Webb (militia)** — Clinton County militiaman; approximately 22; blacksmith's striker from Plattsburgh village. Enlisted the day before the levy notice was issued. Five days' service when Asa joins the column. Affects military vocabulary and walks near the front of the column. Will not maintain this military air after Beekmantown (ch 6). One of Asa's four tent mates. DISTINCT FROM the crew member Webb aboard USS Saratoga — two separate people who share a surname; the two threads never cross.

**Veeder** — Clinton County militiaman from the north townships; approximately 35; broad and quiet. Brought a good ax and a wool coat. Rarely speaks. One of Asa's four tent mates at Plattsburgh. His musket grip is corrected by Sergeant Cole during drill at the ford (ch 8).

**kedge (anchor)** — A secondary anchor, lighter than the bower, used for warping or — in Macdonough's preparation — set well astern so the ship can be hauled around on it. In ch 05, the kedge cable is fully rigged from USS Saratoga's stern post aft and to larboard, the anchor placed in the lake well astern of the ship's counter. Distinct from the bower (the main forward anchor) and the spring lines.

**spring lines (hull)** — Lines running from the bower chain at the hawse back to cleats at different positions amidships on the hull, allowing the ship to change heading by hauling on individual springs without weighing the main anchor. In ch 05, two spring lines are rigged on USS Saratoga from the bower chain to two amidships positions. Part of Macdonough's winding maneuver preparation.

**Beekmantown** — Hamlet on the Plattsburgh road, approximately 8 miles north of Plattsburgh village. Site of Major Wool's September 6, 1814 delaying action. Most of the New York militia broke at first contact north of Beekmantown Corners; Wool's regulars conducted a fighting withdrawal south to the Saranac line. Fourteen to fifteen distant cannon reports (two or three 3-pounders) were audible from USS Saratoga's gun deck at approximately half past eight that morning.

**flannel supply / pass-boxes** — The flannel cartridge supply and the leather pass-boxes (carried by powder monkeys from magazine to gun) are counted and tracked as part of USS Saratoga's battery readiness routine. Cyrus is sent to make this count in ch 05. A routine readiness check, not a plot event.

**Cole, Sergeant Elias** — Noncommissioned officer from Wool's 29th U.S. Infantry; assigned to drill the Clinton County levy at Plattsburgh before the September 6 action. Leads the rearguard during the Beekmantown fighting withdrawal. Characteristic verbal economy: corrections given once; approval expressed as a single 'Good.' Assigns Asa, Holt, and Luce to Pike's Ford after the withdrawal. Appears in chs 6, 8, 10, 12, 14. Not a POV character.

**Luce** — Clinton County militiaman, approximately Asa Beckwith's age (19), from a farm south of Beekmantown Corners. Held through the Beekmantown fighting withdrawal and was assigned to Pike's Ford by Sergeant Cole alongside Asa and Holt. As of chapter 6, Asa is uncertain of the name; it should be established clearly in ch 8.

**Leonard, Captain** — American artillery officer commanding two 3-pounder field guns at Culver Hill during the Beekmantown fighting withdrawal, September 6, 1814. His guns fired round shot and then grape at the British column. A real historical figure; not a POV character.

**29th U.S. Infantry** — U.S. regular infantry regiment under Major John E. Wool that conducted the Beekmantown delaying action on September 6, 1814. Sergeant Elias Cole is assigned from this regiment to drill the Clinton County militia levy.

**cartridge box** — A leather box worn on the militia or infantry soldier's hip, holding pre-made paper cartridges ready for loading. Asa's cartridge box held the cartridges he carried through the Beekmantown withdrawal; at Halsey's Corners he had 3 remaining.

**Bates** — Crew member of gun four, starboard battery, USS Saratoga. Went ashore with approximately eleven other USS Saratoga sailors on September 8, 1814, to fill Macomb's south bank infantry line at Plattsburgh. Very minor character; not further developed in the chapter.

**grape charge (prepared)** — A pre-packaged grape shot charge bound in flannel and twine for use in naval long guns; distinct from a round shot in that the powder is distributed through the charge rather than concentrated. Effective for sweeping decks or inflicting close-range casualties at ranges under two hundred yards. As of September 9, 1814, two per gun in Silas's garlands, replacing two round shot each.

**Île aux Noix** — British naval base on the Richelieu River north of the border, near the Champlain Valley. Site where HMS Confiance was fitting out in late August–early September 1814; known to the crew of USS Saratoga as the reason the British fleet had not yet appeared on Lake Champlain.

**Veeder, Solomon** — Clinton County militiaman, approximately 35 years old, from the north end of the county. Broad in the shoulder, wide in the jaw, rarely speaks. Held through the Beekmantown withdrawal. Assigned to Pike's Ford. His full name is established in ch 8. Appears in chs 4, 8, 10, 14.

**Luce** — Clinton County militiaman (surname only), approximately Asa's age, broad-faced, from country south of Beekmantown Corners. Held through the Beekmantown withdrawal. Assigned to the right-hand (downstream) firing position at Pike's Ford. Name confirmed in ch 8. Appears in chs 8, 10, 14.

**Pike's Ford** — A crossing of the Saranac River approximately three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village, west of the main road, on the south bank road Asa has walked since age fourteen. The ford crosses over a limestone shelf (solid, knee-deep) into three yards of loose shifting gravel at the center (thigh-deep at the deepest), current pulling south. Right downstream edge: alder sinkhole (bank drops three feet, silty). North bank: flat angled stone that turns an ankle; submerged log in willows twenty yards east. Two companies of Clinton County militia defend the south bank. Shot distance from the south bank line to the ford's center: approximately thirty yards. Defended in chs 8, 10, 12, 14.

**sinkhole (alder sinkhole, Pike's Ford)** — The undercut section of the north bank at the right (downstream) edge of Pike's Ford, under the alder stand, fifteen feet from the main crossing. Bank drops three feet; silty bottom; dark in the day. Described to Cole in ch 8 as a hazard for British troops who stray right of the crossing.

**Marsh, Gideon** — Quarter-gunner of the port battery aboard USS Saratoga. Massachusetts man, approximately 28 years old. Precise, not forthcoming, competent. First named in ch 09; appears in ch 13 when Silas crosses to the port side during the winding maneuver.

**vent pick** — A thin iron probe used to clear fouling (carbon buildup) from the vent (touchhole) of a cannon after firing. Part of the routine maintenance toolkit for a quarter-gunner.

**Memorandum book (British patrol)** — A small writing book carried by a British soldier in the reconnaissance patrol that examined Pike's Ford on September 9, 1814. One soldier noted observations in it after studying the ford's surface and pointing out the alder bank and willow section. Referenced in ch 10.

**Clay ledge crossing (upstream of Pike's Ford)** — A section of the Saranac River approximately one mile west (upstream) of Pike's Ford examined by a British patrol on September 9, 1814. Features a gravel flat that reads shallow from the bank but drops twelve feet into a straight clay ledge — unfordable. Asa has known this section since boyhood fishing. The British patrol withdrew without crossing.

**Cole's thumb correction (Veeder, September 9)** — Sergeant Cole's second correction of Solomon Veeder during two-rank drill on September 9, 1814: Veeder's thumb was positioned too far forward on the musket wrist; Cole moved it back a half-inch with one touch and said 'Recoil will find it.' Distinct from the first correction (ch 8: grip placement on forestock).

**Île aux Noix** — British naval base on the Richelieu River, north of the New York border in Lower Canada. The British squadron under Captain Downie departed from Île aux Noix during the night of September 10–11, 1814, and appeared off Cumberland Head the morning of September 11. Silas receives the word 'British fleet out of Île aux Noix' as the signal to go to quarters in ch 11.

**HMS Confiance** — British naval flagship on Lake Champlain, September 1814. A sixth-rate frigate mounting 37 guns, commanded by Captain George Downie RN. Largest vessel in either fleet at the Battle of Plattsburgh. She anchored approximately 300 yards northeast of USS Saratoga at the opening of the engagement. Silas counted 15 of her gun ports through his own port before his field of view cut off the rest. Downie was killed early in the battle when one of Saratoga's shots dismounted a gun that struck him.

**HMS Chubb** — British sloop in the Lake Champlain squadron, September 1814. Single-masted. Rounded Cumberland Head in column behind the gunboats on the morning of September 11, 1814. Part of the British line in the engagement.

**HMS Finch** — British sloop in the Lake Champlain squadron, September 1814. Slightly smaller than HMS Chubb in appearance. Rounded Cumberland Head after the Chubb on September 11. Historically went aground on Crab Island reef during the battle.

**HMS Linnet** — British brig in the Lake Champlain squadron, commanded by Commander Daniel Pring RN. Brig-rigged, two-masted. Rounded Cumberland Head behind the Finch in the British column on September 11. After Captain Downie's death, Pring effectively commanded the British squadron from the Linnet.

**slow match** — Smoldering cord kept in a match tub at each gun on the USS Saratoga gun deck, used as a backup ignition source during action. The gun deck is lit by the slow matches' red glow when battle lanterns are not lit. Each match tub at Silas's section holds two cords. The smell of slow match is the smell Silas associates with quarters and the state of preparation for action.

**priming horn** — Small horn or flask of fine priming powder worn on the belt by a gunner and used to charge the vent (touch-hole) of a cannon before firing. Silas carries one and uses it to prime gun #1's vent on September 11, 1814.

**Beale** — Gun captain of port gun #1, USS Saratoga. Massachusetts man; one of Gideon Marsh's crew. Steps back from the lanyard when Silas Ware arrives at port gun #1 during the September 11 winding maneuver. Minor character; appears in ch 13.

**axle-tree (gun carriage)** — The transverse timber at the base of a naval gun carriage on which the carriage pivots and traverses. When a British shot struck gun #4's carriage at the axle-tree on September 11, the carriage collapsed and the gun fell off its trucks.

**traverse block** — The fitting at the rear of a gun carriage that allows the carriage to be trained (traversed) left or right. Gun #3's traverse block was broken by enemy shot on September 11, 1814, rendering the gun unable to train — it was fired once more in its fixed position and then abandoned.

**breeching rope** — The heavy rope or cable rigged to the cascabel of a naval gun, running through the side of the ship; it absorbs the gun's recoil and limits how far the carriage runs back. Each recoil is checked by the breeching rope snapping taut.

**Holloway** — Member of one of the two companies assigned to Pike's Ford. Sent by Sergeant Cole with Webb (militia) to cover the willows during the British flanking attempt on September 11, 1814. First and only appearance is ch 14. No first name established.

**Mead** — Crew member of gun #3, starboard battery, USS Saratoga. Rammer-side position. Killed in action September 11, 1814 during the naval battle of Plattsburgh Bay. One dead from Silas's section. First named in ch 15.

**Sawyer** — Crew member of gun #1, starboard battery, USS Saratoga. Wounded September 11, 1814, by a deck splinter above his left eye — deep but not serious; will recover. First named in ch 15.

**prize procedures** — The administrative and practical work following the capture of enemy vessels in the September 11, 1814 naval battle: assignment of prize crews to HMS Confiance, Linnet, Chubb, and Finch; longboats working between the American squadron and the captured ships; the gunner's mate's damage report on the American vessels' batteries. Title of ch 15.

**The limestone outcrop (Pike's Ford bend)** — A limestone outcrop on the rising south bank of the Saranac River at the first curve upstream of Pike's Ford, a few hundred yards above the main crossing. From this elevation, the ford's full shape is visible from above — the shelf, the gravel trough, the north-bank alders and willows. Asa Beckwith first views the ford from this angle on the morning of September 12, 1814.
