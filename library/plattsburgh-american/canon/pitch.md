# Pitch — *A Line of Smoke*

Lake Champlain, the first week of September 1814. Two American lives move toward the same Sunday morning from different sides of Plattsburgh Bay. **Silas Ware**, twenty-two, is a quarter-gunner on the U.S.S. *Saratoga* — Master Commandant Thomas Macdonough's flagship — responsible for a section of four long twenty-fours on the starboard battery of a ship that has been sitting at anchor in a Vermont-winter-built squadron waiting for a British fleet that is finally coming. **Asa Beckwith**, nineteen, is a Clinton County militiaman at Pike's Ford on the Saranac River, three miles upstream from the sawmill where he has worked since his father came home too sick to work. The two men never meet, never see each other, never know the other exists. Their threads cross at the level of a single morning: on Sunday, the eleventh of September, Macdonough's squadron engages Downie's at anchor in the bay while British infantry attempts to force the Saranac at three points. The naval cannonade is audible from the upper fords. The story opens in the quiet before the British army crossed the border and closes on the evening of September 12 — the day after. What happened between is not the story of a battle. It is the story of what it costs to be the body in the place where history is made, rendered without announcing what it cost or what it meant.
