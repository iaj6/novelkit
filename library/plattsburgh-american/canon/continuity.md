# Continuity — *A Line of Smoke*

Hard facts that all downstream phases (plotter, drafter, editors) must not contradict. Numbered for reference.

---

## Characters

1. **Silas Ware** is fictional, 22 years old, Maine-born, quarter-gunner on USS *Saratoga*, starboard battery, four long twenty-fours in his section.
2. **Asa Beckwith** is fictional, 19 years old, Clinton County militiaman, assigned to Pike's Ford on the Saranac River; millwright's apprentice at his uncle's sash sawmill, three miles upstream of Plattsburgh village.
3. **Silas's older brother** died at the River Raisin, January 1813. Silas does not speak of this to anyone. This fact is not announced in the narrative; it surfaces obliquely at most once.
4. **Asa's father** served at Sackett's Harbor and came home with a chronic fever; he has not recovered. Asa has been the wage earner since age fifteen.
5. **Silas and Asa never meet**, never see each other, never know of each other's existence. They share exactly one sensory thread: the naval cannonade on September 11 is audible from Pike's Ford.
6. All named historical officers (Macdonough, Macomb, Mooers, Pring, Downie, Prevost, Wool, Brisbane) are **never POV characters**; they appear only as seen from below.

## Timeline

7. The book's narrative span: approximately late August 1814 (day-before chapters, before the British cross the border) through the **evening of September 12, 1814** (day-after chapters). No coda beyond that date.
8. The day-before chapters are set before the crisis (before Prevost's army crosses the border, approximately August 31). Silas: routine watch-keeping on the *Saratoga* at anchor. Asa: a day at the sawmill.
9. **September 6**: Beekmantown delaying action — Major Wool (29th U.S. Infantry) with approximately 250 regulars and 700 militia; fighting withdrawal from north of Beekmantown Corners to Halsey's Corners.
10. **September 7–10**: Standoff on the Saranac — British on north bank, Americans on south bank. No major engagement; fortifications improved; militia drilling.
11. **September 11 naval battle**: began approximately 8:00–9:00 AM; lasted approximately 2 hours and 20 minutes; ended in American victory and British squadron's surrender.
12. **September 11 land attack**: began approximately 11:00 AM — after the naval engagement was already underway. British attempted three Saranac crossings: lower bridge (destroyed), upper bridge (village), and Pike's Ford (upstream). All were repulsed.
13. **September 12**: The British army began its retreat to Canada. The American navy began prize and repair procedures.
14. The day-before chapters do not foreshadow. The day-after chapters do not philosophize.

## The Naval Engagement

15. **USS *Saratoga*** (Macdonough, flag): 143 feet; 8 long 24-pounders + 12 42-pounder carronades + 6 18-pounder carronades; complement approximately 240 men; built at Vergennes, Vermont by Noah Brown's team in approximately 40 days (keel laid late February 1814, launched by April 11).
16. The complete American squadron: *Saratoga* (flag), *Eagle* (brig), *Ticonderoga* (schooner/galley, converted from steamboat hull), *Preble* (sloop), plus ten gunboats.
17. The complete British squadron: *Confiance* (flag), *Linnet* (brig, Commander Pring), *Chubb* (sloop), *Finch* (sloop), plus twelve gunboats.
18. **Macdonough's winding maneuver**: let go the stern anchor, cut the bower cable, used a kedge anchor astern to wind the *Saratoga* 180°, bringing the intact port battery to bear after the starboard battery was largely destroyed. This was prepared in advance.
19. Captain George Downie RN was killed early in the battle — struck when his own gun was dismounted by *Saratoga*'s fire. Commander Pring effectively commanded the British squadron thereafter.
20. *Saratoga* casualties: 28 killed, 29 wounded. Total American naval casualties: 52 killed, 58 wounded.

## Structure and POV

21. **16 chapters total; strict POV alternation** (Silas/Asa or Asa/Silas — the plotter chooses which opens; once chosen, the alternation holds).
22. Each POV gets exactly **8 chapters**: 1 day-before + 5 approach-and-standoff + 1 climactic action + 1 day-after.
23. Chapter lengths: roughly 1,800–4,500 words; average approximately 2,500 words; target total approximately 40,000 words. Day-before and day-after chapters tend shorter; September 11 action chapters run longer.

## Voice

24. Silas's register: spare, technical, naval vocabulary in stride, interior states surfaced as physical observation (not announced), short declarative sentences predominating.
25. Asa's register: closer to nineteen-year-old consciousness, river and mill vocabulary, sound prominent, simile drawn from practical experience.
26. The difference between the two registers must be felt within the first paragraph of any chapter.

## Hard Constraints

27. No invented intelligence subplot, spies, secret messages, or coded signals.
28. No "fate of the nation" narration. The historical significance of the battle is never announced.
29. No anachronistic dialogue. Period vocabulary from the research dossier.
30. No real people invented into the narrative beyond the named historical officers already in the brief.
31. No direct connection between the two POV threads except the audible cannonade on September 11.
