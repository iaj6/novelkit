# Plotter Guidance — *A Line of Smoke*

## POV Count and Rotation

**Two POV characters, strict chapter-by-chapter alternation.** Silas Ware and Asa Beckwith. The plotter chooses which POV opens the book (chapter 1); once chosen, the alternation holds without exception through all 16 chapters. No chapter shares a POV with the preceding chapter; no chapter has more than one POV.

**Do not add POV characters.** Even if a minor character (the regular-army sergeant; the gunner's mate; Silas's gun captain) becomes vivid and compelling in the drafting, they are never POV. Historical officers (Macdonough, Macomb, Mooers, Wool) are never POV.

**Do not give Silas access to Asa's thread or vice versa.** The two threads are parallel and non-communicating. The only structural crossover is sensory: on September 11, the naval cannonade is audible from Pike's Ford. That is the only connection.

## The Fixed Structural Frame

The brief specifies this; the plotter must respect it exactly:

Each POV gets **8 chapters** arranged around the September 11 axis:

| Chapter function | Count per POV | Approximate dates |
|---|---|---|
| Day-before | 1 | Late August (before British cross border, ~Aug 28–31) |
| Approach-and-standoff | 5 | Sept 1–10 (British arrival, Beekmantown Sept 6, standoff Sept 7–10, early Sept 11) |
| Climactic action | 1 | September 11 — the main beat of each thread's engagement |
| Day-after | 1 | September 12 |

With strict alternation, this means 16 chapters total: eight Silas, eight Asa, interleaved. The plotter assigns specific dates and beats to each of the 16 chapters.

**The day-before opening and day-after close are non-negotiable.** The plotter has discretion within the five approach/standoff chapters per POV but must work backward from the fixed September 11 climactic and September 12 day-after beats.

## Historical Beat Anchors

The following beats are historically fixed and must appear in their correct threads:

**In Silas's thread:**
- Day-before: routine watch-keeping on *Saratoga* at anchor (late August, pre-crisis)
- Approach: the British fleet's approach; *Saratoga* at readiness; the sight of the enemy vessels rounding Cumberland Head
- Climactic: the gun battle at anchor — Silas on the starboard battery; the starboard battery being largely destroyed; the winding maneuver (Macdonough's stern anchor let go, bower cable cut, ship brought 180° on the kedge); Silas shifts to the port battery for the final exchange
- Day-after: September 12 on the *Saratoga* — the prize procedures, the wounded, the repair of the ship, the silence after

**In Asa's thread:**
- Day-before: a day at the uncle's sash sawmill (late August, pre-crisis)
- Approach: call-up (late August); march to Plattsburgh; the September 6 Beekmantown action (Asa either participates in the fighting withdrawal or learns its results); the standoff days (Sept 7–10) on the Saranac south bank; the sergeant's instruction in two-rank loading; the early hours of September 11
- Climactic: the British attempt on Pike's Ford; the ford defense; the sound of the naval battle audible to the east; the British retreat beginning after the naval engagement
- Day-after: September 12 on the Saranac — the British army in retreat; what remains at the ford; Asa's walk or station in the aftermath

## Chapter-Shape Preferences

For this book, the following chapter shapes are appropriate:

**Day-before chapters:** Single-scene. The entire chapter is a day (or an afternoon, or a morning) at the mill or on the ship, at normal work. No time-skip within the chapter; a continuous unit of time. The prose observes; it does not narrate a plot. No dramatic event. The shape is: start in work, end in the ordinary close of the day.

**Approach/standoff chapters:** Single-scene or episodic (a few connected scenes from the same day or span of days). Time elision is permitted within a chapter — moving from morning to afternoon, or summarizing two days in the same chapter — but should not be announced with heavy transition prose. Cut the dead time; land in the next alive moment.

**Climactic action chapters:** Single continuous scene. The chapter does not begin before the engagement or end after the aftermath. It begins at commitment (the first shot; the British entering the ford) and ends in action (the *Saratoga* completing its wind; the British pulling back from the ford). These chapters will run longer than the average (target 3,500–4,500 words).

**Day-after chapters:** Single-scene. A contained span of the day: a morning, an afternoon. Quiet. Inventory. What is there and what is not.

**Avoid:** interleaved-POV within a chapter, chapters that cover more than one of the structural frame functions, chapters that begin with long exposition rather than the POV character already in their world.

## Episode-vs-Arc Structure

This is a continuous-arc structure — not episodic. The chapters flow into each other across narrative time; each chapter advances the timeline and the approach to September 11. The structure is not self-contained encounters; it is a compression engine that reduces the calendar from late August to September 12.

Within the approach/standoff block (chapters 3–12 in a 16-chapter alternating structure), the compression is deliberate: some chapters cover a single afternoon; others can elide two or three days within one chapter. The plotter should vary this to control pacing — shorter chapters for days of high tension (the standoff, the eve of battle); longer chapters for days that establish the world (the day-before, the full action of September 11).

## Ending-Mode Distribution

Across 16 chapters, the target distribution is:

- **4 literary fade** — the two day-before chapters and the two day-after chapters; these are the only chapters that should fade
- **3 resolved beat** — a unit of action or time is complete; the character is settled; the next problem is not yet visible
- **5 mid-action or hard cut** — ending in motion or in the arrival of a new fact; the narrative is leaning forward; used for the approach/standoff and climactic chapters
- **2 unresolved / tension held** — ending with the problem still open, the sound still present, the next day's question unresolved; used sparingly in the standoff block
- **2 turn** — a fact that shifts the chapter's register arrives in the final beat, then the chapter ends without comment

**Do not produce runs of the same ending mode.** If two mid-action endings appear in a row in the same POV, the third chapter in that POV should resolve, turn, or fade. The plotter should note the ending mode for each chapter in the outline.

## Chapter Target Words

- **Day-before chapters:** 1,800–2,400 words — short; the quiet, observed day earns its length without padding
- **Approach/standoff chapters:** 2,200–3,200 words — variable; compress where time is dead, expand where the beat is active
- **Climactic action chapters:** 3,500–4,500 words — the longest chapters; the action earns the word count
- **Day-after chapters:** 1,800–2,400 words — short; parallel to the day-before chapters in weight

**Total target:** approximately 40,000 words. The plotter's chapter breakdown should sum to this; do not pad approach chapters to meet a word target.

## Genre-Specific Structural Conventions

This is literary historical fiction centered on a single historical event. The structural conventions that apply:

- **The historical axis (September 11) is immovable.** Both climactic chapters must fall on September 11. The plotter does not move this.
- **The before/after frame is symmetrical.** Each POV opens with one ordinary-day chapter and closes with one day-after chapter. The symmetry is part of the book's argument (see *canon/themes.md*); do not break it.
- **The historical beats are fixed facts, not adjustable for plot.** Beekmantown is September 6. The naval battle begins approximately 8–9 AM and lasts approximately 2 hours 20 minutes. The land attack begins at approximately 11 AM. The *Saratoga* winds mid-battle after the starboard battery is destroyed. The British army retreats September 12. The plotter works around these facts; they do not bend to structural preference.
- **No convergence between the two threads.** The structure does not build toward a meeting of the two POV characters; there is no scene where their worlds overlap. The reader holds both threads; the structure does not resolve them into one.
- **The significance of the battle is never announced in structure or scene.** No chapter should end or begin in a way that signals "this is the moment that mattered." The plotter's chapter summaries should describe what happens, not what it means.
