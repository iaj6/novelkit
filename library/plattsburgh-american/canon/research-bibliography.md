## Source Gaps and Limitations

1. **Macdonough's full letter text:** The ibiblio.org transcription was unavailable (connection error). Quotes were confirmed in multiple secondary reproductions and are treated as reliable. Researchers needing the authenticated primary source should consult: *American State Papers: Naval Affairs*, vol. 1 (Gales and Seaton, 1834), or the Navy Department records at the National Archives (Record Group 45).

2. **Macomb's after-action report (full text):** The historiclakes.org transcript was unavailable (connection error). The full text was published in *Official Correspondence with the Department of War, Relative to the Military Operations of the American Army Under the Command of Major General Izard* (William Fry, Philadelphia, 1816). NARA Record Group 107 (Separate Enclosures M-136(8)) holds Macomb's map and enclosures.

3. **British court martial transcripts (Robertson, Pring, 1815):** No publicly digitized transcription was located. The trial record is held at The National Archives (UK), Admiralty records (ADM 1 series). Secondary sources confirm the acquittal verdict.

4. **Smyth's 1812 Infantry Regulations:** The PDF at 1812marines.org was not readable by the fetch tool. A physical or fully digitized copy is at Archive.org (search: "Smyth regulations field exercise infantry 1812"). The command sequences listed in Section 9 are partially inferred from general period military sources.

5. **Period newspapers (Plattsburgh, Burlington, Albany, 1814):** Not consulted; newspaper archives for these titles at this date would be found at NYSL (New York State Library), Vermont Historical Society, and the Library of Congress Chronicling America database.

6. **Upper Bridge location:** The precise location of the "upper bridge" crossing above the lower Saranac (distinct from Pike's Ford) is not fixed by any source consulted.

7. **Saranac River sawmill before 1814:** No specific documented mill has been found at the 3-mile-upstream location used in the brief. The fictional premise is historically plausible; see Section 12 and the inference note there.
