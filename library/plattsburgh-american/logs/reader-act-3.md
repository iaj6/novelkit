# Reader Act 3 Assessment — *A Line of Smoke*
## Chapters 12–16 (Before Light, Port Battery, The Ford, Prize Procedures, The Retreat)

---

### Overall

Act 3 is the book's strongest act. The climactic chapters deliver on every structural promise the approach chapters made — the kedge resolves, the submerged log resolves, Cole's "stand easy" arrives exactly as prepared, the brother's absence holds in its converted form — and the two day-after chapters execute the brief's most difficult requirement (observe what remains; do not philosophize) with genuine restraint. The pacing holds throughout. The two registers are distinct and correctly registered in every chapter. This act does not have showstopper failures.

---

### What's Working

**Ch 12 — "Before Light"** is the act's single best chapter. The opening — Asa waking in the dark to the ford's sound, gauging the river level by pitch alone without looking — is the Frazier method working as intended: landscape as interior access, the character's frame of reference doing the work that exposition would do elsewhere. The chapter's structural arc (dark → dawn → light → wait → cannonade arriving as pressure-before-sound) is clean and unhurried. Cole's single word "Today" at the water's edge carries exactly its intended weight and not a syllable more. The arrival of the naval cannonade — "a change in the quality of the air from the east... Not a sound. Just the difference" — executes the brief's explicit spec for Asa's September 11 experience. The detail of the pressed chest-pressure preceding the reports, and the accumulation from single reports to the continuous rolling of the bay battle, is the most technically precise rendering of that experience in the manuscript. The closing image — the north bank empty while the smoke grows above the ridge — is correctly shaped as an approach-chapter close: the day is not over, and the chapter doesn't pretend it is.

**Ch 13 — "Port Battery"** uses the loading sequence as formal structure throughout the action. The repeated command cycle (*Sponge. Load. Run out. Prime. Fire.*) is form-as-content: Silas's consciousness runs in procedure, and the prose runs in procedure, and the two are the same thing. The deterioration of the battery is well-organized — gun #4 first, then gun #3 frozen in traverse, Fry's going-down handled in four words and moved past, the powder incident and its resolution in five seconds, and then the two-gun end. *"Fry. He notes the fact. He moves to gun #2."* — this is Thread 2 advancing exactly as designed, and the brevity is the point. The winding maneuver is precisely rendered: "She was not moving; the *Saratoga* was rotating" is the O'Brian contract at its sharpest — showing the geometry from a fixed position without any overview or strategic narration. The passage through the port frame, the *Confiance* moving right, then the *Linnet*, then open water — this is exactly what "action comprehensible through what the character can see from where he stands" looks like.

**Ch 14 — "The Ford"** delivers on every approach-chapter setup. The first volley's near-unison ("Not in true unison — he had not expected unison — but close enough") is the drill chapters' promise paid. The loading sequence becoming automatic is the ragwheel analogy made good: "one tooth forward on each upstroke, whether you thought about it or not." The flanking attempt is handled correctly — heard rather than watched, the knock of a man losing his footing on the submerged log, then the water not cooperating with the effort to stand. *"He did not look. He turned back to the main ford."* — this single pair of sentences resolves Thread 3's submerged log, dispatches the flanking attempt, and shows Asa's operational focus all at once. Cole's final line — "Check your flint" — is the chapter's prize. It lands as flat military deflation of the moment and as Cole-as-character in one phrase. Asa's shaking hands at the end, filed rather than named as fear or triumph, are correct.

**Ch 15 — "Prize Procedures"** executes the hardest register for Silas: the day-after inventory. The four-gun accounting (gun #4 irreparable, gun #3 frozen in traverse, gun #1 cracked at the joint, gun #2 the best of them) is the right shape for Silas's psychology. The visit to Fry below — "Battery's going to need work." / "I see that." — is the chapter's best dialogue. Mead's empty berth is handled without sentimentality: the kit in order, the bag stowed the way Mead kept it, nothing unusual about the kit. The prize fleet observed from the main deck is economical and accurate: the *Confiance* sitting low, the *Linnet* in better order, the *Finch*'s mast at an angle above Crab Island. The port cover's hairline of September light at the close is the right image — precise, physical, not symbolic in any announced way — and "He went to find paper and ink" is the flattest, most correct ending sentence in the manuscript.

**Ch 16 — "The Retreat"** holds the day-after register throughout. The abandoned British camp — canteen on its side with the strap buckled, cartridge box, folded blanket left with care — is well-observed and unglossed. Cole's "They're going home" is the chapter's clearest line and should remain untouched. The ensemble of company men in their separate postures (Holt with whetstone and knife, Webb eating with the patience of a man not tasting anything, Veeder watching the north bank in silence, Luce looking at his hands) is the closest the book comes to photographing a collective state — and it earns it because the chapter has done the work. The walk to the limestone outcrop and the first view of the ford from above — the shelf's tilt visible in the water, the willows' false cover obvious from a height — is excellent payoff for Thread 3's coda: Asa seeing what he knew by feel. His brief thought of his father, turned away from before it deepens, correctly executes the deliberate non-resolution of Thread 5. The river's note in the final paragraph is the literary fade the brief asked for.

**Brief-adherence summary:**
1. POV voices distinct? Yes. "He came awake in the dark and lay still and heard the ford" (Ch 12) vs. "The lanyard pulled taut and gun #1 fired" (Ch 13) — unmistakable within sentence one.
2. Silas's spare/technical register holds? Yes throughout.
3. Asa's sensory/sound register holds? Yes. The cannonade as pressure-before-sound is correctly executed.
4. Historical significance announced? No. The canonical failure sentence ("he could not know yet what the day would mean") never appears in any form.
5. Day-after chapters philosophize? No. Both end in action (Silas going for paper and ink; Asa standing with the river) rather than annotation.
6. POVs meet? No.
7. Officers interiored? No. Macdonough's orders reach Silas through Eldridge's voice; the command chain holds.

### What Isn't Working

**"The starboard battery was finished" (Ch 13)** — One explicit announcement in an otherwise correctly spare chapter. The paragraph before this line has already inventoried the four guns' states. The declaration is the chapter doing something Silas's register doesn't do: naming what the scene has already shown. It's minor — the prose recovers in the next sentence — but it's the chapter's only moment of authorial announcement rather than notational prose.

**"He looked at this once and noted it" (Ch 14)** — A single sentence-after-the-sentence. The discolored ford water ("a red-brown spreading and thinning as the current took it") is correctly observed and needs no annotation. "He looked at this once and noted it" tells the reader how to process an image that the reader can already process. The line pattern is one the style guide explicitly flags as a failure mode (*post-scene subtext naming*). One instance, not a sustained pattern.

**"He was the same" on Cyrus (Ch 15)** — The passage on Cyrus — "He was the same. Twelve years old and the same: the same narrow face, the same set to his shoulders" — reaches for a resonance that hasn't been fully earned. Cyrus carries ammunition throughout Ch 13 and is twice mentioned by name without physical description. "He was the same" wants to carry freight (the constancy of this boy amid the wreck of the battery) that a reader who hasn't been given Cyrus's face before will be unable to feel. The emotional beat is real; the grounding isn't.

**The wren in Ch 16** — "A wren somewhere in the alders downstream was going on about something" is very close — syntactically and in exact phrasing — to the style guide's own exemplar passage for Asa's register: *"Upstream, behind the willows, a wren was going on about something."* This is not a failure of the prose (the wren works thematically and is correctly placed), but if the drafter took the style guide's sample sentence as a literal target, there is a risk that what should be a characteristic Asa construction has become a house phrase. Worth noting for revision.

---

### In-Act Pacing

Correct. Ch 12 moves at the pace of the wait itself — the darkness, the slow come-up of light, the sustained cannonade from the east. Ch 13 is the act's longest chapter and needs to be: the battery deteriorates over a long morning, and the chapter's pace mirrors the battery's deterioration. The powder-spill incident (one escalation after the battery is effectively gone) adds length without adding necessary pressure; it could be shortened without loss. Chs 14, 15, and 16 contract appropriately. The day-after chapters are the right length for their task.

---

### Thread Resolutions (Act 3)

- **Thread 1 (Anchor Cables):** Resolves cleanly in Ch 13. The preparation held; the winding executes; the port battery fires.
- **Thread 2 (Brother's Absence):** Advances in Ch 13 (Fry). Deliberately unresolved in Ch 15 — the accounting continues; the brother is never named; the grief stays converted. Correctly executed.
- **Thread 3 (Father's Charge):** Resolves in Ch 14 (the log). Coda in Ch 16 (the ford seen from above). Both beats land.
- **Thread 4 (Beekmantown's Lesson):** Resolves in Ch 14 ("stand easy"). Coda in Ch 16 ("they're going home"). Clean.
- **Thread 5 (Man Upstream):** Deliberately unresolved in Ch 16. Asa turns south before the implications deepen. The book ends before he walks the three miles home. Correct.

No thread is dropped. No thread is announced.
