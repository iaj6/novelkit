# Reader Assessment — Act 1
*Chapters 01–06: The Garland, The Head Race, First Reports, The Levy, Spring Lines, Beekmantown Road*

---

## Overall Impression

Act 1 is doing its most difficult job well: establishing two distinct registers, entering five threads, and holding the day-before chapters to genuine ordinariness — without foreshadowing, without cheating the reader toward what's coming. The prose is disciplined throughout. Both POV voices are distinct in the first paragraph of every chapter, the period vocabulary is in stride, and the historical record is handled without inflation. The act earns the acceleration it sets up.

---

## What's Working

**Register distinction is crisp and consistent.** The two POVs are immediately differentiable from the first sentences. Silas's chapters open in declaration and enumeration — "He had the morning watch in four hours and the guns to check before it" — while Asa's open in sensation and arrival — "The river was running full for August. He heard it through the alder brush before he was out of the woodlot." This holds across all six chapters without exception. The brief asks for the difference to be felt in the first paragraph; it is felt.

**The day-before chapters are genuinely ordinary.** Chapter 01 and Chapter 02 observe without signaling. The garland count, Cyrus's line-of-run to the gun, Tanner's faulty sponge stroke — these are the texture of a working watch on a ship at anchor, not setup. Chapter 02 earns particular credit: the mill world is fully realized (the pitman arm's long complaint, the ragwheel indexing one tooth per upstroke, the sawdust drifting down to the tail race), and the knot sequence is a genuine working complication that has nothing to do with what's coming. A reader who did not know the book's title would not know these chapters are a before.

**Thread 2's entry in Chapter 01.** "He did not think about his brother." Placed at the chapter's end, in the one moment of unstructured time in an otherwise fully occupied day. Exactly what the threads document specifies — the absence as the thing — and it's clean. The prose doesn't explain what it means that he doesn't think about it. It doesn't need to.

**Cole's introduction in Chapter 06.** The sergeant is established entirely through behavior: corrections delivered short, the demonstration of the half-cock position, the fact that watching Asa go through the loading sequence and moving on without a word. His stillness during the rout — "His musket was at order arms, the butt on the road beside his right foot. The militia came past him on both sides and he stood in it" — does more character work than a paragraph of description would. The first "Good" lands. Thread 4's metronome is set correctly.

**The Beekmantown sequence (Chapter 06) renders the militia break without editorializing.** "The rest of them were on the road to Plattsburgh. Most had gone at the first volley, before Cole's voice cut through. They had gone, and that was a fact, and there was not much to think about it." The prose does not flinch and does not explain. This is the register working correctly.

**Macdonough seen from below (Chapter 03).** He comes through the battery, crouches to examine touch-holes, speaks to Eldridge, moves on. "He did not speak to Silas." He is never interiored, never inflated. Silas goes back to seating a flint that did not need attention. The detail of Silas pretending to work during the inspection is natural and human without being announced as such.

**Chapter 04's Black workers on the fortification.** "Free men worked where wages were paid; the trench was being dug and someone was digging it." One sentence, filtered through Asa's practical lens, not erased, not centered. The brief is met precisely.

## What's Not Working

**Chapter 03 is the act's flattest chapter.** It is functionally complete — Thread 1 enters correctly, the action-rate drill escalation is established, the spring-and-kedge observation is filed — but the accumulation of drill sequences gives it a procedural texture the other chapters avoid. The Fry exchange at mess ("Well" / silence) is the chapter's best moment and it's two lines long. The smoke on the fourth ("the kind that comes from many small fires") is the chapter's best sensory observation and is quickly dropped. What remains is mostly repetition of the drill framework (the same loading sequence described across multiple passes) without the granular texture that makes Chapter 01's drill sequences specific. This isn't a structural failure, but Chapter 03 reads as the weakest chapter of the six.

**Chapter 05's kedge geometry passage runs slightly long for Silas's usual filing mode.** When Silas assembles the full spring-and-kedge arrangement in Chapter 05, the prose spends several sentences tracing: bower cable, spring lines, kedge cable, then works through the winding geometry explicitly — "If the bower cable were let go... the stern still had the kedge. Hauling the kedge line in with the bower gone, the stern would be drawn aft and to larboard; the bow would swing in the opposite direction." This is not wrong — Chapter 05 is where Silas assembles the complete picture and the payoff in Chapter 13 depends on the reader following his reasoning — but it extends noticeably beyond his normal note-and-file habit. In Chapter 03 he stores the odd cable angle cleanly in a single sentence. In Chapter 05 he traces the geometry for a full paragraph. The drafter has a reason for the difference (the picture is now complete), but it pushes slightly against Silas's register of brief observation and storage. Worth monitoring: if the logic unfolds this fully here, the reveal in Chapter 09 (where he hears the maneuver named and stores the completed fact "in its right place") may feel redundant.

## Voice Assessment

No register failures detected. Both voices hold throughout Act 1.

One construction to monitor across the book: the "He noted X" / "He filed it as Y" pattern appears in multiple Silas chapters (ch01: "found nothing else wanting"; ch03: "He filed it the way he filed a discrepancy in the garland count"; ch05: "He filed it the way he filed a discrepancy in the garland count" — identical phrasing). The exact repetition of "filed it the way he filed a discrepancy in the garland count" across both ch03 and ch05 is a micro-tic. The formulation is right for the register; the verbatim repetition is not. This is a low-severity concern but worth flagging early before it compounds.

Asa's chapters are appropriately noisier than Silas's without becoming interior-monologue. His similes are practical: "like cloth being torn carefully and at length" for the sash blade; "the way a fire goes when you stop feeding it" for his father's lucid moment receding; "the way a chain of cuts on a log went" for the fighting withdrawal. These are his similes, not the author's. Correct.

## Thread Development

All five threads enter or advance as designed:

- **Thread 1 (Anchor Cables):** Enters ch03 cleanly. Advanced with the full geometry assembled in ch05. Functional.
- **Thread 2 (The Brother's Absence):** Enters ch01. Exactly as designed.
- **Thread 3 (The Father's Charge):** Enters ch02. Advanced in ch04 (the father's lucid instruction; Eli's echo the next morning). Advanced again in ch06 ("I know that ford" / "Good"). The two-direction instruction is well-executed.
- **Thread 4 (Beekmantown's Lesson):** Enters ch06. Well-executed.
- **Thread 5 (The Man Upstream):** Enters ch02. Advanced in ch04 (the lucid hour; the fever taking him back; Asa taking the musket down from the wall). Correctly carrying the weight without being named directly.

No thread is premature; none is underserved. The weave is on schedule.

## Pacing

Act 1 moves well. The day-before chapters (01, 02) are the right weight — shorter, dense with specific texture, unhurried. The approach chapters accelerate correctly: by ch06 Asa is in a fighting withdrawal and the ford assignment is in place. The alternating POV rhythm is working as a structural principle; the reader never stays in one world long enough to lose the other. No dragging identified. The flattest chapter (03) is also one of the shorter ones, which helps.
