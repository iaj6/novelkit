# Reader Act 2 Assessment — *A Line of Smoke*
## Chapters: 07-at-anchor, 08-pikes-ford, 09-the-kedge, 10-two-ranks, 11-cumberland-head

---

### Overall

Act 2 is the book's best sustained stretch to this point. The approach chapters have found their rhythm — patient, technical, accumulative — and the act ends on a moment of genuine scale that earns its weight. Three of the five chapters (08, 09, 11) are at or near the manuscript's ceiling. One (07) is thin without being broken. One (10) is quiet and excellent. The two registers remain distinct; the threads advance correctly; no historical officer is interiored; no significance is announced. The act does its job.

---

### What's Working

**Ch. 08 — Pike's Ford** is the act's standout and possibly the manuscript's best chapter to this point. The ford survey in the first third is remarkable: it reads as tactical reconnaissance while also planting every piece of hardware the climax will need — the limestone shelf, the loose gravel trough, the sinkhole under the alders, the submerged log in the willows twenty yards east. The log is delivered in one sentence and not revisited, which is exactly right. The chapter also contains the ragwheel analogy — "one tooth advancing on each upstroke, the pawl catching it and the carriage moving forward, not because the gearman decided to advance but because the wheel came around and the pawl was there" — which is the best instance of Asa's practical simile in the manuscript: mill vocabulary applied to infantry drill, from his world, earning its length. Thread 5 (The Man Upstream) advances with a single line at the campfire — "He thought briefly of his father three miles upstream in the house and then he did not" — which handles the thread's silence correctly: named and immediately set aside.

**Ch. 09 — The Kedge** does the difficult thing well: showing Silas's mind working out the geometry of the winding maneuver through the dry, declarative accumulation the brief calls for. The overheard carpenter's fragment — *let go the stern, cut the bower, haul on the kedge* — is heard from below through the hatch grating, partial and compressed, exactly as a man on the gun deck would receive it. The chain of inference that follows ("Stern let go. Bower cut. Kedge hauled. The hull rotating...") renders the technical fact through the character's attention without pause for explanation — pure O'Brian contract. The visit to the port battery and the cracked flint — noted, not claimed at supper — is the right form of interiority-as-action. The brief gesture toward Thread 2 ("things he had stored there — in the winter before last — that did not belong to any chain that resolved into action") is oblique enough to hold the register and precise enough to do its work.

**Ch. 11 — Cumberland Head** handles the fleet's arrival correctly. The pre-dawn wait is structured by the accumulation of sensation — the slow match smell, the cold of the iron breech, the lake going from invisible to pewter to clear — before any action arrives. When the fleet appears, it comes in the right order: mast tip, then spar, then sail, then the column resolving gun by gun. Silas counting the *Confiance*'s ports and stopping at fifteen — knowing there are more beyond his field of view — is the right register for encountering overwhelming force: no announced dread, just the count stopping where visibility stops. The chapter ends on the single word *Fire*, passed through the chain, arriving level and unhurried. This is the correct ending for the chapter's function: the action begins; the chapter ends.

**Ch. 10 — Two Ranks** is quiet and effective. The British reconnaissance scene is one of the act's best moments: the forward soldier reading the ford's surface, Asa watching and knowing that the surface is all the soldier has. "He's reading the surface — that's all he's got" comes across as thought, not narration — Asa's knowledge confirmed through the enemy's limited vantage. The midnight watch sequence — the sound in the brush, the musket cocked by degrees, the wait, the nothing — is sustained correctly in the senses. Cole's arrival ("You waited. Good.") earns its brevity; the word *good* lands because the chapter has not prepared us for praise.

**Register differentiation**: The two voices remain firmly distinct across all five chapters. Silas opens in technical work (vent fouling, garland checks, fleet geometry); Asa opens in sensory landscape (the road following the south bank, the ford's sound before the road shows it). No blending within the act. Historical officers remain external: Macdonough does not appear directly; Wool is a name mentioned in past tense; Macomb surfaces only as the source of a request for volunteers. Cole, the sergeant, is correctly a fictional character seen from the outside, no interiority.

---

### What Isn't Working

**Ch. 07 — At Anchor** is thin. It advances Thread 1 correctly (the grape allocation shift, Silas's backward deduction toward close action), and the moment when the crew's work stops spontaneously at the sound of distant musket fire — "the work had stopped by itself" — is one of the act's better small observations. But the chapter suffers from a structural repetition: the musket fire is dramatized in one section, then recapitulated in the next when the crew talks about it at the mess table. The mess-table scene covers no new ground — the speculations (probe at the lower bridge? nervous militia?) are plausible but arrive after the reader has already processed the event. Silas's technical calculation in the mess scene (the grape charges waiting until range closes) recovers some value, but the chapter ends with two settling movements in close succession: the coffee going around and "the ship moved the way it always moved at anchor." The first of these would be enough; both together soften the chapter's close without deepening it.

**Thread 2 (The Brother's Absence) remains ghostly.** The thread description specifies that Ch. 11 — the flint-checking three times before dawn — is "grief-as-preparation at its highest concentration." But on the page, the triple flint check reads exclusively as competence; nothing in the prose establishes it as a form grief has taken. The only available connection is the one glancing reference in Ch. 09 ("in the winter before last — that did not belong to any chain that resolved into action"), which is too oblique to activate Thread 2 in a reader who hasn't read the thread description. This is partly a design constraint — the brief calls for interiority-as-action — but the effect is that Thread 2 is the least felt of the five threads. A reader who does not know what to look for may not be looking for anything here.

**Minor over-articulation in Ch. 08**: The ragwheel analogy builds its case through concrete mechanism, then the prose adds: "The parts were in the body. His attention could go to the ford." The first sentence names what the ragwheel already showed. Not a significant problem — it barely clears the threshold — but it's the one moment in the act where a word arrives after the show is done.

---

### Voice Stability

No register slippage in either thread across these five chapters. Silas's interiority consistently surfaces as physical notation; Asa's consistently surfaces as sensory accumulation before settling. No interior monologue in Silas; no ornamental simile in Asa (the ragwheel earns its length). No thesis restatement in any chapter. No foreshadowing (appropriate — these are approach chapters, not day-before chapters, but the no-signaling discipline is maintained).

---

### In-Act Pacing

The alternation between Silas's technically patient chapters and Asa's more socially textured, sonically alive chapters creates natural variety. The act builds cleanly: Ch. 07 is the standoff's patience; Ch. 08 plants the hardware; Ch. 09 gives Silas the geometry; Ch. 10 sharpens the drill; Ch. 11 brings the fleet. The climb is uninterrupted. Ch. 07 is the weakest individual chapter but does not disrupt the act's arc.

---

### Thread Check (In-Act)

- **Thread 1**: Well advanced. Grape allocation (07), winding geometry (09), fleet arrival (11). Strongest thread in the act.
- **Thread 2**: Technically present (09, 11) but felt only by a reader already tracking it closely. Functioning below its stated weight.
- **Thread 3**: Planted precisely in Ch. 08. The submerged log is in place; the father's instruction is in active use.
- **Thread 4**: Strong. The ragwheel analogy (08) and the drill's improvement (10) advance the thread with clarity and economy.
- **Thread 5**: Correctly advanced through a single line (08) and through its own absence (10). Working as designed.
