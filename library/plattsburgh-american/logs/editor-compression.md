> "The week had been a listening quiet — a quiet maintained, a quiet that was a form of attention. This was different."

The showing that follows (men not watching for anything, simply present in the morning) already communicates the contrast. These two sentences diagram the previous week's quiet rather than trusting the reader to feel the difference. Cut both; kept only the announcing clause ("quiet in a way that was different from the quiet of the preceding week") as setup.

**Cut 2 — disclaimer on the father passage:**
> "He did not stand there comparing the two of them."

The juxtaposition of the two men's service is made through bare parallel facts, which is the Asa register doing its job. Labelling what he is *not* doing names the comparison in the act of denying it, and explains the cognitive architecture. Cut; the parallel stands without the disclaimer.

**Cut 3 — label on the emotional distance:**
> "These were two things at some distance from each other."

Closes the father passage by stating the relationship between the two facts. The parallel presentation already holds that distance; the closing sentence is architecture-labelling. Cut.

**Cut 4 — elaboration on "since he was a boy":**
> "Since before he could name what he was hearing, back when he only knew that this water had a sound and the sound told him things he did not need to think about to know."

"He had been hearing this since he was a boy" is the earned single interiority sentence (lean-into: "brief earned interiority — a single precise interior sentence… keep it brief, keep it once, move on"). The second sentence unpacks and diagrams what the first already does. Cut; the chapter closes on the short sentence as intended.

### Redundant transition / re-summary

**Cut 5 — recap of the chapter's events within the final scene:**
> "— fading while he walked upstream, fading while he stood at the bend and looked back at the ford's shape —"

Embedded in: "It had been fading through the morning — fading while he walked upstream, fading while he stood at the bend and looked back at the ford's shape — and somewhere in that time it had gone under the river and stayed there."

The reader has just witnessed those two movements. The parenthetical re-names them. Cut the aside; the surrounding sentence stands.
