## What the sample does well

- **Opening convention honored precisely.** "He had the starboard battery and the morning watch, and he began with the guns." — physical, technical, in motion, no landscape delay, no interior reflection. Exactly what the guidance specifies.
- **Naval vocabulary in stride from the first sentence.** "Starboard battery," "morning watch," "gun deck," "hatchway," "roundshot," "garland," "gunlocks," "cascabel," "prime," and "gun port" appear organically, never italicized, never glossed. The O'Brian contract is met.
- **Silas's interiority is bounded and physical.** The one near-interior sentence — "It was a practice he had begun without deciding to begin it; there was nothing to find that was not already found, but the second pass cost a minute and cost nothing else" — is a single sentence embedded in physical action, describing a habit not a feeling. This is within the guidance's tolerance.
- **Chapter ending is non-manufactured and tonally correct.** "Southwest. Light. He noted the wind. He checked the prime on gun one and found it dry." — Terse, technical, ordinary. The day-before chapter ends on routine competence. No emotional freight, no signal of what is coming. Perfect register.

---

## What the sample drifts on

- **One sentence borders on literary observation.** "He could not see the Vermont mountains across the water; they were there." This is elegant and restrained, but it contains a trace of the literary-prose move of asserting an absence and then grounding it. It stays within bounds — it is a physical fact stated precisely, not an emotional observation — but a slightly less controlled version of this sentence would be the Frazier attractor pulling slightly against the Shaara baseline. Not a failure; worth monitoring across a full draft.
- **(Nothing else.)** No other drift is detectable. The two items above are either non-issues or minor notes at the margin.
