He had the starboard battery and the morning watch, and he began with the guns.

The gun deck was dark below the lantern at the hatchway, the watch already on deck, the mess hammocks overhead moving with the ship's slow lean on the cable. Silas went to gun one first. He counted the roundshot in the garland by touch — eighteen shot, cold as the planking underfoot, heavy and right. He counted them again. He moved to gun two and found the same. Gun three. Gun four. Seventy-two roundshot total, all present, all seated in the garland iron. He went back along the battery the other way. It was a practice he had begun without deciding to begin it; there was nothing to find that was not already found, but the second pass cost a minute and cost nothing else.

The *Saratoga* lay at anchor off Cumberland Head. She moved on her cable: a slow pull north when the wind came down the lake, a slow return, the cable paying taut and then easing. The planking settled and resettled with it. Silas had slept in that rhythm for months. He noticed when it changed. Tonight it had not changed.

He checked the flints on the gunlocks by the lantern's edge. Gun three: right. Gun four: right. Gun one — he turned the flint in his hand and felt the edge. Chipped. A fresh chip, not from the last drill. He put it in his jacket pocket. Spare from the locker in the morning, before the watch change.

He stood at the end of the battery with his hand on the cascabel of gun one and listened. Somewhere forward and below him, a man was deeply asleep and snoring. Out through the open gun port the lake was dark and unmarked. He could not see the Vermont mountains across the water; they were there.

The watch bell struck four. He went up through the hatch and took his station at the battery. The sky above the mountains was still black. Over the next two hours it went from black to the dark green of late summer hills, and then to pale, and then to the ordinary September light.

Southwest. Light. He noted the wind. He checked the prime on gun one and found it dry.
