## 03-first-reports.md

**Three fixes applied (word/clause level):**

1. `"had a different quality from the days before"` → `"ran differently from the days before"`. "Quality" is abstract; Silas presents the world as observable fact. Active verb removes the abstraction without touching the structure the following sentence builds on.

2. `"He watched it a fifth time and a sixth, building a complete picture of the error before he corrected it."` → `"He watched it a fifth time and a sixth before he corrected it."` The participial clause named the interior cognitive process ("building a complete picture") rather than showing it. Silas's register: the watching IS the picture-building; the prose does not say so.

3. `"By that time he had corrected Tanner's sponge stroke."` → `"He had corrected Tanner's sponge stroke."` "By that time" is a retrospective narrator's transition marker, not Silas's immediate observational mode. The paragraph that follows provides temporal context on its own.

---

**One item requiring writer judgment (not fixed):**

> *"A man corrected once learns. A man corrected twice learns that the first correction did not take."*

This two-sentence aphorism is practical enough to be Silas, but its balanced-clause formulation reads slightly as author's voice rather than working attention. The style guide specifies "the sequence of facts is the sequence of a working man's attention" — a true aphorism has a crafted symmetry that working attention does not. It may be intentional. Writer should review whether Silas would formulate this as a rule or simply note the fact: *Tanner had not corrected on the first pass. He watched five rounds, then six.*

## 05-spring-lines.md

Three word/clause-level voice fixes applied. Chapter is otherwise clean.

**Fix 1 — Asa-register sensory phrase in Silas's chapter (character-voice-drift)**
Original: "What came now was lighter, smaller, arriving at a range that made it more a pressure in the back of the ear than a report he could name with certainty."
Fixed: "What came now was lighter, smaller, arriving at a range he could not put a caliber to."

Rationale: "A pressure in the back of the ear" is precisely the somatic/weather register canon/style.md assigns to Asa ("cannon fire enters as weather, as pressure"). Silas's method is classification and range — he measures; he does not register body sensation as metaphor. Replaced with a caliber-and-range construction native to Silas.

**Fix 2 — Adverbial dialogue tag (style-guide violation)**
Original: "He stood in the battery and said it plainly: the regulars — Wool's people —..."
Fixed: "He stood in the battery and said it: the regulars — Wool's people —..."

Rationale: canon/style.md prohibits adverbial tags. "Plainly" is redundant here anyway — "without further comment" at the end of the passage already carries Eldridge's manner.

**Fix 3 — Authorial editorial in Silas's internal inference (character-voice-drift)**
Original: "A probe, or a test, or a nervous gun captain with too much time and a lanyard in his hand."
Fixed: "A probe, or a test."

Rationale: The first two items are operational military inference, appropriate to Silas's register. The third is a sardonic character sketch of an imagined person's emotional state ("nervous," "too much time") — the author's voice, not Silas's. Silas clocks a pattern and names it operationally. He does not editorialize about unseen people's feelings.
## 07-at-anchor.md

**Register slip — sound-texture / crew-psychology inference (Silas chapter)**

Line (final mess scene):
> "the low, settled sound of men who had been at anchor long enough to stop counting the days"

`canon/style.md` assigns sound-prominent, sensory-rich description to Asa's register. Silas processes the world as observable fact, numbers, and the sequence of a working man's attention. Reading a crew's collective mental state ("stop counting the days") from the quality of mess noise exceeds his observational mode — it's an interpretive inference about the crew's psychology, which is closer to Asa's noticing texture. The sentence also carries a slight lyricism ("settled sound," elision of time) that belongs to Asa's register.

The three words that do the most damage are "who had been at anchor long enough to stop counting the days" — they transform a sound into a psychological reading. A Silas-register version would stop at the physical: "the low noise of men at the mess table" or "the ordinary mess noise of men settled in." But shortening or cutting the clause means sentence deletion (compression's territory). Flagging for the writer to decide whether to trim or keep.

## 09-the-kedge.md

**Two word/clause fixes applied directly:**

1. `"He had filed the lecture and not thought of it since."` → `"He had stowed the lecture and not thought of it since."`  
   *Rationale:* "Filed" carries a clerical/administrative register that sits outside Silas's naval vocabulary. The style guide specifies Silas's metaphors are naval ("the wind, the angle of the sun, the trim of a loaded gun"). "Stowed" is his word; it also echoes "stored" two paragraphs later, where the same mental action recurs with the correct word already in place.

2. `"calling it by its name: *winding*"` → `"calling it by its name: winding"`  
   *Rationale:* The style guide prohibits italicizing period vocabulary ("In stride, not in italics"). The italics announce *winding* as a term being introduced to the reader — exactly the kind of glossing move the style guide forbids. The other italicized passages in the chapter (overheard dialogue fragments; Silas's mental rehearsal of tactical steps) are presentational devices for speech/recollection, not vocabulary introductions, and were left unchanged.

---

**Unfixable — writer's judgment required:**

> *"He stored it where confirmed facts went. He knew that place. There were things he had stored there — in the winter before last — that did not belong to any chain that resolved into action."*

This is the chapter's single earned-interiority sentence about the brother's death, and it is brief and followed immediately by a return to action — both of which the style guide sanctions. The concern is the explanatory clause: **"that did not belong to any chain that resolved into action"** describes the internal architecture of Silas's grief (memories classified by whether they lead to action). This edges toward the forbidden "emotional structural analysis" failure mode (*"His grief had become a practice, a form of attention rather than a feeling"*). 

The sentence is oblique enough that it may pass. But if the writer wants to tighten it: replacing the clause with something physical-and-concrete — e.g., "There were things he had stored there — in the winter before last — that he had not touched since" — would eliminate the architectural explanation while preserving the reference. Writer's call.

## 11-cumberland-head.md

**Two word/clause-level fixes applied.**

---

### Fix 1 — Mill simile in Silas's register (corrected in file)

**Original:** "Something like the sound of a mill at the far end of a valley when you know a mill is there."

**Problem:** Mill simile is Asa's register, not Silas's. Per `canon/style.md`: Silas's metaphor is naval — "the wind, the angle of the sun, the trim of a loaded gun." Per `canon/characters.md`: Asa is the one whose similes come from the mill and river. A mill-valley image in Silas's chapter is a character-voice slip.

**Fixed to:** "Something like the sound of a harbor in a fog before you raise it — a whole that has not yet broken into its parts."

Anchors the simile in Silas's Maine coasting background (approaching harbors in low visibility); "raise it" is navigation vocabulary; the clause preserves the original sense (a composite of indistinct sounds not yet resolved into their components).

---

### Fix 2 — Abstract "quality of stillness" construction (corrected in file)

**Original:** "he could tell by the change in the quality of their stillness, which was different from the stillness of waiting."

**Problem:** "The quality of their stillness" and "the stillness of waiting" as a named, doubled abstraction is Asa's register — the style of noticing subtle sensory/attentional states with aesthetic precision. Silas observes crew behavior in physical terms (station, posture, position), not in terms of the aesthetic quality of a collective psychological state.

**Fixed to:** "he could tell by the way each man had gone still — not the stillness of waiting."

Grounds the observation in a physical gesture ("gone still"), preserves the contrast with waiting-stillness, and delivers it in a short declarative fragment consistent with Silas's clipped sentence rhythm.

**Flagged for author judgment — borderline philosophizing:**

> "He could not find the defeat in how they moved. It was there, somewhere, in the direction of the movement and not in the movement itself."

These two sentences (in the section beginning "It looked almost the same as their advance") cross from concrete observation into abstract aesthetic analysis of what defeat looks like. It approaches the register the style guide flags as "editorializing" about what happens in battle.

A word-level fix won't resolve this; it would require either cutting the second sentence or grounding it in something Asa can see rather than a distinction he's philosophizing. Leaving the decision to the author. If the intent is to show Asa's mind working on what he's witnessed, the first observation ("It looked almost the same as their advance… how much the withdrawal looked like the advance. Men moving through the ford with purpose and in order in both directions") does that work already.
