# Chapter 05 — Spring Lines

**One-line summary:** The British army appears on the Saranac's north bank; the kedge lines are rigged on the *Saratoga*'s main deck; the lake stays quiet and empty of British sails.

**POV:** Silas Ware  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Approach 2

---

## Setting / Time

USS *Saratoga*, Plattsburgh Bay. September 5–8, 1814 — from the day before Beekmantown through the first day of the Saranac standoff.

Time span: Three to four days compressed to four scenes.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Multi-scene** — four distinct moments from different days, each briefly rendered in scene. The scenes are connected by the recurring image of the lake: each time Silas looks at the water, it is empty of the British fleet. The emptiness is the chapter's tension.

---

## Ending Mode

**Unresolved / tension held** — the chapter ends at night, with Silas in his hammock listening to distant cannon fire from the river direction. The sound stops. The lake is quiet. The question (where are the British ships?) is not answered. The chapter closes without comment.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

1. **Scene 1 — Distant cannon, September 6 (Beekmantown day):** From the gun deck during the morning watch, Silas hears — faintly, from the north — the sound of small artillery. Not the deep report of ship's guns; lighter, more distant. (Wool's two 3-pounders at Beekmantown Corners, roughly eight miles north, at the edge of audible range on a clear morning.) Several men on the ship react; some do not. Silas notes it: light artillery, north-northeast, intermittent for twenty minutes, then silence. He does not leave the gun deck.
   
   Later in the morning, a longboat from shore comes alongside. The news passes down the chain: *The militia engaged the British column north of Beekmantown. Most of the militia withdrew. The British are on the road to Plattsburgh.* A pause from the gunner's mate: *Wool's people held some of them.* Silas asks nothing more. He goes to his guns.

2. **Scene 2 — The kedge arrangement, September 6 (afternoon):** Silas is on the main deck on his off-watch when he sees a working party rigging additional cables from the *Saratoga*'s stern post. A kedge anchor — lighter than the bower, designed for warping — has been put in the water astern, at an angle, with a cable running back to the ship. There are also spring lines already rigged at a different angle, running to the bower. He looks at the arrangement for a moment, tracing the geometry: stern anchor, kedge cable, bower cable. *Something about position in current*, the boatswain's mate told him in ch 3 (or would have, in a referenced earlier conversation). Silas doesn't believe that is the whole explanation.
   
   He does not ask. He stores the arrangement: stern anchor astern, kedge cable at angle, springs to the bower.

3. **Scene 3 — The British army visible, September 7:** From the gun ports on the gun deck, crouching low enough to see toward the north bank, Silas can make out — beyond the trees on the north bank of the river — a column of smoke from what must be the British encampment. The smoke is distant but real. The lake between the ship and the bank is neutral water. The north bank belongs to the British now; the south bank to Macomb's people. The ship sits in the bay, between them.
   
   The powder monkey (the boy from ch 1) is watching through the adjacent port with his hands on the sill. He does not say anything. Silas tells him to go and see whether the flannel supply has been restocked since the morning count. The boy goes.

4. **Scene 4 — Night cannon fire, September 7–8:** Silas is in his hammock — off-watch, it is past midnight — when he hears cannon fire from the direction of the river. Not the north, which would be the British; the south bank, the American position. He lies still and listens. The reports are spaced: a gun, then fifteen or twenty seconds, then another gun. Not a sustained action; a probe, or a test. The gun deck around him is dark. He can hear other men awake, listening.
   
   The firing stops after perhaps half an hour. Silas listens to the silence. The lake outside the gun ports is quiet; he can hear it through the wood.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The British army is on the north bank. The British fleet has not appeared. The lake is empty. The anchor arrangement is unexplained. The night is quiet after the cannon stops.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

Where is the British naval squadron? Silas knows they must have ships; the intelligence (passed down in fragments through the chain of command) has mentioned *Confiance* under construction at Île aux Noix for months. She has not appeared. This chapter ends in that absence.

---

## Continuity Notes

- The distant cannon on September 6 morning: consistent with Beekmantown (small artillery; Wool's 3-pounders). Audibility at 8 miles on a clear morning is marginal but plausible; the text should mark it as faint and uncertain, not definitive.
- The kedge anchor arrangement: this is the second time Silas notices it (first glimpse was Scene 4 of ch 3). Here it is fully rigged, not just partially assembled. Consistent with Macdonough's preparation (continuity #18).
- The British army visible from the gun ports: historically the British encampment on the north bank was visible from the south bank and from the bay. The smoke detail is plausible.
- The night cannon fire (Scene 4) is not a specific historical event but is consistent with the probing actions described in the research during the standoff. Keep ambiguous (British testing, or American nervous fire, or both).
- The British fleet's absence from the lake during this period is historically accurate: *Confiance* was still being fitted out at Île aux Noix through September 10.
