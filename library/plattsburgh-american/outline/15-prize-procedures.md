# Chapter 15 — Prize Procedures

**One-line summary:** Silas accounts for his dead and damaged guns on the morning after the battle; the prize ships ride at anchor in the bay; the gun deck is quieter than any gun deck he has known.

**POV:** Silas Ware  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Day-after

---

## Setting / Time

USS *Saratoga* at anchor, Plattsburgh Bay. September 12, 1814 — the day after the battle.

Time span: A morning, from shortly after dawn to midday. The chapter covers three to four hours and does not reach past noon.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Bottle** — one location (the ship), a compressed morning. The prose observes without commenting. The chapter renders what is there and what is not there. It does not philosophize.

---

## Ending Mode

**Literary fade** — the chapter ends with Silas standing at the wreckage of his starboard battery, looking at the empty gun port, as the afternoon light starts on the lake. He closes the port. The chapter fades.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

*There is no dramatic goal. The beats are the beats of an accounting — what remains, what does not.*

1. **Waking on September 12:** Silas wakes in his hammock to the sounds of a ship at anchor — but the sounds are different. Fewer voices; the gun deck below him is quieter than the normal morning. The ship smells of smoke and wet wood. He lies still for a moment before getting up.

2. **The gun deck — starboard side:** He goes to the starboard battery first. This is what he does each morning. The battery is wrecked: gun #4's carriage in pieces against the hull, the gun itself lying at an angle on the deck. Gun #3's axle shattered; the gun aimed at nothing. Gun #1: the carriage damaged, the vent spiked by the recoil of a final un-primed firing attempt. Gun #2: operational, but the tackle is shot through and the recoil has cracked the side-platform. He moves from gun to gun and makes his count. Two guns may be repaired; two cannot.
   
   The garlands are empty.

3. **The wounded:** Silas walks to the berth deck where the wounded are lying. This is a brief scene; he does not linger. He locates each man from his gun crew. **Jonas Fry** is there — the arm wound was deep; the surgeon has bound it; Fry is awake and conscious, lying very still, which is not how Fry usually holds himself. He looks at Silas. Silas says: *"Battery's going to need work."* Fry says: *"I see that."* There is nothing else to say; Silas nods and moves on.
   
   Two other men from his section are wounded; both will recover. The dead from his section: one man from gun #3, killed in the action. His name is given here (name to be established in drafting); his berth space is empty.
   
   The powder monkey — the boy from ch 1, ch 11, ch 13 — is not among the wounded. Silas finds him on the gun deck helping with the cleanup, carrying debris up from below. He is unchanged; a twelve-year-old boy is unchanged in ways that a grown man cannot be. Silas notes this.

4. **The prize ships in the bay:** On the main deck, Silas can see the bay. The British ships are there, under American prize crews — *Confiance*, low in the water, a large vessel made ungainly by her damage; *Linnet* closer in, relatively intact; the remnants of the British gunboats at anchor. Prize-crew longboats are moving between the ships. The bay this morning looks like a different bay from yesterday's — the same water, the same hills, but populated differently.
   
   He watches a longboat row out to the *Confiance* with a working party. She sits low. She has taken a great deal of water. He thinks about her size — he noted it from the gun port yesterday morning — and about how long she fired before the end. She was a hard ship to kill. He notes this and goes below.

5. **Gideon Marsh and the port battery:** Silas finds Marsh on the port side of the gun deck, checking his guns. Marsh's battery is less damaged — the port guns were in action for the last thirty minutes of the battle, not two hours. Two of Marsh's guns have minor damage; two are operational. Silas looks at port gun #1 — the gun he fired yesterday. The vent is still faintly warm to the touch. He puts his hand on it and then takes it away.
   
   He says to Marsh: *"Your number-two gun's flint is still good."* (From ch 9, Marsh replaced the cracked flint before the battle.) Marsh nods. This is a joke that neither of them laughs at. They both know it.

6. **Crab Island:** Boats are going to Crab Island — the small island in the bay, the hospital, and now the burial ground. Silas does not go. He watches the boats from the deck rail for a moment. The island is low and green and the boats are small against it. He goes back to the gun deck.

7. **The accounting:** Silas stands at the wreckage of gun #1. The carriage: wood and iron, broken. The place where Fry stood, the place where he himself stood when he fired the first shot. He looks at the closed gun port — the port that faced the *Confiance* all morning. The lake outside is visible at the crack of the port cover: pale green water, the Vermont mountains across it, unchanged.
   
   He does not think about what the day meant. He thinks about what the starboard battery needs: new carriages for two guns; new tackle throughout; new garland for gun #2; a new gun captain for gun #1. He will write these things in the gunner's mate's report. He will write them now.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The battle is over. The accounting is made. Silas knows what he has and what he has lost. He does not say what this costs him.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

None. The chapter is a closing, not an opening. It does not reach forward.

## Continuity Notes

- Jonas Fry's wound: serious but not fatal; consistent with his introduction in ch 1. He survives; he will not be on the gun deck for the repairs. His recovery is not narrated beyond this chapter.
- The boy (powder monkey from chs 1, 11, 13): alive and working. His survival is noted by Silas with the same precision he notes everything.
- Prize ships in the bay: *Confiance*, *Linnet*, *Chubb*, *Finch*, and the gunboats; all surrendered after the battle. *Confiance* and *Linnet* are the most prominent in the bay. *Chubb* was captured early in the battle; *Finch* ran aground on Crab Island shoals. See `canon/research.md` and `canon/glossary.md`.
- Crab Island: hospital and burial ground after the battle. Simeon Doty's burial account (burial trenches; "redcoats and bluecoats put in together") is in `canon/research.md` (cite:09b). The chapter does not use this quote directly; Silas watches the boats going to the island from a distance.
- The port gun #1's warm vent: a small sensory detail carrying the weight of what happened there. The prose does not explain what it is carrying.
- The chapter does not mention the Ghent negotiations, the war's significance, or the future. It is September 12, 1814, and the guns need repairs.
