# Chapter 10 — Two Ranks

**One-line summary:** The standoff deepens; British soldiers are visible on the north bank of the ford; a long midnight watch teaches Asa something about waiting; the sergeant's brief praise is the chapter's turn.

**POV:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Approach 4

---

## Setting / Time

Pike's Ford, south bank of the Saranac River. September 9–10, 1814 — two days before the battle.

Time span: Two days; the chapter's scenes cover morning drill, an afternoon confrontation with the visible enemy, an evening rumor, a midnight watch, and a pre-dawn moment.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Multi-scene** — four or five scenes across two days, all at the ford. The chapter's rhythm is the rhythm of a standoff: sustained waiting interrupted by specific moments that require attention.

---

## Ending Mode

**Mid-action / hard cut** — the chapter ends mid-watch on the second night. Asa is alone on the south bank at 2 AM, the ford running below him, the north bank empty. The chapter cuts there — not because anything happens, but because the standing-in-the-dark is the chapter's territory, and the chapter departs while he is still in it.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

1. **Scene 1 — Morning drill (Sept 9):** The sergeant runs the two-rank drill again. The company is improving; even the slowest men are beginning to hold the sequence. Asa has the sequence completely; he runs through it without conscious attention, which worries him slightly — he has started thinking about the ford's bottom while his hands do the loading, and he is not sure whether this is good or bad. He decides it is good: he has made the sequence like the ragwheel, automatic, and his mind can go elsewhere while his hands work.
   
   The sergeant calls a halt and corrects Veeder's grip (Veeder's thumb is too far forward; the recoil will bruise it). Veeder accepts the correction without comment.

2. **Scene 2 — British patrol on the north bank (Sept 9, afternoon):** Three British soldiers appear on the north bank, upstream of the ford by perhaps forty yards — not at the ford but near it, moving along the bank. They are in red coats; they move carefully, looking south. They stop and look at the ford. One of them points at the cleared brush on the south bank.
   
   Asa watches them. He is behind the main firing position; he has a clear view. The sergeant has told them: if you see reconnaissance, watch; do not fire. They are too far for a reliable musket shot, and firing would confirm the position.
   
   Asa watches the British soldier who pointed. The soldier is looking at the cleared south bank, looking at the ground, looking at the ford itself. He is reading the ford. Asa finds himself thinking: *he doesn't know about the sinkhole under the alders, north side. He doesn't know the bottom shifts at the center.* This knowledge is not comfort, exactly; it is something more specific. It is an advantage.
   
   The British soldiers move on, upstream.

3. **Scene 3 — The rumor (Sept 9, evening):** A rider from Plattsburgh brings word to the camp: there was a probe at the upper bridge in town last night; the regulars held it. The British also tried to find a ford above Pike's Ford, further upstream — they were seen by pickets and withdrew without crossing. The ford above is deeper and rocky; it would be difficult for a formed column. Asa knows this. He has fished that section.
   
   At the cook fire, the rumor is discussed. Holt thinks the British will try the lower bridge, not the ford. Veeder says: they'll try everything at once. The sergeant, eating nearby, says nothing. Asa thinks Veeder is right. He thinks the sergeant also thinks Veeder is right.

4. **Scene 4 — Midnight watch (Sept 9–10):** Asa draws the midnight watch. He is alone on the south bank at the main position, Holt doing the upstream position, Veeder asleep. The ford sounds different at night — louder, in the absence of other sounds; the current more present. He can hear where the current runs fastest without seeing it. He knows the ford in the dark the way he knows the mill in the dark.
   
   He walks the bank without a light — from the right-hand position (downstream edge) to the left (upstream edge), counting his steps. At the far left position he stops and listens. The north bank is quiet. He can hear, very faintly, the sound of the British encampment: a fire, voices too distant to make out, what might be a horse. The enemy is real and close.
   
   After a moment, a sound from the north bank directly across: something in the brush. Not a military sound — he thinks: large animal, maybe, or a man moving carelessly. He brings his musket up, cocks it quietly, waits. Listens. Nothing more.

5. **Scene 5 — Pre-dawn, Sept 10:** The sound in the brush does not recur. Asa uncocks his musket carefully. The sergeant is there — appeared without sound from somewhere to the right. He says: *"You waited."* A pause. *"Good."* Then he moves on down the bank.
   
   Asa is alone again. The night is still; the ford is running. He does not feel reassured, exactly. He feels located.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The ford is known; the enemy has been seen; the waiting has a texture now. Asa has stood the midnight watch without making a mistake. The sergeant said: *You waited. Good.* This is the chapter's measure of progress.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

When will they come? The chapter does not ask this directly. It ends with Asa in the dark at 2 AM, the question present in everything around him but unvoiced. The chapter cuts mid-watch.
