# Chapter 12 — Before Light

**One-line summary:** Asa is awake at the ford before dawn on September 11; the naval cannonade arrives from the east as pressure before it resolves into sound; the north bank of the ford stays empty; the chapter ends in the wait.

**POV:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Approach 5

---

## Setting / Time

Pike's Ford, south bank of the Saranac River. September 11, 1814 — from approximately 3 AM (Asa awakes) to approximately 9 AM (the naval cannonade underway; the land attack not yet begun).

Time span: Approximately six hours. The chapter's substance is the wait; the compression is in the early hours; the chapter expands as the morning light arrives and the naval cannonade begins.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Single scene** — one continuous span of time at one location. The chapter does not leave the ford. It is present to the wait and to the sound of the battle that Asa cannot see.

---

## Ending Mode

**Unresolved / tension held** — the chapter ends with the naval cannonade ongoing from the east, the ford empty, the British troops not yet in motion. Asa is watching the north bank. The chapter holds this moment without resolving it — the attack has not come, but it is coming, and the chapter ends in the gap between.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

1. **Pre-dawn — the waking (~3 AM):** Asa wakes in the dark. Not a sound that woke him; something else, or nothing. He lies still for a moment, listening. The cook fire is out; the camp is quiet. The ford is audible — the constant sound of the water, which he knows better than he knows most sounds. He gets up and goes to the main position on the south bank.

2. **The ford in the dark:** He stands at the bank and looks north. The north bank is invisible — the alders are black masses against a black sky. The ford is running. He gauges the water level by sound: he can tell from the pitch of the current over the limestone shelf that the level is close to what it was two days ago. Right. Not risen in the night.
   
   After a few minutes, Sergeant Cole materializes from downstream and stands beside him without saying anything. They look at the north bank together. After a time the sergeant says: *"Today."* Not a question. He walks on.

3. **The company stirs (~4 AM):** By four the whole company is up without orders — the men seem to have woken the way Asa did, by the weight of the day. They take their positions on the south bank. The fire is not relit; no one says to leave it, but no one lights it. The south bank is dark and still.
   
   Asa is at the main position. Holt is to his right; Veeder is to his left. Beyond Veeder, another ten men from the two companies stretching upstream. The south bank is fully manned before first light.

4. **First light (~5:30–6 AM):** The light comes up slowly, as it does in September. First a gray resolution of shapes — the alders, the far bank, the ford's surface. Then, slowly, color. The north bank emerges empty. No British troops visible. The ford is clear. The sky to the east is pale yellow, then brighter.
   
   Asa looks at the ford in the morning light. He can see the limestone shelf, the gravel trough, the alders overhanging the sinkhole. Everything is as he knows it. He is aware of wanting the day to be over. He is aware that the day hasn't started.

5. **The wait (~6–8:30 AM):** A long wait. The chapter renders this with compression — some of it in summary: the morning, the cold, the men standing at their positions or crouching behind the cut brush. Asa eats something — hardtack from his jacket — without tasting it. The north bank stays empty.
   
   One thing breaks the wait: at about seven in the morning, smoke rises from the British encampment to the west — visible above the distant tree line, not from the ford. The British army is awake and moving. Veeder, watching the same smoke: *"They're getting ready."* He says it quietly. No one answers.

6. **The naval cannonade (~8:30–9 AM):** Something changes in the air. Asa notices it in his chest before he hears anything — not a sound but a pressure, a change in the quality of the morning. The first gun from the bay: he does not hear it as a gun. He hears it as weather — the way a thunderstorm will knock the air before the sound arrives. Then another. Then, building, the naval cannonade in earnest: a sustained rolling pressure from the east, felt more than heard, the individual shots lost in the accumulation.
   
   He looks east. Through the tree line to the east and south he can see nothing directly, but above the trees — above the low ridge — there is a smudge of gray-white smoke, growing. The lake is out there, beyond what he can see. The battle he cannot see is in that smoke.
   
   He looks back at the north bank. Still empty.
   
   The sergeant is watching the north bank. He has not looked east. He looks at the north bank the way he looked at it at three in the morning, and the way he has looked at it every morning for four days. The naval cannonade continues from the east. The sergeant watches the north bank.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The naval battle has begun. Asa hears it without seeing it. The north bank of the ford is empty. The land attack has not yet come. The chapter holds this moment.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

When will the British come to the ford? The sound from the east is the war in one thread; the empty north bank is the war in this thread. The chapter ends in the gap between them. The question is not voiced; it is the sound of the cannonade and the silence of the north bank at once.

## Continuity Notes

- The naval cannonade experienced from the ford as pressure/weather before sound: the central sensory crossover described in `canon/continuity.md` #5 and #31. The only direct sensory thread connecting the two POVs. Rendered here as Asa's chapter requires: as weather, not as narration; as pressure in the chest before it is identified as cannon fire.
- The smoke above the tree line to the east: consistent with what would have been visible from upstream of Plattsburgh, given the positions of the fleets in Plattsburgh Bay.
- Sergeant Cole's "Today": one word, delivered as a fact, not a dramatic pronouncement. Consistent with his characterization.
- The naval battle's timing: began approximately 8:00–9:00 AM (continuity #11). Asa hears the cannonade beginning roughly at 8:30 AM, consistent with this.
- The land attack will begin approximately 11 AM (continuity #12). This chapter ends before that; the chapter's entire duration is the gap between the naval battle beginning and the land attack starting. Asa has roughly two hours of waiting ahead of him when the chapter cuts.
- The chapter does not tell Asa (or the reader) what is happening in the bay. He knows only what he can perceive from the ford. Consistent with the rule: *no access to information he could not have.*
