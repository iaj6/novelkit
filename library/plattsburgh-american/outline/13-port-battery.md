# Chapter 13 — Port Battery

**One-line summary:** The starboard battery is destroyed gun by gun; the *Saratoga* winds; Silas crosses to the port battery and fires into the battered British line.

**POV:** Silas Ware  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Climactic action

---

## Setting / Time

USS *Saratoga* at anchor, Plattsburgh Bay. September 11, 1814 — approximately 8:30 AM (opening fire, picking up from the hard cut at the end of ch 11) to approximately 10:50 AM (the ship winds; the port battery fires; the British line in distress).

Time span: Approximately two hours and twenty minutes of continuous action, rendered in scene.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Action-heavy** — the chapter's substance is the battle. The prose stays in Silas's immediate sensory world throughout: what he can see, hear, feel, and smell from his position on the gun deck. No bird's-eye view of the battle; no overview of the tactical situation; the action is comprehensible because the reader understands what Silas is trying to do, not because the prose provides a map.

---

## Ending Mode

**Mid-action / hard cut** — the chapter ends with the port battery firing and the British line in visible distress (the *Confiance*'s guns going silent one by one). The battle's conclusion is not narrated; the outcome is not announced. Hard cut in the act of firing.

---

## Word Target

~4,000 words. This is the longest chapter; the action earns the count.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

### PHASE 1: The Opening Exchange (~8:30–9:30 AM)

1. **The first shot:** The chapter opens on the lanyard in Silas's hand. The order comes; he fires. The gun recoils hard, driving back on its tackles; the smoke fills his section of the gun deck in seconds. He is already at the breech calling the sequence: *"Sponge — load — shot — wad — run out — prime."* The crew moves. This is the sequence. This is the only thing.

2. **The rhythm of action:** For the first thirty minutes, the starboard battery fires at a rhythm of approximately two rounds in five minutes per gun. The smoke accumulates; the gun deck becomes a world of smoke and sound and the specific labor of the loading sequence. Silas moves between his four guns — correcting a man's grip, helping muscle gun #2 back to battery when a tackle parts (a splice gives; they jury-rig it in forty seconds), calling the count on gun #3 when the crew loses the sequence.
   
   The *Confiance* is the weight on the other end of this exchange. Her guns are heavier than his; when her shot strikes the *Saratoga*, the ship does not just shudder — she flinches, a specific, articulated shudder that carries from the planks up through Silas's feet. He notes this as he notes everything: the weight of what he's working against.

3. **First casualties — the powder monkey:** Sometime in the first hour, a British shot comes through a gun port and strikes the deck. Silas hears the impact and sees the result: one of the powder monkeys (not the boy from ch 1 — one of the others, from the adjacent section) is down. The boy from ch 1 keeps running. Silas registers this without stopping — the crew needs cartridges, the boy is bringing them, the sequence continues.

4. **A moment on the *Confiance*:** Through the smoke and the port, at some point in the first hour, Silas sees a commotion on the *Confiance*'s deck. He cannot tell what it is at this distance and through the smoke (it is Downie being struck and killed by his own gun dismounted by *Saratoga*'s fire). He notes it — *something on the flagship's deck* — and fires gun #1.

### PHASE 2: The Destruction of the Starboard Battery (~9:30–10:30 AM)

5. **Gun #4 dismounted:** A shot from the *Confiance* or the *Linnet* — he cannot tell which, in the smoke — strikes gun #4's carriage. The carriage collapses; the gun falls off its trucks and rolls against the hull. One man from the #4 crew is pinned; two others haul him clear. The gun is useless. Silas absorbs this: three guns.

6. **The gun captain of #1 killed:** Jonas Fry. A splinter — a large fragment from the deck planking when a roundshot strikes the deck nearby — takes him below the arm. He does not fall dramatically; he sits down on the deck, which is wrong. Silas reaches him: the wound is serious; the arm is not working right. Two men carry Fry below. A third man takes the lanyard of gun #1. Silas watches for a moment to ensure the new man has the sequence, then moves on.
   
   *Fry.* He notes the fact. He moves to gun #2.

7. **Gun #3's carriage shot through:** Another shot. The carriage frame of gun #3 is broken at the axle; the gun is still technically mounted but cannot traverse or recoil safely. It fires one more shot in its fixed position, then is abandoned. Two guns.

8. **The last gun:** Gun #2 and gun #1 are firing. Silas is between them. The smoke is very thick now; the gun deck is visible for six feet in any direction and no more. The crew is running on instinct; Silas can feel when they are about to lose the sequence and calls the next step before they do.
   
   Then: a shot takes the cartridge of gun #1 mid-loading. The flannel bag is torn; the powder scatters across the deck and is immediately dangerous near the match-tub. Silas calls *"Clear the match!"* The match is moved; the loose powder is swept before it ignites. No fire. But gun #1 has no cartridge. He has one serviceable flannel cartridge left in the pass-box; he moves it to gun #2 and fires.
   
   After that shot: the starboard battery has one operational gun, and no usable cartridge for it.

9. **The silence of the starboard battery:** Silas stands between his guns and looks down the battery. The smoke is beginning to clear slightly — a shift in the wind (the northeast wind has shifted, slightly, during the action; consistent with the historical record). He can see the wreckage: gun #4 on the deck, gun #3 frozen in traverse, gun #1 without cartridge, gun #2 with nothing more to load. The dead and wounded are against the hull or have been taken below. The living crew are standing at their guns or crouching, looking at him. 
   
   He notes the wind shift.

### PHASE 3: The Winding Maneuver (~10:20–10:40 AM)

10. **The stern anchor:** An order comes down the chain, passed from the quarterdeck through the gunner's mate: *"Stand by at the kedge."* Silas does not fully understand this order as it pertains to him. He tells his crew: stand by. A moment later, from aft: the sound of the stern anchor cable releasing. Then from below, a deep lurch as the bower cable is cut rather than cast off — the ship's bow frees and begins to drift.

11. **The winding:** The *Saratoga* begins to move — not to sail, not powered by wind, but to rotate. The bow swings slowly north. The ship is turning on the kedge cable, the anchor set astern and to one side pulling her around in a long, labored arc. The sounds of the ship during the maneuver: the groan of the kedge cable taking the strain, the creak of the hull under forces it was not designed to take, the shouts of the working parties at the capstan.
    
    Silas looks out through his gun port — the starboard port, facing east — and sees the British ships moving relative to him. They are not moving; the *Saratoga* is rotating. The *Confiance* shifts from dead ahead to his starboard quarter, then further. The *Linnet* moves too. The world outside the port is changing.
    
    For the first time since the battle began, he is not working. He watches the port through which the enemy was approaching, and watches the enemy move away from it.

12. **180°:** The ship completes her turn. The port battery now faces the British line. The damaged *Confiance*, the battered *Linnet*, the remnants of the British fleet — all of them are now on the ship's port side.

### PHASE 4: The Port Battery (~10:40–10:50 AM)

13. **Crossing to the port side:** Silas crosses the gun deck. The port battery: four long twenty-fours, Marsh's guns, already run out. Marsh is there with what remains of his crew. He looks at Silas. Silas says: *"I have men."* He has five or six survivors from the starboard battery — men without a gun to serve. He brings them to the port guns.
    
    He takes the lanyard of port gun #1. He looks out through the port. The *Confiance* is there, at close range, battered — her hull punished, her rigging damaged, her deck visible at this distance as a smudge of wreckage. She is still present. She is still armed.
    
    Silas primes the vent. The sequence. It is the same sequence. It is always the same sequence.

14. **Firing:** The port battery fires into the British line. The *Confiance* is taking hits now from the fresh battery — guns that have not been firing all morning. The *Linnet* is still in the fight but her fire is sparse. 
    
    *"Load."* The crew loads. *"Run out."* The gun runs out. Silas is on the lanyard.

## Outcome / Turn

The starboard battery is destroyed. The *Saratoga* has been wound. The port battery is firing into the British line. Silas is at the lanyard of a gun that was not his gun this morning. The chapter ends in the act of firing.

## New Question or Pressure Raised

None stated. The outcome (the British striking their colors, the American naval victory) is not announced in this chapter. The chapter ends in the act; the result belongs to the day-after.

## Continuity Notes

- **Jonas Fry** (gun captain, gun #1): wounded badly here (splinter wound to the arm); taken below. He may appear in ch 15, below decks with the wounded. His wounding is a significant beat; it is not the chapter's climax.
- The winding maneuver: *stern anchor let go, bower cable cut, ship winded on the kedge* — consistent with continuity #18 and `canon/glossary.md` entries for *bower*, *kedge*, *spring line*, *winding*.
- **Gideon Marsh** (port battery quarter-gunner): introduced in ch 9; appears here at his post. Consistent characterization.
- The powder monkey who goes down is *not* the boy from ch 1. The boy from ch 1 continues running cartridges throughout. He will be noted as alive in ch 15.
- Downie's death: he is killed when his own gun is dismounted by *Saratoga*'s fire and strikes him. Silas cannot know what the commotion on the *Confiance* is; he observes it without identifying it. Consistent with continuity #19.
- The wind shift during the battle (the northeast wind shifts): historically documented; Macdonough had prepared for it and Downie had not. It is present in the chapter as something Silas notes when the smoke thins — a change in direction — not as a tactical explanation.
- The chapter does not describe the British ships striking their colors. The battle's conclusion is not rendered; the chapter cuts mid-action. The reader learns the outcome obliquely in ch 15 (the prize ships in the bay) and ch 14 (the naval cannonade stopping, perceived by Asa).
- *Saratoga* casualties: 28 killed, 29 wounded (continuity #20). The chapter renders specific individual losses (Fry wounded; one powder monkey killed) without totaling the cost.
