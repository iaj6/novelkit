# Chapter 04 — The Levy

**One-line summary:** Asa receives the call-up notice, says goodbye to his father and uncle, marches two days to Plattsburgh, and arrives in camp in time to see the naval squadron's masts from the village street.

**POV:** Asa Beckwith  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Approach 1

---

## Setting / Time

Saranac River mill → the Beckwith house upstream → roads of Clinton County → Plattsburgh village. Late August to September 1, 1814.

Time span: Four to five days compressed into a montage of distinct moments: the notice, the evening with his father, the departure, two days on the road, the arrival.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Time-skip / montage** — explicit (but unlabored) forward movement through several days. Each beat is a distinct scene fragment; the transitions between them are cuts, not bridge prose. This is the chapter that moves Asa from his world to the war's world, and the transitions enact that movement.

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

1. **The notice (late August, at the mill):** Asa is in the log yard when Eli comes out of the mill with a paper — a handwritten notice from the Clinton County sheriff, not a printed order. Eli reads it once, holds it out. Asa reads it. *The levy of Clinton County is hereby called to service, to muster at Plattsburgh no later than the first day of September.* His name is on a list at the bottom. Eli says nothing immediately. Then: "I'll manage the mill." Then he goes back inside. Asa folds the paper and puts it in his jacket.

2. **That evening — the father:** Asa goes to the house upstream. His father is in bed; the fever has been bad this week. But his father has a lucid hour — the kind that comes and goes without warning — and Asa sits at the edge of the bed and tells him about the levy. His father listens. He says, carefully: "You know the river better than any man Mooers's got." He pauses. "Tell them." Then the lucidity passes and he goes back to whatever the fever makes of him. Asa sits a while longer, then goes to his room and takes down his musket.

3. **Morning departure:** Eli gives Asa the powder horn and shot pouch that were Asa's father's — the ones his father carried at Sackett's Harbor, left behind when he came home sick. Eli doesn't explain why he has them; he must have taken charge of them at some point. The handoff is practical, not ceremonial. Eli says: *Tell them you know the river.* (He echoes the father's instruction, having not heard it.) Then the uncle goes into the mill and the wheel begins to turn.

4. **The march (two days on the road):** Asa falls in with the Clinton County levy on the main road south. Forty or fifty men from farms and trades, some of whom he knows by name, some by face, some not at all. Two men who will matter at the ford are given brief sketches:
   - **Rufus Holt**, about 35, a farmer from north of the village, solid and deliberate; he carries a long rifle he has used for hunting every October for fifteen years. He knows Asa slightly; Asa's uncle had planked his barn floor.
   - **Isaac Webb**, about 22, a blacksmith's striker from the village who enlisted of his own will the day before the levy notice came. He's been in the company five days and is already affecting a military air. He will not maintain this air after Beekmantown.
   
   Two days of walking; the road running south between farm lots and woodlands, the lake visible at intervals to the east, gray-blue and flat. The men talk. Asa mostly listens. The talk is about the British army, about Macdonough's fleet on the lake, about how many men Prevost has. The numbers in the talk keep growing.

5. **Arrival at Plattsburgh village:** The levy marches into town past the redoubts on the south bank of the Saranac — freshly dug earthworks, the turned earth dark against the dry grass. **Free Black laborers** are working in the fortification trenches — digging, carrying earth in barrows — under the direction of a regular-army corporal. Asa notices this without remark; free Black men are part of the valley's labor. The American regulars in blue coats are drilling in a field south of the river. 
   
   At the end of the main street, through a gap between buildings, the lake is visible — and on it, at anchor in the bay, the masts and yards of Macdonough's squadron. Asa stops for a moment to look. He can count six or seven masts. He has seen the *Saratoga* before, at a distance, from upstream; he didn't know it then for what it was. He knows it now. He looks for a moment, then the column moves and he moves with it.

6. **First night in camp:** Asa's company is assigned a bivouac south of the river, near the lower bridge. He shares a tent with Holt and Webb and one other (name TBD in drafting). The river sounds close but strange — this is the lower Saranac, fifty yards from the lake, running faster and wider than it does at the mill. He knows the sound is the same river, but it doesn't sound like his river. He lies on his blanket and listens to it until he falls asleep.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Asa is in camp. He has crossed from the ordinary world of the mill into the military world of the levy. His place is established; his company is known; the camp is around him. His father's instruction is in his pocket with the call-up notice: *You know the river. Tell them.*

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

Where will he be posted? He doesn't know yet. The chapter closes without the answer — the assignment to Pike's Ford comes in ch 6, after Beekmantown.
