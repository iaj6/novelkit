# Chapter 11 — Cumberland Head

**One-line summary:** Pre-dawn September 11: the *Saratoga* is already at quarters when the British fleet rounds Cumberland Head; by the time the opening gun fires, Silas has his hand on the lanyard.

**POV:** Silas Ware  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Approach 5

---

## Setting / Time

USS *Saratoga* at anchor, Plattsburgh Bay. September 11, 1814 — from approximately 4 AM (pre-dawn quarters) to the opening of the naval engagement (~8:30–9:00 AM).

Time span: Approximately four to five hours. The chapter renders this as a single continuous approach to action, compressed in some places and expanded in others.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Single scene** — one continuous unit of time: the pre-dawn wait at quarters through the moment the first shot is fired. No time-skip within the chapter; the reader experiences the wait as Silas experiences it, which is the chapter's point.

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

1. **Pre-dawn — quarters in the dark (~4 AM):** The ship went to quarters in the night — a word passed down the chain that the British fleet had left Île aux Noix and was on the lake. The gun deck when Silas arrives: dark except for the slow-match tubs at each gun, the smoldering cords producing their faint red light and their particular smell. The gun ports are half-open. His four guns are trained out; the powder monkey is at the magazine hatch, the flannel cartridges coming up one at a time in their leather pass-boxes.
   
   Silas does not check the flints. He checked them at midnight, then again at two, and again just now before he left his hammock. He knows they are right. He takes his position at gun #1 and waits.

2. **The wait (~4 AM–7 AM):** Two or three hours of waiting. The chapter renders this with compression — the wait is real but not dragged out at length. The gun deck in the dark: the sound of the ship (cable, planking, the water against the hull); the sound of the men (breathing, occasional whispered exchange, the shift of a foot on the deck); the slow light of the match-tubs. 
   
   First light comes through the gun ports: gray, then pale, then the clear September morning. No sound from the lake. Silas looks through his port: the water is calm. Light wind from the northeast — he notes this. The Vermont mountains across the lake are bright in the early light.

3. **Sound before sight (~7:30–8 AM):** The British fleet is heard before it is seen. From the north: the creak and splash of multiple vessels — oars on the gunboats being rowed into position, the groan of rigging adjusting, sounds that do not resolve into a clear picture from inside the gun deck. Then the shape of a mast tip above Cumberland Head, glimpsed through the port at the extreme angle of view.
   
   A murmur on the gun deck. Fry, at gun #1: *"There."* Silas says: *"Watch your post."* Fry watches his post.

4. **Cumberland Head (~8:00–8:30 AM):** The British fleet rounds the head in column. Silas watches through his port. He cannot see the full approach from his angle — the port's field of view is limited — but he sees what he can: first the gunboats, then the *Chubb*, then the *Linnet*, and then the *Confiance*. The *Confiance* is large. He registers her dimensions (she is a 36-gun frigate, far larger than anything in the American squadron) without drama: she is the fact that determines everything about the next several hours.
   
   He counts the vessels he can see. He counts the gun ports on the *Confiance*. He cannot see all of them from his angle. He stops counting at fifteen.

5. **The British take position (~8:15–8:30 AM):** The British fleet moves to its fighting position. From the gun deck, the sounds: chains, anchors, the creak of vessels coming to rest. Then a quiet. Both fleets at anchor. The sound of the wind through the rigging.
   
   Orders come down the chain from Macdonough, passed through the gunner's mate to Silas: *Run out.* He tells Fry. The gun crews run out the starboard long twenty-fours; the trucks squeal on the deck planks; the muzzle of gun #1 appears in its port. Silas primes the vent. He takes the lanyard of gun #1 himself; this is not his normal position (that is Fry's), but the first shot is his, and he knows it, and Fry steps aside without being told.
   
   The slow match is smoldering in the match-tub.

6. **The order (~8:30–9:00 AM):** The order comes. One word, passed from the quarterdeck down the chain: *Fire.*

## Continuity Notes

- The naval battle began approximately 8:00–9:00 AM (historically; see `canon/continuity.md` #11). This chapter places the opening shots at approximately 8:30–9:00 AM, consistent with the record.
- The wind: *light northeast* on the morning of September 11 (historical fact; see `canon/research.md` and world description). Silas notes it; the northeast wind is a tactical fact Macdonough has prepared for (wind shift during battle).
- The *Confiance* identified by Silas through the port: she had 37 guns (not 36 — the sources vary slightly between 36 and 37; the drafter should use the `canon/research.md` figure of 37). Silas seeing her size is a registered fact, not an emotional moment.
- Cumberland Head: the long peninsula forming the northeastern boundary of Plattsburgh Bay; the British fleet had to round it to enter and engage. This forced the British into a column formation during approach, under fire from the American line — a tactical disadvantage Macdonough designed his position to create.
- Silas takes gun #1's lanyard for the first shot: this is not explicitly historical but is consistent with his character. The first shot of the chapter is also the last beat of the chapter, not the first shot described in narrative detail (that belongs to ch 13).
- The chapter does not name Downie, does not access any British perspective, does not describe the battle's progress or outcome. It ends at the threshold.
