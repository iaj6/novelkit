# Chapter 03 — First Reports

**One-line summary:** Word arrives that Prevost has crossed the border; drills on the *Saratoga* sharpen; Silas notices an unusual arrangement in the anchor cables without understanding it.

**POV:** Silas Ware  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Approach 1

---

## Setting / Time

USS *Saratoga* at anchor, Plattsburgh Bay. September 1–5, 1814 — from the first news of Prevost's crossing to the day before the Beekmantown action.

Time span: Four or five days, compressed to three or four distinct moments.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Multi-scene** — several distinct scenes from different days, each rendered briefly but in scene; connected by the accumulating fact that the British are closer with each day that passes.

---

## Ending Mode

**Mid-action / hard cut** — the chapter ends mid-drill, with the crew running out gun #1 at speed. The prose cuts on the gun coming into battery. The drill is not finished; the chapter doesn't wait for it to finish.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

1. **Scene 1 — The news (Sept 1 or 2):** A morning gun drill is interrupted: a longboat from shore comes alongside with dispatches. The gunner's mate receives them and reads; the word passes down the battery in pieces — "Prevost's column" and "Champlain, New York" and "cavalry scouts." Silas's crew pauses. He tells them to finish the drill. They finish the drill. Afterward, at mess, Fry (gun captain, gun #1) says: "Well." Silas says nothing.

2. **Scene 2 — Macdonough's inspection (Sept 2–3):** Macdonough is seen — from below, on the gun deck — making a thorough inspection of the starboard battery. He does not speak to Silas directly; he speaks to the gunner's mate. The gunner's mate afterward tells Silas that the Master Commandant wants to see the gun sections drilled at action-speed, not maintenance-speed. Silas registers this as a change in category. He calls his crew for an evening drill that is faster and harder than anything they have run since the squadron was at Vergennes.

3. **Scene 3 — Smoke to the northeast (Sept 4):** On the afternoon of September 4, several men on the fo'c'sle watch a low smudge of smoke to the northeast. (This is the skirmish at Chazy, approximately 20 miles north on the road.) Silas watches it from the deck for a moment. The smoke is gray-white — not a building fire, which burns darker. He doesn't say what he thinks it is. He goes back to his guns.

4. **Scene 4 — The anchor cables (Sept 5):** The ship's carpenter and his mate are working on the stern anchor cable arrangement. Silas, passing on the main deck, notices something new: additional falls running from the hull to the bower anchor in a configuration he hasn't seen before — not the normal mooring spring. He stops and looks at it. He does not ask what it is. He stores the observation: *a spring line running to a separate anchor, not to the bower chain.* He notes it the way he notes a discrepancy in the cartridge count — without alarm, but without dismissing it.

5. **Scene 5 — Evening drill, Sept 5:** Silas runs gun #1's crew through the loading sequence at full speed. Three rounds, timed by his mental count. The gun captain (Fry) is running the crew hard. The Vermont farmer on gun #3 — the one with the slow sponge stroke — has improved; Silas corrected the arm motion two days prior and the correction has held. Three rounds and a quarter in five minutes. Silas tells Fry: again.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The ship is no longer drilling at maintenance speed; it is drilling at action speed. Silas has stored an unexplained observation about the anchor cables. The war has come south.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

What is the anchor cable arrangement for? The chapter does not answer this. Silas stores it without pressing.

---

## Continuity Notes

- Jonas Fry first named in ch 1; consistent here.
- The smoke at Chazy (September 4) is a historical fact; the skirmish is not described in detail because Silas cannot see it, only its smoke.
- The anchor cable arrangement Silas notices in Scene 4 is the embryo of what he will understand fully in ch 9 (the kedge) — the spring-and-kedge system. Consistent with continuity note on Macdonough's preparation (continuity #18).
- Macdonough is seen from below; he does not speak to Silas; consistent with continuity #6.
- The chapter does not name the British general or the battle to come; it reports what the crew knows — dispatches, the word "Prevost," smoke to the north.
