# Chapter 01 — The Garland

**One-line summary:** Silas runs his ordinary pre-dawn check and daytime routine on the gun deck; nothing happens; the guns are right.

**POV:** Silas Ware  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Day-before

---

## Setting / Time

USS *Saratoga* at anchor, Plattsburgh Bay. ~August 28, 1814 — approximately three days before Prevost's army crosses the border. A Monday or Tuesday; the day is chosen because it is ordinary.

Time span: the morning watch (4 AM to 8 AM) through end of day and last light. Approximately 16 hours compressed into a chapter-length observation.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Bottle** — one location (the ship, with access to main deck and fo'c'sle in the evening), near real-time compression of a single working day. No dramatic event occurs. The prose observes without foreshadowing.

---

## Ending Mode

**Literary fade** — the chapter ends with Silas going below at last light as the watch changes. The day is complete; nothing is unresolved; the next day will be the same.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

*There is no dramatic goal. The beats are the beats of work.*

1. **Pre-dawn, below the morning watch:** Silas counts the roundshot in the garland of each of his four starboard guns in the dark — by hand, by feel. Eighteen per gun. He finds eighteen. He checks the flints on the gunlocks. One flint is chipped; he notes it for the morning. No one else is awake in the gun deck. The ship moves on its anchor cable; the planking creaks.

2. **Morning watch (4–8 AM):** On watch at the battery. The gray pre-dawn light through the gun ports, then the dawn coming up over the Vermont mountains across the lake. Clear sky; the hills going from black to green to the yellow of late summer. The lake flat and unmarked. Silas notes the wind: southwest, light. He notes the height of the sun above the hills (force of habit; a measurement without a purpose today).

3. **Breakfast (8 bells, end of morning watch):** Below decks. The ship's cook has salt pork and biscuit; there is coffee. A brief scene at mess with the gun captain of gun #1 (name: **Jonas Fry**, a Connecticut man, twenty-six) and two of the crew. They do not discuss the war. Fry complains about the biscuit. Silas says nothing; he eats. He exchanges the chipped flint with the spare he keeps in his jacket pocket.

4. **Morning gun drill (mid-morning):** Not a battle drill — maintenance and sequence rehearsal. Silas walks Fry's crew through the loading sequence: sponge, cartridge, shot, wad, run out, prime, fire. He is watching the **powder monkey** (name to develop in drafting; approximately 12 years old, possibly of African descent — consistent with the roughly quarter-Black composition of comparable lake squadron crews). The boy's job is to sprint from the magazine hatch to the gun and back. He is fast; Silas watches his route and notes one place where he veers wide around a deck ringbolt. Silas does not correct him; he watches the second run to see if it recurs. It does. He corrects it on the third run: step over the ringbolt, don't go around it.

5. **Afternoon — cartridge inspection with the gunner's mate:** The **gunner's mate** (name TBD in drafting) comes through the battery with the magazine record. Silas's count of flannel cartridges matches the record. While the gunner's mate is there, Silas mentions the chipped flint (now replaced). The gunner's mate nods. He asks how the new man on gun #3 — a Vermont farmer who enlisted in Burlington three months ago — is coming with the sequence. Silas says: slow on the sponge stroke; his arm is wrong. The gunner's mate says he'll have a look. He does not have a look; he moves down the battery. Silas notes this without comment.

6. **Evening — fo'c'sle:** Off-watch; the lake at dusk. Silas sits on the fo'c'sle with three or four other men. Someone plays a jaw harp, badly. The Vermont mountains have gone dark across the water; a line of pale sky remains above them. Silas does not think about his brother. He thinks about the new man on gun #3 and the sponge stroke — too short; leaves powder residue; will produce a misfire or worse. He will correct it in the morning drill.

7. **Last light:** The watch changes. Silas goes below. The gun deck is quiet; the guns are covered against the evening damp. He lies in his hammock and is asleep almost immediately.

## Outcome / Turn

Nothing happens. The guns are right. The day is the same day it was at the start. This is the outcome. The chapter's argument is in its refusal to foreshadow what follows.
