# Chapter 07 — At Anchor

**One-line summary:** Two days into the standoff; the *Saratoga*'s crew has settled into a sharpened routine; the gun crew is better than it was; Silas watches the smoke of the British encampment from the gun ports.

**POV:** Silas Ware  
**Tense:** Past  
**Function:** Approach 3

---

## Setting / Time

USS *Saratoga* at anchor, Plattsburgh Bay. September 7–9, 1814 — the standoff days; British on the north bank, Americans on the south bank; no British fleet on the lake.

Time span: Two days compressed to four or five scenes; the chapter has the quality of time suspended between events.

---

## Chapter Shape

**Bottle** — one location (the ship), two days' worth of time compressed. The chapter's mood is the standoff mood: everything ready, nothing happening, the readiness itself becoming its own kind of labor. The prose stays on the ship and renders the specific texture of waiting in a state of preparation.

---

## Ending Mode

**Resolved beat** — the chapter ends with the watch change, the guns covered for the night, Silas going below. The gun deck is right; the day is complete; the next day will be the same, or it won't. The chapter folds closed on this small resolution.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

1. **Morning (Sept 7 or 8) — the smoke to the north:** From the gun ports, crouching to the sill, Silas can see across the bay toward the north bank of the Saranac. Not the river itself — the trees line the bank — but above the tree line: three or four plumes of smoke from the British encampment. He watches them for a moment. They are the same gray as the morning. He notes the direction and goes to his guns.

2. **Gun drill — the improvement:** Fry runs gun #1's crew through the loading sequence. They are faster than they were a week ago; the sequence has become automatic. The Vermont farmer on gun #3 — the man with the slow sponge stroke — has internalized the correction; his arm moves right. Silas watches them without expression. Three rounds in four minutes and forty seconds by his mental count. He does not say this is good; he says: *"Again."*
   
   The powder monkey runs the cartridges without being told to vary his route. Silas notices this; the boy has worked out that the route along the starboard bulwark is faster in certain crew positions. This is initiative. Silas notes it.

3. **Macomb's call for volunteers:** News comes down through the gunner's mate: Brigadier General Macomb has asked the fleet for volunteers to fill out the infantry line on the south bank. The numbers of regulars are thin; militia are arriving but are not reliable. About a dozen men from the *Saratoga* will go ashore. Silas is not among them; the gunner's mate has not put his name on the list. He does not ask to be included. His place is the starboard battery. The men who go ashore are given a brief handshake on the deck; the departure is not ceremonious.

4. **The shot-to-grape ratio (afternoon):** The gunner's mate comes through the battery with an amended ammunition allocation: more grape shot and canister relative to roundshot than was allocated last week. Silas recounts his section's garland. The change is real: two round shot per gun have been replaced by prepared grape charges. He notes this without comment. At close range — if the British ships come in close — the grape is the better choice. He stores the implication.

5. **Distant musket fire from the river line (evening):** During the evening watch, there is a sustained exchange of musket fire from the direction of the river — the south bank, presumably the lower bridge or the village defenses. It lasts perhaps twenty minutes. The crew on the gun deck is still; they all listen. The ship is not under fire; this is land fire, a mile or more away, muffled by distance and the tree line. Silas listens. He counts the individual shots for a while, then stops counting.

6. **End of day:** The watch changes. The guns are covered with their tarpaulins against the night damp. Silas goes below. He eats his supper — salt pork, hardtack, coffee — at the mess table with Fry and two of the gun crew. They talk about the musket fire from the south bank, speculating on what happened. Silas listens; he does not speculate. He is thinking about the grape allocation.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The ship is ready. The gun crew is better than it was. The standoff is becoming a routine, which is its own danger — but Silas does not say this, because Silas does not think in those terms. He notes what is right and what needs correction. What is right: the drill. What needs watching: the grape allocation implies close action, which implies the British ships will come in tight.

---

## New Question or Pressure Raised

When will the British fleet appear? The lake has been empty for three days now. The army is here; the ships are not. The chapter ends in this suspension without naming it as suspense.

## Continuity Notes

- Jonas Fry and the powder monkey consistent with ch 1 and ch 3.
- The Vermont farmer on gun #3 is given a minor arc: slow sponge stroke (ch 1) → corrected by Silas (ch 3) → holding the correction here. This is the kind of small technical detail that establishes the gun section as a working unit.
- Macomb's call for fleet volunteers is historically attested (some sailors did serve ashore at Plattsburgh). Consistent with `canon/research.md`.
- The grape allocation change: historically consistent with close-action preparation. The British fleet would enter the bay in a confined space; close-range munitions made sense.
- Silas does not go ashore; he stays with his guns. This is not cowardice; it is the form of competence he has organized his life around.
