# Chapter 05 — The Report

**POV:** Dr. Imani Reyes  
**Tense:** third-person limited past  
**Section:** Approach  
**Target words:** ~2,400  
**Chapter shape:** multi-scene (yard; workstation; brief interaction; yard again; quarters)  
**Ending mode:** literary-fade  
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure

---

## One-Line Summary

Reyes writes and sends the formal report — the admission that she cannot determine the creature's nature — then returns to the yard in the evening to stand at what she cannot change.

---

## Setting / Time

The compound. The containment yard in the morning; her workstation in the compound; a brief encounter in a hallway or meeting room; the yard again at dusk; her quarters. Weeks before the dinner, but closer than Ch03. The dinner is now several weeks away, perhaps three or four.

---

## Goal

Reyes must write and send the report the president is waiting for — the formal admission that the study has not produced actionable intelligence about the creature's nature, origin, or significance. She has known this was coming; the act of sending it is different from knowing it.

---

## Obstacle

The obstacle is not external. The report must be accurate, and accuracy means stating what she cannot state: that she is increasingly certain the creature is intelligent, but has no evidence that would survive review. She cannot put that certainty in the report. She can only put what the methodology yielded, and what the methodology yielded is insufficient.

---

## Action Beats

- Morning at the yard. She runs a new acoustic protocol — a range of frequencies she has not tried in this configuration. The creature produces no response she can log as signal. She notes the absence and packs up. The absence is data; it is not the data she needs.
- At her workstation she opens the report template and begins drafting. The report's structure is formal: methodology employed, results obtained, conclusions supportable, conclusions unsupportable, recommendation. She writes through the first two sections steadily. The professional vocabulary in stride: *assay*, *protected contact*, *knock-down threshold attempts*, *behavioral response battery*, *cognitive task protocols*. No apology in the language. Just what she tried and what it produced.
- The section on unsupportable conclusions is where it slows. She writes: *This study cannot confirm or deny the specimen's capacity for higher cognition.* She looks at the sentence. It is exactly true and exactly insufficient. She writes the recommendation: *Proceed without behavioral intelligence.* She sends the report.
- A brief, functional interaction — with a presidential aide, or a logistics officer, or someone in the command apparatus who is coordinating the send-off. The meeting is procedural: confirmation that her report has been received, a question about whether she'll attend the ceremony. She answers plainly. Her presence at the send-off is not required but not prohibited. The interaction is short. She is not unkind; she is not particularly warm; she answers what is asked.
- That evening at the yard. She does not bring a formal field kit. She brings a notebook and the acoustic monitor out of habit; she sets neither up. She stands at the outer perimeter as the light changes.
- The hearing motif: the yard is quieter on her left than her right. The ring that is not constant but is not gone. She thinks — briefly, in the register she allows herself — about the vocalization eleven months ago. The question she has never been able to settle: attack, or the only word it had. The report is sent. Whatever answer there might have been is still behind the veil.
- She walks back in the dark. The chapter ends on the compound at night — the lights in the distant buildings, the yard behind her, the plain fact of where she is.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The report is sent. This is a small but irreversible act: she has formally confirmed that the marines will go in without intelligence, and she is the person who confirmed it. The literary fade is appropriate here — the chapter ends on an image (the compound at night), not on a declared meaning. The weight is in the act of having sent the document, not in Reyes's naming of that weight.

---

## New Question or Pressure

Is she responsible for what happens to the marines? The question is not asked in the prose. It is present in the structure of the chapter — she writes the report, she sends it, she stands at the yard that produced nothing — and Reyes's register does not permit direct statement of what this means to her. The reader feels it in the inventory.

---

## Continuity Notes

- **Establishes:** the formal report sent to the president, confirming the study's failure to produce actionable intelligence. From this point, it is canonical that Reyes has admitted in writing that she cannot determine the creature's nature.
- **The methodology inventory** in the report section is a place to expand on what has been tried. The reader should come away with a sense of the scope of the effort: sedation attempts (all failed), physical examination attempts (all failed), acoustic protocol battery, cognitive bias tests, behavioral observation across all seasons and times of day. Eleven months of effort.
- **The hearing motif** gets its first extended use here — not stamped on the chapter, but present as Reyes stands at the evening yard. The ring, the left ear's quality of silence, the memory of the vocalization. This should be two or three sentences at most.
- **The interaction with the aide/official** is brief and functional. It establishes that Reyes has the president's ear and his staff's deference — she is the only expert — but it should not be a scene that goes on long enough to develop.

---

## Craft Notes for Drafter

The report-writing section is the chapter's center of gravity. Reyes's professional language should carry it — the form of a formal scientific recommendation written by someone who knows the recommendation is a failure. The drafter should resist the temptation to dramatize the sending of the report (no pausing with hand over the send button; no moment of decision). She writes it, checks it for accuracy, sends it. The drama is in the accuracy of the language, not in the gesture of sending.

The evening yard scene is the chapter's emotional close. It should be short — four or five paragraphs — and entirely sensory and observational. Reyes does not name what she is doing there or why. She is there because she goes there. The chapter's final image should be specific and quiet: not the creature (it is behind the veil, not visible), but the yard itself, the failing light, the ring in the left ear.
