# Chapter 06 — Dry Runs

**POV:** Corporal Eli Brandt  
**Tense:** third-person limited past  
**Section:** Approach  
**Target words:** ~2,400  
**Chapter shape:** action-heavy (organized around a full training day)  
**Ending mode:** resolved-beat  
**Opening texture:** drill-or-practice

---

## One-Line Summary

A full training day: five dry runs of the mission sequence, banter that is the medium of coping, and the gap in Brandt's response to a direct question that is as close as the chapter gets to his interior.

---

## Setting / Time

The drill field and a cleared warehouse space on the compound grounds. Pre-dawn to late afternoon. The Contingency is several weeks away; the unit has been running this sequence long enough that it is starting to become automatic, which is not the same as being ready.

---

## Goal

The immediate goal is physical and procedural: run the sequence correctly five times in a row. The chapter's deeper concern is the texture of what men do with fear when fear has no outlet — how the day is inhabited.

---

## Obstacle

Repetition is its own obstacle. By the fourth or fifth run, the body is tired and the mind has learned to predict the sequence so well that it stops producing useful information. A CSO on his fifth dry run knows every corner; what he doesn't know is what it will be like when the sequence is real and the corners have something in them. This is the gap that can't be drilled out.

---

## Action Beats

- Pre-dawn. The unit assembles at the drill space. The Gunny runs them through the day's schedule without ceremony: five runs, break, three runs, end-of-day admin. Nobody argues. Someone makes a comment about the hour; someone else responds; the exchange is fast and profane and moves on.
- First and second run. The sequence in physical detail — not gear-fetish detail, but the body's knowledge of it: the specific sequence of movements, the positions, the timing between elements. The unit runs clean. The Gunny adjusts one element after the second run; they incorporate the adjustment and go again.
- The break. Geedunk, water, the specific physical relief of bodies that have been working. The banter is at full pitch here — complaint-heavy, referential, the in-group language running at native speed. The jokes are callbacks to earlier unit history. A newer member of the unit tries to match the register and slightly overshoots; the response is mild mockery, not cruelty. The president's son is in the break too, drinking water, unremarkable, in the laugh when something is funny.
- Third and fourth run. The fatigue is real now. Someone misses a timing beat; the Gunny calls it; they run it again. The fifth run is slower but cleaner — the correction has landed.
- An exchange between Brandt and a unit member he's known since selection — not the Gunny, someone closer to his rank. The other man asks, again or for the first time depending on the day, why Brandt put his name on the list. The question is not hostile; it's the question men ask each other when they have time and nothing better to cover. Brandt's answer: the gate is the only door still open. He says this. He means more than this and doesn't say the more. The other man thinks about it for a moment, nods, and changes the subject. The subject does not return.
- End of day. Admin — a brief check-in with the Gunny about tomorrow's schedule, gear maintenance, the routine winding-down. Brandt's body is tired in the honest way of a day's work. The day resolves: the five runs are complete, the unit is together, tomorrow will run again.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The day is done. The five runs are logged. The resolved beat is the small completion of a training day that did what training days do: it practiced, corrected, and ended. Brandt's conviction — the gate is the only door left open — has surfaced briefly and been accepted without interrogation. The chapter's turn is the absence of complication: the unit functions, the work continues, the banter is the container for everything that can't be said directly.

---

## New Question or Pressure

The question under the chapter's surface: what does Brandt actually believe he'll find on the other side? The gate is the last real door; but a door to what? This question is not raised in the prose. It lives in the gap between what Brandt says (the gate is the only door still open) and what he believes (something waits on the other side that is worth the one-way cost). The reader may feel this gap. The prose does not point at it.

---

## Continuity Notes

- **The unit's social dynamics** should be established more fully here than in Ch02 and Ch04. By now the reader should have a sense of at least two or three named unit members: the man Brandt talks to (perhaps the one who asked the question in Ch04, recurring), the unit FNG, and the president's son as a peripheral presence.
- **The president's son** drills, breaks, and functions within the unit. He is not given special dialogue or special interiority — he is simply present. If he says anything, it should be in the flow of unit banter, indistinguishable from what anyone would say.
- **The Gunny** is the chapter's operational spine. His corrections are brief and technical; he does not motivate or philosophize. He runs the drill.
- **No reference to the ceremony specifics** — Brandt may know that the send-off involves a dinner and a platform, but the chapter is organized around the drill, not around anticipation of the night.

---

## Craft Notes for Drafter

The physical texture of the drill is the chapter's main material. The drafter should resist the temptation to make the dry runs dramatic — they are not. They are repetitive, corrective, exhausting. The point is the quality of presence required to run the same sequence five times without mental retreat. The banter in the break is the chapter's emotional center: this is where the coping register operates at full capacity, and it should feel natural rather than performed.

Brandt's answer to the question — the gate is the only door still open — should be delivered without emphasis. Not a speech; not a confession. A sentence he has available. The other man's response (a nod, a subject change) is the chapter's primary piece of interiority: it tells us that this answer has landed as true, and that no further discussion is available or wanted.

The resolved beat should feel genuinely small. The day was what it was. The unit is together. That is enough.
