# Chapter 13 — The Yard

**POV:** Dr. Imani Reyes  
**Tense:** third-person limited past  
**Section:** The send-off night  
**Target words:** ~3,600  
**Chapter shape:** bottle (one location, near real-time)  
**Ending mode:** turn  
**Opening texture:** arrival

---

## One-Line Summary

Reyes at the creature's yard during the roll call; she sees it more fully than ever before; the creature vocalizes — differently than the attack, not aimed at her, at something she cannot identify.

---

## Setting / Time

The containment yard. The same evening as Ch11 and Ch12, during the roll call — which Reyes can sense at a distance through the platform's periodic activations. Near-real-time; this chapter covers roughly the middle third of the roll call.

---

## Goal

Reyes came to the yard because she was pulled here, and she is here now. Whatever she was hoping to find or observe, it is not articulated — even to herself. She is the scientist at the end of her methodology, standing at the outer barrier with the cloth veil between her and what she has spent eleven months trying to understand.

---

## Obstacle

The creature is veiled. She cannot see it fully, or she couldn't — until the veil is no longer adequate, or she moves to a new angle, or something in the creature's own behavior changes the geometry of what she can observe. The obstacle is everything it has always been: the impenetrable interface between her and the thing she cannot prove.

---

## Action Beats

- Reyes pushes through the service door from the corridor into the yard's outer space. The compound's ceremony sounds are muffled here — the hall is audible as a murmur, the string quartet distant. The yard is dimly lit. She has her field kit but sets nothing up.
- She moves to the outer barrier and stands. The cloth veil is in place. But the creature is present — she can hear it, or the yard has a quality that it has when the creature is active. Something different from other nights.
- Behind her, at the compound's edge: a low vibration. Not sound exactly — or sound at the bottom of what she can feel through her chest and through the damaged left ear more than through the right. The platform. It has activated. One name has gone through.
- The creature, behind the veil: a sound. Not the vocalization — not yet. A lower register, the kind the acoustic monitor catches but that requires attention to hear directly. Reyes adjusts her position to the left of the outer barrier, where the veil's drape leaves a gap she has never tried to use before. She can see a portion of the inner space.
- She sees the creature. Not fully — not the way she would if she were inside the inner barrier. But more than she has seen in months. What she sees is bestial: the scale of it, the form, the quality of movement that is unlike anything she has a frame for. She observes and her hands record nothing; the field log is not open. She is just watching.
- The platform again, felt through the compound's walls more than heard. Another name. The creature reacts — Reyes cannot say how with certainty, but it reacts. Not to her presence; she is still. Its attention, if it has attention in any meaningful sense, is directed elsewhere. Toward the hall, or toward the vibration from the platform, or toward something she cannot identify.
- More names. The roll call is progressing. Each platform activation is felt through the yard — subtle, not dramatic, but present. The creature's behavior between activations and during them is the chapter's primary observation. Reyes watches. She does not have equipment to log what she is seeing. It does not match her previous logged observations. It is different tonight.
- Then the vocalization. Not the attack — not the sustained, aimed sound that cost her the hearing eleven months ago. Something else: shorter, less sustained, directed not at Reyes but at the direction of the hall, at the sky, at something outside the yard's walls. The sound is in a range Reyes can hear, partially — the right ear catches more of it than the left. The physical effect is mild but real: a pressure in her chest, a momentary threshold drop in the left ear that resolves in a few seconds.
- The yard goes still. The creature is present, the veil in its new angle, the gap showing more than she has seen in months. Reyes stands at the outer barrier and does not move.
- The chapter ends on the turn: the vocalization was not the attack, was not an accident, was aimed at something she cannot identify — the hall, the gate, the night. She is certain of this and cannot prove it. This is the most she has ever had.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The turn is the vocalization — specifically its direction and character. This is not the creature attacking Reyes; this is the creature doing something in response to the platform's activations, or to the night's events, or to something inaccessible to Reyes's observation. She cannot conclude what it means. She has more tonight than she has ever had, and it is still not enough. The turn recontextualizes the eleven months of study without resolving the question: the creature is responsive to something on this night in a way it has not been before, and Reyes cannot say what that something is.

---

## New Question or Pressure

Is the creature responding to the gate being activated? Does it know what the gate is? Is knowing even a concept that applies to it? These questions are the chapter's legacy — they carry into Ch15 and Ch16 without being answered.

## Continuity Notes

- **Physical description of the creature:** this is the chapter where the reader gets the fullest view. The description must be rendered through Reyes's observational frame — precise, hedged, the vocabulary of a scientist seeing something the existing vocabulary cannot accommodate. What it looks like: bestial in form, scale that is specific (larger or smaller than what you might expect, but always specific), movement that has no domestic animal analog. The prose should not describe it as monstrous in the genre sense; it should describe it as specifically, deeply foreign. The specifics of its appearance are the drafter's to create, within the constraint that the creature is never given a comfortable frame — not cute, not cosmic, not explicable.
- **The platform's vibrations:** felt through the compound's floor and walls, not heard as a sound. This is the chapter's chronological connection to Ch12 and Ch14: the platform is activating during this chapter, one name at a time, and the creature's behavior correlates.
- **The vocalization:** distinct from the attack vocalization in Ch05's memory. Not aimed at Reyes. Not sustained. More like what the research notes call a *contact call* — a vocalization directed outward, at something outside the enclosure. The drafter should not use that term explicitly (it implies a frame Reyes cannot support), but the quality of it is: this is not a defense; this is a response to something Reyes cannot see.
- **The hearing motif:** the left ear catches the vocalization's lower registers differently than the right. The pressure is mild compared to the attack vocalization; Reyes's hearing does not worsen. The chapter's sensory detail should include the specific quality of how the sound arrives through her damaged hearing.
- **The two POVs do not meet:** this chapter and Ch12 are simultaneous. Brandt is in the formation inside the hall; Reyes is in the yard. They are on the same compound, the same night, the same half-hour, and they never see each other or know of each other.

## Craft Notes for Drafter

This is the book's longest Reyes chapter and its emotional spine. The brief's description of the send-off night's central image — "she drifts from the hall, follows a sound to the veiled cage, sees the thing whole, and the scream" — is realized here. The challenge is to render this scene in Reyes's register without it becoming horror or revelation. It is neither. It is what it always has been: an observation she cannot interpret definitively. The creature is simply more visible tonight than usual, and it does something she has not seen it do before, and she still cannot conclude from it.

The platform's correlating activations — felt through the compound walls — are the chapter's structural mechanism: they connect this scene to the roll call in Ch12 and Ch14 without the two POVs ever meeting. The creature's reactions around the platform activations are the chapter's key observations, and they must be rendered with Reyes's characteristic epistemic restraint: *consistent with*, *cannot rule out*, *no prior observation supports a different interpretation*. The prose neither confirms that the creature knows what the gate is nor denies it.

The vocalization at the chapter's close is the turn. It must be handled without drama: the sound arrives, Reyes feels its effects, it ends, the yard goes still. The significance is in what Reyes observes about its direction — not at her, but at something else. That's all. The chapter ends on her standing at the barrier. The field log is still closed.
