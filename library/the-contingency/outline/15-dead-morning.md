# Chapter 15 — Dead Morning

**POV:** Dr. Imani Reyes  
**Tense:** third-person limited past  
**Section:** Day-after  
**Target words:** ~2,200  
**Chapter shape:** single scene / interiority-heavy  
**Ending mode:** elliptical  
**Opening texture:** interior (Reyes awake in the dark hours)

---

## One-Line Summary

Reyes in the dead of the early morning — she goes to the empty platform, then to the yard; what the night did not resolve is what she carries into the dark.

---

## Setting / Time

The compound. The dead hours — 0200 to perhaps 0430. The ceremony is over. The hall is empty or being cleaned. The platform is gray and still. The yard is quiet. Reyes has not slept or has woken and knows she will not sleep again.

---

## Goal

There is no productive goal left. She goes to the platform because she needs to see it empty. She goes to the yard because she goes to the yard. The chapter is about what a person does with themselves when the thing they were working toward has ended and nothing has resolved.

---

## Obstacle

The obstacle is the absence of any available action. The marines are gone. The creature is behind the veil. The platform is empty. Her certainty is unchanged and unchanged means unproven. There is nowhere to take the weight of the night and nothing to do with what she knows.

---

## Action Beats

- Reyes is in her quarters — awake, or just awake. The compound is quiet, not the quiet of a night-before but the quiet of aftermath. She hears the cleanup sounds from the hall: chairs being stacked, something being moved out. These sounds resolve and stop.
- She gets up. She does not dress formally — she is a person who has not slept or who stopped sleeping and no longer expects to. She moves through the compound.
- The platform. She goes to see it. It is in the hall or adjacent to it: the gray slab, slightly elevated, nondescript. Nobody is guarding it; the ceremony is over. She stands near it. She does not touch it. What it looked like with a person on it — the wash of light, the absence that followed — she knows from reports and now, from having missed seeing it directly (she was at the yard). What it looks like without a person on it: a slab of gray material, a piece of functional equipment that still works.
- She thinks about the technology. The country that can barely cater a dinner has a working teleport. The gate works; the catering doesn't. This thought arrives not as irony — she is past the register of irony — but as the fact of the world she has spent eleven months in. The gate is real. The marines are gone. The platform is empty.
- The hearing: in the quiet compound, at this hour, the ring in her left ear is more present than usual. Nothing to mask it. She is aware of the partial silence on the left side, the way the platform room's ambient sound arrives unevenly.
- She leaves the platform and walks to the yard. The service corridor, the service door, the outer perimeter. The yard is quiet. The cloth veil is in place. The creature is present, or she believes it is — the specific quality of the yard when the creature is in it is different from the yard when it isn't.
- She stands at the outer barrier. She thinks about the vocalization she heard tonight — the one that wasn't the attack, that was aimed at something she couldn't identify. She runs through what she observed, methodically, the way she always does. Two of the three metrics she can hold in memory: the direction of the vocalization, the timing relative to the platform's activations. One interpretation. Several interpretations. No conclusion.
- The question she has always had: was the creature's response to the platform tonight the response of something that knows what the gate is? Was it a contact call? Was it recognition? She cannot answer. She cannot rule out that it is. She cannot rule out that it isn't. The only word she has in any direction is *consistent with*, and *consistent with* is not a conclusion.
- She stays at the outer barrier in the dark. The chapter ends on this: Reyes at the yard, the platform somewhere behind her, the vocalization from the night unrepeated, the certainty she can't use.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The elliptical ending: the chapter refuses to resolve because there is nothing to resolve. The night happened. The platform fired fourteen times. Brandt is gone. The creature vocalized at something Reyes cannot name. She is at the outer barrier in the dark and she has what she has always had: certainty without evidence. The chapter ends here, in the middle of the holding, not at its conclusion.

---

## New Question or Pressure

What will she do with what she cannot prove? This question presses but is not answered in this chapter. It carries into Ch16.

---

## Continuity Notes

- **The platform empty:** described precisely and without decoration. It is functional equipment. It is also the thing that took fourteen people somewhere she cannot go and cannot describe. Both are true; the prose does not choose between them.
- **The timing of this chapter:** the dead hours, well before dawn. The compound is between the end of the ceremony and the beginning of the regular workday. This is the specific temporal pocket of aftermath.
- **The yard at this hour:** the creature is present but not active in any way Reyes can log. The veil is in place. This is the same yard she has stood at for eleven months and it is different tonight only in that she is here after a ceremony that has ended and cannot be undone.
- **The hearing motif:** most present at this hour, in the quiet. The ring, the left ear's partial silence, the way sounds arrive unevenly. Not foregrounded; present.
- **No connection to Brandt:** Reyes does not know Corporal Eli Brandt existed. She was never told who the marines were individually. She watched none of the roll call. She does not know that any individual believed more or less than any other. The platform took fourteen people; she knows only that.

---

## Craft Notes for Drafter

This chapter is the book's second-quietest (after Ch16). The challenge is to render genuine aftermath without sentimentality and without the flatness that can come from a chapter whose purpose is primarily to hold. The chapter earns its weight by being specific — specific about what the platform looks like empty, specific about the yard in the dead hours, specific about Reyes's methodological mind running through the vocalization's evidence even at 0300.

The interiority here is the most extended of any Reyes chapter, but it must remain anchored in the physical throughout. She is at the platform; then she is at the yard. The thinking happens in those locations, in that body, in the specific sensory conditions of the dead hours. The prose does not lift above her.

The elliptical ending is the book's second-to-last chapter's function. It does not resolve; it holds. The holding should feel honest rather than evasive — this is not a chapter that is refusing to say what it knows; it is a chapter that is representing, faithfully, a person who does not have a resolution available. The final image is Reyes at the outer barrier. Nothing more.
