# Chapter 03 — What She Tried

**POV:** Dr. Imani Reyes  
**Tense:** third-person limited past  
**Section:** Approach  
**Target words:** ~2,200  
**Chapter shape:** single scene / interiority-heavy at close  
**Ending mode:** turn  
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure

---

## One-Line Summary

The Contingency has been named and a deadline set; Reyes runs a new behavioral protocol at the yard, the creature does something she cannot interpret or dismiss, and the chapter ends on a datum she has no frame for.

---

## Setting / Time

The containment yard; then briefly Reyes's workstation in the compound. Mid-morning. Several weeks after Ch01 — the Contingency has been officially designated and the send-off date is known. The clock is real now.

---

## Goal

Reyes wants a single piece of conclusive behavioral evidence that the creature is intelligent — something that could be documented, that would hold up to review. She has a deadline she didn't have before.

---

## Obstacle

The same obstacle as always: the methodology fails because the subject fails to cooperate with methodology. What changes is the weight of the failure — Reyes now knows that failure is the likely final outcome, and the marines will go in without intelligence either way.

---

## Action Beats

- Reyes arrives at the outer perimeter with a modified setup — a cognitive bias test frame, one she has tried before in a different configuration. She has been reconfiguring her approach since the announcement. The inner cloth veil is in place.
- While she prepares the setup, her mind runs through what she has tried. Not a formal inventory — that is what the log is for — but the specific quality of a researcher who has exhausted a method and is reaching for the next one. What the sedation trials produced (nothing; no sedative has achieved the knock-down threshold). What the acoustic stimuli produced. What the cognitive task setup produced before she lost the equipment. Her vocabulary is technical throughout; the emotion is in the inventory's length.
- She runs the protocol. The setup is designed to present ambiguous stimuli that an intelligent subject would respond to with a bias — differently depending on whether it is anxious or calm, and in a patterned way that would be statistically distinct from random. The acoustic monitor is logging.
- The creature does something. The veil moves. There is a sound — not the vocalization, nothing close to that; just a low register that the monitor catches before she does. A shift in the thermal trace. She watches. She notes the time and takes a second reading.
- The datum is this: the movement pattern is not random by two of the three metrics she is watching. Two of three is not significance. She knows this. She writes it down anyway with the exact language of what it is: *consistent with a negative judgment bias response*; *cannot rule out environmental interference*; *not replicable on this timeline with current equipment*.
- She packs up. At her workstation she enters the note. She looks at it in the log. She cannot send this to the president. It is not evidence. It is a shape that might be evidence if she had more time, different equipment, a subject that cooperated.
- She closes the log. The dinner is in a fixed number of weeks. She will be at the yard again tomorrow.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The turn is the datum. Something happened that she cannot interpret and cannot dismiss. Two of three metrics showed a non-random pattern on a single trial. This is not proof of anything. But it is different from nothing, and Reyes has had eleven months of nothing. The chapter ends on this unresolvable new shape — not with her certainty increasing, exactly, but with her uncertainty becoming more specific.

---

## New Question or Pressure

Is the datum real, or is it the shape that her need to find something imposes on noise? The question is not answered. The chapter doesn't ask it out loud — Reyes's register doesn't permit that kind of declaration — but the reader feels it in the precision with which she notes what the evidence is and isn't.

---

## Continuity Notes

- **Establishes:** the Contingency has been named and the send-off scheduled (date not given; "several weeks away" is sufficient); Reyes has been told the marines will go in regardless of what she finds.
- **The cognitive bias test** from the research file enters in stride — not explained, just used. The reader does not need to understand it formally; Reyes's precise professional disappointment with the results is what carries the meaning.
- **The inner barrier** is not entered. Reyes works from the outer perimeter throughout. The fact that she has been inside only twice and will not go again should be present as context without being stated as rule — it lives in how she sets up: always at the outer barrier, always the same perimeter stations.
- **The hearing motif** has a natural place here: the acoustic monitor catches the creature's low-register sound before Reyes does, and she adjusts the monitor's output to her right ear. Brief, not foregrounded.
- The conversation with the president (or a proxy) in which Reyes formally delivered her failure — the admission that they cannot learn what the creature is — has already happened before this chapter. It is background here; the drafter should not dramatize it unless it surfaces in Reyes's interiority as a specific memory.

---

## Craft Notes for Drafter

The inventory of what Reyes has tried is the chapter's spine. Keep it specific and professional — each item in the catalog is a named methodology that failed in a particular way. The register is the opposite of despair: it is the voice of someone who has been failed by her tools and is trying the next one. The emotion is in the list's length and specificity, not in any sentence that names it.

The datum at the end must be handled with precision: not ambiguous in the hand-wavy sense, but ambiguous in the specific epistemological sense of a researcher who knows exactly what "consistent with" and "cannot rule out" mean. The prose should neither inflate the significance of what she observed nor deflate it. She saw what she saw. It is not enough. She noted it exactly.
