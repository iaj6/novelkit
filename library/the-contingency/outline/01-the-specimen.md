# Chapter 01 — The Specimen

**POV:** Dr. Imani Reyes  
**Tense:** third-person limited past  
**Section:** Day-before  
**Target words:** ~2,000  
**Chapter shape:** bottle (one location, near real-time)  
**Ending mode:** literary-fade  
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure

---

## One-Line Summary

Reyes's ordinary evening at the containment yard — procedure, non-result, the creature present and unreachable.

---

## Setting / Time

The containment yard behind the compound's service wing. Late afternoon into dusk. The compound is calm; there is no ceremony scheduled, no visitors, nothing unusual in the building behind her. A day like the last thirty days.

---

## Goal

Reyes wants what she always wants here: some behavioral datum that resolves itself into meaning. She does not expect one. She comes anyway.

---

## Obstacle

The creature does not yield to observation. Every attempt to study it by force has already been made; what remains is the form of study that produces nothing usable — watching, logging, waiting. The obstacle is not the creature's resistance so much as the fundamental condition of the work: the act of study changes what is being studied, and the subject has long since stopped responding to most stimuli.

---

## Action Beats

- Reyes arrives at the outer perimeter with her field kit. She checks the sensors mounted on the outer chain-link — respiration indicator, thermal trace, acoustic monitor. She logs the readings. Numbers within the existing range; nothing has changed since yesterday.
- She settles at the outer barrier on her folding stool and opens her observation log. The inner cloth veil is in place — has been since the second removal three weeks ago caused the equipment loss. She notes: veil intact. Time. Temperature.
- She runs a passive acoustic sample — the monitor logs whatever the yard produces. She does not try to introduce stimuli today; she tried the battery of acoustic stimuli last week and the week before. She is past the stimulus phase for now.
- She observes for fifty minutes. The cloth veil shifts once — movement behind it, or wind, she cannot determine which. She logs the shift. Time, duration, whether wind from outside the compound could account for it. The variables are all there. They do not resolve.
- She packs the kit. Walks back through the service corridor, past the building's kitchen (cold tonight, no deliveries scheduled), through the hall's back passage. The hall is dark. She does not linger. She goes to her quarters.
- In her quarters she transcribes from the field log to her formal record. Eleven months of entries like this one. She closes the file and does not open it again tonight.

---

## Outcome / Turn

There is no turn in the conventional sense. The chapter's function is establishment: this is what ordinary looks like in Reyes's life. The creature is present and unreadable. The methodology is exhausted and she comes back anyway. The prose observes without foreshadowing; there is no signal that anything will change.

The turn, such as it is: the cloth veil shifted once. She logged it. She does not know if it was movement or wind. She will not know. This is the shape of every day.

---

## New Question or Pressure

None raised explicitly. The reader has met Reyes and her world; they have felt the quality of the work before knowing its stakes.

---

## Continuity Notes

- **Introduces:** the containment yard (outer perimeter, inner barrier, cloth veil, chain-link construction); the creature's presence and inaccessibility; Reyes's professional vocabulary in stride (*assay*, *protected contact*, *hot animal*, *specimen*, *the knock-down threshold* may appear in her mental inventory); the field log and observation record; the compound layout (service wing, hall, kitchen area).
- **The hearing motif** is available but should be used sparingly here — perhaps the acoustic monitor's output is slightly different on her left side, or she adjusts the earpiece to her right ear out of habit. Not foregrounded. Just present.
- **The inner barrier visit** (where the vocalization occurred) is not dramatized here, but its consequence — the cloth veil, the fact that she will not go inside the inner barrier again — should be present in the physical setup of the yard as Reyes encounters it.
- No mention of the Contingency in this chapter. The send-off has not been scheduled yet, or if it has been announced in the broader compound, it has not yet touched Reyes's immediate world.
- The creature is not described fully here. The reader gets the yard, the veil, the evidence of a presence. Full physical description is deferred to Ch13.

---

## Craft Notes for Drafter

This chapter carries the weight of the book's opening register. Reyes's voice must be established distinctly in the first paragraph. The scientific vocabulary enters without italics or gloss. The chapter is deliberately undramatic — it is an ordinary day, and the prose trusts that ordinariness to do its work. No foreshadowing, no hovering significance, no sentence that announces what this night will eventually become. The heading exemplar is Never Let Me Go's early chapters: the horror assembles beneath the ordinary; the ordinary is not a frame for it, it is the thing itself.

Length discipline: this chapter earns its 2,000 words through density of specific procedure, not through narrative expansion. If the drafter finds themselves adding a scene after the yard, cut it. The chapter ends in Reyes's quarters, the file closed, and nothing more.
