# Chapter 02 — First Light

**POV:** Corporal Eli Brandt  
**Tense:** third-person limited past  
**Section:** Day-before  
**Target words:** ~2,000  
**Chapter shape:** time-skip / montage (covers roughly 0400–2100)  
**Ending mode:** resolved-beat  
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure

---

## One-Line Summary

Brandt's ordinary training day before the list existed — PT, drill, chow, close quarters, the texture of enlisted life not yet chosen for the gate.

---

## Setting / Time

The marine compound on the federal grounds: barracks, PT course, drill area, chow hall, barracks again. Pre-dawn to lights-down. A day like the last sixty days; no mission yet named, no ceremony scheduled.

---

## Goal

No explicit goal. Brandt does his job. The chapter is about texture, not trajectory — what a day is when you're an enlisted man in a special-operations unit that is waiting for the thing it was made for.

---

## Obstacle

There is no antagonist. The obstacle is the temporal condition of this life: hurry up and wait. Brandt is well-trained and underemployed. The gap between capability and deployment is the central friction of the chapter.

---

## Action Beats

- Before 0400. Brandt wakes without an alarm — a habit the training made, a body that doesn't need permission to start. He dresses in the dark without waking his bunkmate. Outside: the compound at first light, the PT course.
- The run. Two or three other men also up early. The compound in the cold pre-dawn, their breath, the specific muscle-knowledge of a body that has done this route enough times to stop needing to think about it. The banter while running is minimal — just presence, acknowledgment. The register: short, a little profane, comfortable. This is not combat; this is morning.
- Chow. The chow line. At least one named unit member is introduced here — someone Brandt has been around long enough to have a running dynamic with. The gallows register is present in how they eat, complain about the food, observe what's on the tray. The food is what it is. Someone has a specific complaint about a specific item. This is the register.
- Morning drill. The unit runs a sequence — not the Contingency sequence (that hasn't been designed yet), just the standard rotation. Five or six men at a time through a specific pattern. Repetition. The Gunny runs it without comment; correction happens fast and moves on. The drill is not dramatic. It is the thing they do.
- The long afternoon. The gap between morning training and any further obligation. Close quarters: the barracks, someone playing something on a device, a card game, the specific social physics of men in a shared space with nothing urgent to do. The banter is different here — slower, more referential, callbacks to unit history. It is a language they share. A new man (FNG) says something that reveals he doesn't share it yet. The older hands don't explain; they just move on.
- Evening. Brandt cleans his gear — the specific ritual of upkeep that is not just maintenance but the thing you do when you have nothing else to do and you're waiting. It's something to do with your hands. The day wraps. The barracks settle.
- Lights-down. A beat of quiet. Brandt doesn't think anything important. He's tired in the specific way of a body that has earned it. The chapter ends on this — the small, complete resolution of a day that was what it was.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The day ends. Nothing has changed. This is the resolved beat: the day was whole. Brandt is exactly where he started, has done what he was supposed to do, and the unit is intact. No turn in the external world; the resolution is simply the completion of a day.

## Craft Notes for Drafter

The voice contrast with Ch01 must be immediate. Reyes's first paragraph was measured, formally balanced, precise in scientific vocabulary. Brandt's first paragraph should be shorter sentences, different vocabulary, the texture of a body in motion rather than a mind at the barrier. The chapter earns its 2,000 words through the density and authenticity of the enlisted register — not through plot. Do not manufacture incident. The day was uneventful and the prose should honor that. The two failure modes to avoid: (1) generic military SF furniture (gear fetishism, tactical cool), and (2) banter that is too witty, too scripted — the *Generation Kill* warning. The humor is repetitive, call-and-response, referential. It does not land jokes for the reader; it is simply how these men talk.
