# Characters — *The Contingency*

## POV Characters

### Dr. Imani Reyes — Age 52 — she/her

Xenobiologist. Former field researcher, now the person who knows the most about the creature, which is to say: the person who has spent eleven months learning about the limits of knowing. She told the president plainly that they cannot learn what the creature is, and that the marines will go in without intelligence. That admission cost her nothing professionally; there is no one else. She has the run of the compound and the president's ear as a result of her failure, which is not lost on her.

**Inner drives.** The need to know is the deepest thing she has. What torments her is not failure in the abstract but a specific epistemic situation: she is increasingly, unprovably certain the creature is intelligent, and she cannot make that certainty actionable. The creature has not confirmed it. The equipment has not confirmed it. The methodology has not confirmed it. The act of attempting study alters what is being studied. She goes back to the yard each night anyway.

**Voice and register.** Spare, precise. Her interiority arrives in the vocabulary of method — what she has tried, what has not worked, what she cannot rule out. She does not name her grief or dread directly. The emotional content reaches the reader through the structure of what she observes and cannot resolve. Her professional vocabulary (*containment*, *assay*, *protected contact*, *the knock-down threshold*, *specimen*, *hot animal*, *wild-caught individual*) enters without explanation. She does not lecture; she inhabits.

**The hearing.** Her left ear is partially deaf. On her second visit inside the inner barrier, the creature made a sound directly at her — sustained, aimed, nothing like its previous sounds. She lost the hearing in stages in the days that followed: the first day just a pressure, the second day the threshold shifted and did not shift back. What she recovered is incomplete. On the left side, voices arrive as if through water. A ring that is not quite constant but is not gone. She has never been able to decide whether the vocalization was an attack or the only word it had. That unresolvability is the thing that keeps her returning.

**Relationships.** She has the president's attention when she needs it. She does not have allies or colleagues in this story — the work has been lonely by nature. The creature is the closest thing she has to a subject she cares about, and she cannot know it.

### Corporal Eli Brandt — Age 27 — he/him

Critical Skills Operator, USMC, E-4 (Corporal). He is on the Contingency because he put his name on the list, and he put his name on the list because he believes. The gate is the last real door left open. Going through at the point of the spear is worth the one-way cost. The belief is sincere and is never undercut by the prose as naïveté. The book may let the reader fear he is wrong; it never lets the narration sneer at him for hoping.

**Inner drives.** Conviction, not recklessness. He is not suicidal; he is purpose-organized. In a country that has run out of futures, the gate is a future, and he wants to be part of whatever is on the other side. This is the rare man in the story who is not primarily defined by what he cannot know — or rather, he knows he doesn't know and has chosen to go anyway. That choice is the difference between him and everyone else.

**Voice and register.** Faster, coarser. The enlisted register in its natural habitat: profane, complaint-heavy, dysphemistic, referential to in-group events and running jokes. The gallows humor is not decoration and not a wink — it is how frightened men carry weight they cannot put down. The profanity is punctuation and compression, not shock. Sentences shorter, blunter. USMC vocabulary in idiom (*soup sandwich*, *Gunny*, *boot*, *FNG*, *zero-dark-thirty*, *hurry up and wait*) without phonetic spelling or dialect exaggeration. The sincerity underneath is never announced; it lives in what he does, not what he says.

**Physical texture.** The unit has been drilling on an accelerated clock for six weeks. Brandt carries this in a specific way: PT schedule, dry runs, the weight of gear in the staging area, the administrative lines he has stood in, the forms he has signed in triplicate. He is fit in a way that is specific to the training, not to genre description.

**Relationships.** His unit — fourteen people in an improvised barracks who have been living in each other's space for six weeks. The president's son is in the same formation, name on the same list, and the unit has not fully processed this fact. Brandt watches him from the enlisted angle: as a fact, not a symbol.

## Secondary Characters (Non-POV)

### The President — Age ~62

Not a satirical figure and not a symbol. A real, diminished man holding a real, diminished office. He was elected — there are still elections. He presides over an apparatus that can still fold space and can barely cater a dinner. He hosts the send-off because it is what the office requires; he reads the names because that is the roll call. He does not know his son's name is on the list until he reads it from the page. The moment of that reading — the beat between the word and the understanding — is the emotional axis of the send-off night and must not be over-explained. His grief is rendered without being milked. He is seen from below: through Brandt in the formation, through Reyes across the hall. He is not a POV character.

### The President's Son — Age ~29

Never a POV character. Seen only from below. In Brandt's chapters: in the formation, one name in the line, drills at the same hours. In Reyes's chapter of the send-off: a figure glimpsed across the hall before the roll call begins. His reasons for putting his name on the list are never given from inside his perspective — not in flashback, not in narration, not in dialogue that explains it. He is present in the story as a fact. His father reads his name aloud.

### The Creature

Not a character in the narrative sense — it has no POV, no translated thoughts, no language the prose decodes. It is an animal (possibly more than an animal) that came through the gate and is now caged in the yard behind the hall. Bestial in form. Cannot be sedated. Resists all study attempted by force. Made one vocalization — aimed at Reyes, sustained, unlike any prior sound — that cost her the use of her left ear at full capacity. That vocalization has not been repeated in the eleven months since. The prose neither confirms nor denies its intelligence. It is referred to as *the specimen* in Reyes's register.
