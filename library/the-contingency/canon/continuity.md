# Continuity — *The Contingency*

Hard facts that must not break in later drafting. Numbered list.

1. The story is set in near-future America, several generations after a gradual resource collapse. The United States still exists as a functional government. The grandeur is largely gone; the technology is not.
2. The gate folds space to one destination. Nothing sent through the gate has ever returned. The mechanism of this one-way quality is never explained — by any character, by any narration, by any dialogue.
3. Dr. Imani Reyes is a xenobiologist, approximately age 52. She is the book's first POV character.
4. Corporal Eli Brandt is a US Marine Corps Critical Skills Operator, E-4, approximately age 27. He is the book's second POV character.
5. The two POV characters never meet, speak, or know of each other. The send-off ceremony is the only event they share in the same physical space.
6. The chapters alternate POV — Reyes, Brandt, Reyes, Brandt — until Brandt's final chapter. His final chapter ends at the moment of the platform flash. His thread does not resume.
7. The book contains approximately 16 chapters. Reyes has approximately 9; Brandt has approximately 7. The asymmetry is intentional and structural, not a flaw to balance.
8. The book's four-part structure is non-negotiable: **Day-before** / **Approach** / **The send-off night** / **Day-after (Reyes only)**.
9. The creature came through the gate and is caged in a walled yard behind the banquet hall. It cannot be sedated. It has resisted all study by force. It is not given a POV, a language, or a translated thought.
10. Reyes's partial deafness (left ear) was caused by the creature's vocalization during her second visit inside the inner barrier. Whether that vocalization was an attack or communication is never resolved.
11. The creature's nature and intelligence stay unresolved throughout the book. The prose neither confirms nor denies what it is.
12. The president reads the names at the roll call. He reads his son's name before he understands what he is reading. This beat is the emotional axis of the send-off night.
13. The president's son is in Brandt's unit. His reasons for putting his name on the list are never explained from inside his perspective — not in narration, not in flashback, not in dialogue.
14. The camera never crosses the gate. No scene takes place on the far side.
15. No combat occurs anywhere in the book. The only violence the reader witnesses is the creature's vocalization and its physical effects on Reyes.
16. The book ends in Reyes's POV, the morning after the send-off, with the platform empty and the next list already forming. At least one central thread — the creature's nature, or Reyes's choice about what to do with what she believes — stays deliberately unresolved.
17. The president and the president's son are never POV characters.
18. The Contingency unit is approximately fourteen marines, mapping to the MSOT structure: two tactical elements led by Gunnery Sergeants, a team commander and a team chief above them.
19. The earnest register holds throughout. The prose never winks at the reader, never invites ironic distance from the characters' hope, and never signals that the grandeur of the send-off is absurd rather than diminished.
