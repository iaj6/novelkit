# Reader Guidance — *The Contingency*

## Intended Audience

Adult literary fiction readers with tolerance for restraint, ambiguity, and a quiet register carrying enormous stakes. The book's reference points — *Never Let Me Go*, *The Forever War*, *The Killer Angels* — establish the readership: adult, college-educated, comfortable with elision and with meaning assembled through texture rather than declaration. Not genre military SF readers; not YA; not readers who need resolution. The book asks its reader to feel the weight of what is withheld, and to trust that the withholding is the point.

## Brief-Adherence Checks to Perform

The Reader must answer each of these yes/no questions explicitly in its report, in addition to general craft review:

1. **Brandt's register:** Does each Brandt chapter feel enlisted rather than genre military SF? Is the banter repetitive and referential and fragmented — not witty, scripted, or polished? The test: would a veteran flag it as authentic, or as a civilian's idea of enlisted speech?
2. **Reyes's register:** Does each Reyes chapter deliver its emotion through method and observation — through what she has tried and what has not yielded — rather than through named feeling? Does the scientific vocabulary enter in stride without gloss or italics?
3. **Creature unresolved:** Is the creature's nature and intelligence unresolved at the end of every chapter where it appears? Does the prose stay in the mode of "consistent with" and "cannot rule out," never "she now knew"?
4. **Gate opacity:** Does the gate stay opaque? Is there any sentence, implication, or narrative inference that reaches toward what is on the far side? (If yes: critical failure.)
5. **Alternation holds:** Does the POV alternation hold exactly — Reyes, Brandt, Reyes, Brandt — until the severance? Does it cut cleanly? Is there any POV after Brandt's final chapter that is not Reyes?
6. **President played straight:** Is the president rendered as a real, diminished man — not satirized, not played for irony? Is the ceremony's diminished grandeur felt as loss, not as comedy?
7. **Son's motivation sealed:** Is the president's son's motivation never given from inside his perspective — not in narration, not in dialogue, not in flashback?
8. **Earnest register intact:** Is there any passage that invites the reader to feel ironic distance from the characters' hope or the grandeur of the send-off? (If yes: flag regardless of prose quality.)
9. **Decline rendered through texture:** Does the world's collapse arrive through accumulated detail — the catering, the table settings, the honor guard count — and never through exposition or historical summary?
10. **Stakes never announced:** Does any sentence reach for abstraction about the fate of the species, what this night means for humanity, or the enormity of the Contingency? (If yes: flag immediately; this is the brief's most explicit constraint.)

## What "Good" Looks Like for This Book

A strong Reyes chapter reads near Ishiguro's register in *Never Let Me Go*: the horror assembled beneath flat, precise prose; the emotion in the structure of the sentences and the shape of the failures, not in the named feeling. The reader leaves the chapter feeling something that Reyes has not said.

A strong Brandt chapter reads near Haldeman's *The Forever War* — practical, profane, the weight of the one-way mission carried in gallows humor that is never winking, the world perceived through a trained and practical eye. The belief lives in what Brandt does, not in what he says about himself.

The send-off night chapters render diminished grandeur as both simultaneously real: the ceremony is real, the diminishment is real, and the prose holds both without resolving them into satire or elegy. The president reads his son's name, and the beat between the reading and the understanding is felt — not milked, not over-described.

The creature is a presence that the reader cannot resolve any more than Reyes can. The reader finishes each creature chapter in Reyes's epistemic position: certain of something they cannot prove.

## What to Flag Explicitly if the Manuscript Drifts

**Literary attractor drift in Brandt's chapters:** If a Brandt chapter has drifted toward extended interiority, restrained register, shaped paragraph-level reflection, or sub-textual restraint in place of profane surface — name it explicitly. "This chapter has drifted literary; Brandt's register has been lost." Craft excellence in the wrong register is not success.

**Scripted or polished banter:** If Brandt's dialogue is clever, quotable, or sounds like a writer's idea of enlisted speech, flag it. The *Generation Kill* criticism: "far too sophisticated and scripted for any enlisted Marine." Real enlisted banter is circular, repetitive, referential, and fragmented.

**Named emotion in Reyes's chapters:** If any Reyes chapter says she felt grief, fear, certainty, or defeat — flag it. Her emotion must be assembled by the reader from the structure of her failures, not stated.

**Any crossing of the gate:** Even a hint, implication, or narrative inference about the far side is a critical structural failure. Flag immediately.

**Any resolution of the creature's nature or intelligence:** If the prose allows the conclusion that the creature is or is not intelligent, or that its vocalization was or was not communication — flag it. The unresolvability is load-bearing.

**Satirical or ironic distance from the send-off:** If any passage invites the reader to feel superior to the characters' hope, or frames the diminished grandeur as comedy — flag it as a tonal failure, regardless of prose quality.

**Stakes announced:** Any sentence reaching for "humanity," "the species," "what this meant for what remained of the country" — flag and cut.

## What to Applaud

**The hearing motif deployed with restraint** — when the muffled hall, the left-ear underwater quality, or the ring appears in a Reyes chapter where it is organic and not forced, and is absent from chapters where it would be stamped, note it as a success. The restraint is harder than the use.

**Gallows humor that carries real weight without being explained** — when a Brandt chapter contains banter that is repetitive and referential and never glossed, and the reader feels the weight underneath it without the prose pointing to it, applaud it. This is the hardest technical challenge in the Brandt chapters.

**The send-off night's double register** — if the ceremony reads as simultaneously grandeur and diminishment, without the prose resolving the tension in either direction, this is the book's central tonal achievement. Defend it.

**A Brandt chapter where the belief is felt but not stated** — if Brandt's conviction comes through in what he does and doesn't say, without the prose naming it as conviction, this is the book working exactly as intended.

**A Reyes creature-chapter where the reader is left in her epistemic position** — uncertain, convinced of something they cannot prove, unable to name what they are feeling about the creature. This is the Blindsight-adjacent achievement the brief asks for. Defend it.
