# Plotter Guidance — *The Contingency*

## POV Count and Rotation

Two POV characters: Dr. Imani Reyes and Corporal Eli Brandt. The chapters alternate in strict sequence. The plotter decides which POV opens the book; once decided, the alternation holds exactly until Brandt's final chapter. After the flash at the gate, all remaining chapters are Reyes. The alternation is the book's clock; its breaking is the book's grief. The plotter does not balance the POV count — the asymmetry (approximately 9 Reyes / 7 Brandt) is the point.

No third POV. No chapter from the president's perspective, the president's son's perspective, or any other character's perspective. Minor characters are seen only through Reyes or Brandt.

## Chapter-Shape Preferences

This is literary adult fiction. Most chapters are single-thread: one setting, one temporal span, one question being worked or one texture being inhabited.

**Reyes's chapters** tend toward the shape of *a failed or partially successful attempt to understand something*. She is at the specimen's enclosure, she is at the dinner with the creature audible beyond the wall, she is in the dead morning. The chapter's internal shape is the shape of her inquiry — what she tries, what doesn't yield, what she is left with.

**Brandt's chapters** tend toward *the texture of waiting and drilling and the social life of men about to ship out*. The chapter is organized around a specific event or exchange or stretch of time in the unit — not a plot event in the genre sense, but the particular quality of a drill, a meal, a night in the barracks, the processing line.

Interiority-heavy or meditation-heavy chapters are not the default shape; they are the exception, used when the chapter has earned them. Most chapters are grounded in physical situation throughout.

## Episode-vs-Arc Structure

**Day-before section:** Close to episodic — each is a self-contained day. Reyes at her work on an ordinary day before the Contingency was named; Brandt in the texture of an ordinary enlisted day before his name was on the list. The prose observes; it does not foreshadow.

**Approach section:** The chapters begin to accelerate and connect. Reyes's failing attempts against the clock of the dinner; Brandt's waiting and drilling as the ship-out approaches. This section is more continuous — events in one chapter can pick up time that the other chapter's POV was experiencing simultaneously.

**Send-off night section:** The densest chapters of the book, the most continuous in time. Reyes's send-off night chapter is organized around the creature's yard; Brandt's is organized around the formation and the platform. These chapters share the same night, same compound, and must be chronologically consistent with each other.

**Day-after section:** Reyes only. Single morning. The platform empty; the next list forming. This section is one to two chapters at most.

## Ending-Mode Distribution

Target distribution across approximately 16 chapters. Do not cluster the same mode in runs — vary deliberately.

- **Resolved beat:** ~4 chapters (day-before and approach chapters; moments of Reyes's smaller comprehensions, or Brandt's unit coherence)
- **Literary fade:** ~4 chapters (approach and day-after chapters; particularly Reyes's; the book's final chapter)
- **Mid-action or cut:** ~4 chapters (Brandt's approach and send-off night; Reyes's approach chapters when the clock is pressing)
- **Turn:** ~2 chapters (a chapter ending on new information that recontextualizes; Reyes's creature chapters are candidates)
- **Elliptical:** ~1 chapter (deliberately open question, a chapter that refuses to land)
- **Coda:** ~1 chapter (aftermath or reflective beat — potentially the final chapter, if the fade is built as aftermath)

**No cliffhangers.** Cliffhangers are not the mode of this book. The cumulative tension is structural — the reader knows the destination — and manufactured episode-hooks undercut that.

## Chapter Target Words

Chapter lengths vary with the beat and section.

- **Day-before chapters:** ~1,800–2,200 words. Short; these are ordinary days before the naming.
- **Approach chapters:** ~2,000–2,800 words. The middle weight of the book.
- **Send-off night chapters:** ~2,800–4,000 words. The book's heaviest chapters; they carry the roll call, the creature's yard, the formation, the platform. These may run toward the longer end.
- **Day-after chapter(s):** ~2,000–2,500 words. The dead morning; should not sprawl.

Total target: approximately 40,000 words across 16 chapters.

## Genre-Specific and Brief-Specific Structural Conventions

These are non-negotiable. The plotter works within them; it does not have discretion to alter them.

1. **The four-part frame** is fixed: Day-before / Approach / The send-off night / Day-after (Reyes only). The plotter assigns chapters to sections and makes structural choices within each section.

2. **Brandt's final chapter ends at the gate.** The flash is the ending. There is no scene of Brandt on the platform anticipating. There is no narration from beyond the gate. The thread ends.

3. **The day-after section is Reyes only.** It contains, at minimum: the empty platform and the next list already forming. What Reyes is left holding — the creature, her own unresolved choice, the knowledge that wasn't knowledge — is what the reader gets.

4. **The president reads his son's name.** This beat falls in the send-off night section, in whichever chapter covers the roll call. The father reads the name before he understands what he is reading. This is the emotional axis of the send-off night and must be given room.

5. **The creature makes its presence felt on the send-off night.** In the Reyes send-off chapter, she leaves the hall, follows something (a sound, a pull), and reaches the creature's yard. What happens there — the creature seen whole, some form of vocalization or confrontation — is the spine of the original image the brief describes.

6. **The two POVs never meet.** The outline must not contain a scene, exchange, or even a visual recognition across a crowded room between Reyes and Brandt. They share the compound and the night; they do not share any moment of contact.

7. **The camera never crosses the gate.** No outline chapter, no flash-forward, no framing device that goes to the far side. The gate is the book's horizon; it is never crossed.
