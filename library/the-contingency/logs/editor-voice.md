## 04-voluntary.md

**Opening clause — potential Reyes-register construction in a Brandt chapter**

Line: "He read it the way you read a name you already know will be there: the recognition flat, the eye moving on before the fact had registered."

The nominalized, formally balanced construction — "the recognition flat, the eye moving on before the fact had registered" — leans toward Reyes's register (measured, analytical, noun-phrase-heavy) rather than Brandt's (shorter, declarative, concrete). Because this is the opening clause, where the register is being established, the drift is more noticeable than it would be mid-chapter. The compression pass left it, and the rest of the chapter is solidly Brandt, so this may be intentional (a slight literary elevation before the register settles). Worth the writer confirming it's a deliberate choice rather than a carry-forward from a Reyes-mode drafting session.

No other voice issues. Chapter is otherwise clean.
## 05-the-report.md

**Borderline editorial generalisation — clipboard line (Chen scene)**

> "He consulted it in the way people consult clipboards when the information they need is not on the clipboard."

The construction "in the way people consult X when…" is a universalising, slightly wry authorial observation. Reyes's register is spare and tightly grounded in immediate clinical perception; she observes specific behaviour, not a general category of behaviour. This construction has the cadence of an ironic narratorial aside — which the style guide explicitly prohibits ("The narrative does not editorialize"). It would need to become a specific, direct observation (e.g., "He consulted it; his eyes did not move." or similar) to sit cleanly in her register. However, doing so requires more than a word/clause change and touches the meaning of the beat, so left for writer's judgment.

**Fix applied in this pass:** Sentence exceeding Reyes's 45-word cap split into two. In the dusk section, "…approximately four seconds, and the following day the threshold had shifted and had not shifted back." was 50 words as a single sentence; split after "approximately four seconds." — now 37 words and 13 words respectively.

## 08-processing.md

**Mixed reported/direct dialogue mode within single exchanges.** The chapter uses reported speech for some exchanges and direct speech for others, and within individual exchanges the two modes alternate without clear purpose. The manifest section uses reported throughout:

> Brandt said the item was there or it wasn't.  
> Tafoya said the previous format had a separate line and it was more correct.

The coffee section opens in reported speech, shifts mid-exchange to direct, reverts to reported, then returns to direct:

> Kendrick said it was terrible.  
> Tafoya said why was he still drinking it.  
> "It's hot," Kendrick said.  
> "That's the best you've got," Tafoya said.  
> Osei said the admin annex coffee was the same and Kendrick said it was not the same —  
> "Intermittent terrible is worse," Kendrick said.

The style guide calls for "spare tags" and trusts banter to carry itself; it does not prohibit reported speech. The mixing may be intentional deadpan distancing (the manifest section in particular benefits from the flattening). But the coffee exchange loses register energy at the reported-speech moments — these are exactly the lines where Brandt's unit banter should be most alive. If the deadpan mode is the choice, the exchange should stay consistently reported throughout. If the banter is the choice, convert the reported lines to direct. The current hybrid undercuts both effects.

## 13-the-yard.md

**Register slip — "close on" (tactical vocabulary in Reyes's analytical voice)**

> "She did not close on what the notation meant."

`close on` is enlisted/tactical idiom — marines close on an objective. It appears nowhere in Reyes's documented register, which is scientific-procedural. This line follows a precise angle-of-rotation estimate and sits inside her analytical mode; the tactical verb is jarring there. Possible writer intent: she has absorbed compound vocabulary after eleven months embedded with the unit. If intentional, it should recur once elsewhere to read as characterization rather than accident. If inadvertent, a clean Reyes-register substitute would be something like: *She did not reduce what the notation meant to a claim she could log.* Writer judgment required — not touched.

## 14-the-platform.md

**Sentence requiring writer judgment — register abstraction in Brandt section**

The sentence mid-walk, just before Brandt reaches the slab:

> "The hall had the particular quality it had been building toward for the last hour and a half."

This floats above Brandt's sensory register. "Particular quality" is abstract — it names something without describing it. Brandt's observation elsewhere in this chapter is concrete (the floor's compliance, the crew member's expression, the cloth gradations). This sentence doesn't describe what he perceives; it names that a quality exists and defers to the reader to fill it in. That's more a narrative-voice gesture than a Brandt one.

It cannot be repaired at the word or clause level without introducing new prose, so it's left for the writer. Options: replace with a single concrete percept Brandt notices at that moment (the temperature, the sound pressure, the stillness of the guests), or cut (if compression agrees the sentence is pure gloss). The surrounding paragraphs already do the atmospheric work.

## 15-dead-morning.md

**Four sentences over the 45-word cap — four fixed, one flagged.**

`canon/style.md` sets a hard cap of 45 words per sentence for Reyes ("no sentence should exceed 45 words"). Four sentences in this chapter materially exceeded it. Three were clean splits at existing em-dashes or coordinating conjunctions; one required a single new period before a fragment. All four have been applied in the draft.

**One sentence exceeds the cap but requires writer judgment before splitting:**

> "The irreconcilable readings sat in the same row they had occupied since month two, joined now by the behavioral record of a single night and unchanged in their irreconcilability: the specimen had come through the gate and was what the gate's origin point had produced; the specimen was what people became there, a version of an outcome; the specimen was the place's response to being entered, sent back as a kind of answer; the specimen was what had been returned the last time a contingent went through, before the gate's one-way character was understood."

This runs ~94 words. The four semicoloned clauses following the colon are each 13–21 words — short, clear, genuinely periodic — and their anaphoric structure ("the specimen was…" × 4) enacts the chapter's epistemic paralysis. Splitting the list into four separate sentences would shift the register toward Brandt's staccato rhythm and break the enclosure the colon establishes. The easiest fix is to end the sentence after "irreconcilability" and let the four hypotheses run as a freestanding list (with or without the semicolons). But the choice of format — list-within-sentence versus standalone list — changes the rhetorical feel enough that it warrants a writer's call, not a voice editor's word-swap.
