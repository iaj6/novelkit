Measuring key sentences:

- "The acoustic monitor casing had a crack at the lower left corner that she had photographed in September and would photograph again tonight." — 24 words (within range)

This is slightly shorter than the 18–25 word average target, but the mix reads correctly — it mirrors Ishiguro's rhythm of short declaratives punctuating longer analytic constructions. Not a failing.

**Chapter-opening convention (Reyes: sensory anchor first, then earned reflection):**
Opens on "The thermal trace was within the existing range" — immediately in observation, in the field-log check. Sensory + procedural anchor is the first sentence. No abstract sentiment, no thematic statement. This exactly matches the convention.

**POV strategy:** Single POV, no head-hopping, camera stays in Reyes's perception. Correct.

**Dialogue density:** No dialogue. Given that Reyes is alone at an outdoor observation post, this is appropriate — the guidance calls for 20–30% in general but explicitly notes she is often alone or in brief exchange.

**Voice cadence vs. Ishiguro:** The Ishiguro register is flatly precise, with horror assembled beneath procedure. Comparison:
- Sample: "She had stopped deciding whether that was reassuring." — deflection of emotion through methodological stance; no feeling named, no feeling denied
- Sample: "That was the language the record required." — horror in the gap between what she observed and what the record's language will admit
- Sample: "She turned to the next line and waited for nothing to happen again." — a closing line that is quiet, procedural, and devastating in what it implies without naming

These constructions match the Ishiguro attractor the brief specified.

## What the sample does well

- **Scientific vocabulary in stride.** "Thermal trace," "respiration indicator," "survey mark," "acoustic-stimulus trial," "chemical-marker study," "exterior-wind sensor" — all enter without italics, explanation, or gloss. This is Reyes's air, handled correctly.
- **Epistemic mode around the creature.** "Consistent with wind. Cannot rule out interior-initiated movement." — this is exactly the hedged, non-resolving language the brief's central constraint requires. The creature's movement is observed and noted but never concluded upon.
- **Emotion via structure, not naming.** "She had stopped deciding whether that was reassuring" and "she did not fully trust herself to stay away" surface feeling obliquely — as behavioral and methodological stances, not as named states. This is the Reyes register.
- **The final line.** "She turned to the next line and waited for nothing to happen again." — literary-fade ending that lands cleanly, trusts the reader, and does not gloss what has just happened. Correct ending mode for a day-before Reyes chapter.

## What the sample drifts on

- **Sentence length skews slightly short.** The 18–25 word average target is aspirational; many sentences in this sample fall in the 7–13 word range. This produces a slightly more staccato rhythm than the "measured, sometimes formally balanced" description suggests. It's close enough to accept — the register is still clearly Reyes, and short beats clustering before a long periodic sentence is defensible — but a future chapter that runs this short throughout might feel more clipped than the Ishiguro model intends.
- **The creature is never named "the specimen."** The enclosure is called "a veiled enclosure" and "the inner barrier." Since Reyes isn't directly studying the creature in this scene — she's doing a circuit check — this is contextually justified. Still worth flagging that the brief's vocabulary ("the specimen") should appear in chapters where she is actively working on it.
