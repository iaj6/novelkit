# Editor — Pacing Pass
## Macro arc assessment

**Overall shape.** The four-part structure (Day-before → Approach → Send-off Night → Day-after) is well-engineered. The opening is appropriately short (~4,000 words across two chapters); the send-off night section carries its weight at ~13,600 words with the heaviest chapters placed correctly; and the close is proportionally quiet. The severance at Ch14 is the book's formal grief mechanism, properly prepared across eight chapters of alternation. No structural failures at the level of overall architecture.

---

**Midbook sag: Ch06–Ch08.** This is the book's most vulnerable stretch, and it is real. The three chapters at the centre of the Approach section all carry limited new information and land in low-energy states:

- Ch06 (*Dry Runs*): Another full training day, another deflected answer to the why-did-you-volunteer question. The story-arc entry notes "Tafoya asks the question Kendrick asked, and Brandt gives the same answer" — which is the same emotional beat as Ch04, adjacent in the Brandt thread. What differentiates these two chapters needs to be clearly felt on the page; if Ch04 already deposited the deflection at full weight, Ch06 risks being a restatement. Both chapters close on resolved-beats, tightening the pattern further.

- Ch07 (*Eleven Months*): A mental inventory of eleven months of failure. The story-arc entry explicitly says "the chapter refuses to land," and the chapter map confirms an elliptical ending. A single retrospective inventory chapter is legitimate; placed immediately after Ch06's lateral movement, it adds a second consecutive chapter that is absorbing prior information rather than advancing it.

- Ch08 (*Processing*): Deliberately low-energy administrative material. A mid-action cut on paperwork is not inherently wrong — the weight of the irreversible decision absorbed without crisis is a key Thread 2 beat — but as the third chapter in this run, it extends the sag another beat before Ch09 rescues the trajectory.

Ch09 (*The Outer Barrier*) is a genuine escalation — Reyes crossing from method to speech — and Ch10 (*The Night Before*) is a strong pre-climax beat. The sag recovers before the send-off night begins. But the three-chapter hollow is worth examining in the per-chapter pass: can Ch07 give the inventory a sharper forward edge? Can Ch06 distinguish itself from Ch04 more aggressively?

---

**Repeated beat: the deflection.** Ch04 and Ch06 share the same structural nucleus — someone asks Brandt why he volunteered; he gives the one answer he can say out loud. The brief specifies his conviction as the book's engine, and this scene does necessary work. But placed in consecutive Brandt chapters, it risks becoming a tic rather than a development. If Ch06 is to earn its place, the repetition must be legible as escalation (more acknowledged behind it, per the story-arc note) rather than restatement.

---

**Ending-mode patterns.** Two runs are worth flagging:

1. *Resolved-beat cluster at Ch02, Ch04, Ch06*: The first three Brandt chapters all close on resolved beats. This flattens the early Brandt arc into uniform settling. By Ch06 the reader may have learned to expect that Brandt chapters land and rest. The chapter-map shows Ch08 breaks the run (mid-action cut), which is good, but three consecutive same-mode closes for the same POV is one too many.

2. *Mid-action cut cluster at Ch08–Ch12* (broken only by Ch10's literary-fade): Four of five chapters in this range end mid-action. Individually this is a legitimate escalation tool; as a cluster, repetition may dull the effect. Ch10 is well-placed as the break.

---

**Climax/coda balance.** Ch14 is properly weighted at ~3,800 words — the longest chapter, correctly placed. The coda (Ch15–16, ~4,400 words combined) runs slightly longer than the climax, which is structurally justifiable given that the book's final argument lives in Reyes's aftermath. The risk is that both coda chapters are retrospective (re-mapping what she observed, sitting with what she cannot write) without enough variation between them. If Ch15 and Ch16 share the same emotional posture — Reyes at the barrier, log open, certainty unwritable — one of them is standing in for the other. The per-chapter pass should verify that these two chapters are genuinely distinct in texture and movement, not structural echoes.

---

**Thread health at the macro level.** All five threads are properly loaded and deliberately unresolved. Thread 5 (What the Creature Knows) is the book's quietest formal achievement — the only cross-POV connective tissue, never announced, building across Ch13–Ch14–Ch15. Thread 3 (The Son's Name) front-loads its work and pays off precisely in Ch14; the spacing feels right. Thread 4 (What She Carries Out) is necessarily the slowest thread and risks feeling thin in the day-after section if Ch15 and Ch16 do not distinguish their versions of the same undecided state.

---

**Summary priorities for per-chapter pass:**
1. Ch06 — verify it is a genuine escalation of Ch04's beat, not a restatement.
2. Ch07 — the inventory needs at least one forward-facing edge; its refusal to land must feel earned, not merely stalled.
3. Ch08 — largely defensible on its own terms; watch only for compounding flatness following Ch06–07.
4. Ch15 and Ch16 — ensure they are distinct in texture, movement, and emotional posture; the coda cannot afford to read as two chapters of the same beat.
