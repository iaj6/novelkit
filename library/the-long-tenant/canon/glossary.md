# Glossary — The Long Tenant

*Alphabetical. Architects, plotters, and drafters add to this file as new terms, names, and objects enter the story.*

---

**Account book** — the physical form of Della's household ledger: a commercially available ruled composition or account book, with columns for date, description, and amount, adapted by Della for both financial records and personal notation. The back room of the Verrill Street house is where she kept it.

**Blizzard, February 1983** — the Megalopolitan Blizzard, February 9–12, 1983; the historical storm anchoring Della's death night. Deposited 35 inches of snow at Glengary, West Virginia; left 34,000+ homes in Charleston and Pineville without power; reached deep into the Appalachian interior.

**CAT software** — Computer-Aided Transcription; the software system that translates a court reporter's stenotype strokes into readable English text, using the reporter's personal dictionary.

**Cale Markey** — Della's older brother; approximately 3–5 years her senior. Left Renick approximately 1980 after a dispute over their father's estate. Came to the door of the Verrill Street house on the night of February 11, 1983, during the blizzard. Knocked from approximately 9:40 to 9:52 PM. Della heard him and did not go down. Survived; present whereabouts not pursued by the narrative.

**Certified transcript** — the document produced by a court reporter, signed and sealed, attesting that it is the verbatim record of a proceeding. Ellen's professional product.

**Coroner's inquest file** — the physical file produced by the county coroner's investigation of Della's death, held at the county records office. Contents: the death certificate, the coroner's investigation report, witness statements (including Mabel Duff's), the physician's cause-of-death report, and the inquest verdict. The file exists in paper form at the start of the novel and is weeks from being digitized.

**Death certificate (Della's)** — the standardized form recording: deceased's name (Della Markey), address (Verrill Street, Renick), age (37), manner of death (accident), immediate cause (hypothermia/exposure), date (February 12, 1983). The death certificate records the mechanism, not the choice.

**Della Markey** — see *Characters*. The dead tenant; first-person present tense; born approximately 1946; died February 11–12, 1983, age 37; daughter of a Renick millwright; estranged from her brother Cale; kept the household ledger; died in a cold she could have walked out of.

**Deponent** — the person being questioned under oath in a deposition; Ellen's professional vocabulary.

**Drafts / rough draft** — the first uncertified pass of a transcript produced from CAT software; not ready for use as an official record; cleaned and certified by the reporter before filing.

**Ellen Vance** — see *Characters*. The living tenant; third-person limited past tense; age 44; freelance court reporter of twenty years; buys the Verrill Street house.

**Flue** — the passage in a chimney through which combustion gases exit the house. An exterior-wall chimney's flue can lose draft in extreme cold, feeding cold air back into the firebox and killing the fire.

**Hypothermia, stages** — clinical progression: mild (32–35°C: shivering, alert, decisions possible); moderate (30–32°C: shivering weakens, confusion, disorientation); severe (below 28°C: unconsciousness, cardiac arrest). The window of conscious choice is the mild stage. Della's decision not to open the door occurred while she was still in mild hypothermia, capable of choosing.

**Inquest** — a formal proceeding, convened by the county coroner, to determine cause and manner of a sudden or questionable death. May involve a jury of six. For Della's death: the inquest found accidental death, hypothermia, consistent with the storm conditions and the failed stove.

**Kept sentence (Record A)** — see *Record A*.

**Ledger** (Della's household ledger, Record B) — see *Record B* and *markey-ledger-1983*.

**Mabel Duff** — working name for the neighbor whose inquest statement is the false crossing. She attests she saw no visitors on the evening of February 10 and daylight hours of February 11. Technically true; she was not watching at 9:40 PM in the storm. Ellen initially reads her statement as confirmation of the kept sentence; the ledger later disproves the inference.

**Maker's mark** — the identifying mark burned, stamped, or labeled on furniture from the Renick Furniture Works. Paper label inside the drawer or brand on the back/underside of a piece. The desk in the front room of the Verrill Street house carries one.

**markey-ledger-1983** (recordId) — the stable identifier for Record B, Della's final ledger entry. Text is canonical and fixed. Every chapter that quotes it must call `read_record('markey-ledger-1983')` and reproduce the returned text verbatim inside quotation marks.

**Membrane, the** — the book's central structural discipline: the two POVs never meet as persons; all information crossing between them crosses indirectly through physical objects, public records, or written documents. Never broken.

**Millwright** — a skilled tradesman who installs, dismantles, maintains, and repairs machinery in industrial facilities. Della's father's trade; a position of precision and systematic method. The millwright's training (measurement, record-keeping, attention to tolerance) is the origin of Della's ledger-keeping discipline.

**On the record / Off the record** — the basic distinction of court reporting. "On the record" means every word spoken is being captured for the certified transcript. Ellen would know this distinction as the organizing principle of language: there is the record and there is what is not in the record, and they are different things.

**P (proposition)** — the fixed spine of the mystery: *Someone came to Della's door on the night of February 11, 1983 — her brother Cale — and Della heard him and chose not to go down.* This proposition is the epistemic center of the book. Della wrong-believes ¬P until her final chapter. Ellen moves from unaware → suspects → wrong-believes ¬P (false crossing) → knows P. The reader learns P at the A/B side-by-side.

**Read-back** — a court reporter re-reads aloud from the transcript a portion of testimony, on request (for a jury, or when a witness or attorney requests clarification of what was said). Ellen has done this thousands of times.

**Record A (the kept sentence)** — recordId: `kept-sentence`. Exact text: `Nobody came to that door.` — Della's recurring phrase in her chapters; the mercy she made; the lie she chose. Met early, in Della's chapters; it returns. Its wording is canonical.

**Record B (the ledger entry)** — recordId: `markey-ledger-1983`. Della's final household ledger entry, recovered from the back room late in Ellen's chapters. Records the power out, the stove failing, the knocking at near quarter to ten, "three years I have not written his name but I knew it," the refusal to go down, the stopping of the knocking at 9:52, and ends "Nobody came to that door." — the same words as Record A, now meaning the opposite. Its wording is canonical and fixed.

**Renick** — fictional town on the Sandy River, Greenbrier County region of West Virginia. Modeled on the historical Renick community (Greenbrier Valley, limestone country, the Chesapeake & Ohio Railroad corridor). A furniture manufacturing town in decline.

**Renick Furniture Works** — the furniture plant that once employed much of the county; Della's father worked there as a millwright; the plant contracted through the 1980s–1990s and closed. The maker's mark on the desk in the Verrill Street front room reads *Renick Furniture Works*.

**RPR** — Registered Professional Reporter; the foundational NCRA certification for court reporters. Ellen's certification.

**Sandy River** — the river on which Renick sits. Not romantic; it backs up in spring and smells like silt in summer.

**Scopist** — a person hired to edit and clean up the rough CAT draft of a transcript before the reporter certifies it. Part of Ellen's professional context.

**Stenotype machine** (also: steno machine) — the specialized chorded keyboard used by court reporters to transcribe at 225+ words per minute. Approximately 22 keys, phonetically arranged; multiple keys pressed simultaneously to represent phonetic syllables, words, or standard phrases. Ellen's primary tool.

**Travel clock** — the wind-up clock on Della's mantle; accurate even when the power goes out. Ticking through the night of February 11, 1983. The clock by which Della timed the knocking: started near 9:40, stopped at 9:52.

**Verrill Street** — the street in Renick where the book's house stands; a block of narrow, late-nineteenth-century worker's houses built for the mill town. The house is tall and narrow; the back room on the upper floor is the room with the ledger.

**Voice-to-text / digital audio recording** — the technology displacing court reporters. Cheaper than a live stenographer; inadequate for multiple simultaneous speakers, accents, technical vocabulary, and the ancillary duties (marking exhibits, swearing witnesses, read-back) a reporter performs. Displacing the profession contract by contract.

**Back room** — The smaller room on the upper floor of the Verrill Street house, facing the back yard; approximately 10 by 12 feet; no radiator, no heat register; flat-panel closet door with no hardware; window sealed in its frame. Della kept her household ledger here. The room has an anomalous cold with no identifiable source. The door will not stay closed: first found ajar in Chapter 1 (Ellen's walk-through); also ajar on her return from the diner. The latch engages when tested but the door is repeatedly found open.

**Elkins** — Town approximately one hour's drive from Renick; location of the arbitration deposition on Ellen's calendar during Chapter 1.

**Martinsburg** — West Virginia city where Ellen lived before buying the Renick house; the rest of her possessions remain in storage there.

**Meade Street** — The main commercial strip in Renick; three blocks from Verrill Street; contains the Meade Street Diner, a hardware store, a pharmacy, and former storefronts. The plant building (Renick Furniture Works) is visible from Meade Street on the far block.

**Roll-top desk** — The heavy hardwood roll-top desk in the front room of the Verrill Street house; included with the house sale. Nine pigeonholes in the raised top; three drawers in the front. Maker's mark burned into the inner edge of the upper right drawer: 'RENICK FURNITURE WORKS / Made in Renick, W.Va.' Ellen uses it as her work desk.

**The Riverside Stove** — Della's wood-burning stove in the Verrill Street kitchen. A Riverside make, purchased from a man in Ronceverte in 1961 by Della's father and installed by him and two men from the mill. It has an exterior-wall chimney on the north wall — the weaker design, per her father's teaching. It failed on the night of February 11, 1983, when sustained extreme cold reversed the flue draft; Della could not relight it. Its failure is the proximate physical cause of her death. No mechanical defect; the stove did what stoves do in those conditions.

**Della's Account Book (household ledger)** — The household account book Della keeps on the kitchen table. Same column method as her father's books — date, description, figure if applicable. She has his last three books still in the front room drawer. The pen she uses is a Sheaffer with a steel nib (black ink) given to her by her father when she started keeping the book. The 8:42 PM entry for February 11, 1983 — power out, stove out, temperature, lamp oil, cord wood — is the beginning of the ledger entry that will become Record B. The kept sentence (Record A) appears at the bottom of this entry.

**The Kept Sentence (Record A)** — 'Nobody came to that door.' — Della's recurring phrase across her chapters; the mercy she made to live inside her own death. First appears as the final line of the 8:42 PM ledger entry in Chapter 2, in the context of a stove-failure record. It will be met again and again in her chapters; its meaning inverts when set beside Record B.

**Exterior-wall chimney draft reversal** — The physical mechanism of the Riverside stove's failure on February 11, 1983. In sustained extreme cold, the exterior-wall flue cannot keep the air inside it warm enough to maintain draft; the column chills from outside in; draft reverses; cold air comes back down the flue into the firebox. Once this occurs, ordinary relighting fails. Della tried warming the flue with newspaper (using the last of the kindling) — it warmed some but not enough. Her father had taught her this failure mode explicitly; she recognized it immediately.

**Meade Street Hardware Store** — Hardware store on Meade Street in Renick. Run by a man in his 70s who has been there since the plant was running. Ellen shops here in Chapter 3; he tells her the Verrill Street house has been sitting empty for a while.

**Meade Street Resale Shop** — Resale shop on Meade Street in Renick with a plate-glass front. Carries multiple pieces of Renick Furniture Works furniture with Est. 1908 maker's marks. Ellen buys a twelve-dollar ladder-rung chair here in Chapter 3.

**The Twelve-Dollar Chair** — Straight-backed ladder-rung hardwood chair purchased by Ellen from the Meade Street resale shop for $12. Maker's mark: 'Renick Furniture Works, Est. 1908. Made in Renick, W.Va.' Placed across from the desk in the front room of the Verrill Street house.

**Hardware Store Man** — Unnamed man in his 70s who runs the Meade Street hardware store in Renick; has been there since the plant was operating. Tells Ellen the Verrill Street house 'has been sitting empty for a while' in Chapter 3. Potentially holds local knowledge about Della Markey and the house's history.

**thumb-bar latch (Verrill Street front door)** — The front-door latch on the inside of the Verrill Street house — a cold-steel thumb-bar type. Tested and confirmed functional by Della on the night of February 11, 1983. The same door Ellen's timeline shows from the outside.

**three-test rule (Della's father)** — The discipline Della's father taught from his millwright work: test a latch, flange, or fitting three times before trusting it. One test catches easy failures; three tests tell you the thing holds. Della applies it to the front-door latch in Chapter 4 and to her house circuit generally.

**back-room shelf** — The shelf above the window in the back room, on the upper floor of the Verrill Street house. Della's normal storage location for her household ledger. Empty on the night of February 11, 1983 — she brought the ledger to the kitchen table before the power went out.

**Harold Markey** — Della Markey's father; millwright at Renick Furniture Works; purchased the Verrill Street house in 1951; died October 1978; left the house to Della by quit-claim and will; his estate inventory listed the roll-top desk among household effects.

**Greenbrier County Courthouse** — Three-story stone building in Lewisburg, WV; houses the county clerk's office (first floor, deed and probate records) and the coroner's office (third floor, inquest files, still on paper as of the present-day chapters). Ellen drives there in Chapter 5 to pull the chain of title.

**Della Markey death certificate** — Greenbrier County coroner's document certifying Della Ruth Markey's death on February 12, 1983; immediate cause: hypothermia; manner of death: accidental. The document Ellen reads in Chapter 5; the official account against which Record B (the ledger) will later be set.

**Della Markey estate file** — Greenbrier County probate file for Della Markey's intestate estate, open 1983–1991. Contains the death certificate, eight years of failed Cale Markey correspondence, and the administrative close-out transferring the property to the county.

**Route 219** — The road running south from Renick through the Greenbrier Valley to Lewisburg; approximately twenty minutes between Renick and the county seat. Ellen drives it in Chapter 5 to reach the courthouse.

**torch-paper method** — Technique for priming a cold exterior-wall chimney flue: a newspaper cone (tight base, loose top) is lit and held up into the firebox throat to warm the lowest section of the flue until the draft shifts from reversed to upward pull. Della's father taught her this method when the Riverside stove was installed in 1961. Applied in ch06; succeeded briefly but failed because the flue was chilled beyond what the torch could sustain.

**house's own cold** — Della's distinction (her father's vocabulary): the cold that builds from inside a building when the heat source fails, as opposed to storm-cold which has direction and comes from outside. Non-directional; comes from the walls, ceiling, floor, and still air. Named explicitly in ch06 when Della sits on the kitchen floor at approximately 9:15 PM.

**Mabel W. Duff** — Verrill Street neighbor, three houses north of 412 Verrill Street; knew Della Markey since the Markeys owned the house. Gave an inquest statement dated February 20, 1983, opening on 'the evening of February tenth.' Her statement covers February 10 and daylight February 11 — the wrong window for the 9:40 PM knocking. She is the vehicle of the false crossing in Ch11: Ellen will initially read her statement as confirming nobody came to the door that night.

**Greenbrier County Coroner's Inquest File — Della Markey, 1983** — The full coroner's inquest file for Della Ruth Markey, held on the third floor of the Greenbrier County courthouse in Lewisburg, WV. Folder tab: MARKEY / 1983. Contains: death certificate, three-page investigation report, two-page pathologist's opinion, inquest proceedings, and three witness statements in official longhand. Key finding: no mechanical defect in the Riverside stove; exterior-wall chimney construction consistent with draft reversal in sustained extreme cold. Inquest verdict: Accidental Death, February 22, 1983.

**no mechanical defect** — The specific phrase from the coroner's 1983 investigation report describing the finding on the Riverside stove and stovepipe. Ellen notes it as distinct from the inquest verdict's paraphrase 'failure of her heating source.' The distinction — the stove was sound; the conditions made it unable to function — is the professional gap Ellen marks but cannot yet name.

**the tenants** — Della Markey's settled category for the living people who have occupied the Verrill Street house since her death. She cannot see or hear them in the ordinary way; she perceives them as warmth at the wrong angle, pressure in a room, a light falling wrong for her season, or a door not where she left it. The category first named in Ch08; established as prior experience, not new. The warmth outside the back-room door in Ch08 is her perception of Ellen Vance.

**della-oil-lamp** — The oil (kerosene) lamp Della uses on the night of February 11, 1983. Burns amber light. Sits on the kitchen table throughout the night. Reservoir three-quarters full at approximately 9:05 PM. Wick trimmed at an angle per her father's instruction using scissors kept on the shelf above the stove, left near drawer (sticks at the third inch).

**wick-trimming method** — Della's method, taught by her father: scissors from the left near drawer on the shelf above the stove (sticks at the third inch; lift the handle to pull); trim the wick at an angle, one edge higher than the other, not straight across — so the flame spreads even rather than peaked. A peaked flame gutters in a draft, smokes, and wastes oil.

**Lester K. Caulfield** — Utility worker at the Renick Service Center who discovered Della Markey's body through the east kitchen window on February 12, 1983, while assessing power restoration. Called the county sheriff and waited outside until the deputy arrived. Gave a witness statement at the 1983 inquest (dated February 20, 1983).

**Deputy Glenn R. Harman** — Greenbrier County Sheriff's Office deputy who secured the scene at 412 Verrill Street on February 12, 1983. Entered through the east kitchen window (defective latch). His three-page witness statement (February 21, 1983) is the most detailed scene description in the inquest file and explicitly names the household ledger open on the kitchen table.

**Dr. Pauline Raker** — Greenbrier County Medical Examiner who authored the 1983 pathologist's opinion on Della Markey's death. Found cause of death to be hypothermia, manner accidental, with no alcohol, drugs, or underlying conditions. Estimated time of death between 10:00 PM February 11 and 4:00 AM February 12, 1983; body temperature on discovery approximately 79°F.

**Len Duvall** — Plant worker at Renick Furniture Works; one of two men who helped Harold Markey carry the roll-top desk to 412 Verrill Street in 1964. Named in Della's memory in Chapter 10.

**the tenants (Della's category)** — Della's settled category for perceiving the living occupants of the house in the years since her death: experienced as deliberate footfall, warmth at a wrong angle, a light from the wrong direction, a door not where she left it. She cannot see or hear them as persons. First established in Chapter 8; elaborated in Chapter 10 as the chapter's organizing taxonomy.

**inside cold vs. storm's cold** — Della's (and her father's) taxonomy for two distinct types of cold in the house: the storm's cold has direction and can be felt against the face; the inside cold is non-directional, simply present everywhere, building from inside when the heat source dies. Used throughout Della's chapters as her working vocabulary for what is happening to the house on February 11, 1983.

**Verrill Street front walk** — Concrete walk in front of 412 Verrill Street, poured by Harold Markey in 1955. Referenced in Della's chapters as an anchor of the father's physical presence on the property.

**Verrill Street elm** — Elm tree in the front yard of 412 Verrill Street, planted by Della in 1970. Bends in high wind; has bent to the hard mark and come back in every hard winter since planting.

**Mabel Duff Statement** — The two-page witness statement given by Mabel W. Duff of 434 Verrill Street to the Greenbrier County inquest, February 20, 1983. Covers the evening of February 10th and daylight hours of February 11th; truthfully attests no visitors were seen during those hours; does not cover the night of February 11th (the knocking came at 9:40 PM). The vehicle of the false crossing in Ch11. Registered as record 'mabel-duff-statement'.

**Records Room Clerk** — Female staff member of the Greenbrier County records reading room. Present for all three of Ellen's visits (Ch07, Ch09, Ch11). In Ch11 she informs Ellen that the digitization schedule has moved to approximately six weeks (equipment came in early).

**412 Verrill Street** — The street address of the house Della Markey grew up in and died in (February 11–12, 1983), and which Ellen Vance now occupies. Established in the inquest verdict quoted in Ch09; confirmed in Ch11.

**Harold's kitchen chair** — The oak straight-backed chair Harold Markey used at the kitchen table for his evening accounts. A crack in the second slat dates to 1968 and has not worsened. Della uses it at the same table on the night of February 11, 1983.

**Kitchen thermometer, 412 Verrill Street** — Wall thermometer mounted by the kitchen door. Records indoor temperatures tracked by Della on February 11, 1983: 41°F at 7 PM, 40°F at 8:30 PM, 39°F by approximately 9:20-9:35 PM and dropping.

**Ellen's rough draft (the false account)** — The professional summary Ellen produces in ch13 from the inquest file: a timeline of Della Markey's death, sourced to the documentary record, complete and internally consistent. Format: left column of the two-column notebook page. Contains the false conclusion that nobody came to the door. Filed in the notebook with the photocopied file pages.

**The two-column notebook page** — Ellen's professional notation system applied to the investigation: left column holds what the record gives (facts, sources, verified); right column holds the [inaudible] bracket items — what the record does not account for (back-room cold, door found ajar, quality of attention). The convention is from her court-reporting practice: an inaudible passage is noted, bracketed, and the proceeding continues.

**[inaudible] column (Ellen's notebook)** — Ellen's term for the right-hand column of her investigation notebook, where she places unexplained observations from the house that have no documentary source. These are bracketed, not interpreted, and do not enter the official account. The back-room cold, the door found ajar three times, and the quality of attention in certain rooms are in this column.

**Markey Travel Clock** — A wind-up travel clock with a cream face, black numbers, and a dark wood case; on the mantle in the front room of the Verrill Street house during Della's chapters. Family heirloom brought from Kanawha County by Della's grandmother's family. Wound by radio signal on Sundays and at the start of any major storm (Harold Markey's rule). The clock's hands provide the structural spine of ch14 (9:32 / 9:36 / 9:37 / 9:38) and will become significant in Record B ('knocking stopped at nine fifty-two'). Found wrapped in cloth in a drawer in the front room by Ellen in the present day.

**machine tells you before it tells you** — Harold Markey's mill-floor principle: a tolerance-change is felt in the plant floor before it becomes a noise; learned only by standing in the plant long enough for the floor to carry it through the soles of your shoes. Thirty-one years in the Renick Furniture Works. Della internalized the principle and applies it to the night of February 11, 1983 in ch14.

**Roy (courthouse records clerk)** — Long-tenured records clerk in the Greenbrier County courthouse probate section; has worked there approximately thirty years; knew the Markey family from county life; confirms Harold Markey's death (c. 1978), Cale's departure (c. 1979-80), and the argument over the estate; has no knowledge of Cale ever returning to Renick.

**della-markey-estate (Probate File)** — Greenbrier County Circuit Court probate file for Della Ruth Markey, intestate; petition opened March 22, 1983; estate held open through 1990; county acquired real property February 1991. Key items: house appraised $19,500; 1977 Chevrolet Nova ($900); household contents ($640); savings account at Greenbrier Valley Bank ($2,312 at death); household ledger listed in inventory (found open on kitchen table, February 12, 1983, left in situ). Heir search: three certified letters to three addresses for Cale H. Markey, 1983-1986, all returned.

**Chillicothe, Ohio** — Town in central Ohio; last known address for Cale H. Markey in the 1983 probate file for Della Markey's estate. Specific address: 441 Bridge Street. Letter returned undelivered.

**Greenbrier Valley Bank** — Bank in Renick/Greenbrier County area; held Della Markey's savings account at time of her death (balance $2,312).

**Transom window (Verrill Street front door)** — A narrow four-pane window set into the frame above the front door at the Verrill Street house. Each pane is approximately one handspan high. In Della's chapters, this is the window above the latch she will not look through — the window through which Cale's silhouette would have been visible had she looked up during the knocking. First established in Ch16.

**Markey household ledger (running ledger, 1979–1983)** — Della Markey's hardbound household account ledger, approximately 200 pages, running from January 1979 through her death in February 1983. Kept in her father Harold's column system: date and time on the left, entries running from there; no strikethroughs; a line under a closed account. Contains the kept sentence (Record A) and, in the final entry (February 11, 1983), Record B. Cale's name last appears in the October 1979 entry. The ledger was found open on the kitchen table by Deputy Harman on February 12, 1983; inventoried with the estate; left in situ.

**Morrow and Kilhenny** — Law firm on Davis Avenue, Elkins, West Virginia (third floor of sandstone building). Venue for the worker's compensation deposition Ellen takes in ch17.

**Robert Varga** — Deponent in the Elkins worker's compensation deposition (ch17). Mid-forties; back injury from roofing work on Route 20. Employed by the roofing company for nine years before the injury.

**chording** — The stenographic technique of pressing multiple keys simultaneously on the stenotype machine to produce a syllable or word in a single stroke. Ellen's primary professional skill.

**CAT (Computer-Aided Transcription)** — The software system that translates Ellen's stenotype keystrokes into readable text in real time on a monitor during a deposition. The rough draft it produces requires certification before the transcript is official.

**brief dictionary** — Ellen's personal file of steno briefs — coded syllable combinations built up over twenty years of proceedings that allow her to chord complete words and phrases in single strokes. Her professional fingerprint; not interchangeable with another reporter's.

**The Knocking (February 11, 1983)** — The central event of proposition P: Cale Markey's knocking at the front door of the Verrill Street house on the night of February 11, 1983, from approximately 9:40 to 9:52 PM. Three impacts at a time with deliberate even pauses. Heard by Della from the foot of the stairs; not answered. First rendered in real time in ch18; recorded in Record B (markey-ledger-1983) as 'knocking at the front door near quarter to ten — three raps, then steady.'

**Travel clock (della-travel-clock)** — Small travel clock kept by Della Markey on the mantle of the Verrill Street front room. Originally brought from Kanawha County by her grandmother's family; passed to Harold Markey's room; moved to the mantle when Harold moved into the back bedroom. Della (and Harold before her) set it by radio on Sunday mornings. Keeps accurate time. Reads 9:42 PM during the knocking in ch18; reads 9:52 PM when the knocking stops (per Record B). Also referenced as 'my father's clock' in Della's chapters (ch14, ch16).

**Markey Household Ledger** — A commercially-available ruled account book kept as a household account from April 1971 (Harold Markey's hand) through Della Markey's death in February 1983 (Della's hand from October 1975 onward). Contains Cale Markey's appearances in entries from 1975 to 1979, Harold Markey's death entry (October 1978), and the final February 1983 entries including Record B. Found by Ellen in ch19 in a cardboard box on the bottom shelf of the back-room closet.

**Renick Methodist** — A Methodist church in Renick where Harold Markey was buried October 20, 1978.

**Markey Household Account Book** — The ruled account book kept by Harold Markey from April 1971, then by Della from October 1975 after the hand-change midmonth. Brown cloth board cover worn at two corners; binding loosened past the halfway mark. Normal storage: the shelf above the back-room window. Contains the household records through Cale's appearances 1975–1979, Harold's death October 1978, and the final entry of February 11 1983 (Record B). Della reads to January 31 1983 and holds the page there in ch20.

**Travel Clock** — An eight-day clock in a dark wood case with a brass face; lighter than it appears; wound and kept on the back-room mantle since Della's time. Ellen winds it once in the spring of the present-day narrative; it runs continuously. In ch. 24 Ellen carries it from the back-room mantle to the desk in the front room, where it stands beside the maker's mark. A sensory anchor for Della's stopped night ('the travel clock on the mantle: wound, accurate, ticking through everything that stops') and, at the book's close, an object that has crossed from 1983 into the present.
