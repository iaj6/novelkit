# Plotter Guidance — The Long Tenant

## POV Count and Rotation

Two POV characters only: Ellen Vance (third-person limited past tense) and Della Markey (first-person present tense). Strict alternation across 24 chapters. The plotter chooses which POV opens; the alternation holds from there until it breaks asymmetrically near the end.

**The break**: Della's final chapter is the one in which her wrong belief corrects — the night seen whole, the kept sentence revealed as the lie she wrote herself. After Della's final chapter, the book continues only in Ellen's chapters. There is no Della chapter after the correction. The resulting structure is approximately 13 Ellen / 11 Della; this imbalance is the structure, not a flaw to even out.

The book closes in Ellen's chapters, in the house, with the door open.

## Chapter-Shape Preferences

**Della's chapters**: Tight and contained. Each re-enters the same stopped winter night (approximately 9:30 PM to past midnight, February 11, 1983) from a new angle. Each chapter accretes one or two new concrete physical facts Della has managed to re-grip — a sense datum, an object named and handled — while circling the central refusal she has not yet allowed herself to fully know. The clock does not advance; the knowledge accretes. Length: 1,800–2,400 words. Resist any shape where Della reflects on her life broadly or monologizes about what she knows; each chapter should feel forensic, not ruminative.

**Ellen's chapters**: Breathe and build. Each moves forward in real time through Ellen's spring and summer in the house. The shape varies: some chapters are primarily document work (the records room, the inquest file, the deed search); some are house work (the physical occupation and observation of the house); some are the work of the current trade (a deposition, a contract that has thinned). Length: 2,000–3,400 words. One or two of the longer chapters are the big discovery chapters (the inquest file, the back room, the ledger).

**Stalling chapters**: Two or three chapters across the 24 should deliberately not advance the central mystery — characters sitting with what they cannot yet know, a haunting's dead air. These are not failures; they are structural requirements. Mark them in the chapter map with a note explaining what they accomplish in terms of texture and pacing. At least one stalling chapter should occur in each major section (early, middle, late), and one should fall after a significant discovery so the reader has time to sit with it.

## The Chapter Map — Required Tags Per Chapter

`outline/00-chapter-map.md` must include, for every chapter:

1. **Chapter number and POV** (e.g., Ch 2 — Della)
2. **Ellen's clock** or **Della's clock** label (Ellen: specific date-range; Della: always "the stopped night of February 11, 1983")
3. **The specific object, record, or physical fact that crosses the membrane this chapter** (what information moves between the timelines, and via what vehicle — a document, an object moved, a record found)
4. **The exact seeded belief this chapter moves** (from the epistemic ledger): which knower, from which stance, to which new stance — or, if a stalling chapter, what the chapter deepens without moving
5. **The ending mode** (resolved-beat, literary-fade, turn, mid-action-cut, cliffhanger, elliptical, coda, declarative-close)

This per-chapter tagging makes the convergence auditable. Without it, "advances the truth" and "deepens a wrong belief" are indistinguishable to a mechanical review.

## The Documentary Spine — Placement Requirements

**Record A (the kept sentence, `kept-sentence`)**: First appears in Della's chapters 1–3. Returns throughout Della's circling chapters. Its weight accumulates by recurrence and context, not by elaboration. It should be present in a majority of Della's chapters, but not always in the same position or function.

**Record B (the ledger entry, `markey-ledger-1983`)**: Found by Ellen late — approximately chapters 19–21, in the back room behind the shut door. Its discovery is a major chapter-closing beat for Ellen. The A/B side-by-side moment — when Ellen has read the ledger and the kept sentence's meaning inverts — should fall at approximately chapter 21–22. This is the convergence.

## The False Crossing — Placement and Construction

The false crossing must fall in the middle section, approximately chapters 11–14. Ellen reads Mabel Duff's inquest statement (or the name the plotter assigns this neighbor). The statement attests no visitors on the evening of February 10 or the daylight hours of February 11. Ellen's inference: nobody came to the door that night — which aligns with the kept sentence she has been tracking.

This belief (Ellen `wrong_believes` ¬P) must persist for at least 2–3 chapters before the ledger disproves it. The false crossing should produce a chapter in which Ellen has apparently confirmed the county's account AND Della's kept sentence — a moment of apparent resolution that is structurally the farthest point from the truth.

## Ending-Mode Distribution

No two consecutive chapters close in the same mode. Across 24 chapters, target approximately:
- **literary-fade** (object/atmosphere close): 6 chapters — primarily Della's chapters, some Ellen's quiet chapters
- **resolved-beat** (a question answered or a sub-arc completed): 5 chapters
- **turn** (new information recontextualizes): 4 chapters — placed at the discovery chapters and the A/B side-by-side
- **mid-action-cut** (momentum into the next chapter): 3 chapters
- **elliptical** (deliberate ambiguity): 3 chapters — including Della's final chapter
- **declarative-close** (flat factual statement): 2 chapters — appropriate for Della's flattest chapters
- **coda** (reflective aftermath): 1 chapter — the book's penultimate or final Ellen chapter

No cliffhangers in the conventional genre sense (unresolved peril). The book's tension is epistemic, not physical.

## Chapter Target Words

Total target: approximately 60,000 words across 24 chapters. Average: ~2,500 words per chapter.

- Della's chapters: 1,800–2,400 words (tight, circling)
- Ellen's standard chapters: 2,200–2,800 words
- Ellen's major discovery chapters (inquest file; ledger): 2,800–3,400 words
- Stalling chapters (either POV): 1,800–2,200 words

## Genre-Specific Structural Conventions

**The two-sided haunting requires a converging knowledge-set.** Every chapter must either:
- Move a named knower from one epistemic stance toward another (even if toward a wrong belief — the false crossing is a movement), OR
- Deepen the texture of a stalled knowledge-state (Della re-gripping the night from a new angle without advancing)

Neither is a dead chapter. A chapter that does neither — that simply describes without any epistemic movement — is a structural failure.

**The non-monotonic convergence**: The mystery must not advance in a straight line. After the false crossing, Ellen moves backward (she now believes the wrong thing more firmly than before). At least one of Della's stalling chapters should produce a moment where she grips the night *and pulls back* — re-establishes the kept sentence more firmly — before the next approach. The reader should feel the mechanism getting harder to face before it gives way.

**The membrane rule never breaks.** No chapter proposes a mechanism by which the two POVs communicate directly. No dream sequence, no vision, no moment where Ellen hears Della's voice. All information crosses via physical objects, public records, and written documents. If a plotter's chapter concept requires direct contact, it requires a different concept.

**The unresolved ending is load-bearing.** The plotter should not schedule a chapter after Della's final chapter that resolves what happens to her. Ellen's closing chapters are in the house; the door is open; what the open door means is for the reader to hold. The plotter should resist the temptation to add one more beat that answers the question the book has chosen to leave in shadow.
