# Continuity — The Long Tenant

Hard facts. These must not break in any chapter, outline, or revision.

---

## The Death

1. Della Markey died on the night of February 11–12, 1983, in the house at Verrill Street, Renick, West Virginia. The official county ruling: accidental death, exposure/hypothermia. The cause as recorded: a storm, the power out, a stove that failed, a woman who lived alone.

2. The actual cause includes a factor the county's record does not contain: Della chose not to leave the house and chose not to open the door when Cale knocked, and she died in a cold she could have walked out of. This makes the county's "accidental" ruling technically correct on the mechanism and factually incomplete about the choice.

3. The historical event anchoring the death night is the Megalopolitan Blizzard, February 9–12, 1983 — documented power outages of 34,000+ homes in West Virginia; 35-inch snowfall totals in Glengary, WV; deep inland Appalachian reach.

4. Della's wood stove failed because the exterior-wall chimney lost draft in extreme cold. The flue went cold; draft reversed; the fire could not be re-established. This is the physical mechanism, consistent with documented stove failure modes in extreme cold. There was no mechanical defect; the stove did what stoves do in those conditions.

---

## The Spine: Who Came, and What Della Did

5. The person who came to Della's door was **Cale Markey**, her older brother, estranged for approximately three years. He came on the night of February 11, 1983, during the storm.

6. Cale knocked at approximately **9:40 PM** on February 11, 1983. The knocking stopped at approximately **9:52 PM** by Della's travel clock.

7. Della heard him. She recognized his knock. She chose not to go down. She did not open the door.

8. Della wrote what happened in her household ledger during or immediately after the knocking stopped — the knocking, the hour, the refusal. She then wrote the line that inverts everything: **"Nobody came to that door."** She died in the same night.

9. **The truth of the last night (P)**: *Someone came to Della's door on the night of February 11, 1983 — her brother Cale — and Della heard him and chose not to go down.* This proposition is the fixed spine. It is never changed by any drafting phase. It must implicate Della's own choice and knowledge, not an external failure.

---

## The Two Canonical Documents

10. **Record A — The kept sentence** (recordId: `kept-sentence`): The exact text is: `Nobody came to that door.` This is the phrase Della returns to in her chapters. It is flat, declarative, without qualification.

11. **Record B — The ledger entry** (recordId: `markey-ledger-1983`): The exact text is Della's final ledger entry. Its wording is fixed. It records the power out, the stove failing, the knocking at near quarter to ten, "three years I have not written his name but I knew it," the refusal to go down, the stopping of the knocking at 9:52, and ends: **"Nobody came to that door."** — the same five words as Record A, now meaning the opposite. Both documents must be read via `read_record` and reproduced verbatim when quoted in any chapter.

## The False Crossing

12. Somewhere in the middle of the book (approximately chapters 11–14), Ellen reads a neighbor's inquest statement — **Mabel Duff** (or the name the plotter assigns) — that attests no visitors were seen on the evening of February 10 or the daylight hours of February 11. Ellen initially reads this as confirmation that nobody came to the door. This is the false crossing. The statement is not a lie; Mabel observed daylight hours; the knocking came at 9:40 PM in the storm, in full dark, when Mabel was inside. The ledger later disproves the inference Ellen drew, not the statement itself.

## Structure and Alternation

13. The book has 24 chapters, alternating Ellen/Della. The plotter may choose which POV opens; the strict alternation holds until the truth lands. Approximately 13 Ellen chapters / 11 Della chapters — the imbalance is the structure, not a flaw to correct.

14. The alternation breaks asymmetrically at the close. Della's final chapter is the one in which her belief corrects — the night seen whole. There is no Della chapter after it. The book closes in Ellen's chapters, in the house, with the door open.

15. Two or three chapters are deliberately stalling — chapters in which neither POV advances significantly toward the truth. A haunting requires dead air. These are features, not failures.

---

## The Characters

16. Ellen Vance: age 44, present day. Freelance court reporter, twenty years' experience. Third-person limited past tense. Buys the Verrill Street house. Never sees Della as a figure, never hears words, never holds a séance.

17. Della Markey: born approximately 1946; age 37 at death; died February 11–12, 1983. First-person present tense. Her present tense is the stopped winter night of her death; it does not advance. She is not confused about being dead. Her error is her belief, not her condition.

18. Cale Markey: Della's older brother; approximately 3–5 years older than Della. Left Renick approximately 1980 after a dispute over their father's estate. Survived. His present-day whereabouts are not pursued.

---

## The Membrane: Hard Rules

19. The two POVs never meet as persons. Ellen never sees a figure, hears words, or holds a séance. Della never hears the living clearly; she perceives them as weather and disturbance, never as speech or thought she can read.

20. Every piece of truth crossing between them crosses indirectly — a physical object, a public record, a thing written down — never through direct contact. This rule does not break.

21. The supernatural is never explained. No lore, no rules, no medium, no scene that adjudicates what Della is. She simply is; the house simply does what it does.

---

## The Resolution

22. The death is resolved fully and fairly. The reader can assemble the complete account from what is on the page — the documents, the objects, the records. Neither Della's version (*nobody came*) nor the county's file (accidental death, alone) is the true account; both are partial. The third account, which recontextualizes both, is on the page by the close.

23. **One thread is deliberately unresolved**: what becomes of Della once she knows. Her final chapter ends on a concrete physical action caught mid-gesture — her hand on the latch, the ledger let go — on the object, not on a feeling or a fate. The prose does not say whether she is released, remains, or simply has no more to say. This is not a failure of resolution; it is the book's single chosen shadow.

24. The book closes in present-day, in Ellen, in the house, with the door open. This is the final image.

---

## Period and Procedure Accuracy

25. The inquest file from 1983 is held at the county level in the county clerk's or coroner's office, in physical form at the start of the book. The county is mid-digitization; the file is weeks from being scanned. Ellen's access is in person, at the records room. Access to coroner records is public in West Virginia.

26. A freelance court reporter's displacement by digital audio recording happens contract by contract, not by a single layoff. By 2022, approximately 21,300 stenographic reporters worked in the US, down from a projected 27,700 in 2018. Ellen's loss is the thinning of the call log, not a termination notice.

27. Della's father was a millwright at the Renick Furniture Works — a skilled tradesman trained in precision and systematic record-keeping. Della inherited the house from him. The household ledger-keeping is a direct transmission of the millwright's discipline for systematic record.
