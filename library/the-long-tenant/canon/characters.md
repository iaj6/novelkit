# Characters — The Long Tenant

---

## Ellen Vance — POV 1 (Third-person limited, past tense)

**Age**: 44, present day  
**Role**: Protagonist; the living tenant; the one who reads the record  

**Background**: Twenty years as a freelance court reporter — depositions, arbitrations, administrative hearings, custody proceedings. Certified RPR. Her work travels: she is contracted through agencies and law firms and follows proceedings wherever they are taken. She is technically excellent. She is professionally defined by the discipline of the exact word.

**What she is losing**: The contracts are thinning — digital audio recording and voice-to-text are taking court business county by county, cheaper and faster. She has not been laid off; she is simply receiving fewer calls from agencies. She does not say this aloud. She buys the Renick house with what she has left because the remaining work travels and a house there is what she can afford.

**Inner drives**: The guarantee that the exact word was said and preserved — not the gist, not the comfortable paraphrase, not *what he meant was*. Her whole professional identity is the non-interpretive record: she will reproduce what was said, not what was intended. She arrives at a house whose central mystery is a document that is both a perfect record and a perfect lie. She is the first person in forty years with the discipline to notice the difference.

She is forty-four and has spent two decades making the record of other people's lives without making much of a record of her own. The house is, without her framing it this way, the question she cannot yet ask herself. A marriage dissolved; she does not elaborate. She is not in Renick to find meaning. She is precise and underemployed and angry in a way she will not name.

**Voice and register**: Third-person limited, past tense. Restrained, concrete, procedural — the same discipline she applies to a deposition. Her lexical field (*on the record, off the record, certified transcript, deponent, mark it, stricken, rough draft, read-back, objection, for the record*) stays hers and does not migrate into Della's chapters. She observes her own interior with the same disciplined distance she applies to testimony. She notes what she cannot account for in the house and moves on. The prose does not convert her into a believer and does not decide for the reader what the house is.

---

### Ellen's Knowledge-Set and Membrane Rules

**What Ellen can know and discover**:
- Anything written in a public or physical record she can access: deed and probate records, the death certificate, the coroner's inquest file, local newspaper coverage, the county records room
- Physical objects present in the house — their condition, placement, what they imply
- What living people in Renick tell her
- Della's household ledger (Record B), which she finds late, in the back room behind the shut door; she cannot have accessed it before the narrative places her there

**What Ellen cannot know**:
- Anything Della experienced, felt, or decided that was not written down and physically preserved
- What the house is — she can observe its behavior and decline to interpret it
- Della's name, biography, or story until the records give it to her; she finds the house before she finds its history
- Cale Markey's role or the detail of the last night until Record B gives it to her

**Ellen's epistemic progression (the spine)**:
1. **Unaware** of Della, of the death, of any prior history — she buys a house in a cheap town
2. **Suspects** something happened in the house; notes deniable signs (the door, the cold, the attention in certain rooms) without committing to an interpretation
3. Begins to find the documentary record: deed, death certificate, inquest file. Reads a neighbor's inquest statement (Mabel Duff) attesting that no visitors were seen on the evening of February 10 — and *wrong-believes* ¬P: reads this as confirmation that nobody came to the door that night. The false crossing.
4. **Knows P** after finding and reading Record B (the ledger entry) in the back room: the entry records the knocking at near quarter to ten, the hour of 9:52, and ends on the words "Nobody came to that door" — the same words as the kept sentence, now meaning the opposite.

## Della Markey — POV 2 (First-person, present tense)

**Age at death**: 37; died the night of February 11–12, 1983  
**Born**: approximately 1946, Renick, West Virginia  
**Role**: The dead tenant; the keeper of the wrong belief; the one who made the mercy  

**Background**: Daughter of a millwright at the Renick Furniture Works — a skilled tradesman, trained in precision, measurement, and systematic record-keeping. Grew up in the house on Verrill Street, which her father owned and she inherited. Worked in the plant's office (records clerk or similar). Never married. Estranged from her older brother Cale Markey for approximately three years before her death — over the settling of their father's estate, or the ordinary irrecoverable family rupture when one person leaves and the other stays.

**Her kept sentence**: *"Nobody came to that door."* — the mercy she made to live inside her own death. She returns to it not by restating it but by re-gripping, each chapter, one more cold physical object of the night she has refused. She has maintained this sentence for forty years. She is wrong about it. She knows she is dead. She is not confused about her condition. Her error is not her state but her belief about her last night.

**What she actually did**: She heard the knocking at near quarter to ten on the night of February 11, 1983. Three years she had not written her brother's name, but she recognized his knock. She chose not to go down. She sat with the travel clock and watched the cold come. She wrote what happened in the ledger — the knocking, the hour, the refusal, the stopping of the knocking — and ended the entry "Nobody came to that door." She died in a cold she could have walked out of.

**Inner drives**: The refusal is not confusion — it is will. The alternative to *nobody came* is that she let herself die over a refusal: pride, or grief that Cale came so late and she could not forgive him for all the years before the storm. That is the other thing, and the other thing is hers. Her chapters circle toward the full memory not as a narrative of progress but as the forensic accretion of concrete physical facts she manages, each chapter, to re-grip one step further.

**Voice and register**: First-person, present tense. Plain, flat, unliterary — a millwright's daughter keeping a record, not a narrator making art. Her lexical field (*the stove, the damper, the flue, the draft, the latch, the door, the ledger, the cold, cord wood, kindling, the column of figures, the clock, the shift, the plant*) stays hers and does not migrate. No metaphor she wouldn't reach for naturally. Her interiority is not withheld; it is simply the interiority of a specific working woman, plain in its expression and specific in its objects.

---

### Della's Knowledge-Set and Membrane Rules

**What Della knows permanently**:
- The house in its 1983 state — rooms, objects, the stove, the back-room ledger
- The night of February 11–12, 1983 — re-entered from new angles, never advanced past the hour the knocking stopped
- Her own physical sensations through the progression of hypothermia: the cold she felt, the failing warmth, the specific weight of the blanket
- That she is dead; that the house has had other tenants since; that she is here still
- Her own ledger: she knows it exists in the back room; she does not allow herself to fully recall the last entry

**What Della cannot know or perceive**:
- Anything that happened after February 1983 — no present-day facts, no news, no history since her death
- Ellen as a person — she perceives the living as pressure, footfall, warmth at a wrong angle, unaccountable light or sound that is not 1983 sound; never as speech, thought, intention, or identity
- What records the county made of her death, or what the inquest found
- What became of Cale after he stopped knocking

**Della's epistemic state**:
- **Wrong believes** ¬P ("Nobody came to that door") throughout most of the book — the mercy she has maintained for forty years
- **Knows P** at her final chapter: the circling closes on the complete memory of that night — the knocking was his, she chose not to open, she wrote the lie herself, she died in the cold she could have walked out of. What happens next is deliberately unresolved.

## Cale Markey — Off-page, load-bearing

**Relationship**: Della's older brother  
**Age in 1983**: Approximately 40–42 (older than Della)  
**Status at present-day story time**: Alive, somewhere; his present whereabouts are unknown to Ellen and not pursued  

**Role in the death**: He drove to Renick on the night of February 11, 1983, during the Megalopolitan Blizzard, and knocked at the front door of the Verrill Street house. He knocked for approximately twelve minutes (near 9:40 to 9:52 PM by Della's travel clock). He stopped. He left. He did not know she died inside.

**History**: Left Renick approximately 1980, after their father's death and a dispute over the estate. A tradesman or laborer — a millwright's son, probably in a related trade. He came back once, at the worst possible time, and she would not let him in.

**In the record**: His name appears implied in Della's ledger ("three years I have not written his name"). He may appear in probate records as a potential heir to the estate, which is how Ellen might eventually confirm who knocked.

## Mabel Duff — Functional; the false crossing

**Role**: A neighbor on Verrill Street who discovered Della's body after the storm lifted, or who gave testimony at the 1983 inquest. Her statement attests that she saw no visitors on the evening of February 10 and the morning of February 11. This is technically true — she was watching during daylight hours; the knocking came at 9:40 PM, in the storm, in full dark, when Mabel was inside. Ellen initially reads Mabel's statement as confirmation that nobody came to the door that night. This is the false crossing. It is not Mabel's lie; it is an honest statement covering the wrong window.
