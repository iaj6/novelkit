# Bibliography — *The Long Tenant*

- `[cite:01]` — "What is Court Reporting." National Court Reporters Association (NCRA). https://www.ncra.org/home/the-profession/Court-Reporting. Used for: core definition of the profession, freelance vs. official court reporting, stenotype speed standards, realtime capability.

- `[cite:02]` — "Court Reporters v. Digital Recording and Voice Recognition: A Comprehensive Breakdown." CLE Reporting. https://clereporting.com/court-reporters-v-digital-recording-and-voice-recognition-a-comprehensive-breakdown/. Used for: quality arguments against digital replacement (crosstalk, speaker identification, ancillary duties), the verbatim discipline, accuracy rate comparison.

- `[cite:03]` — "Verbatim Reporters, What We Do." StenoStrides. https://stenostrides.com/what-we-do/. Used for: "guardians of the record" framing, impartiality, scope of the profession, 70% outside-courtroom statistic.

- `[cite:04]` — "Court Reporter." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_reporter. Used for: Ducker Report projections vs. actual 2022 count (27,700 → 21,300), state-level applicant decline (85%), digital recording alternatives, official vs. freelance distinctions.

- `[cite:05]` — "Court Reporting for Attorneys — Terminology & Acronyms, Part 1." Pohlman USA. https://pohlmanusa.com/what-attorneys-should-know-about-court-reporting-part-1-terminology-and-acronyms/. Used for: comprehensive professional vocabulary list (RPR, CSR, CRR, CCR, scopist, rough draft, realtime, stipulation, deponent, ELMO, ASCII, expedited transcript).

- `[cite:06]` — "How Does a Court Reporter Machine Work? Guide for Legal Professionals." Gallo Legal. https://www.gallo.legal/how-does-a-court-reporter-machine-work/. Used for: stenotype machine technical description (22 keys, chording, phonetic layout), CAT software, briefs system, 225+ wpm capability, professional training requirements.

- `[cite:07]` — "How AI Is Changing Courtroom Recording and Transcription." JAVS. https://www.javs.com/2026/02/09/how-ai-is-changing-courtroom-recording-and-transcription/. Used for: 40%+ decline in training programs since 2015, Kentucky implementation of digital recording, AI quality limitations in legal contexts (accents, crosstalk, technical vocabulary).

- `[cite:08]` — "Coroner's Records & Inquest Case Files." Learn Genealogy. https://learngenealogy.com/coroners-records/. Used for: coroner jurisdiction for unexpected deaths, inquest jury process, content of inquest files, county-level storage, digitization status.

- `[cite:09]` — "You Won't Find This Information on a Death Certificate: Coroners' Records Revealed." Family History Daily. https://familyhistorydaily.com/genealogy-resources/coroner-records/. Used for: detailed content of inquest files (witness testimony richest source, necrology reports, police statements, physical ephemera), death certificate vs. inquest file distinction, modern access methods.

- `[cite:10]` — "Allegheny County Coroner Case Files." University of Pittsburgh Archives & Special Collections. https://pitt.libguides.com/coronercasefile. Used for: physical organization of coroner case files, what they contain (physician notes, affidavits, photographs, ephemera), access procedures (Aeon system, $12 per file), date range limitations.

- `[cite:11]` — "Autopsy and Coroner's Reports." Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press (RCFP). https://www.rcfp.org/open-government-sections/a-autopsy-and-coroners-reports/. Used for: state-by-state public access status for coroner reports, common exceptions (ongoing criminal investigation, photography restrictions), access procedures by state.

- `[cite:12]` — "Preserving County Records: How Document Digitization Builds a Lasting Legacy." Revolution Data Systems. https://www.revolutiondatasystems.com/blog/preserving-county-records-how-document-digitization-builds-a-lasting-legacy. Used for: the digitization-and-disposal reality (each agency decides what to keep or destroy), Federal Records Act 2014 amendment enabling disposal of originals, the urgency of the window before records are lost.

- `[cite:13]` — "Furniture Industry: Modern Industry." NCPedia. https://www.ncpedia.org/furniture/modern-industry. Used for: High Point as national marketing center, peak employment at 90,000, decline from 1990s globalization, 47 companies closing in the 1990s, regional quality of NC furniture objects.

- `[cite:14]` — "The Rise and Fall of North Carolina's Furniture Industry." Anderson Woodworks. https://andersonwoodwork.net/2025/06/01/the-rise-and-fall-of-north-carolinas-furniture-industry/. Used for: the social and cultural impact of plant closure on communities, specific company closures (Thomasville, Henredon, Lexington), empty buildings and shrinking populations, loss of high-paying positions.

- `[cite:15]` — "History of the Renick Community." West Virginia Division of Culture and History. https://archive.wvculture.org/history/agrext/renick.html. Used for: the real Renick community in Greenbrier County WV (Scotch-Irish settlement, Greenbrier Valley location, desk factory, lumber operations, creamery, Chesapeake & Ohio Railroad 1900, Bank of Renick 1909).

- `[cite:16]` — "History." Richwood Chamber of Commerce. https://www.richwoodchamberofcommerce.org/history. Used for: Richwood WV as a model furniture/timber mill town (B&O Railroad 1900, largest clothes pin factory, largest tannery, 200,000 acres of timberland, population peak 1920s ~7,000, 1929 collapse, mining automation 1980s causing sudden decline).

- `[cite:17]` — "The Pathophysiological Mechanisms of the Onset of Death Through Accidental Hypothermia." PMC / National Institutes of Health. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4462406/. Used for: clinical stages of hypothermia (mild/moderate/severe and their temperature thresholds), mental deterioration in moderate stage (delusions, hallucinations), EEG silence at 19–20°C, the diagnostic warning that hypothermic patients "should not be pronounced dead until warm and dead."

- `[cite:18]` — "Hypothermia (Low Body Temperature): Signs, Causes & Treatment." Cleveland Clinic. https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21164-hypothermia-low-body-temperature. Used for: indoor hypothermia risk at sustained temperatures of 60–65°F, vulnerable populations (elderly, ill, malnourished).

- `[cite:19]` — "History of Wood Heat: Evolution of Sustainable Heating." For Green Heat. https://www.forgreenheat.org/history-of-wood-heat. Used for: post-1973 oil-embargo surge in wood stove use (0.9% → 8.2% of homes by 1981), nearly 2 million stoves sold annually in early 1980s, 450 manufacturers by 1982, half of rural Americans burning wood by 1981.

- `[cite:20]` — "How Chimneys Work." WoodHeat.org. https://www.woodheat.org/how-chimneys-work.html. Used for: wood stove draft mechanics, cold chimney failure (exterior chimneys reversing draft in extreme cold), stack effect, replacement-air requirements, why a cold chimney is extremely difficult to re-establish.

- `[cite:21]` — "The Cold Air Outbreak of December 1983." National Weather Service, Southern Region. https://www.weather.gov/oun/events-19831215. Used for: December 15–31 1983 cold air outbreak, 151 national deaths, record Oklahoma City temperatures (−3°F lows), extreme cold in eastern two-thirds of nation, deaths concentrated among unprepared elderly.

- `[cite:22]` — "February 1983 North American Blizzard." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_1983_North_American_blizzard. Used for: Megalopolitan Blizzard dates (Feb 9–12), West Virginia snowfall totals (Glengary 35 inches, Woodstock VA 32 inches), 34,000+ homes without power in Charleston and Pineville WV, 46 deaths, inland Appalachian extent.

- `[cite:23]` — "Millwright." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millwright. Used for: millwright trade history (origins in watermill construction, industrial revolution evolution), core skills (drive shafts, bearings, gearing, alignment, rigging, welding), "itinerant engineer of high reputation" characterization, Fairbairn description of precision skills, modern 45% unionization rate.

- `[cite:24]` — "Monthly Labor Review." U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 1983. https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/1983/. Used for: 1983 economic context (unemployment, manufacturing job losses), as source for recession period working conditions.

- `[cite:25]` — "Early 1980s Recession." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_1980s_recession. Used for: peak unemployment 10.8% November 1982, manufacturing sector lost 1.7 million jobs 1979–1982, real median household income fell 5.7% by 1983, state-level extremes (MI, WV, AL reaching 23.4% in some metro areas), adult women's unemployment rose to 9.2%.

- `[cite:26]` — "Hypothermia Stages: Symptoms, Definition, Death." eMedicineHealth. https://www.emedicinehealth.com/how_long_does_it_take_to_die_from_hypothermia/article_em.htm. Used for: staged symptom progression (mild: shivering/slurred speech; moderate: loss of coordination, drowsiness, confusion; severe: unconsciousness, cardiac arrest), the insidious quality of hypothermia's cognitive impairment (many victims unaware).

- `[cite:27]` — "Economic Change: From Traditional Industries to the 21st Century Economy." NCPedia Anchor. https://www.ncpedia.org/anchor/economic-change-and-traditional-industries. Used for: post-WWII pressure on traditional industries (tobacco, textile, furniture), the 1970s–80s as onset of decline, foreign wage competition and labor-saving technology as dual drivers.

- `[cite:28]` — "The Deposition Process: A Guide for Legal Teams." Lexitas Legal. https://www.lexitaslegal.com/resources/deposition-process-guide-legal-teams. Used for: deposition structure (notice, oath, examination, cross, transcript), off-the-record procedure (requires all-party agreement), certified transcript, deponent definition.

- `[cite:29]` — "Shotgun House." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotgun_house. Used for: narrow rectangular worker housing (12 feet wide, rooms arranged one behind the other) as the dominant working-class housing form in the post-Civil War South and mill towns; frequently owned by factories and rented to workers for a few dollars a month.

- `[cite:30]` — "How to Fix Chimney Draft Problems." Mother Earth News. https://www.motherearthnews.com/diy/drafty-chimney-problems-zmaz86jfzglo/. Used for: specific chimney draft problem causes in winter, practical framing of the failure modes a rural homeowner would actually encounter.
