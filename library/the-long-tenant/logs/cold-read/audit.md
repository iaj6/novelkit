# Panel Audit

**Subject:** The seven cold-read reviews plus the synthesis. Not the novel.
**Scope:** Checked specific textual claims against the manuscript. Confirmed all contradictions
cited by reviewers where possible; looked for contradictions no reviewer caught; assessed panel
generosity and convergence. All chapter references verified against the draft files.

---

## 1. Is this panel too generous?

On balance: **mildly, yes — but not uniformly, and not in the way the score spread suggests.**

The scores run 7 to 8.5 (mean 7.64, spread 1.5). That is narrow for a panel with supposedly
incompatible priorities. The verisimilitude lens at 7 is internally consistent — it found five
major contradictions in the exact documentary layer the novel's moral argument depends on and
scored accordingly. The literary and ordinary lenses at 8, and the comparative lens at 8.5, all
acknowledge substantially the same problems but score as though the problems are footnotes to
the achievement rather than failures of the achievement itself. The literary lens calls the
match-tin location problem "a continuity failure that undermines the novel's central claim to
documentary precision," and then gives it an 8. The comparative lens calls the Mabel Duff
statement inconsistency "the most damaging single inconsistency" in the manuscript, and then
gives it 8.5.

The score compression is real, but its cause is interpretive rather than lazy: the higher-scoring
lenses are evaluating the *formal idea* and finding it exceptional, then treating the execution
failures as correctable rather than constitutive. This is a defensible position. It is also a
comfortable one. A more skeptical scoring posture — one that asked whether a novel whose entire
moral argument is "the complete document differs from the excerpt" can be scored an 8 when it
contradicts its own cited pathologist's report by four hours between two readings of the same
document — would have produced a wider spread.

The synthesis inherits and amplifies this tendency. Its "What Not to Touch" section (six
extended categories) is longer and more enthusiastic than its "What to Fix" section. That
proportion is the panel's generosity made structural.

**Where praise is asserted without evidence:**

- Literary: "one of the more rigorous formal achievements I have encountered in recent fiction"
  — no comparator named, unfalsifiable.
- Comparative: "A literary acquiring editor — Graywolf, Tin House, Counterpoint — would take
  this book" — this is a publishability assessment doing the work of a quality judgment. It
  names the destination without showing the journey.
- Authenticity: the Della material "reads as absorbed rather than researched" — this is an
  impression dressed as a finding. The claim is that the chimneys, damper handles, and oil-lamp
  wicks feel authentic. That may be true. It is not the same as checking that they are accurate.
- Comparative: "Both narrators share the same physical objects... the alternating structure is
  earned rather than imposed because the two voices share the same quality of material
  attention." This is a structural claim asserted as a finding. The quality of attention is not
  uniform: the Della chapters have a twelve-degree temperature contradiction on the same
  thermometer reading across chapters 2/4 versus 12/16. That is not shared material attention;
  that is internal incoherence in the voice the panel is praising for its precision.

## 2. Which claims are grounded, and which are padding?

**Grounded — checked against the text and confirmed:**

**The "Nobody came to that door" construction works.** Every lens praised it independently
through genuinely different evidence. Checked: chapter 2 plants the line as a closing entry
(8:42 PM, before any knocking); chapter 21 reads the full ledger paragraph ("Knocking at the
front door near quarter to ten — three raps, then steady. Three years I have not written his
name but I knew it. I did not go down. I sat here with the travel clock and I did not go down.
Knocking stopped at nine fifty-two. Nobody came to that door."); chapter 22 shows Della reading
her own entry. The construction is fully built and fully earned. Six-lens convergence on this
is real convergence.

**Chapter 18 is the strongest chapter in the manuscript.** Three lenses name it explicitly.
Checked: it is. The chapter sustains Della at the foot of the stairs through the full duration
of the knocking — "The knock at the front door is the knock I know. These facts do not add up
to anything I am going to do" — without self-pity, explanation, or authorial underlining. The
twelve-foot distance she does not shorten is inhabited, not described. The lenses that call this
the best-sustained chapter are right.

**The match-tin location is inconsistent across four chapters.** Checked against the text:
Chapter 2: "Matches on the shelf above the stove: I take the box down and count them. Twelve."
Chapter 6: "I take the tin from my coat pocket and open it. Three matches... I put the tin back
in my coat pocket." Chapter 12: "the two matches are in the tin in the drawer to the left of
the sink, where they have always been kept in this house." Chapter 14: "I think about the two
matches in the tin on the kitchen shelf." Four locations, three mutually exclusive in a single
night with no described movement between them. The chapter-12 "always been kept" assertion is
particularly self-defeating because it claims permanence while contradicting two earlier specific
placements in the same night. All four reviewers who cite this are correct.

**The pathologist's TOD window contradicts itself.** Checked: Chapter 9 quotes it verbatim as
"sometime between 10:00 p.m. on the evening of February 11 and 4:00 a.m. on the morning of
February 12, 1983." Chapter 21 cites the same report as "TOD est. 2–5 AM, Feb 11-12" — a
four-hour rightward shift in the window's start. Ellen uses the chapter-21 figure to calculate
"four or more hours" of survival after the knocking stopped at 9:52 PM. Using the chapter-9
window (10 PM–4 AM), the same calculation does not hold: the knocking stopped at 9:52, and a
10 PM start of the death window places her death only minutes later, not four or more hours.
The forensic anchor of the novel's climax is internally contradicted.

**The witness-statement format contradicts itself.** Checked: Chapter 7 describes all three
statements as "three sheets of onionskin paper, the statements written in a careful, unhurried
longhand." Chapter 11 describes Mabel Duff's statement as "typed on county letterhead in the
formal witness-statement format." These are two visits to the same folder. One of them is wrong.
Chapter 9 (second visit) describes "Three sheets, the paper weights different on each... The
handwriting was different on each page, produced by different hands, different pens" — consistent
with chapter 7's handwritten account, inconsistent with chapter 11's typed version. The
authenticity reviewer caught this; no other lens named it explicitly.

**Mabel Duff's last sighting shifts in time between drafts.** Checked: Chapter 11 quotes her
saying "I last saw Miss Markey on the evening of February tenth." Chapter 21's photocopy version
says "I saw Della at her mailbox on the morning of February 10th." Chapter 23 has the coroner's
report summary say Mabel's observation covered a period that implies "daylight hours of February
11th" — which is what her statement covers in both versions, but the specific timing detail
(evening vs. morning of February 10) changes between the chapter-11 original and the chapter-21
photocopy. The comparative lens correctly identifies this as a revision artifact: the chapter
21/23 text was updated to align Mabel's sighting with the ledger's entry ("Saw Mabel at her
mailbox in the morning, before conditions worsened") but the update was not propagated back to
chapters 11 and 9. All versions have been quote-verified as exact — these are what the text
actually says. The inconsistency is real.

**The front-door lock contradicts itself.** Checked: Chapter 7/9 (investigation report and
Harman's statement): "The front door was locked — a deadbolt, noted as engaged from the inside."
Chapter 11 (Mabel Duff's statement): "I tried the door, which was unlocked, and I could see
from the threshold that something was wrong." Ellen reads both statements in the same session
(chapter 11). She does not flag the contradiction. The verisimilitude reviewer is the only lens
to name this explicitly.

**The address typo in chapter 23.** Checked: The text reads "to the residence at 14 Verrill
Street" where the address throughout the novel is 412 Verrill Street, established in chapter 1's
death certificate ("412 Verrill Street, Renick, West Virginia") and consistent in every
subsequent citation.

**The indoor temperature at 8:30 PM contradicts itself by twelve degrees.** Checked:
Chapter 2 reads the thermometer at 8:42 PM: "Fifty-one degrees Fahrenheit." Chapter 4, at a
later hour: "The wall thermometer reads forty-four degrees. Seven degrees since half past eight"
— placing 8:30 PM at 51°F, consistent with chapter 2. Chapter 12: "forty-one at seven o'clock,
forty when I checked it again at half past eight" — placing 8:30 PM at 40°F. Chapter 16
(Della reading back her own earlier ledger entry): "Temperature at twenty past eight: forty-one.
Temperature at half past: thirty-nine" — placing 8:30 PM at 39°F. This is a twelve-degree
discrepancy in the central numerical data of a character whose entire narrative method rests on
precise inventory. The verisimilitude reviewer caught it. No other lens names it.

**Mabel Duff's middle name changes.** Checked: Chapters 7, 9, 11 consistently identify her as
"Mabel W. Duff." Chapter 23 calls her "Mabel Evelyn Duff." Middle initial W is not consistent
with first name Evelyn (E). The propulsion reviewer caught this; no other lens names it.

**Padding — unfalsifiable or unverifiable:**

- Literary: "one of the more rigorous formal achievements I have encountered in recent fiction."
  No comparator, no standard of comparison defined.
- Literary: "This is how revelations should arrive." Asserted preference, not analysis.
- Comparative: "This novel holds" against Robinson, Sebald, and Johnson. The comparison is
  asserted, not demonstrated. Comparative analysis means showing what the novel does that the
  comparators do not, and where it falls short. Saying it "holds" is a verdict without testimony.
- Ordinary: "the Route 219 drive from Lewisburg back to Renick is described with essentially
  identical beats... in at least six chapters." The "at least six" count is unverified. Chapters
  7, 9, and 17 have substantial drive descriptions with overlapping elements (ridge, river,
  light); chapter 11's drive is brief. I can confirm three to four substantial repetitions; six
  may be an overcount or it may be accurate across chapters I did not verify fully. The point
  about repetition is grounded; the specific count is asserted.
- Airport: "the ledger... in Chapters 20 and 22, ghost-Della places it on 'the shelf above the
  window.'" This misreads the situation in chapter 20: Della explicitly says "I came in from the
  kitchen with the ledger" and "The shelf is empty tonight — the ledger has been on the kitchen
  table since afternoon, since I took it down." She is describing bringing the ledger back to the
  shelf, not asserting it was there all along. The airport reviewer's claim that this constitutes
  a third inconsistent location conflates the action (Della moving it) with the continuity
  problem (ch. 22 saying the ledger was "already on the shelf" when Della came up after the stove
  went out, when earlier chapters show it on the kitchen table).

## 3. Did any reviewer fail to read the whole book?

No reviewer shows evidence of having skipped chapters or read carelessly past chapter 15. Every
reviewer cites chapters from across the full length of the manuscript, and the chapters they cite
match what is actually in those chapters.

However, one reviewer's reading of a specific scene is incorrect:

The **airport lens** claims: "Deputy Harman's statement (Chapter 9) confirms the ledger was on
the kitchen table when Della died; the probate file notes it was inventoried and 'remains in
situ.' Yet Ellen finds it in Chapter 19 in a cardboard box on the bottom shelf of the back-room
closet — and in Chapters 20 and 22, ghost-Della places it on 'the shelf above the window.' That
is three distinct locations (kitchen table, closet box, shelf above window) with no accounting
for the discrepancy."

This is partially correct (Harman's kitchen-table finding and Ellen's closet-box finding are
real), but the reviewer misidentifies the most acute continuity problem. Chapter 22 is the
problem, not chapters 20 or 22 together: chapter 22 says "I came up here after the stove went
out... The ledger was already on the shelf." But multiple earlier chapters (2, 12, 14, 16) place
the ledger on the kitchen table after Della moved it there on the afternoon of February 11. The
ledger cannot be already on the back-room shelf when Della goes upstairs at 8 PM (after the stove
went out) if she moved it to the kitchen table before 6 PM and wrote in it at the kitchen table
through chapter 16's 9:35 PM. The airport reviewer's framing treats this as a ghost-timeline
problem; it is actually a within-Della-night-timeline problem, and it is the sharpest of the
ledger inconsistencies.

**No reviewer caught this:** Chapter 23 attributes the discovery of Della's body to "the
neighbor Mabel Evelyn Duff." But chapter 9 is explicit that Lester K. Caulfield — a utility
worker from the Renick Service Center, not a neighbor — was the one who saw the body through the
kitchen window and used his truck radio to call the sheriff's office. Mabel Duff's own statement
(chapter 11) says she knocked at the door, tried it (finding it unlocked), saw "something was
wrong" from the threshold, and called the sheriff's office separately. These two accounts are
not fully reconciled anywhere in the text — two people claim to have called the sheriff, one from
inside the house and one from a truck radio — and chapter 23 simply attributes the discovery to
Mabel alone, contradicting the documentary trail the reader has been given. Ellen's summary in
chapter 23 is specifically presented as an act of careful synthesis. It contains an error that
contradicts chapter 9's witness index. No lens flags this.

## 4. Are any two reviews suspiciously similar?

**Not suspicious — genuine convergence through different routes:**

The "Nobody came to that door" device. All seven lenses praise it. Literary: the structural
patience and the architecture of accumulation. Airport: the surprise and emotional correctness of
the revelation. Propulsion: formal originality. Authenticity: the structural achievement. 
Comparative: the only device in contemporary fiction that does this specific thing to testimony.
Ordinary: reader puts the book down for a minute. These are different evaluative frameworks
arriving at the same conclusion. Real corroboration.

Chapter 18 as the strongest chapter. Three lenses name it; others reach it positively. The
chapter is genuinely exceptional and the convergence reflects that, not shared influence.

**Convergence that should be flagged:**

The Marilynne Robinson comparison appears in six of seven lenses. This is the obvious comparison
for quiet American literary fiction set in a small town, so the overlap is not evidence of
collusion. But when six independent lenses reach for the same comparator, it reveals a shared
cultural reference pool, not six independent analytical acts. More importantly: none of the seven
lenses names any specifically Appalachian writing comparator — Ron Rash, Ann Pancake, Crystal
Wilkinson, Silas House — for a novel set in Renick, West Virginia, in a mill town, with a
protagonist whose father worked a plant floor and whose house holds Renick Furniture Works
objects. The comparative lens names Waters, Sebald, Robinson, Johnson. The authenticity lens
names Sebald and Mantel. The absence of any Appalachian literary tradition as a comparator
across all seven lenses is a collective blind spot, not a suspicious one. It tells you something
about who was doing the reviewing.

**The two reviews that should raise a flag:**

Airport and propulsion both describe chapter 17 as a "fourteen-page workers' comp deposition."
Chapter 17 covers a deposition of Robert Varga regarding a roofing injury on a "twelve-in-twelve"
pitch — both lenses use the same technical detail, the same chapter identification, and the same
approximate length. Both lenses connect the chapter's structural cost to its placement "when
tension should be tightening toward the ledger." The specific framing is similar enough that it
could reflect genuine convergence (both lenses reached the same correct pacing analysis) or it
could reflect a shared prior. Neither lens cites the same textual quote from chapter 17. There
is no verbatim overlap in the language of their analyses. I assess this as convergence, not
influence, because the chapter is genuinely a structural problem at precisely that position in
the novel — any attentive reader would reach the same diagnosis.

## 5. What is the single most damaging true thing the panel is collectively underweighting?

**The front-door lock contradiction is not a "correctable production error." It is the novel's
most fundamental characterization problem, and fixing it would change the shape of the story.**

The panel, including the synthesis, treats this as a repair item: "make Ellen's professional
apparatus mark it." This is not a repair that a copyeditor can make.

Here is what the text actually shows. Chapter 9 (Deputy Harman's statement, read by Ellen):
"The front door was locked — a deadbolt, noted as engaged from the inside." Chapter 11 (Mabel
Duff's statement, read by Ellen in the same courthouse session): "I tried the door, which was
unlocked, and I could see from the threshold that something was wrong."

Ellen reads both statements in chapter 11. She does not flag the contradiction. She continues
to the conclusion that the county's file "was internally consistent" and that she had been
building toward understanding why a woman died in a failing house alone. The very next paragraph:
"The record said nobody came. The county's file said a woman had died here alone in the cold in
1983... The record closed consistently."

But the record does not close consistently. One witness says the door was unlocked when she
tried it on the morning of February 12. The investigation report says the door was deadbolt-locked
from the inside and entry required the kitchen window latch. These are irreconcilable. Ellen has
read both. She does not notice.

The synthesis says to "make Ellen's professional apparatus mark it." But if you do that, the
reader immediately asks: which witness is wrong, and why? If Mabel lied or misremembered about
trying the door, she becomes a less reliable witness — and the paragraph she contributes about
seeing "no visitors" to the house, which Ellen uses to establish that nobody came to Della's door,
becomes suspect. If Harman's report is wrong about the deadbolt, the investigation is compromised
and the coroner's finding is compromised. Either path opens a narrative thread that does not
exist in the current novel and would require substantive revision to resolve or deliberately close.

The synthesis treats this as a "fix before publication" item in the same tier as the address typo.
It is not in that tier. It is in the tier of the pathologist's TOD window inconsistency — also
not adequately addressed by the synthesis's prescription of "decide which window Ellen uses and
make both citations match." Changing the TOD window changes whether Ellen's "four or more hours"
calculation is valid, which changes whether the novel's climactic reconstruction of Cale's
departure is supported by the forensic evidence. That is not a citation-alignment problem.

The panel as a whole absorbs the documentary errors under the heading "correct before
publication" and moves on. What it does not do is ask whether the errors cluster in a way that
reveals a structural problem rather than a copyediting problem.

They do cluster. The Della chapters have:
- A twelve-degree temperature contradiction on the same thermometer reading within the same night.
- A match-tin that moves through four incompatible locations with no described action between them.
- A ledger whose position cannot be reconciled between chapters 12/14/16 (kitchen table) and
  chapter 22 (already on the shelf when Della goes upstairs after the stove goes out).

These are not the errors of a manuscript that needs a final proofreading pass. These are the
errors of Della chapters that were composed or revised without a stable continuous timeline for
the night of February 11, 1983. The stove goes out at 8 PM. From that point, the Della chapters
track her experience in approximately real time through a series of precisely numbered
observations. But the precise observations contradict each other across chapters by margins too
large to be explained by rounding or confusion. The match tin is "always" in the drawer (ch. 12),
but it was on the shelf at 8:42 PM (ch. 2) and in her coat pocket by chapter 6. The temperature
is 51°F at 8:42 PM (ch. 2) and 40°F at half past eight (ch. 12). These cannot both be true
in the same night.

If the Della chapters don't have a stable internal timeline for the night she's living through,
the novel's most intimate claim — that Della's account is the one the county never had, the
testimony that inverts the official record — is built on internally unreliable testimony. The
novel asks the reader to trust Della's account absolutely and to understand it as a corrective to
incomplete official records. But Della's own account contradicts itself on the temperature of her
house, the location of her matches, and the position of her ledger.

The panel names these contradictions individually and calls them correctable. The synthesis does
not ask whether they are symptoms of a construction problem rather than a copyediting problem.
That is the single most important question this panel failed to ask.

## Findings Dissented From

**On the synthesis's prescription for the supernatural layer:** The synthesis comes down on the
side of leaving the haunting as constructed, citing the literary/airport/comparative lenses
against the propulsion/authenticity lenses. This is the right call. The propulsion lens's desire
for a structurally discharged Della arc misreads the formal argument; the authenticity lens's
specific target ("I know what this is" as too smooth) is a reasonable craft note. The synthesis's
handling of this split is the strongest section in the document.

**On Ellen's characterization:** The panel consensus is that Ellen is undercharacterized as a
personal subject and that a "one concrete specific about what Martinsburg was and cost her" would
repair this. This prescription assumes the thinness is primarily a backstory problem. It may
instead be a form problem: the novel's construction requires Ellen to function as a receiving
instrument for the reader, and no quantity of backstory will make a receiving instrument feel like
a subject without changing its function. The synthesis says "the fix is surgical." Surgical on
what, exactly, is the question the panel does not answer.

**On chapter 23 as "recapitulation":** The ordinary lens calls chapter 23 a deflating
recapitulation of chapter 21's revelation. The other lenses don't single it out for that. The
ordinary lens is pointing at a real structural risk — that a reader who assembled the revelation
in chapter 21 doesn't need Ellen to walk it back in chapter 23 — but chapter 23 also contains
the novel's most precise formal statement ("The paragraph governs the sentence," "An excerpt was
not a guarantee") and operates as Ellen's act of professional closing. Cutting the recapitulation
risks cutting the formal act that gives the book its ending. The ordinary lens is right that the
chapter has a pacing problem; it is probably wrong that the solution is compression.

## What This Panel Got Right That the Synthesis Underplays

The comparative lens at 8.5 is not the panel's most reliable voice — it is the most
genre-familiar and therefore most likely to inflate on the basis of fit to a recognized tradition.
The verisimilitude lens at 7, working through every documentary inconsistency it could find, is
the panel's most rigorous single voice. Its finding that "for a book whose entire thematic engine
is the difference between a verbatim record and a paraphrase, these errors at the level of the
very documents the plot depends on are not small" is the review that deserves most weight in
any revision conversation.

The synthesis treats all seven scores as roughly equivalent data points. They are not. The
verisimilitude lens's score is the most earned. The comparative lens's score is the most
aspirational.

The synthesis gets the repair priorities in the right order — documents first, then pacing,
then character — but it underestimates the difficulty of the document repairs and overestimates
the ease of the character repair. Fix the TOD window, fix the door lock, fix the temperature,
and fix the match tin, in that order, and do not assume they are independent. They are probably
symptoms of the same compositional problem.
