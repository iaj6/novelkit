## 13-the-account-as-it-stood.md

**One pattern for writer judgment — generic "you" in a strict third-person chapter.**

The chapter is on-register throughout; Ellen's stenographic vocabulary, procedural observation, professional two-column notation, and the uncanny-as-bracket technique all hold cleanly. One technical deviation appears twice and may be intentional:

The chapter slips into generic second-person (past tense) to render Ellen's internalized professional protocols:

> "You produced the draft first. You read it back. You certified what you could certify." (paragraph beginning "She produced rough drafts the way she had always produced them…")

> "you did not elaborate the bracket. You noted it. The proceeding continued." (paragraph beginning "The quality of stillness on the other side of the door…")

The style guide is explicit: *"Third-person limited, past tense. Always."* These are past-tense "you" constructions, so they don't violate the tense rule, and they're not omniscient. But they are not third-person. The technique renders professional protocols as universal rules rather than personal habits — "this is how it is done" rather than "this is how she does it" — and the effect is coherent with Ellen's character.

The fix would be mechanical: "She produced the draft first. She read it back. She certified what she could certify." and "she did not elaborate the bracket. She noted it." Both substitutions scan correctly and stay on-register. But they lose the quality of externalized protocol — the sense that these are rules of the trade, not just Ellen's choices.

**Recommended action**: Writer to decide whether the generic-you constructions are a deliberate technique (keep) or an unintended register blip (fix by substituting "she" throughout). Because the pattern is applied consistently and purposefully in both instances, I have not altered the draft.
