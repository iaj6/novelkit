The house was narrower than the listing photograph had suggested, and Ellen had suspected as much. She stood on Verrill Street with the car door still open behind her. Two and a half stories. A covered porch, three boards wide. The paint on the trim had been white, once — there were still lines of it in the grain. The listing had said *move-in ready*, which was a way of saying structurally sound.

She got the key from the lockbox on the porch railing and let herself in.

The front room held the desk. It was a large piece — heavy hardwood, turned legs, a flat writing surface, two banks of drawers. She went through it methodically. In the bottom left drawer: nothing. In the inner left drawer: a maker's mark burned low on the edge, small block letters, *RENICK FURNITURE WORKS.* She pushed the drawer shut. An asset left in place; no one had come for it. She noted it the way she would note a marked exhibit: in evidence, not yet examined.

The staircase was steep and close to the wall. She had to angle her shoulders to go up without catching the plaster. The landing at the top was cold — not drafty, not damp, the windows fit their frames well enough — and outside it was April, cool and overcast, with the river smell coming off the Sandy from two blocks down. The cold on the landing was not April cold. She registered this and moved on.

Two rooms. The front bedroom: a window over Verrill Street, a curtain rod with no curtain. The back room: smaller, a single window facing the yard, a shallow closet, the floorboards level and dry. The cold here was specific. She stood in the doorway and took the room's measure — the way she sized a room before the parties arrived, clocking the furniture, the angles, the distance between where the principals would sit. Then she closed the door, fitted the catch, and went back downstairs.

She found the legal pad in the first box she opened. *Back room latch — check/adjust.* Below that: *third stair from bottom — tread flexes (left edge holds).* The list continued: the furnace filter, the heat registers, the front window catch that was stiff. The list had a shape to it. The list was a beginning.
