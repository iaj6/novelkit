**Uncanny handling:**

## What the sample does well

- **Register is correct and stable throughout.** Third-person past tense, no slip to present tense, no omniscient drift. The sample is recognizably adult literary fiction in exactly the restrained-procedural register the brief asked for.
- **Professional vocabulary is natural, not strained.** "In evidence, not yet examined" / "marked exhibit" are absorbed into the observation without announcing themselves as vocabulary-building.
- **The uncanny lands correctly.** "She registered this and moved on" is the precise gesture the brief and guidance specify — noted without commitment, no conversion, no explanation. This is the hardest anti-default to honor and the sample handles it without hesitation.
- **The chapter-opening convention is clean.** Physical arrival, specific observation, no extended interiority before the action establishes itself. The maker's mark (*RENICK FURNITURE WORKS*) grounds the furniture-town backdrop economically.

## What the sample drifts on

- **Average sentence length is below the 15–22 word target.** The sample runs approximately 13 words per sentence (rough count). This is partly structural — the fragments ("Two rooms." / "Two and a half stories." / "The cold here was specific.") are clearly intentional and belong to Ellen's rapid professional cataloguing mode — but the cumulative effect is sparer than the guidance's "deliberate clause-building" description. The guidance also references Marilynne Robinson / *Housekeeping* as the patience anchor; the sample does not yet hit that register's willingness to breathe. This is a minor drift, not a register failure.
- **One sentence exceeds the 35-word maximum.** The long sentence in paragraph four ("The landing at the top was cold — not drafty, not damp, the windows fit their frames well enough — and outside it was April…") runs approximately 40 words. A single violation; does not break the register.
- **Della's register is unverified.** The sample contains only an Ellen chapter. The two-register architecture's second half — the first-person present-tense flat-diction Della voice — cannot be assessed. The next sample iteration should include at least one Della paragraph to confirm the register split lands.
