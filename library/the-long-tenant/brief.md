A contemporary literary ghost story in the pipeline's restrained register, told from BOTH sides of the

## Premise

Forty years ago a woman died in the cold in a tall, narrow house on Verrill Street, in Renick — a town
on the Sandy River that made furniture until it didn't. The county recorded an accidental death: a
storm, the power out, a stove that failed, a woman who lived alone. The woman has never recorded it as
an accident, and she is still in the house. She is certain of one thing, and she is wrong about it.

**Ellen Vance**, forty-four, is a court reporter — a stenographer who has spent twenty years making the
verbatim record of other people's worst days, depositions and custody hearings and the slow machinery
of testimony, in a trade that is now being taken from her by digital recording and voice-to-text. The
work that defined her — the human guarantee that *these were the exact words* — is being declared
redundant a contract at a time. She buys the cheapest sound house in Renick because the remaining work
travels and the house is what she can afford with what's left. She does not arrive looking for a ghost.
She arrives precise, underemployed, and angry in a way she will not name — and she begins, without
meaning to, to notice the house: a door that will not stay how she leaves it, a cold with an edge, the
sense of a second attention in the rooms. And in a back room she finds something no machine ever kept:
a household ledger in a stranger's hand, forty years old, still legible.

**Della Markey** (working name), thirty-seven when she died in the winter of 1983, has the run of the
house and almost nothing else. Her present tense is one winter night wide and does not advance. She
perceives the living the way the living perceive weather — pressure, a stranger's noise in rooms that
are hers. She keeps a ledger, the way her millwright father taught her to keep one, and in death she
keeps one sentence: *nobody came to that door.* She returns to it the way the dead in this book return —
not by restating it, but by re-gripping, each time, one more cold object of the night she has refused:
the latch, the dead stove, the page. She has told herself *nobody came* for forty years because the
truth is the other thing, and the other thing is hers.

The truth of the last night is fixed, and it is this. Someone *did* come to the door that night, in the
storm, while the house went cold — someone Della had waited years for and had finally, deliberately,
taught herself to stop expecting. She heard the knocking. She did not go down. She sat in the failing
warmth and would not let herself believe it was them, or would not forgive that it was them, and she
listened to the knocking stop — and she wrote it in her ledger as it happened, the hour, the choice,
in her own hand, and then she died in a cold she could have walked out of. *Nobody came* is the mercy
she made to live inside her own death. The ledger is the indictment, and it has sat behind the one door
she keeps shut for forty years, and the new tenant — a woman whose whole life was the guarantee of the
exact word — is the first person with the habits and the reason to read it back.

The two never meet at the level of people. Ellen never sees a woman; Della never hears a word clearly.
Every piece of the truth crosses between them sideways — a deed, an inquest file the county is weeks
from dissolving into a database, an object lifted out of a drawer, a line of a stranger's own ledger —
and each crossing corrects a belief on one side or the other, not always toward the truth, not in a
straight line. By the end the death is known, fully and fairly, and it is what neither of them believed.
What becomes of Della once she knows is the book's one deliberately unanswered thing.

## Length and shape

- Target length: ~60,000 words
- Number of chapters: 24

The chapters alternate POV — Ellen, Della, Ellen, Della — across two clocks. Ellen's runs forward
through one spring and summer in the house. Della's circles inside a single stopped winter night, the
same hour re-entered from new angles as what she lets herself remember widens. The plotter chooses which
POV opens; the strict alternation then holds **until the truth lands.**

The alternation breaks, asymmetrically, at the close. Della's final chapter is the one in which her
belief corrects — the night she actually had, seen whole — and there is no Della chapter after it.
Roughly thirteen Ellen / eleven Della; the imbalance is the structure, not a flaw to even out. What
happens to Della once she knows is the one thread the book leaves in shadow on purpose (see Constraints).
The book closes in the present, in Ellen, in the house, with the door open.

Chapter lengths vary with the beat (roughly 1,800–3,400 words). Della's circling chapters run shorter
and tighter; Ellen's record-and-house chapters breathe. The convergence does **not** advance on a metronome:
two or three chapters should visibly stall — characters sitting with what they cannot yet know — because a
haunting needs dead air and a machine that advances every chapter has none.

## Voice

Two registers, and the difference must be felt in the first sentence of any chapter — the two POVs are
different people *and* different relations to time. They never blur into each other or into a third,
omniscient voice.

- **Ellen's chapters**: **third-person limited, past tense.** The prose of a professional listener whose
  trade is the exact word and who distrusts the paraphrase, the gist, the *what he meant was.* Her
  lexical field is the record — *entered, struck, the transcript, on the record, cannot be appealed* — and
  it should stay hers and not migrate into Della. Restrained, concrete, procedural: how a deed search
  actually works, what a 1983 inquest file feels like in the hand, the specific labor of making a house
  sound. Her loss — the trade going out from under her — reaches her sideways, in that vocabulary, never
  declared. She is exactly as skeptical as she is; the prose never converts her into a believer, and never
  decides for the reader what the house is.

- **Della's chapters**: **first-person, present tense** — the voice of a stopped clock. Her *now* is the
  winter of 1983 and does not move; the living pass through her rooms as cold and footfall and
  unaccountable light. Her diction is a millwright's daughter's, plain and of her time, drawn from the
  household and the mill and the ledger — keep it flat and unliterary (the kept sentence is deliberately
  banal; its power is that B inverts it without changing a word). She advances **only** by accreting one
  more concrete physical fact of the night she refused — a new object re-gripped, a new sense-datum — never
  by restating the same certainty in prettier words. Each return re-enters the *same hour*; it does not move
  to the next. The circling is forensic, not lyrical — the mirror of Ellen pulling records.

## Audience and register

- **Target audience age range**: adult literary
- **Voice pace**: medium — Ellen's chapters breathe and build; Della's tighten and circle; neither sprawls
- **Reading-grade target**: college
- **POV count intent**: two co-leads across the membrane — one living (third/past), one dead (first/present) —
  strict alternation that goes asymmetric and severs at the resolution
- **Chapter-ending convention**: mixed by design, and the pacing pass enforces variety: **no two consecutive
  chapters may close in the same mode; at least one chapter closes on a thing found and at least one on a thing
  refused.** No manufactured cliffhangers, no uniform closing mode. Quiet endings welcome where a chapter's
  substance calls for one; the book may close quietly without every chapter doing the same.

## Exemplar passages

Voice anchors (named for the architect — paste actual openings here if you want sharper register):

- **Ali Smith, *How to Be Both*** — a dead first-person-present voice braided against a living contemporary
  one, the dead narrator perceiving the present partially and out of their own time. The closest structural
  model for the two-clock, two-register architecture. The most important exemplar.
- **Sarah Waters, *The Little Stranger*** — a haunting rendered through a skeptical, procedural observer who
  never quite concedes the supernatural; dread built from a house and a record, not apparition. The model for
  Ellen's register and for holding the uncanny without explaining it.
- **Hilary Mantel, *Beyond Black*** — the dead as mundane, unromantic fact; no mysticism, no rules of the
  afterlife. Anchor for the book's restraint about mechanism and for Della's flat diction.
- **Marilynne Robinson, *Housekeeping*** (texture only) — domestic interiority, a house as a pressure on a
  life. Anchor for the Ellen chapters' patience without austerity.

## Constraints

- **The membrane is the discipline: the two POVs never have a conversation.** Ellen never sees a figure,
  hears words, or holds a séance; she perceives Della only through deniable signs — a door that moves, a
  cold with an edge, the sense of being attended. Della never hears the living clearly; she perceives them as
  weather and disturbance, never as speech or thought she can read. Every piece of the truth that crosses
  between them crosses **indirectly** — a physical object, a public record, a thing written down — never
  through direct contact. This is the book's central rule, the equivalent of the three who never meet in
  *coldwater reach* and the single audible cannonade in *plattsburgh*.

- **The truth of the last night is fixed here (do not re-invent it).** Someone Della had waited for and taught
  herself to stop expecting came to the door that storm night; Della heard them, chose not to open, recorded
  the choice in her own ledger as it happened, and died of a cold she could have escaped. Her kept sentence
  (*nobody came*) is the lie she has lived inside; her ledger is the disproof. The architect finalizes the
  human specifics — who came, the exact wording of the kept sentence and the ledger entry — but **P, the
  proposition, is given, not chosen**, and it must keep implicating *Della's own choice and knowledge that
  night*, never an outsider's mere failure to reach her. **Forbidden** as too generic, exactly the way
  "she doesn't know she's dead" is forbidden: the note that arrived too late, the messenger turned back by the
  storm, the misdelivered letter — any version where the failure is external rather than Della's. The book
  earns its line — a woman *unable to stop being wrong* about her own last night — only if she was complicit.

- **The death resolves, fairly — this is the book's break from the library's withholding habit.** The truth is
  fully delivered and the reader could assemble it from what is on the page: the records Ellen pulls, the
  objects in the house, the two documents below. It is neither Della's belief (*nobody came*) nor the county's
  file (accidental death, a woman alone); it is the third thing that recontextualizes both. Plant honestly,
  pay off honestly. (Only one thread is withheld — see below — and the dead air of the stalling chapters keeps
  the resolution from feeling scheduled.)

- **Two canonical documents carry the mystery; their wording is load-bearing across the whole book.**
  - **Record A — the kept sentence.** The flat line Della returns to (e.g. *"Nobody came to that door."*),
    met early, in her chapters, recurring across her circling.
  - **Record B — the ledger entry.** A few lines in Della's own hand, recovered from behind the shut door,
    recording that someone came, the hour, and that she did not go down (e.g. an entry that times the knocking
    and ends on her own reason for refusing). Met late, in Ellen's chapters. Set beside A, B inverts A's
    meaning without changing a word of A.
  The drama is which text each character treats as true: Della has only A; the county had only its file; Ellen
  finds B and sets the two side by side. See Structure for how the phases must register and re-quote these so
  the wording cannot drift.

- **One crossing must land falsely.** Somewhere in the middle, a piece of "truth" crosses and corrects a belief
  toward something a *later* object disproves — e.g. Ellen pulls a neighbor's inquest statement that seems to
  confirm Della truly was alone and unvisited, re-reads the night that way, and only later does the ledger
  disprove the disproof. Convergence is non-monotonic while staying fair-play. This is the friction that keeps
  the book haunted rather than mechanical.

- **The supernatural is never explained.** No lore, no rules of haunting, no medium, no scene that adjudicates
  what Della *is.* She simply is; the house simply does what it does; the prose renders the uncanny as fact,
  the way the historical books render period. (Cf. *the cold signal* never describing the signal.)

- **No "she doesn't know she's dead" twist.** Della knows she is dead and is not confused about it. Her error is
  not her condition but what she believes happened on her last night. Avoid every adjacent gimmick — the
  reader-fooled reveal, the apparition jump. Her error is an ordinary character's *wrong belief* that later
  corrects; the narration is reliable, and only Della-the-person is wrong.

- **One thread stays unresolved: what becomes of Della.** The factual death is answered; the metaphysical
  aftermath is not. Her last chapter ends on a concrete physical action caught mid-gesture — her hand on the
  latch, the ledger let go — **on the object, not on a feeling or a fate.** The prose does not tell the reader
  whether she is released, remains, or simply has no more to say. Resist the warm-light exit; resist equally the
  horror sting. Leave it where *coldwater reach* leaves the breakwater question — in shadow, on purpose.

- **No sentimentality.** A dead woman, a failed marriage and a dying trade, a town going under — render them at
  the historical books' temperature: observed, specific, trusted to the reader. The deepest feeling lives in the
  shut latch, the inquest file, the ledger line — never in declaration.

- **Period, class, and place are facts, not lectures.** 1983 is rendered from inside a specific working life,
  not as a decade of references; Renick's decline and the people still in it are texture, not subject; render
  without monologuing.

- **Renick, the Sandy River, Verrill Street, and the people are invented.** No real persons. Generic real
  procedures (inquests, deed/probate records, court reporting) are rendered correctly per the researcher's
  dossier; the town and everyone in it are fiction.

## Structure — how each phase must handle the haunting

<!-- Operative direction. A two-sided haunting with a verbatim-record spine, a converging knowledge-set, and a
non-linear dead-POV clock breaks the pipeline's defaults (single forward clock, parallel threads that never
reconcile, no load-bearing exact-wording documents, time-blind fact audit). The run has epistemic: true, so the

**Architect** — build canon for a two-knowledge-set haunting and SEED the spine (do not delegate it):

- In `canon/characters.md`, define the two POV knowledge-sets explicitly: what Della knows (the house, 1983,
  her version of the night) and what she **cannot** know (anything after her death, the present, the contents of
  any record she never saw); what Ellen knows and can discover, and what she **cannot** perceive (Della as a
  person, anything not written or physically present). The membrane rules live here.
- **Seed the irony ledger** with `record_knowledge`, at `canon` tier, with `@reader` and each character's stance
  toward the fixed proposition P over discourse time: Della `wrong_believes` ¬P (she believes *nobody came*) until
  a fixed reveal chapter, then `knows` P; Ellen `unaware` → `suspects` → `knows`; `@reader` learns at the A/B
  side-by-side. The plotter then moves named knowers along *this seeded ledger*; it must not be left to discover
  which proposition the mystery turns on. (This is the M5.5/M5.6 lesson: agents do the bookkeeping reliably but
  not the judgment of which proposition the irony turns on — so it is seeded here.)
- **Register the two documents** with `register_record`, each once, at `canon` tier, under **distinct recordIds**
  (e.g. `kept-sentence` as kind `other`; `markey-ledger-1983` as kind `log`) with exact text fixed. They are
  *meant* to differ; two recordIds carrying intentionally-different texts are never compared to each other, so
  their divergence — the spine — is safe by construction. Never register two different bodies under the *same*
  recordId; that is the only thing the drift audit flags.
- **Time-scope the 1983 world.** Register Della's 1983 house/world facts with `storyTimeLabel` set to her night
  (the schema field is opt-in for exactly this non-linear case) and/or on time-distinct entity ids, so 1983
  house-state and present-day house-state never share an (entity, attribute) value-slot. The fact audit is
  time-blind and will otherwise clash them as false contradictions.
- `canon/agent-guidance/drafter.md` encodes the hard register split as enforceable anchors: every Della chapter
  is first-person present, every Ellen chapter third-person past; the first sentence establishes person and
  tense; the voice pass rejects any bleed. Give each POV its own non-shared lexical field (Ellen: stenographic /
  legal-record; Della: millwright-household 1983) so the separator is not topic-identical, and re-state the
  anchors per chapter so neither drifts toward the book's literary average. Also: both documents are
  read-before-re-quoted (`read_record` first, reproduce verbatim).

**Plotter** — `outline/00-chapter-map.md` runs the strict alternation and tags every chapter with its POV, its
clock (Ellen forward / Della the circled night), and — per chapter — the **specific object, record, or deed that
crosses the membrane** and the **exact seeded belief it moves** (from the architect's ledger). Build the
alternation to sever at Della's correction; place Record A early in Della's chapters and Record B late in
Ellen's, with the A/B side-by-side at a fixed chapter; place the one false crossing in the middle; mark the two
or three deliberately stalling chapters. Naming the crossing and the belief per chapter is what makes "advances
every chapter" auditable rather than asserted — without it, deepening-a-wrong-belief and advancing-the-truth are
indistinguishable to a mechanical weave check.

**Threads** — the book-spanning threads: (1) the **death-truth**, advancing in nearly every chapter (each moves a
named knower toward P, or deepens a wrong belief before it breaks); (2) **Record A** through Della's circling;
(3) **Record B and the shut door** along Ellen's recovery; (4) the **epistemic convergence** (below). A chapter
that only deepens Della's circle or only advances Ellen's record search is **not** a dead chapter — each is its
POV's load-bearing thread.

**Continuity / world audit** — three cautions specific to this book:

- **Verbatim fidelity is by PREVENTION, not detection.** `register_record` fixes A and B once; the deterministic
  audit (`findRecordDivergences`) flags only **re-registration** drift — the same recordId registered again with
  a different body. It does **not** scan prose for a misquoted re-quote; that single-line drift has no anchor to
  catch. The guarantee is that every chapter re-quoting A or B calls `read_record` first and reproduces the
  returned text exactly inside the quotation marks (only the surrounding frame may vary). A and B differing from
  *each other* is correct and is never compared.
- **The convergence is EPISTEMIC, not factual.** Model Della's belief and the record's version as stances on
  their holders via `record_knowledge` (`wrong_believes` / `believes`), **not** as bare facts on a shared
  (entity, attribute) slot — the time-blind fact audit would clash those as false positives (the M7 run proved a
  belief that flips over time lands as a false contradiction). "Converge" is auditable only as the epistemic
  correction landing at the fixed reveal chapter (`wrong_believes` ¬P → `knows` P, superseded), verified with
  `dramatic_irony` / `who_knows` — not as fact-consistency across the two timelines.
- **Della's knowledge is legitimately frozen, and the two timelines are time-scoped.** Do not flag Della's
  not-knowing the present, or her 1983-anchored facts, as contradictions. Expect — and ignore — any cross-timeline
  "contradiction" on the house between 1983 and present-day state; with the facts time-scoped (above) these should
  not arise, and where they do they are known false positives, not errors. The *final reconciled account of the
  death*, by contrast, must be internally consistent across the closing chapters; there, contradictions are real.

## Research scope

Including this section enables the researcher. This is a largely invented, contemporary story; the researcher
grounds the **real procedures and real period texture** the haunting stands on, so Ellen reads true as a
professional and Della as a woman of 1983. Do not research ghosts. Keep it sharp.

```
- Freelance court reporting / stenography as a trade — and its decline: how a working court reporter operates,
  the discipline of the verbatim record, the habits of mind around exact wording vs. paraphrase, and the real,
  current displacement of stenographers by digital audio and voice-to-text. Grounds Ellen's competence, register,
  and the specific shape of what she is losing.
- County death investigation, then and now: how an accidental death was investigated and recorded in the early
  1980s (coroner / medical-examiner practice, inquest files, death certificates), and how a private person today
  pulls an old inquest file, a death certificate, deed and probate records, and local newspaper coverage —
  including the real phenomenon of counties digitizing and dissolving old paper records. Grounds the documentary
  spine and Ellen's urgency.
- A small American furniture/mill town in decline, 1980s to now: work, housing, who stays, what a closed plant
  and an emptied main street actually are; a maker's mark and the afterlife of the objects such a town made.
  Grounds Renick in both timelines without the stock "dying town" wallpaper.
- Everyday life of a working woman in such a town in 1982–83: domestic labor and objects, household record-keeping,
  a hard winter; the period from inside a specific class and place, not as cultural references. Grounds Della.
- A killing winter on a small inland river: the concrete realities of cold, storm, power loss, and a failing stove
  in an isolated northern house — the physical facts a death by the weather actually involves, rendered without
  romance. Grounds the mechanism of the last night.
```
