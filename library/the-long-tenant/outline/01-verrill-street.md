# Chapter 1 — Verrill Street

**POV**: Ellen Vance (third-person limited, past tense)  
**Summary**: Ellen arrives at the house on Verrill Street, takes possession, and notices — without interpreting — that the back room will not stay as she leaves it.  
**Clock**: Ellen's clock — April, present day; first day in the house  
**Chapter shape**: multi-scene  
**Ending mode**: literary-fade  
**Target words**: ~2,600  

---

## Setting / Time

The house at Verrill Street, Renick, West Virginia. A narrow, two-and-a-half-story late-nineteenth-century worker's house, close to the street. April — cool and overcast; the Sandy River smells of spring silt. The house is between tenants; it has been empty for a while. Ellen has come alone with the contents of a car and the key from the lockbox.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Multi-scene: arrival and walk-through; a brief excursion into town (the diner on Meade Street); return to the house. The prose stays close to Ellen's professional discipline — she moves through the house the way she would move through a deposition room: she clocks what is present, what is out of order, what she cannot account for. No interpretation. The first chapter establishes her register immediately and allows one anomaly to enter the record without adjudicating it.

---

## Goal

Get inside the house and take stock of what she has bought.

---

## Obstacle

The house is not quite what she expected — not threatening, not beautiful, just particular. The back room on the upper floor is cold in a way that is not the season's cold, and the door she closes firmly is ajar when she returns from supper.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Arrival**: Ellen parks on Verrill Street; the house is narrower than the listing photo suggested; she gets the key from the lockbox. Spring light, thin and white.

2. **Walk-through, ground floor**: Front room — the desk. Heavy hardwood, a maker's mark burned into the inner drawer edge: *Renick Furniture Works.* The desk came with the house; it belongs to no one now. She notes it without sentimentality — an asset left in place. The front windows face the street; the staircase is steep and close.

3. **Walk-through, upper floor**: Two upper rooms. The landing is cold in a way that has nothing to do with the weather outside. The back room: the door is slightly ajar; she goes in; it is small, with a window facing the back yard, a closet, and nothing else. The cold in the back room is specific — not drafty, not damp, just cold in the way a room is cold that has been cold for a long time. She goes back out and closes the door firmly.

4. **Making notes**: Ellen, downstairs, with a legal pad: the things that need doing. The third stair from the bottom flexes wrong (she notes: replace tread or reinforce). The back-room latch may need adjustment. The heat registers need cleaning. The record of the house, begun.

5. **The diner on Meade Street**: Brief excursion. She eats at the only place open on the strip. The counter, the overhead TV, a few locals. She is a stranger and she is not dressed for company. The town outside is the town the brief describes — the plant building on the far block, the faded brick — but Ellen is not thinking about the town yet. She is thinking about the house.

6. **Return**: She drives back to Verrill Street; parks; goes up through the front door. The house is dark, waiting. She goes upstairs. The back-room door is ajar.

7. **The noted anomaly**: She looks at it. She looks at the latch. The latch appears functional. She closes the door again, more deliberately this time — she tests that the catch has engaged. She goes back downstairs. She does not go back up to check it.

8. **The evening**: Ellen at the desk in the front room with her legal pad, working through the list. The house around her is quiet — not silent, but quiet in its own particular register. The third step on the staircase does not creak if you go far enough to the left. She learns this on the way up to bed.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Ellen is in the house; she has taken possession; the house has taken possession of her attention. The back-room door that will not stay closed is the first anomaly on the record. She has noted it and has not committed to an interpretation.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

Why will the back room not stay closed? (Ellen files this as a mechanical matter — the latch — and moves on. The reader is not so sure.)

---

## Membrane Crossing

**Vehicle**: The back-room door's behavior. No documentary crossing yet; this is the house's first deniable signal. The back room belongs to both timelines; the door's refusal to stay is the first shared fact between Ellen's present and Della's stopped night.

---

## Belief Movement (Epistemic Ledger)

- **Ellen**: unaware → unaware. She knows nothing of Della or the death; she has noticed an anomaly she attributes to a faulty latch. No epistemic movement on proposition P; the chapter simply establishes her starting position.

---

## Continuity Notes

- **New facts introduced**: Ellen arrives in April; the house is taken possession of; the desk is in the front room with the maker's mark (*Renick Furniture Works*) on the inner drawer edge; the back-room door is on the upper floor and will not stay closed; the third step flexes wrong.
- **Voice anchors**: Third-person limited, past tense. The professional register from the first sentence — Ellen moves through the house as through testimony: noting, not glossing. The desk gets noted the way an exhibit gets marked. The anomaly gets noted the way an inaudible passage gets bracketed.
- **Mood**: Low-key arrival; muted; the book's long, restrained breath beginning. No dread; no uncanny flourish. The cold in the back room is registered as fact and not elaborated.
