# Chapter 11 — No Visitors

**POV**: Ellen Vance (third-person limited, past tense)  
**Summary**: Ellen returns to the county records room and reads Mabel Duff's inquest statement; the statement attests no visitors to the house on February 10 or the daylight hours of February 11; Ellen reads this as confirmation that nobody came to the door that night — the false crossing lands.  
**Clock**: Ellen's clock — early June, present day  
**Chapter shape**: single scene (document-heavy), with transit  
**Ending mode**: turn  
**Target words**: ~3,400  
**Note**: FALSE CROSSING CHAPTER — Ellen wrong_believes ¬P from this point forward. The chapter's turn is the wrong turn; the new information re-contextualizes toward the wrong reading. The prose does not signal the error.

---

## Setting / Time

The county records reading room; then Ellen's car on the drive back to Renick; then the house on Verrill Street. Early June. She has come back specifically to finish the witness statements.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Single scene with transit bookends. The chapter is longer than the previous records-room chapters because it contains the false crossing's full weight: not just the reading of the statement but Ellen's processing of it, her assembling of what she now believes she knows, and the drive back with the apparently completed account. The prose stays entirely in Ellen's professional register — she reads Mabel Duff's statement the way she reads any testimony; she applies her trained ear for exactness; she finds the statement consistent with everything else in the file and with what she has felt in the house. She is completely wrong, and the prose does not tell us so.

---

## Goal

Finish reading the witness statements and get the complete documentary account of Della Markey's death.

---

## Obstacle

There is no obvious obstacle. Ellen reads the file; the file is consistent. The obstacle is invisible: the statement covers the wrong window of time, and Ellen is a professional listener who will hear exactly what was said.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Return visit**: Ellen signs in; the records clerk nods; the file is ready. The clerk mentions the scanning schedule has moved up: "Looks like we'll start the '83 files in about six weeks." The original papers may be in a box in a warehouse after that, or destroyed. Ellen has today.

2. **Finishing Deputy Harman's statement**: She re-reads the end of it — nothing she missed; the scene description is complete. She turns to Mabel Duff's statement.

3. **Mabel Duff's statement, in full**: She reads it carefully.

   Mabel Duff, neighbor, lived two houses down on Verrill Street since 1967. She knew Della Markey. She saw Della regularly — at the mailbox, sometimes at the corner store. She describes Della: private, kept to herself, polite but not social. She attests:

   *"I saw no visitors to the Markey house on the evening of February 10th or during the daylight hours of February 11th. The car was in the drive on both days. I saw the curtains drawn on the morning of February 11th; I assumed Miss Markey was staying in on account of the weather, which was already very bad by then. I last saw Miss Markey in person at the mailbox on the morning of February 10th. She said the weather was coming in and she was getting in her wood. That was the last I saw her."*

4. **Ellen's professional reading of the statement**: She reads it in order, as she reads testimony: she receives it without immediately interpreting it. No visitors on the evening of February 10 or the daylight hours of February 11. Mabel last saw Della at the mailbox on February 10. The car was in the drive. The curtains were drawn.

5. **The professional instinct — confirming consistency**: She checks this against the rest of the file. The death occurred sometime between 10:00 PM February 11 and 4:00 AM February 12. Mabel's observation covers February 10 evening and February 11 daytime. That's the relevant window. Nobody came.

6. **Ellen assembles the account**: She sits back from the file. She has, now:
   - The death certificate: accidental death, hypothermia, February 12, 1983
   - The coroner's report: stove failed (no mechanical defect), storm, body found in kitchen
   - The pathologist: she was capable of decision for several hours before the cold became incapacitating
   - Mabel Duff: no visitors. Nobody came to the door.
   
   And she has felt, in the house on Verrill Street, the particular weight of a sentence she cannot account for but has been carrying since she moved in: *nobody came to that door.* The phrase has been with her since the first day — she would not have named it that way, but that is what the house has been offering her. And the file says the same thing.

7. **The (wrong) resolution**: It fits. The inquest got it right: a woman alone in a blizzard, the stove that failed, nobody who came to the door. The accidental death was what it was. And what Ellen has been feeling in the house — the cold with an edge, the door that moves — is something she is going to have to hold beside the known facts and decide whether it is evidence of anything. She photographs the remaining pages. She thanks the clerk.

8. **Drive back**: She drives back to Renick. The highway, the hills, the town below the ridge. She is assembling the account in the professional part of her mind: the proceeding has a record; the record is internally consistent; the verdict follows from the evidence. The house on Verrill Street comes into view as she crests the ridge.

9. **Back at the house**: She sits in the car for a moment outside the house. Della Markey died alone in a blizzard in a failing house on Verrill Street, and nobody came to the door, and the county got it right. That is what the record says.

   She goes inside. The back room door is ajar. She closes it.

## Membrane Crossing

**Vehicle**: Mabel Duff's inquest statement. The false crossing: Mabel's statement is true — she did not see visitors on February 10 evening and February 11 daytime — but it does not cover 9:40 PM on February 11. Ellen reads it as covering the full relevant window; the statement does not explicitly say it does not cover the night. This is the honest false crossing: no one lied; the statement is exact; the inference Ellen draws from it is wrong.
