# Chapter 22 — What She Wrote

**POV**: Della Markey (first-person, present tense)  
**Summary**: Della opens the ledger to February 11; she reads her own final entry; she reads the kept sentence at the end of the paragraph that records the knocking and the refusal; she knows P; her hand goes to the latch of the back-room door; the chapter ends.  
**Clock**: Della's clock — stopped night of February 11, 1983; approximately 9:52 PM  
**Chapter shape**: bottle  
**Ending mode**: elliptical  
**Target words**: ~2,400  
**Note**: FINAL DELLA CHAPTER — the belief corrects here; the alternation severs after this chapter; there is no Della chapter after this one. What happens to Della once she knows is the book's deliberately unresolved thread.

---

## Setting / Time

The back room of the Verrill Street house, February 11, 1983. The clock, which she can hear from the back room through the open door, says 9:52. The knocking stopped at 9:52.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Bottle: Della in the back room with the ledger. This is the chapter that closes the circling — the night seen whole. She reads what she wrote. The kept sentence is at the end of the paragraph that records the knocking, and she reads it in context, and she understands what she did. The chapter's ending is deliberately elliptical: her hand on the latch of the back-room door — the door she has kept shut for forty years — and the chapter ends on that gesture, caught mid-movement. Not resolved; not abandoned; caught.

---

## Goal

Della does not have a goal in this chapter. The chapter is what happens when the circling that has been building across eleven chapters closes on the central fact and Della can no longer not-turn the page.

---

## Obstacle

There is no longer an obstacle in the conventional sense. The chapter is the absence of the obstacle she has maintained. What remains is what she does with the knowing.

## Action Beats

1. **In the back room**: Della is in the back room. The ledger is on the shelf where she left it in chapter 20, open to January 31. The clock says 9:52. The knocking has stopped.

2. **The clock, confirmed**: 9:52. She knows this time. She knows it is the time the knocking stopped because she is the one who wrote it down. She is in the back room at 9:52 PM on February 11, 1983, with the ledger open to January 31 in front of her.

3. **Turning the page**: She turns the page.

   February 1983. The first entry: February 9. She reads past it. February 10. She reads past it. February 11 — the early entry (power out, stove cold, lamp, temperature). She reads it. She reads her own hand.

4. **The final entry — approaching**: She reads the February 11 late entry. She reads: *Knocking at the front door, near quarter to ten.* She reads this. It is in her hand. She wrote this. She knows she wrote this.

5. **Reading the full entry** (exact text from Record B, `markey-ledger-1983`, reproduced verbatim — Drafter must call `read_record('markey-ledger-1983')` first):

   *February 11 — Power gone since 4, stove cold since before dark, could not relight. Oil holding. Blanket. Temperature in the kitchen, last check: 35 degrees. Knocking at the front door, near quarter to ten. Three years I have not written his name but I knew it. I did not go down. I sat with the clock and I counted the knocking and at 9:52 by the travel clock it stopped. I will not go down now. The cold is in the house and it is very cold and it will be colder before morning.*

   *Nobody came to that door.*

6. **Sitting with it**: She reads the last line. She reads the paragraph above it. She reads the last line again.

   She wrote this. She knows she wrote this. She remembers writing this. She remembers: the knocking at near 9:40. She recognized it. She sat with the travel clock. She counted the knocking. At 9:52 it stopped. She wrote: *Nobody came to that door.*

   She wrote the truth — the knocking, the hour, the refusal — and then she wrote the mercy over it, in the same ink, on the same line. The same five words. At the end of the paragraph that records everything she refused to name.

7. **What she knows now**: She knows: he came. Cale came. She heard his knock and she knew it was his knock — *three years I have not written his name but I knew it* — and she chose not to go down, and at 9:52 the knocking stopped, and she wrote it down. She wrote both things: the knocking and the *nobody came*. She has known this for forty years. She has been maintaining a sentence she herself wrote the disproof of in the line above.

8. **Why she did not go down**: She does not explain this to herself now; she does not need to; she knows. Three years of silence. He came too late and she could not forgive him for that, or she could not make herself believe it was him, or she could not trust that he had come for her and not for some other purpose — the house, the estate, an errand — and she sat in the failing warmth and she refused. She let the knocking stop. She died in a cold she could have walked out of.

9. **What the mercy was**: The mercy was: *nobody came,* which meant: she was not the one who let him stand outside in the storm for twelve minutes and then sat still while he drove away. Nobody came. The door was not opened because nobody came. This is the version she made to live inside her own death, and she has been living inside it for forty years.

10. **The ledger set down**: She sets the ledger down on the shelf. She does not close it; she leaves it open to February 11. It has been open to January 31 for forty years (in some sense); now it is open to February 11. The account is on the page.

11. **Her hand on the latch**: She turns from the shelf. The back-room door is open — she opened it when she came in. She goes to the door. She puts her hand on the latch.

12. **The ending**: Her hand is on the latch. The chapter ends here. What she does next — whether she opens it or closes it, whether she walks through it or stays, whether she is released or remains — is not on the page. Her hand is on the latch, and the chapter ends.
