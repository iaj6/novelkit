# Chapter 8 — The Oil Lamp

**POV**: Della Markey (first-person, present tense)  
**Summary**: Della tends the oil lamp and moves through the dark house; she carries the light through the upper rooms; she perceives — without naming it — the presence of the living as warmth at the wrong angle; Record A recurs.  
**Clock**: Della's clock — stopped night of February 11, 1983; approximately 9:05–9:25 PM  
**Chapter shape**: bottle  
**Ending mode**: elliptical  
**Target words**: ~1,900  

---

## Setting / Time

The Verrill Street house, February 11, 1983. The house is going cold. The oil lamp is on the kitchen table; Della carries it with her into the dark rooms. Outside, the storm. Inside, a cold that is deepening.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Bottle: Della and the lamp, moving through the house. The chapter is sensory and close — the circle of light, what it shows and what it doesn't, the specific quality of different rooms in different states of cold. The first membrane moment from Della's side: she perceives Ellen's presence as warmth at the wrong angle. She cannot account for it; she does not try to. The chapter ends on the ambiguity of the warmth — what it is, what it might be — without resolving it.

---

## Goal

Maintain the lamp; move through the house; know what she has.

---

## Obstacle

The cold is deepening and the lamp is the only light. The house beyond the lamp's circle is dark and going colder. And there is something — a warmth where there should not be warmth — that she cannot account for.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Tending the lamp**: Della at the kitchen table. She checks the oil — enough for several more hours. She trims the wick; her father showed her: trim at an angle with the scissors on the shelf, or the wick smokes and you waste oil. She does this precisely. The lamp burns steady.

2. **Her father's instruction**: A brief, unbidden memory of her father at the kitchen table, showing her how to trim a wick. Not sentimental — instructional. She is doing what he showed her. The ledger-keeping and the lamp-tending come from the same source: the systematic care of a millwright for the things in his keeping.

3. **Moving through the house**: She takes the lamp and moves through the dark rooms. The front room: cold. The desk — her father's desk, the one from the plant; the maker's mark she could find with her eyes shut. She does not sit at it tonight. She holds the lamp up to see the room.

4. **The lamp and the walls**: The lamp shows the walls in its own color — not white, not the cold gray of daylight, but the amber of oil heat. The walls are the walls she grew up with. She knows every crack.

5. **Upstairs with the lamp**: She goes upstairs. The third step, without thinking; she steps over it. The landing. The upper rooms: she looks in; she does not go in. She is checking.

6. **The warmth at the wrong angle**: In the upstairs hallway, outside the back-room door, she pauses. There is something she cannot account for. A warmth — not heat, just the memory of warmth, or the idea of it — coming from somewhere inside the back room. She knows the back room is cold; she has been past that door tonight; the cold from it is the specific cold of the back room. And yet there is warmth, faintly, at a wrong angle. Not from any heating source she has. Not from the lamp, which she is holding in the other direction.

   She stands still. She has had this before — the tenants; the living who have passed through her house since she has been in it. They are not in her time. They are weather. She cannot see them; she cannot hear them; she can only feel the pressure they leave on a room, the way you feel a change in the air before it rains. The warmth at the wrong angle is a tenant. It is not hers to do anything with.

7. **Record A**: She comes back downstairs. At the kitchen table she opens the ledger. She reads back what she has written: the temperature, the stove, the lamp. The last line: *"Nobody came to that door."* She reads it. It is what it is.

8. **Closing**: She sits with the lamp. The circle of light. The rest of the house — and the warmth that was there, briefly, at the wrong angle, and is not there now — is dark beyond it.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Della has tended the lamp and moved through the house and returned to the kitchen. The warmth at the wrong angle is noted and filed under *the tenants* — she has had this before; it is not frightening; it is simply unaccountable. The chapter ends on the lamp's circle and what lies beyond it — the ambiguity of the warmth that was there and is gone.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

What is the warmth at the wrong angle? (Della has a category for it — the living, the tenants — but the reader knows it is Ellen in the house. The membrane is thin here; neither crosses it; neither can.)

Not speech, not identity, not even intention — just the thermal trace of a living person in a room at a different time. The membrane does not break: Della has a category for it (*the tenants*) that keeps her from having to look at it.
