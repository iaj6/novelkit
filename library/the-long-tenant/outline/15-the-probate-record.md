# Chapter 15 — The Probate Record

**POV**: Ellen Vance (third-person limited, past tense)  
**Summary**: Ellen returns to the county clerk's to look more closely at the probate file for Della's estate; she finds Cale Markey's name and the notation that he could not be reached; a county records clerk who has been there thirty years confirms the brother's existence and departure; Ellen's wrong belief begins to crack.  
**Clock**: Ellen's clock — mid-June, present day  
**Chapter shape**: multi-scene  
**Ending mode**: resolved-beat  
**Target words**: ~3,000  

---

## Setting / Time

The county clerk's office (the probate section); then the courthouse steps; then the drive back to Renick; then briefly the house. Mid-June. The days are long; the Sandy River is lower and clearer than it was in spring.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Multi-scene: the probate office, a conversation with the long-tenured records clerk, the drive back, the house in the evening. The chapter is the point at which Ellen's false account begins to loosen — not because she has the right answer yet, but because the account she assembled in chapter 13 had a hole she didn't fill (who was the brother, did he stay in contact, did he ever come back). She has told herself the account is complete; the account turns out not to be.

---

## Goal

Look more closely at the probate file for Della's estate; understand who the legal heirs were and what became of them.

---

## Obstacle

The probate file gives Cale Markey's name but not much more — he is listed as next of kin, notified by post at a last-known address, and the notation says the correspondence was returned undelivered. He could not be reached; the estate was held; it eventually reverted to the county for sale. The clerk knows more than the file does.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Return to the probate section**: Ellen at the counter in the probate section of the county clerk's office. Different clerk today — a younger one; he pulls Della Markey's estate file. She signs for it and takes it to the reading table.

2. **The probate file**: She reads through. Della Markey died intestate — no will. The estate: the house (valued at a modest figure for a 1983 rural property), the furniture and household contents, a modest savings account (depleted by the estate proceedings), the household ledger listed as part of the inventory. Next of kin: Cale Markey, brother, last known address (a town in Ohio, a street address). Correspondence sent. Correspondence returned: *addressee not found.*

3. **Cale Markey: the notations**: The estate was held in trust for several years as the county waited for next of kin to come forward. Nobody came. The county eventually sold the property (to a series of subsequent owners before Ellen). Cale never claimed the estate and was never located.

4. **The question Ellen had not asked**: She had not asked why Cale never came to claim his sister's estate. A brother, estranged — she knew that from the probate index — and then simply gone. She had not thought about this as a question. She thinks about it now.

5. **The long-tenured clerk**: As she is making copies, the clerk who has been in the courthouse records office for thirty years comes through on his way to the filing room. He recognizes her — she's been in several times now. He stops to chat.

   She asks: did you know the Markeys?

   "Sure. Harold Markey worked at the plant — millwright. Good man. Died, let me think, maybe '79, '80. His daughter Della stayed in the house. There was a son — Cale — but he left after Harold died. Some trouble about the estate, you know how families are. Cale left, and then when Della died in that blizzard, well. We sent letters. Nothing."

6. **The key question**: Ellen asks: did Cale ever come back to town, as far as you know? Before she died?

   The clerk thinks. "Not that I heard. He was gone by — must have been '80, '81. After the argument." A pause. "Course, I wasn't watching his every move."

7. **The crack in the account**: She is back in the car, driving to Renick, and the question has settled into her. Mabel Duff saw no visitors on February 10 evening and February 11 daytime. Cale left in 1980 or 1981. But: what if he came back? Not to the town — not so anyone would have seen him arriving at the hardware store or the gas station — but specifically to the house, on that night, in the storm? Mabel's statement covers daylight. The storm knocked out power before dark on February 11.

   This is not a conclusion. She has no evidence for it. But the account she wrote in chapter 13 suddenly has a question mark in the middle of it that wasn't there before.

8. **Back at the house**: She goes in. The back room door is closed. She stands in the front room at the desk, looking at her notebook. She opens to the rough draft. She draws a line under it: *Question: Cale Markey. Did he come back?*

   She does not know how to find out. The probate file doesn't say; the inquest file doesn't say; the neighbor didn't see.

9. **Closing**: She closes the notebook. The house is quiet. The June evening comes in off the Sandy River. The account she wrote is no longer quite the account she has. Something is missing from it, or something is in it that shouldn't be there, or — she doesn't know yet.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Ellen's wrong belief has begun to crack: the account she assembled is no longer felt as complete. She does not yet suspect P specifically (she does not yet believe someone came), but she now has a specific, named absence in the account: where was Cale Markey on the night of February 11, 1983? The chapter closes with this question on the page.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

Did Cale Markey come back to Renick on the night of February 11, 1983? Ellen does not know how to find out from the records available. But the question is now on the page.

---

## Membrane Crossing

**Vehicle**: The probate file (Cale's name, the returned correspondence); the county clerk's oral testimony (confirming the brother, the estrangement, the departure). Cale Markey crosses from the documentary record into Ellen's active investigation. He is now a named potential actor in the night she has been reconstructing.

---

## Belief Movement (Epistemic Ledger)

- **Ellen**: wrong_believes ¬P → **suspects P** (the crack opens; she doesn't yet believe Cale came, but she is no longer certain nobody came; the account has a named hole in it). This is the turning point that restores the investigation after the false resolution of chapter 13.
- **@reader**: knows more than Ellen (the reader has been with Della through chapters 6, 8, 10, 12, 14; the reader's suspicion of P is strong; seeing Ellen arrive at the right question is the beginning of the reader watching the gap between the POVs close).

---

## Continuity Notes

- **New facts established**: Cale Markey's name is confirmed in the probate file; his last known address at death of estate was a town in Ohio; the correspondence was returned undelivered; the estate was held for years before the county sold the property; the long-tenured clerk confirms: Cale left approximately 1980–81 after a dispute about Harold's estate; no confirmation he ever returned; Ellen has written the question on the page.
- **Voice anchors**: Third-person limited, past tense. The probate file is read in the professional register — she moves through it the way she'd move through a deposition exhibit. The conversation with the clerk is dialogue rendered plainly (no interpretive tags); what the clerk doesn't know is as significant as what he says. The crack in the account is Ellen's professional realization that a witness statement covering one window doesn't cover all windows.
- **Mood**: Investigative energy renewed; the false resolution is loosened. The chapter ends on a question, not a revelation — but the question is the right one, and it is now written down.
