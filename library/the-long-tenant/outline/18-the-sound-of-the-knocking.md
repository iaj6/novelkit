# Chapter 18 — The Sound of the Knocking

**POV**: Della Markey (first-person, present tense)  
**Summary**: Della at the foot of the stairs when the knocking begins — three deliberate impacts on the front door, then more; she stands still; she does not move toward it; she does not yet name whose knock it is; the clock reads 9:42; the chapter ends as the knocking continues.  
**Clock**: Della's clock — stopped night of February 11, 1983; approximately 9:40–9:45 PM  
**Chapter shape**: bottle  
**Ending mode**: turn  
**Target words**: ~2,200  

---

## Setting / Time

The Verrill Street house, February 11, 1983. The house is cold: 35°F inside. The lamp burns. Della is at the foot of the stairs when the knocking begins. The travel clock on the mantle reads 9:42 during the chapter.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Bottle: Della at the foot of the stairs, then in the front hall, then standing still. The chapter is the central event of P — the knocking at the door — rendered from Della's inside, in real time. She hears it; she knows what it is (in some sense she will not complete); she does not move toward it. The chapter does not reach 9:52; it ends while the knocking is ongoing. The turn is the arrival of the knocking — new information (for the reader, who has suspected; for Della, who will not name it) that recontextualizes everything before it.

---

## Goal

Della does not have a goal in this chapter, except to stay where she is. She is managing the approach; she has been managing the approach for multiple chapters.

---

## Obstacle

The knocking at the front door: three impacts, deliberate and distinct. Not the storm — the storm is constant; this is intermittent and intentional. She is at the foot of the stairs and she is not going to the door.

---

## Action Beats

1. **At the foot of the stairs**: She is at the foot of the stairs with the lamp, looking up. She was going up — she was going to check the landing, or the back room, or simply move — and then: a sound. Three impacts on the front door. Clear, distinct, deliberate. She stops.

2. **Identifying the sound**: She identifies it, mechanically, the way a millwright identifies a sound in the plant: not wind (the wind is continuous, not intermittent); not ice falling from the eaves (ice falls in a cascade, not in threes); not a branch (no branches on the street side of the house near the door). Three impacts. Then a pause. Then three more.

3. **The pattern**: She stands at the foot of the stairs and counts the spaces between the impacts. They are too regular for accident. Deliberate. A fist on wood, three at a time, with pauses between sets as if waiting for a response.

4. **Standing still**: She does not move. She is at the foot of the stairs; the lamp is in her hand; the door is twelve feet in front of her, around the corner of the hall. She does not move toward it.

5. **What she knows**: She knows — in the way the body knows before the mind names it — that the person outside is known to her. The knocking has a quality. The interval between strikes, the force applied, the timing of the pauses: these are familiar. She has heard this knock before. She has heard it at other doors, in other weathers, in other years, before there were three years of silence between herself and the person who is knocking.

6. **"Three years I have not written his name"**: She says this to herself. Not aloud; there is no one to say it to. But she says it: three years she has not written his name. The knock is in the pattern she knows. She is at the foot of the stairs and the lamp is in her hand and she is not going to the door.

7. **The clock**: She glances at the mantle — she can see the travel clock from the foot of the stairs, just barely. 9:42. The clock is accurate. It is 9:42 PM on February 11, 1983, and someone is knocking at the front door of the house on Verrill Street.

8. **The kept sentence, held**: She is holding the kept sentence. It is not on the page now; she has not written it in this moment; she is simply holding it the way you hold something against a current. Nobody came to that door. She is holding it.

9. **The knocking continues**: Three more. A pause. Three more. The storm outside is the storm; this is not the storm. She is at the foot of the stairs. The lamp.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The knocking is real, ongoing, and known to Della in a way she will not yet name. The chapter ends with the knocking continuing — not resolved, not stopped. The turn is the arrival of the central fact of P: someone has come. The kept sentence is being held against the fact of the knocking.
