# Chapter 10 — Footfall

**POV**: Della Markey (first-person, present tense)  
**Summary**: Della hears footfall on the floor above her and a wrong light under the back-room door; she moves through the house and feels the present-day tenant's presence as weather; she registers the third step.  
**Clock**: Della's clock — stopped night of February 11, 1983; approximately 9:15–9:30 PM  
**Chapter shape**: bottle  
**Ending mode**: literary-fade  
**Target words**: ~1,900  

---

## Setting / Time

The Verrill Street house, February 11, 1983. The house has been going cold since the stove failed. Della has the oil lamp. Outside: the storm continues. Inside: the cold that is now decidedly present in the rooms.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Bottle: Della in the house, moving in response to what she hears and perceives. First-person, present tense. The chapter's center is the perception of the living — Ellen above her, in the present — as weather: footfall, a wrong light, the specific resistance of the third step. Della has a working category for this (*the tenants*) that lets her experience the membrane without crossing it. The chapter ends in the front room, looking at the storm outside, which is both present-1983-outside and a kind of screen.

---

## Goal

Understand what she is hearing in the house; confirm it is what she already knows it to be — the tenants, the living, the unaccountable.

---

## Obstacle

The weight on the floor above her is more present tonight than usual. The light under the back-room door is not any light she has put there. She has to decide, each time she encounters the living's traces, whether to name them or not.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Hearing the footfall**: Della is in the hallway at the foot of the stairs. She can hear — or feel — a weight on the floor above. A step, then another. Not her steps; she is here. Not the storm — the storm is continuous; this is deliberate and intermittent. She knows what this is. She has heard it before.

2. **The living as weather**: She has been here long enough — however long dead is — to have learned how to hold this. The living pass through her rooms the way weather passes through: without intention directed at her, without awareness of her, leaving only pressure and the faint trace of a body's heat or the disturbance of air. She cannot see them or hear them speak. They are weather.

3. **The wrong light**: She goes upstairs. She intends to check the landing. At the top of the staircase, the landing: empty, hers, the correct 1983 dark. But under the back-room door: a line of light. Not her lamp's light — she is carrying the lamp. Not moonlight — there is no moon tonight, only storm. A wrong light, coming from the wrong side of a door that she has not opened.

4. **Standing outside the back-room door**: She does not go in. She stands and looks at the line of light under the door. The tenant is in there. In her room, in her time, with a different light. She cannot do anything about this. The tenant cannot hear her; the tenant does not know she is standing outside this door.

5. **What she does not think**: She does not think about what the tenant might find in there. The back room has the ledger on the shelf, and the ledger is hers, and the tenant is not in her time and cannot — she does not complete this thought. She turns and goes back down the staircase.

6. **The third step**: She comes down the staircase. The third step. She steps over it as she always does, without thinking — the step has been like this since she was a child; she stepped over it with her father's hand on her shoulder, learning where to put her feet. She steps over it now and the bottom of the staircase is the bottom of the staircase.

7. **The front room**: She goes into the front room with the lamp. The desk is there — her father's desk from the plant; the chair she placed beside it years ago. She holds the lamp up to look at the room. The storm is at the front windows; she can hear it more clearly here — the wind on the glass, the sustained pressure.

8. **Closing**: She stands at the front window and watches the storm — or does not watch it, exactly; she is looking at the dark glass, which shows her the lamp's reflection and nothing beyond it. The street is out there, under the snow, in the dark. The light under the back-room door has gone out. The tenant has moved on.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Della has confirmed what she already knew: the living are in the house, as they sometimes are, and they are not hers to do anything with. The wrong light is gone. The third step is still the third step. The storm is still outside.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

What is the tenant doing in the back room? (Della does not complete this thought. The reader knows what Ellen is doing — beginning to look for the ledger, or moving through the house in her own way — but Della's refusal to complete the thought is itself a kind of knowledge-management.)

---

## Membrane Crossing

**Vehicle**: Ellen's footfall (heard by Della as unaccountable weight on the floor above); the light under the back-room door (Ellen's light in the present day, bleeding through the stopped night). The third step: Ellen noted it in chapter 3; Della steps over it here with forty years of habit. The shared physical feature registers differently in each time.

---

---

## Continuity Notes

- **New facts established**: Della perceives the living as footfall and wrong light, confirming the chapter 8 pattern; the wrong light under the back-room door registers for the first time from Della's side; Della's practice of stepping over the third step is confirmed as forty-year habit; she can see the storm reflected in the front window but not the street beyond it; the tenant's light has gone by the end of the chapter.
- **Voice anchors**: First-person, present tense. Short sentences. The tenant is described in weather terms — pressure, heat trace, disturbance of air. No supernatural vocabulary; no drama about what she is or what they are. The uncompleted thought is rendered as Della turning away from a step, not as suppression with emotional commentary.
- **Mood**: Quiet vigilance. Della is not frightened by the tenant's presence — she has had tenants before. The chapter's unsettledness is in the incomplete thought and in the back-room door. The storm outside is both present (1983) and the chapter's final note — the house surrounded by weather, from outside and within.
