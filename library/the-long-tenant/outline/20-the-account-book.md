# Chapter 20 — The Account Book

**POV**: Della Markey (first-person, present tense)  
**Summary**: Della is in the back room with the ledger; she holds it and reads through the middle years and reaches January 1983 — and stops; she will not turn to February 11; she sets the ledger back on the shelf; the kept sentence is no longer simply fact.  
**Clock**: Della's clock — stopped night of February 11, 1983; approximately 9:42–9:50 PM  
**Chapter shape**: bottle  
**Ending mode**: literary-fade  
**Target words**: ~2,200  

---

## Setting / Time

The back room of the Verrill Street house, February 11, 1983. This is the room Della keeps shut. Tonight, for the first time in the chapter sequence, she has come in. The room is cold — the cold specific to this room. The ledger is on the shelf. Outside, the knocking has not stopped.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Bottle: Della in the back room, with the ledger. This is the chapter's key structural feature: both Della and Ellen are now with the ledger in the same period of the story's telling, different times. Della holds it; she reads toward January 1983 and stops; she cannot turn the page to February 11. The chapter runs parallel to chapter 19 — Ellen finds the ledger from the outside; Della holds it from the inside. The chapter ends on the specific cold of the back room and the ledger on the shelf.

---

## Goal

Della has come into the back room. She is holding the ledger. She does not know why — or she does not name why. She is reading through the account.

---

## Obstacle

The last entry. She cannot read the last entry. She can read everything up to January 1983; she cannot turn the page to February 11. The kept sentence is still being held; the knocking outside is ongoing; the page will not be turned.

---

## Action Beats

1. **In the back room**: Della is in the back room. She came in — she did not decide to come in; she is simply in here, the way she sometimes arrives in a room without choosing to cross the threshold. The lamp is with her; the circle of light shows the shelf, the closet, the window onto the back yard.

2. **The ledger on the shelf**: The ledger is on the shelf where she keeps it. She takes it down. It is familiar in her hands — she has kept this ledger for years; she knows its weight, the worn corners, the way it opens slightly past the halfway mark where she has read it most often.

3. **Reading the middle years**: She opens it to the middle. She reads: the mid-1970s, the household accounts. Her father's entries at the front, then hers. Cale's name appearing and then not appearing, after 1980. Three years she has not written his name.

4. **The knocking, outside**: She is aware, in the back room, that the sound from the front of the house is continuing. The knocking — three at a time, with pauses. She does not name it here; she does not call it anything; it is outside; she is in the back room with the ledger.

5. **Reading toward January 1983**: She reads through 1981, 1982. The plant's hours being cut; the expenses trimmed; the winter preparations. January 1983: the storm coming in, the wood laid in, the entries careful and exact. She reads this page. January 31.

6. **The next page**: The next page is February 1983. She can see the date at the top: February 10, 1983. The first entry. She has not read past January 31.

7. **Will not turn**: She holds the page at January 31. The next page is there; she can feel it under her thumb; she has read it — she knows what is there; she wrote it; she knows exactly what is there. She does not turn it.

8. **What she knows she wrote**: She knows what she wrote on February 11. She has always known. She wrote the stove, the storm, the power out, the cold, the knocking — and the last line. The last line is what she wrote at the end. She wrote it; she knows she wrote it; she has not read it back in forty years.

   *Nobody came to that door.*

   She is holding the kept sentence in one hand and the ledger in the other and the next page is February 11 and she does not turn it.

9. **The cold in the back room**: The cold in this room is specific. It is the cold of a room that holds something that has not been fully reckoned with. She knows this cold. She has been keeping it for forty years.

10. **The ledger, set down**: She sets the ledger back on the shelf. She does not close it properly — it stays open to January 31, the last page she will read. She leaves it.

11. **Closing**: She stands in the back room with the lamp. The cold. The ledger on the shelf, open to January 31. Outside, faintly, the sound of something she is not naming. The lamp shows the shelf, the closet, the window. She stays in the back room for a moment. The cold was the cold.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Della has been in the back room with the ledger — closer to the final entry than she has been in forty years. She has stopped at January 31. The kept sentence is in her hand; the knocking is outside; the next page is not turned. The wrong belief is showing its infrastructure: the effort required to maintain it is now structural, physical, requiring a deliberate not-turning of a page.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

What happens when she can no longer hold the page at January 31? The chapter has made the limit visible: she knows what she wrote; she has always known; she has not read it back. The next chapter (in Della's POV sequence) will be the one where she reads it.
