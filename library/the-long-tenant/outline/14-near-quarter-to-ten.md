# Chapter 14 — Near Quarter to Ten

**POV**: Della Markey (first-person, present tense)  
**Summary**: Della watches the travel clock and feels the approach of the hour she does not name; she moves through the house again; she is aware of the coming hour the way a millwright is aware of a machine about to go; she does not yet reach it.  
**Clock**: Della's clock — stopped night of February 11, 1983; approximately 9:30–9:40 PM  
**Chapter shape**: bottle  
**Ending mode**: mid-action-cut  
**Target words**: ~2,000  

---

## Setting / Time

The Verrill Street house, February 11, 1983. The temperature has reached 37°F — just above freezing inside the house. The oil lamp is burning. Della has the blanket around her. The travel clock on the mantle reads 9:32, then 9:36, then later.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Bottle: Della in the house, aware of the approach of a specific hour. The chapter is structured around the travel clock — she returns to it repeatedly. The clock is moving; the night is not (the night does not advance in Della's chapters, but the clock does, because she is aware of it moving toward the hour that is coming). The chapter ends on the clock at 9:38 — before the knocking begins. A mid-action-cut: the hour is approaching; the chapter stops before it arrives.

---

## Goal

Manage the next hour; hold the house; stay with what she knows.

---

## Obstacle

The hour she does not name is coming. She can feel it the way a body feels a pressure front — not through the senses but in the fact of the approach. The clock is the clock. It moves toward 9:40.

---

## Action Beats

1. **The clock**: Della at the mantle, looking at the travel clock. It is 9:32. The clock is wound — she wound it at the beginning of the storm, on the 10th, when she still had power and was doing the things she did at the beginning of a storm. The clock keeps time in the dark. This is what a travel clock does.

2. **The specific quality of 9:32**: She knows this hour. Not from this night specifically — she cannot be in this night specifically, she can only be in it always — but from the repetition she has lived through, the circle she has been in. 9:32 is before. This she knows.

3. **Moving through the house again**: She takes the lamp and moves. Kitchen — the table, the ledger closed, the thermometer reading 37°. Front room — the desk, the storm at the windows. She does not sit at the desk. She goes to the foot of the stairs.

4. **The staircase**: She stands at the foot of the stairs. The third step. She does not go up. She is at the bottom of the stairs and she is aware that going up is going toward the back room, and the back room is the room she keeps, and tonight she has been in it once already.

5. **The millwright's knowledge**: She has had this before — the awareness of something about to go before it goes. Her father used to say: *a machine tells you before it tells you.* You don't hear it; you feel it in the plant floor under your feet. Something in the tolerances, some vibration just below perception. You learn to trust it. You learn not to ask what it means, because the asking is what makes you miss it.

   This is that feeling. She knows the hour is coming. She does not ask what it means.

6. **Back to the clock**: She returns to the mantle. 9:36. She watches the second hand. The clock is accurate; she has always kept it accurate; the clock does not lie. 9:36 PM, February 11, 1983. She is here, in this hour, as she has always been here.

7. **What she does not allow herself**: She does not think about the hour that comes after 9:38. She thinks about 9:36. She thinks about 9:37. She is a millwright's daughter in a failing house in a blizzard and she is watching the clock and not thinking beyond it.

8. **The kept sentence, held**: The kept sentence is with her — she does not write it; she holds it the way you hold something you need to keep hold of. Nobody came to that door. 9:37. She is watching the clock.

9. **Closing — the clock at 9:38**: She is at the mantle. The clock face. 9:38. The second hand moves. Outside, the storm — the wind on the exterior wall, the sustained pressure. She is watching the second hand. The house is very cold and very quiet and the clock says 9:38.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Della is at 9:38 — two minutes before the knocking begins. The chapter cuts here. She is watching the clock; she is holding the kept sentence; she has not yet heard what she will hear. The hour is arriving.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

What happens at 9:40? (The chapter cuts before it arrives. The reader — who has seen Della circle this night across multiple chapters — knows something comes at 9:40. Della is watching the clock. The next time we see her POV, she will be at the threshold.)

The chapter's crossing is the clock as shared object — it kept time in Della's last night; it is still (presumably) in the house Ellen occupies. But the primary function here is establishing the clock's significance within Della's chapters before it becomes the specific instrument of Record B's record (9:52, knocking stopped).

## Continuity Notes

- **New facts established**: The temperature in the house has reached 37°F; the travel clock is wound and accurate; Della is watching it at 9:32, 9:36, 9:37, 9:38; the chapter cuts at 9:38; the kept sentence is being held rather than written for the first time.
- **Voice anchors**: First-person, present tense. The clock is the chapter's structural anchor — it appears four times, each reading slightly later. The millwright's-knowledge passage (the machine that tells you before it tells you) is the chapter's most extended reflective passage, and it must stay within the flat vocabulary — *tolerances, plant floor, vibration, feeling in the feet.* No literary metaphor. The clock does not symbolize; it tells time.
- **Mood**: Watchful, suspended. The mid-action-cut ending — the clock at 9:38, the second hand moving — is the chapter's form made functional: the chapter itself cuts before the hour arrives, the way Della cuts before she allows herself to know what 9:40 brings.
