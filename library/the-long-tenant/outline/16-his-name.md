# Chapter 16 — His Name

**POV**: Della Markey (first-person, present tense)  
**Summary**: Della is at the front door, testing the latch; she returns to the ledger and turns past the pages where his name last appeared without reading them; she writes the kept sentence; she watches the clock; she does not look out the window.  
**Clock**: Della's clock — stopped night of February 11, 1983; approximately 9:32–9:38 PM  
**Chapter shape**: bottle  
**Ending mode**: literary-fade  
**Target words**: ~2,000  

---

## Setting / Time

The Verrill Street house, February 11, 1983. The temperature inside the house is approximately 37°F. The oil lamp burns. The travel clock on the mantle reads past nine-thirty. Outside: the blizzard, unchanged.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Bottle: Della moving between the front door, the kitchen table, the mantle clock. First-person, present tense. This is the chapter that circles closest to his name — she goes to the ledger and almost reads the page where it last appeared; she touches the front door and does not look out above it; she writes the kept sentence again. The circling is tighter. The clock is moving toward something.

---

## Goal

Hold the night as she has it; stay with the kept sentence; not look out the window above the front door.

---

## Obstacle

The clock is moving. "Three years I have not written his name" is present, though she does not write it. The kept sentence requires more deliberate maintenance than before. She is going through the right motions — the latch, the ledger, the clock — but the hour is pressing.

---

## Action Beats

1. **At the front door again**: Della at the front of the house. Her hand on the inside of the front-door latch. She is testing it: cold steel, engaged, the bolt in its plate. The door is locked. She has checked this already tonight; she checks it again.

2. **Above the door**: There is a small window above the door — the transom-style window that some houses have above the front door frame. She does not look at it. She is looking at the latch. The latch is fine; she does not need to look at the window.

3. **His name**: "Three years I have not written his name." This is in her, not on the page — she has not written it in the ledger, not since he left; she has been careful about it. The ledger is a record and the record should contain what is on the record and he has not been on the record since 1980. She is careful about this.

4. **Kitchen with the ledger**: She goes back to the kitchen. She sits at the table. The ledger is there; she opens it. She is looking at the entries for the night: the stove, the temperature, the oil. She reads them back. Good.

5. **Turning the pages**: She turns back — not far, just a few pages — past the January entries (the storm preparations, the wood, the receipts), past the December entries, past November. She is looking for something; she does not know what. She turns to October.

   October has his name.

   She does not read the October entry. She puts her thumb over it. She can feel the pencil marks through the page — the impression of the letters in the paper — but she does not read it. The October entry is from three years ago. She has not read it in three years. She puts her thumb on it and she turns back to tonight's page.

6. **Writing the kept sentence**: She picks up the pencil. She writes: *Nobody came to that door.* She writes it flat, in the column where it belongs, at the bottom of the night's account. She looks at it. She puts the pencil down.

7. **Back to the clock**: She goes to the mantle. The clock: 9:35. She watches it. 9:35 to 9:36 takes the time it takes. She is here, in this hour, watching the clock.

8. **What she does not do**: She does not go to the window. She does not look out the window above the door. She stands at the mantle and watches the clock and she is not going to the window because there is nothing to see outside the window except the storm, and she has already taken stock of the storm, and looking at it again will not change what it is.

9. **Closing**: She turns from the clock. The lamp is on the kitchen table. The front door is latched. The ledger is closed on tonight's page. The storm on the exterior wall: sustained, constant, not letting up. She stands in the front room with the lamp and the storm is outside and she is in the house and the clock said 9:36 when she last looked at it.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Della has not looked out the window. She has not read his name in the October entry. She has written the kept sentence and checked the latch and watched the clock — all the right motions. The chapter ends on her standing in the front room with the lamp, the clock at 9:36.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

What is in the October entry that she will not read? (The reader knows: his name. The thumb over the page is the chapter's central gesture — what you refuse to read, you still carry.)

The clock marks the approach of 9:40.
