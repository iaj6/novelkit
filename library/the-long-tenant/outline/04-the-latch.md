# Chapter 4 — The Latch

**POV**: Della Markey (first-person, present tense)  
**Summary**: Della moves through the house checking what holds and what doesn't; she tests the front-door latch; she stands outside the back-room door without going in; she rehearses the kept sentence.  
**Clock**: Della's clock — stopped night of February 11, 1983; approximately 9:00–9:30 PM  
**Chapter shape**: bottle  
**Ending mode**: literary-fade  
**Target words**: ~1,900  

---

## Setting / Time

The Verrill Street house, February 11, 1983. It is now past nine o'clock. The temperature in the house has dropped; the thermometer by the kitchen window reads forty-four degrees. Outside: the blizzard, which is not yet letting up. The oil lamp is burning on the kitchen table; Della carries it with her through the dark rooms.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Bottle: Della moves through the house, room by room, lamp in hand. Short declarative sentences. The chapter is the millwright's daughter inspecting a machine — methodical, precise, physical. She checks each door, each window, the latch on the front door. The inspection is how she organizes the night: what holds and what doesn't. The back-room door is the one she does not open.

---

## Goal

Inspect the house; confirm what holds; understand what she has for the night ahead.

---

## Obstacle

The stove is already confirmed dead (from chapter 2). The deeper obstacle — not yet named — is the back-room door, which she cannot open tonight because the back room is where the ledger lives. She is not ready to go there.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Starting at the front door**: Della at the front of the house with the lamp; she moves to the front door. She tests the latch — cold steel, solid, catches cleanly on the plate. The door is locked; it will stay locked. Outside: wind, the storm, the street under snow she cannot see in the dark.

2. **Ground-floor circuit**: She moves through the ground floor: kitchen (she checks the window latch, intact), the pantry (she notes what food is there — she has enough for several days; she is not in danger of starving), the front room. The desk is there; she does not sit at it tonight. The cold has reached the front room now.

3. **The staircase**: She goes upstairs with the lamp. The third step — she knows this step; it has always been like this, since she was a child; her father said he'd fix it and never did. She steps over it with the ease of decades of memory. It is her step, in her stair, in her house.

4. **Upper floor**: She checks the upper rooms — one window has a slight draft; she pushes the frame firmly, enough. The landing is cold. The cold is coming from the back room.

5. **The back-room door**: She stands outside it. Her hand goes to the door handle. She does not open it. This is where the ledger is. This is the room she keeps. She has been in it recently — she keeps the ledger there, on the shelf — but not tonight. Tonight the ledger is for the kitchen table.

   The cold that comes from under the back-room door is the cold of a room that has been cold for a long time. It is different from the storm's cold. She knows the difference.

6. **Returning downstairs**: She comes back down the staircase; past the third step; into the kitchen. The lamp is still burning. The table.

7. **Rehearsing the kept sentence**: She sits at the table. She looks at the ledger, already open to tonight's entry. At the bottom of the column: *"Nobody came to that door."* She reads it back to herself. It is what she knows. It is what the night is. She is not rehearsing it — there is nothing to rehearse; it is simply true, and she is simply reading it.

8. **The closing**: The wind is a sustained pressure on the exterior wall — not howling, just there. She pulls the blanket tighter. The lamp is burning. The front door is latched.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Della has inspected the house and confirmed what holds. The front door is latched. The back-room door is closed, and she is not going in. The kept sentence has been read back. The night has a stable shape — she knows what it holds and what it doesn't — and the not-knowing she maintains is inside that stable shape, not threatening it.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

Why does she stay outside the back-room door? (She says it is where the ledger is, but the ledger is already in the kitchen. The reader is given the closed door and not an explanation.)

---

## Membrane Crossing

**Vehicle**: The front-door latch and the back-room door handle. Both are physical objects present in both timelines. Della tests the front-door latch; she puts her hand on the back-room door handle without opening it. Ellen, in chapter 1, found the back-room door ajar; Della keeps it shut. These are the same door, different times, different relationships to what is behind it. The back-room door begins its accumulation as the book's central liminal object.

## Continuity Notes

- **New facts established**: The front-door latch is functional; the back-room door stays closed at Della's choice; the temperature in the house has dropped to 44°F by 9:00 PM; Della knows the third step well (she steps over it with ease of decades); the back room is cold in a specific way, different from the storm's cold; Della keeps the ledger in the back room (though she has it in the kitchen tonight); outside, the storm is not abating.
- **Voice anchors**: First-person, present tense. Short sentences. The lamp is carried through the chapter as a physical anchor — the circle of light moves with Della and defines what she can see. No metaphor. The back-room door gets neither explanation nor embellishment; it is simply where she stops.
- **Mood**: Methodical, contained. The chapter ends in the kitchen — the lamp's circle, the blanket, the front door latched. Managed. The reader feels the back-room door more than Della seems to.
