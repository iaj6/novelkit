# Chapter 13 — The Account as It Stood

**POV**: Ellen Vance (third-person limited, past tense)  
**Summary**: Ellen sits at the desk with her notes and assembles the account of Della Markey's death as she now believes she has it; the account is complete and internally consistent; she writes it as a rough draft; she tells herself she will go into the back room tomorrow; she does not go.  
**Clock**: Ellen's clock — early June, present day  
**Chapter shape**: interiority-heavy  
**Ending mode**: resolved-beat  
**Target words**: ~2,000  
**Note**: ⊘ STALLING CHAPTER — no new documentary discovery; Ellen is sitting with the account she now wrong-believes. The stall accomplishes: (1) the false resolution is allowed to settle and feel stable; (2) Ellen's professional discipline is shown at its most convinced, which makes its later reversal more significant; (3) the back room is identified as the one loose thread she has not yet dealt with, setting up chapters 19–21. The chapter's resolved-beat ending is the false resolution — it resolves the wrong question.

---

## Setting / Time

The house on Verrill Street, Renick. Ellen's desk in the front room. Morning; the house is quiet; June light comes in off the street. Ellen is in professional mode — she has her notebook, the photocopied file pages spread out, and she is producing what she calls (to herself) a rough draft.

---

## Chapter Shape and Tone

Interiority-heavy: Ellen at the desk, working through her notes. The chapter is organized around the professional act of producing a draft — a summary of testimony, the kind of rough draft a court reporter produces from CAT output before certifying it. She has the materials; she assembles them. The prose stays in her professional register. The "rough draft" she produces is the false account: accurate, complete, and wrong. The chapter ends on a specific procedural decision she makes and then does not make.

---

## Goal

Produce a complete written account of what happened to Della Markey on February 11–12, 1983, based on the documentary record she has assembled.

---

## Obstacle

There is no obvious obstacle. The account is internally consistent; the evidence supports it. The obstacle is invisible: she has the wrong account, and she is about to write it down in her own hand, which is the act that makes it official in her professional world.

---

## Action Beats

1. **The desk in the morning**: Ellen at the desk in the front room, the maker's mark at the inner drawer edge below her eye line. She has spread out the photocopied file pages: the death certificate, the coroner's report, the physician's statement, Mabel Duff's statement. She has her notebook with the deed and probate notes.

2. **The professional methodology of the rough draft**: She produces a rough draft the way she produces any rough draft from CAT output — in order, cleaning up the transcription into readable form. The rough draft of the account: 
   - February 9, 1983: storm begins.
   - February 10: Della Markey seen at mailbox by neighbor; she says weather is coming, she is getting in her wood.
   - February 11, daytime: curtains drawn; no visitors; car in drive.
   - February 11, before dark: power out. Stove loses draft (exterior chimney, extreme cold). Attempts to relight fail.
   - Approximately 10:00 PM or later: Della in kitchen, wrapped in blanket. Death occurs sometime between 10:00 PM and 4:00 AM.
   - February 12: utility worker calls deputy after seeing body through window. Accidental death confirmed.

3. **The completed account**: She reads it back. It is consistent: the storm, the power out, the stove that failed, the woman alone, nobody who came to the door. The inquest verdict was correct. There is nothing in the record that says otherwise.

4. **The professional gap, noted**: She notes, on a separate sheet (the *unexplained* column): the back room's cold. The door that will not stay. The sense of a second attention in the rooms. She puts these in the column marked *not on the record.* She does not call them anything. They are the inaudible passage in the transcript — noted, marked parenthetically, the proceeding continues.

5. **The back room**: She looks at the notes from the inquest file. The deputy mentioned a ledger, left on the kitchen table, inventoried with the house contents. She has been in this house for weeks. She has not found a ledger. She notes: if the ledger was inventoried but not removed, it should still be in the house. The back room is where she hasn't fully looked.

6. **The non-decision**: She tells herself she will go into the back room today; she will look properly. She goes up the stairs. The third step. The landing. The back room door is closed. She puts her hand on the door handle.

   She does not go in.

   She comes back downstairs.

7. **What she tells herself**: The account is complete. The ledger is probably in the back room. She will go in when she's ready. She doesn't need it to complete the account — the account is complete without it. The proceeding is closed.

8. **Closing**: She folds the rough draft and puts it in the notebook with the file copies. She closes the notebook. She goes to make coffee. The house around her is quiet. The account is written. This is the record of what happened to Della Markey.

---

## Outcome / Turn

Ellen has the false account in writing. She has identified the back room as the one un-examined space but has chosen not to go in — the account feels complete without it. This is the false resolution: the proceeding appears closed; the professional has produced the rough draft; the record is certified (in her mind). But the ledger is still in the back room, and she hasn't read it.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

Why doesn't she go into the back room? She's identified it as the likely location of the ledger. The account she's produced should motivate her to go get the last piece. But she doesn't go. The chapter does not explain this; Ellen tells herself it's because the account is already complete without it.

---

No new object crosses; the crossing here is the false account solidifying in Ellen's professional mind.
