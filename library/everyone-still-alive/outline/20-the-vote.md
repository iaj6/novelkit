# Chapter 20 — The Vote
*Vignette 6: The Derelict · Year Two, spring · Lake Ashford · Day 3*

## One-Line Summary
Day 3: the Galena fills the eastern horizon; the Reader of Names is heard on the shortwave and someone asks if the signal is coming from the ship (unanswered); the community meeting votes to give Mikkel the canoe and four days of supplies and send him south; Mikkel takes the canoe; Nadia's logbook records what she did not say.

## POV and Tense
Nadia Ruiz, 62 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Slow-dread maritime, tightening further. Nadia's sentences are noticeably shorter now. The warmth is not gone — she still sees the people she loves clearly and with precision — but every observation is edged with what the observation costs. The logbook is tighter. The chapter's central moral event happens in the meeting, but Nadia's account of it is more devastating than the event itself because of what she records that she did not say.

**Depth-marker:** *Year Two, spring. Day Three.*

## Setting / Time
The raft. Day 3. The Galena is close enough to see its silhouette clearly without binoculars — a ship-shape, dark, against the eastern sky and water.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — Day 3: morning, the shortwave, the community meeting, Mikkel's departure.

## Ending Mode
**Turn** — Mikkel takes the canoe and leaves. The community watches him go. This is the thesis's central moral event, and it happens before the ship arrives.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening — Day 3 logbook entry:** Shorter than yesterday's. Nadia records the position, the time, the weather. The Galena is closer. She writes its distance now: *approximately six miles by Gus's estimate.* She does not write what this means. The number is enough.

2. **The Galena at dawn:** No longer just a mass on the horizon. At dawn it is a shape — the specific shape of a lake freighter, the bow turned west, the superstructure at the rear. No lights on any deck. Nothing visible on the bow. The east wind is steady; the drift is steady.

3. **The shortwave:** Before the community meeting, Nadia takes out the shortwave — the Reader of Names' usual hour is late at night, but she checks the frequency in the morning now, instinctively. The Reader of Names is there. In the morning, for the first time she's heard it in daylight. She doesn't call anyone over immediately; she listens. The names.

4. **The question:** After the broadcast ends, she mentions it to Gus. He comes to the shortwave. Then others come. Someone — Nadia does not record who, in the logbook — asks: *Is that coming from the ship?* The question is asked plainly, without accusation. The shortwave frequency and the ship's possible transmitter could be related. Nadia writes: *The question was asked. I did not answer it because I do not know the answer.* Whether the broadcast is coming from the Galena is not resolved; Nadia's logbook records only that the question was raised and not answered.

5. **The community meeting:** Called by Tomás — he said in Ch 19 that the community needed to decide before the ship arrived, not after. The meeting is at the mast end, all twenty present. The Galena is visible over everyone's shoulder on the eastern horizon.

6. **Gus's proposal:** He does not frame it as accusation. He frames it as *the community's interest in clarity.* There is an unanswerable question: is Mikkel's arrival connected to the ship's approach? The community cannot answer it. They cannot investigate the ship while it is still at distance. What they can do: ask Mikkel to travel south while the ship situation is assessed — not expulsion, not accusation, a temporary separation in the community's interest. He offers the canoe and four days of supplies. This is framed as reasonable. Nadia, in the meeting, does not say what she will write later.

7. **The vote:** The community votes. It is not unanimous. Nadia does not record how she voted. The majority: Mikkel goes south with the canoe and supplies. A minority — Tomás, Petra, two others — vote against or abstain. The vote is valid under the community's rules.

8. **Mikkel's response:** He receives the vote quietly. He does not argue. He says: I have no connection to that ship. He says this once. He says nothing else. He accepts the canoe and the supplies. Nadia writes that his face in that moment was a specific thing she will not forget.

9. **The departure:** Mikkel loads the canoe. Gus helps him — Nadia notes this, that Gus helps him, and what this costs Gus. The community watches. Mikkel paddles south. The chapter does not watch him until he is out of sight; Nadia goes back to the logbook.

10. **Nadia's logbook entry — what she did not say:** This is the chapter's key beat and must be on the page. Nadia writes the meeting accurately — the proposal, the vote, the departure. Then she writes: *What I did not say in the meeting:* and she lists it. The list is short: that she has no evidence. That fear is a reason and not the same as a reason. That she has watched Mikkel work on this raft for three weeks and found no fault. That she did not vote against and did not vote for and she is writing this because if she does not write it no one will.

11. **Chapter close:** The Galena on the eastern horizon. Mikkel's canoe is visible to the south for a time and then it is not. Nadia closes the logbook. The chapter ends.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The central moral event of the book's final movement: the community has expelled Mikkel before the ship arrives and before they know anything. The turn: Nadia's logbook records her own silence. This is the thesis's penultimate instance — the community, at distance from the actual threat, has done its worst.

## New Question / Pressure
Will Mikkel survive? What is on the ship? Has the community's decision destroyed something that cannot be rebuilt?

## Continuity Notes
- **Critical continuity fact established:** The community votes to send Mikkel south in the canoe with four days of supplies, on Day 3 of the Galena's approach. This is the community's worst decision — made before the ship arrives, based entirely on fear.
- **Critical continuity fact confirmed:** The Reader of Names is heard on the shortwave in V6 (Day 3 morning). The question of whether the signal is coming from the Galena is raised and **not resolved** — Nadia's logbook records only that the question was asked.
- **Thread: Reader of Names** — its final appearance in the book. The question of its origin remains open. The book refuses to answer.
- **Thread: Thesis** — the thesis's final instance is now complete: the community has turned on Mikkel before they know what is on the ship. Distance from the real threat has produced the worst behavior.
- **Note to drafter:** The logbook entry "what I did not say" must appear as direct text — Nadia writing, in logbook voice, the thoughts she withheld from the meeting. This is one of the book's two or three most important passages. It should be specific, short, and devastating without annotation.
- **Note to drafter:** Gus helping Mikkel load the canoe is not incidental. It should be one sentence in the logbook — specific, unelaborated — that carries enormous weight.
