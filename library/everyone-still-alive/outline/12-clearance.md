# Chapter 12 — Clearance
*Vignette 4: The Sweep · Year One, deep winter · The Rim towns*

## One-Line Summary
The crew — Cass, Jute, Parish, the kid — sweeps a farmhouse and a laundromat; the vocabulary of "clearance" is established; something is implied but not named about what happens when they find people inside.

## POV and Tense
Cass — **first person, present tense.** This is the vignette's mandatory POV and tense; it must not drift.

**Register:** Cold perpetrator POV. Flat affect, physical precision, moral evasion. Short to medium sentences. Paragraphs are tight — no lyrical sprawl. Horror is enacted through what is noted without commentary. Cass's voice is beautiful in the physical world and oblique in the moral one. Cracks appear in single short sentences and are immediately closed.

**Hard cut from V3:** A year forward in the collapse, a different geography, a first-person voice where the third-person voice was. The floor changes underfoot completely.

**Depth-marker:** *Year One. Winter.*

## Setting / Time
The Rim towns — a circuit of emptied lake-country towns worked by the crew. A farmhouse on the outskirts of the first town; a laundromat on the main street. Deep winter: the hardest yet, because the easy supplies were taken in Year One's first months.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — the sweep. One day, two structures.

## Ending Mode
**Declarative-close** — the chapter ends with Cass making a note in what might be a log, or simply noting something to themselves. Flat, complete, closed.

## Beats

1. **Opening:** We are inside Cass's first-person present before any scene is established — a sentence that places us immediately in the work and the body. *We clear the farmhouse in forty minutes.* Or similar. The scene establishes itself through what Cass notes in present tense — not through orientation or backstory.

2. **The crew:** Jute works the rooms efficiently and without commentary, which is how he indicates approval. Parish talks — a running account of what he sees, names for things, a running observation of the world that functions as noise. The kid watches everything and says little. Cass notes all of this with the precision of someone who has learned to read their crew the way they read a building.

3. **The farmhouse:** Two stories, the kind of farmhouse that had not been updated since before anyone was born. The first floor has been searched before — the pantry is bare, the shelves are cleared, the furniture turned over. The second floor is less disturbed. This is what the crew is here for: what other people didn't reach or didn't think to look for.

4. **What they find in the farmhouse:** Supplies — canned goods behind a false back in a closet, a kerosene lamp, a box of candles, a first aid kit. Jute catalogs what goes in the pack. The kid is assigned the attic. He comes down with a box of emergency flares and a sleeping bag still in its stuff sack.

5. **What Cass implies about the farmhouse:** The attic has something else in it that the kid found first. Cass does not go up. Parish makes a comment — cheerful, brief — and Cass notes it and does not respond. The kid comes down with only the flares and the sleeping bag. Cass notes that the kid has learned something about what goes in the pack and what does not.

6. **The laundromat:** The main street of the Rim town is one block of commercial buildings, all empty. The laundromat has been stripped of useful items but the folded clothes are still on top of the machines. This is what stays: the clean laundry, folded by someone who came in to do their wash and left without it. Deep winter coats in the stack. Jute takes two.

7. **What Parish calls the laundromat:** Something cheerful. He gives the laundromat a nickname — the kind of naming that marks territory, that makes a place's use legible by its name. The kid does not laugh. Cass notes this.

8. **The implication:** By the end of the laundromat search, the implication should be present in the narration without being stated: the crew does not only search buildings. "Clearance" has acquired a meaning beyond searching and securing. The evidence for this is in what Cass notices and does not name: the attic, the kid's face coming down the stairs, Parish's cheerful vocabulary.

9. **The ride back to camp:** The crew moves south in the cold to wherever they are camped that night. Parish talks. The kid is quiet. Jute counts the take. Cass notes the take in the same voice they use for everything else.

10. **Chapter close:** Cass makes note of the day's work. Either in an actual log or in the narrative's present-tense accounting. The note is accurate, flat, and complete. The chapter ends on it.

## Outcome / Turn
The crew is established; the vocabulary of "clearance" is in place; something is implied about the nature of their work that is not yet stated. The turn is structural rather than dramatic: the reader now knows something that the chapter has not said directly, and this gap is Cass's voice.

## New Question / Pressure
What did the kid find in the attic? What does "clearance" actually mean?

- **Establishes:** Crew dynamics — Jute (efficient, no apology, no explanation), Parish (talks constantly, has cheerful names for things), the kid (watching, not yet understanding, learning).
- **Establishes:** Cass's first-person present-tense voice — this is the only vignette in this tense and person.
