# Chapter 22 — What We Keep
*Vignette 6: The Derelict · Year Two, spring · Lake Ashford · Day 5*

## One-Line Summary
Day 5: the Galena arrives; Gus and Tomás board it; the hold contains a small number of weakened infected, sealed; the upper decks hold a shortwave transmitter, currently silent; the ship drifts past; Nadia's last logbook entry; the book ends on her hand against the carved wooden fish.

## POV and Tense
Nadia Ruiz, 62 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Slow-dread maritime at its final point. Nadia's sentences are their shortest. The warmth is still present — she still sees Gus, Tomás, Petra, the children with precision and love — but the warmth is surrounded by what happened. The last image is not warm or cold; it is specific. It belongs to the fish on the mast and Nadia's hand on it. No thesis statement. No final pronouncement. The structure has already made the argument.

**Depth-marker:** *Year Two, spring. Day Five.*

## Setting / Time
The raft and the Galena alongside it. Day 5 dawn through late morning. The east wind has slowed slightly — the ship's drift has slowed but not stopped.

## Chapter Shape
Action-heavy single scene — the ship's arrival and the boarding.

## Ending Mode
**Other (final-image close)** — the last thing in the book is a specific image: Nadia's hand on the carved wooden fish nailed slightly crooked to the mast. No narration follows it. No sentence interprets it. The image is the last sentence.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening — Day 5, predawn:** Nadia wakes before dawn. She goes to the eastern railing. The Galena is close enough now to hear — not engine, not voices, but the specific sound a large object makes moving through water: a displacement, a pressure wave, the hull sound. She stands and listens.

2. **The logbook entry, Day 5:** The shortest in the log. *Day Five. Year Two. Dawn. The ship is here.* Three sentences, maybe four. She closes the book.

3. **Dawn:** The Galena at first light. The community is on deck — all of them, by some instinct, awake and present. The ship's scale at this distance: it is larger than the imagination made it. The name GALENA at the bow. The rust at the waterline. The dark portholes. The anchor chains hanging. The superstructure at the stern.

4. **Gus and Tomás:** Gus says they should board. His reasoning: if the ship passes without boarding, the community will never know what it carries, and not knowing will be worse than knowing. Tomás agrees. They take the aluminum johnboat. Nadia watches from the mast. Gus's last look at her before he rows: she writes it in the logbook (later) as something she will carry.

5. **The boarding:** Gus and Tomás row to the ship's port side, where a ladder is accessible. They climb aboard. The community watches. Nadia watches through the binoculars. The deck: empty, weathered, salt and rust. No crew. No bodies on deck.

6. **What they find in the lower holds:** There are sounds below decks — something moving in the holds. Sealed. The hatches are dogged from outside (by the crew, at some point before the crew died or fled — someone sealed the lower decks). Gus and Tomás do not open the hatches. They listen: movement, irregular, slow. Not many — the sound of three or four things, weakened by cold and starvation, months without heat. The ship has been engine-dead for weeks or months; the lower decks are cold. Whatever is in there is alive but diminished.

7. **What they find in the upper decks:** The radio room. A shortwave transmitter — more powerful than the raft's. The transmitter's power system is dead; it would need shore power to broadcast. The equipment is there; the frequency dial is set to the Reader of Names frequency. The community, watching from the raft, cannot see this. Gus and Tomás note it; they photograph nothing (they have no camera); they carry away what they can: the frequency setting, the model of the transmitter. Whether this was the Reader of Names' transmitter — whether the voice came from this ship — is not answered. The book refuses. The equipment is there; the broadcast has not been heard from here in any certain way; the question remains open.

8. **The ship drifts past:** The Galena's drift — slowed but present — means it does not stop at the raft. Gus and Tomás board back into the johnboat when the ladder angle becomes unsafe. The ship continues its westward drift on the ebbing east wind. Within two hours of dawn, the Galena has passed the raft's position and is moving slowly toward the western shore. It is still drifting; the community watches it go.

9. **What the community knows:** A small number of infected in the lower holds — weakened, sealed. A shortwave transmitter, matching the Reader of Names frequency. No crew. No survivors. No answers. What they faced together could have been managed together. They did not face it together.

10. **The empty canoe:** Still tied to the raft's stern. No one looks at it. No one speaks Mikkel's name. Nadia looks at it. She writes nothing about it yet.

11. **Nadia's last logbook entry:** She takes the logbook from its case. She writes the boarding, the findings, the ship's drift past. She writes: *The holds were sealed from outside. Someone on that ship, at the end, locked the hatches and chose not to be below them.* She writes: *The transmitter. The frequency.* She writes the Reader of Names question and does not answer it: *I don't know. I have written that I don't know.* She writes nothing about Mikkel. She sits with the open logbook. Then she closes it.

12. **The fish:** The fish is on the mast. It has always been on the mast. Slightly crooked — the same angle it had when someone (who has never been named to Nadia) put it there. Nadia closes the logbook and stands. She puts the logbook in its case. She looks at the fish.

13. **The last image / chapter close / book close:** Nadia puts her hand on the fish. The wood under her palm: small, smooth, the grain of it, slightly warm from the morning light. The lake around the raft. The Galena to the west, smaller now. The canoe at the stern. Gus somewhere on the raft behind her. The fish, slightly crooked, nailed to the mast by someone whose name she does not know.

    *Her hand on the fish.*

    *The book ends.*

---

## Outcome / Turn
The Galena's contents are specific and earned: a small number of weakened infected in sealed holds, a shortwave transmitter on the Reader of Names frequency, no crew, no answers. The community could have faced this together. They did not. The thesis is delivered one final time: the monster was never the ship. The last image — the fish, the hand — is the book's final statement: the small made object that traveled two years and six lives and arrived here, under a human hand, slightly crooked, meaning everything and naming nothing.

## Continuity Notes
- **Critical continuity fact established:** The Galena's contents: a small number of weakened infected in sealed lower holds (sealed from outside — the hatches were dogged by someone on the ship before the ship went dark); a shortwave transmitter in the radio room set to the Reader of Names frequency; no crew; no survivors aboard.
- **Critical continuity confirmed:** The Reader of Names question is **not resolved**. The transmitter is on the right frequency; whether it was the broadcast source is not answered. The book refuses to close this.
- **Critical continuity confirmed:** The Galena drifts past the raft's position and continues west — it does not ram the raft.
- **Thread: Reader of Names** — resolved as deliberately unresolved. The transmitter is present; the broadcast is not confirmed as coming from it. Final state: open.
- **Thread: Fish** — the book's last image. Nadia's hand on the fish nailed to the mast. The chain of custody (Jesse → Theo → stranger → raft → mast) is complete. No character knows the full chain.
- **Thread: Thesis** — final instance. The community could have managed the ship's contents together. They did not stay together. The last image makes no argument; the structure already has.
- **Mikkel:** His fate is not resolved. The empty canoe is tied to the raft's stern. Nadia does not write about him in the last entry. His name is not spoken. The book holds this open.
- **Note to drafter:** The last sentence of the book is a physical image — Nadia's hand on the fish. Do not follow it with explanation, reflection, or thesis statement. The sentence is complete as an image. The book ends on it.
- **Note to drafter:** The finds aboard the Galena must be rendered as facts, not revelations. Gus and Tomás discover them with the practicality of people who have survived two years of the collapse. The moral weight is in what these facts mean to the community below — watching from the raft — and that weight is not stated; it is in the structure.
