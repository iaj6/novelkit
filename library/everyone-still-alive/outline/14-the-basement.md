# Chapter 14 — The Basement
*Vignette 4: The Sweep · Year One, deep winter · The Rim towns*

## One-Line Summary
The crew reaches a church with a locked basement; Jute opens it; survivors are inside; the full nature of the crew's work is now fully visible; the kid understands and moves to stand in front of the basement door; Cass's narration records only what his hands reach for and stops.

## POV and Tense
Cass — first person, present tense.

**Register:** Cold perpetrator POV at its final register. The crack from Ch 13 is present in the chapter but Cass does not open it. What the chapter does instead: it renders the kid's understanding and the kid's final act with full physical specificity, and then Cass's narration cuts. The cut is the vignette's answer — the narrator is the monster, and the chapter ends before we know if the story continues or changes.

**Depth-marker:** *Year One. Winter.* (The following day or the day after.)

## Setting / Time
The Rim towns. A church — white clapboard, community hall to the right, cemetery visible through a side window. The basement has a padlocked door in the church's rear hall.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — the church. One structure.

## Ending Mode
**Elliptical** — Cass's narration stops at the physical image of the kid's hands. What follows is not described. The reader is left with the receipt, not the invoice.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening:** The church. Jute has decided the church is the last structure in this town's circuit before they move to the next. Parish says something about churches — a recurring joke, presumably; Cass has heard it before. The kid is quiet. He has been quiet since the hardware store.

2. **The church interior:** The nave is stripped — not by the crew, by the town's previous salvagers. Pew cushions, candles, the hanging cross, a box of hymnals. None of this is useful. Jute moves through it quickly. Cass follows. The kid stays near the door longer than necessary, then follows.

3. **The rear hall:** The church has a community hall behind the nave — tables and chairs, a kitchen, a storage area. And at the far end: a padlocked door. The padlock is significant: it is engaged from the outside. Someone locked this door from the hall side, which means the basement is either secured or — Jute does not say this, but the crew understands.

4. **Jute opens the padlock:** He carries a bolt cutter. The padlock yields. He opens the door. The smell from below: people, warmth, food, and fear. Not the smell of empty buildings. The smell of a basement where people have been living.

5. **The basement:** People inside — how many is not given with precision, but more than one, and they include more than adults. The crew can see them from the doorway. They are alive and they have been hiding for weeks. They have water and what they were storing when they locked themselves in.

6. **The full revelation:** This is the moment the reader has been waiting for since Ch 12. It is fully visible here — not narrated directly, but present as a fact. Jute turns back to the crew. His expression is the same as when they found the propane regulator. Parish starts talking.

7. **The kid:** He is behind Cass. Then he is beside Cass. Then — and this is the chapter's central physical movement — he is in front of Cass, between the crew and the basement door. He has moved without Cass ordering it in advance or registering it fully until it has happened.

8. **What the kid reaches for:** The chapter must specify this, because Cass's narration records it and stops. Options the drafter should choose between: (a) he moves to the bolt on the church's rear door, as if to open it — releasing the survivors, or signaling them to run; (b) he picks up the bolt cutter from where Jute set it down; (c) he simply stands in front of the basement door with his hands at his sides and does not move. The outline specifies: **his hands go to the padlock that Jute has left hanging open in the door's hasp — he is closing it again, locking it from the outside, locking the crew out.** This is the gesture: he is locking the door between the crew and the survivors, using Jute's own bolt cutter or simply the padlock's shackle.

9. **Cass's narration:** It records this physical action — the kid's hands, the padlock, the specific metal sound of the hasp — and then it stops. One more sentence, maybe: Jute's stillness. Or Parish going quiet, which is the strangest thing in the chapter because Parish is never quiet. Then Cass's narration ends the chapter.

10. **What Cass does not say:** Whether Jute allows this. Whether the survivors are saved. Whether the kid survives his intervention. What Cass does. The narration does not go there. This is not evasion — it is the formal limit of the voice. Cass can describe what the kid's hands do. Cass cannot describe what it means and cannot describe what follows.

11. **Chapter close / vignette close:** The padlock in the kid's hands, or the kid's hands on it, or the silence of Parish. One image. Then the chapter is over.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The vignette's thesis is delivered in full: the narrator is the monster, from inside. The turn is the kid's act: he has understood what the crew does and placed himself between the crew and the people in the basement, using the crew's own tool. Whether this changes anything is not answered by the narration. The vignette ends here.

## New Question / Pressure
(Deliberately unanswered.) Did the survivors in the basement survive? Did the kid? This is not a thread to be picked up by another vignette — the book lets it rest here.

## Continuity Notes
- **Full revelation confirmed:** The crew kills survivors they find in structures during their sweeps. This is fully visible in this chapter, though Cass's narration still does not name it.
- **The kid's final act:** He moves to stand between the crew and the basement door and places his hands on the padlock — closing it, locking the crew out. This is visible in the narration; its consequence is not.
- **Continuity constraint:** Cass's narration **must not pre-empt the kid's response.** The chapter ends at the kid's physical action and Jute's stillness (or Parish's silence). No further narration.
- **Thread: Thesis** — the vignette's answer is complete: the narrator has become the thing they feared; this is what happens when the monster is far away and the need is near.
- **Vignette arc complete:** Setup (clearance, implication) → Escalation (hardware store, partial revelation) → Turn (church, the kid's understanding) → Close (the kid's act and the cut). The arc satisfies within the vignette.
- **Note to drafter:** The survivors in the basement must be humanized minimally but specifically — their smell, their presence, the fact of more than one child among them. Enough to make the weight of the scene felt. Not so much that Cass's voice loses its flatness.
