# Chapter 10 — The Barn
*Vignette 3: The Road South · Month Six · Highway 41 · Days 6–10*

## One-Line Summary
Cold deepens and snow arrives; they shelter in a barn with a hostile family who attempt to take their supplies in the dark; Lenn is struck but they escape into the wet cold; Theo asks what those people were afraid of; Lenn does not answer.

## POV and Tense
Lenn ("the man") — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Literary-bleak / McCarthy. The cold is structural — it becomes colder by Day 8 and there is wet snow by Day 9. The barn chapter is the vignette's darkest movement in terms of what the man becomes: he does not answer Theo's question because he knows the answer and knows it is not something a nine-year-old should hear confirmed. The narration does not say this.

**Depth-marker:** *Month Six. Day Nine.*

## Setting / Time
Highway 41 south, further from the smelting plant now. Days 6–10. A barn on Day 9 where another family — a couple and their adult son — has already sheltered. Wet snow falling by night.

## Chapter Shape
Multi-scene — Days 6–8 compressed; Day 9 rendered as the full scene (the barn encounter); Day 10 briefly.

## Ending Mode
**Literary-fade** — the chapter ends with Lenn and Theo walking through wet snow south, Theo's question unasked again (he only asked it once), the man's silence the answer.

## Beats

1. **Opening (Days 6–8, compressed):** The road continues. What has changed since the ash-sky: colder now, the sky lower. They find food twice — a locked house with a broken window where they find canned goods behind cleaning products, and an apple tree behind an empty farmhouse with six hard apples still on it, which Theo takes carefully and wraps in his shirt. Days have a texture now: the road's specific sounds (their footsteps, wind, no cars), the specific smell of emptied towns, what it is to eat standing up in a stranger's kitchen without sitting down because sitting means staying.

2. **Day 9 — the cold and the barn:** A barn south of a cluster of emptied houses. The man sees the tracks before they reach it — boot prints in the mud, heading in. He stops. He listens. Then he decides: the cold coming tonight will be below what they can manage without shelter; the barn has walls. He takes Theo in.

3. **The other family:** Inside: a couple, fifties or sixties, and their son, thirties. They have claimed the far stall, a camp stove, a significant amount of gear. The man nods. The couple do not acknowledge them. The son watches Theo. The man and Theo take the near stall: two sleeping bags, their packs reduced to what's left.

4. **The evening:** No conversation. They eat separately. The snow begins against the barn's roof — wet snow, heavy, the sound specific. The couple's camp stove casts light. The man's interiority: calculation. How much do they have, how much do the others have, is there a claim being made here. He does not reach a conclusion before dark.

5. **The man stays awake — or nearly:** He does not sleep fully. He has not slept fully since Day 1. His body makes this arrangement without his ordering it.

6. **In the dark:** The sound of a person moving, near their stall. The man opens his eyes before he moves. The son, crouching over the pack, his hand on the zipper. The man says — not loudly, just: *stop.* The son does not stop. The man is upright before the next breath and they are grappling in the dark in a way that wakes Theo and the couple.

7. **The struggle:** Brief, confused, loud. The man is hit — not seriously; a forearm across his jaw, his vision stars briefly. He gets the pack. He gets Theo by the wrist. He gets out of the barn.

8. **Outside:** Wet snow. The son has not followed. The man stands in the dark with Theo and the pack and his jaw aching and waits to see if anyone comes out after them. No one does. After a time they walk.

9. **Theo asks the question:** After a long silence on the road, Theo asks: *What were those people afraid of?* The man does not answer. Not because he doesn't know — he knows — but because the answer is *us*, or *nothing in particular*, or *everyone*, and none of these are answers he can give a nine-year-old without saying what the road has taught him about people, which he is not prepared to say. So he says nothing.

10. **Theo:** He does not ask again. He pulls the fish from his pocket — the man sees him hold it for a moment — and puts it back. This is all Theo does about the question.

11. **Day 10:** They find a small house, unlocked, that has been stripped but has a fireplace and dry wood. They stay through the worst of the snow. They do not build a fire (smoke is visible). By afternoon they move on.

12. **Chapter close:** South, wet snow, the road visible ahead for a quarter mile before it bends and the country beyond is unknown. The man walks. Theo walks. Their footprints in the snow fill quickly.

## Outcome / Turn
The third near-death has passed — also from other people, not infected. The turn: Theo's question. The man's silence is the answer, and it is the wrong kind of answer, and the man knows this. The vignette is moving toward its close: Theo still has the fish, and the road is still going south, and the man does not know if the second part of his project (not becoming something Theo will remember as a monster) is succeeding.

## New Question / Pressure
Is the man still good? (He did not hurt the other family, but he has stopped reaching for language about what the road does to people.) How close is the river crossing?

## Continuity Notes
- **Third near-death encounter:** The hostile family in the barn — specifically the son trying to take the pack; the struggle; the escape. No infected. Entirely human.
- **Establishes:** The snow — wet and heavy — arriving by Day 9. The physical world continues to worsen.
- **Thread: Fish** — Theo takes it from his pocket and holds it after the encounter. He does not show it to the man or explain it. The fish is his private logic.
- **Thread: Thesis** — Month Six, no infected; the chapter demonstrates the gradient: the people in the barn were afraid of nothing in particular, and that fear was enough to make them dangerous.
- **Key beat for drafter:** Theo's question (*What were those people afraid of?*) is the chapter's moral center and it must not be answered in the narration. The man's silence is the chapter's answer. Do not follow the silence with an explanatory paragraph.
