# Chapter 05 — The Notebook
*Vignette 2: The Line · Month Two · The Narrows Checkpoint, Highway 9*

## One-Line Summary
Selle's first chapter establishes the checkpoint, the rules in the spiral notebook, the tanker truck, and Pruett's authority — a normal shift, its horror entirely in its normality.

## POV and Tense
Private Daan Selle, 21 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Institutional dread. Controlled, cold, procedural. Selle observes, categorizes, defers conclusions. His inner life reads like an inventory — a list of items whose total he has not yet added up. Sentences are medium-length and precise. The horror is bureaucratic: the language of procedure applied to human life. Do not rush to the horror; let the procedure be the horror.

**Hard cut from V1:** The opening of this chapter should feel like the floor has changed underfoot. The YA-horror register of the previous vignette is gone. We are in a different time, a different body, a different kind of dread.

## Setting / Time
The Narrows checkpoint, Highway 9 at the Ashford River bridge. Month Two of the collapse. November — the river carries ice; cold is structural. The checkpoint: a Jersey-barrier barricade on the eastern approach, a plywood kiosk, a fuel tanker truck (three-quarters full) behind the barrier, a surplus tent, a propane heater, a folding cot, a CB radio.

**Depth-marker:** *Month Two.*

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — one shift. Selle's movements through the checkpoint: the morning briefing, the procedure, the crossings. Compressed time.

## Ending Mode
**Declarative-close** — the chapter ends with Selle making an entry in the notebook. The entry is procedurally complete. He closes the notebook.

## Beats

1. **Opening (hard cut from V1):** Selle at the kiosk. November. The river smell and the cold. He is counting the vehicles in the tanker's shadow as he does every morning — not because they change, but because the count is in the notebook and the notebook has a place for it.

2. **The notebook:** A green spiral notebook, Pruett's handwriting in the front section establishing the rules. Selle has a section at the back for his shift logs. The rules: a numbered list. Selle knows them by heart; he helped formalize them in Week Three when Pruett decided they needed to be written down. The rules are reasonable. They are: travelers must state their origin and destination; they must show their hands; they must not have visible wounds or fever above a certain threshold; they must carry no more than three days of supplies (to prevent hoarding); they must not travel in groups of more than eight unrelated persons.

3. **Pruett:** Arrives at the kiosk at 0800. Gives Selle the overnight summary from the eastern approach watchpost. Two people tried to cross the river to the south in the dark — the watch saw them and they turned back before reaching the wire. This is noted. Normal.

4. **A normal crossing:** A man, alone, mid-forties, farmer's build, heading west to his sister's place in Duluth (or the equivalent). He has a cart with legitimate supplies: a sleeping bag, canned goods, tools. He states his origin (a named town). He shows his hands. His temperature, taken with the checkpoint's thermometer, is normal. Pruett signs his form. Selle opens the barrier and logs the crossing. The man walks west. Selle watches until he is out of sight, as the notebook says to do.

5. **The afternoon:** Two more crossings attempted. The first: a woman with a bicycle and an address in the next town west — approved, logged. The second: a man, younger, carrying too much for one person, unwilling to state a clear destination, who becomes agitated when Selle asks for hands. Pruett turns him back. The man argues. Pruett is patient. The man leaves. Selle writes the entry.

6. **Selle's interiority:** He catalogs. He does not yet draw conclusions. He notes that the turned-back man's agitation could mean many things — fever, fear, deception, exhaustion. He does not rank these in order of likelihood in the notebook, but he does in his head, and then he closes the ordering and moves on.

7. **Pruett on the system:** In the evening, sharing food in the tent, Pruett explains the tanker to Selle — not for the first time, but Selle listens as if for the first time, because Pruett needs him to. The tanker is why the position matters. The fuel in it is a resource no one yet knows how to replace. Holding this position holds the tanker. Pruett says this clearly. He is not wrong.

8. **The notebook again:** Selle makes the end-of-shift entry. The man who was turned back, the woman who passed, the farmer who passed, the two overnight river-crossers. The temperatures. The CB radio check. The date. He signs it.

9. **Chapter close:** Selle closes the notebook. Outside, the river carries ice. The propane heater ticks. He is not troubled by anything he has written. That is the chapter's last beat — not that something is wrong, but that nothing in the notebook has told him anything is wrong.

## Outcome / Turn
The checkpoint is established: its purpose, its rules, its people, its logic. The turn is not a dramatic event — it is the chapter's tone: a reasonable system, maintained reasonably, by a reasonable person who has not yet examined what the system is doing.

## New Question / Pressure
What happens to the turned-back man? (Not asked directly — just left open.) What is Selle slowly becoming, shift by shift?

## Continuity Notes
- **Establishes:** The Narrows checkpoint — Highway 9 at the Ashford River bridge; the plywood kiosk; the Jersey barriers; the fuel tanker (three-quarters full); the CB radio; the surplus tent; the spiral notebook of rules.
- **Establishes:** Sergeant Cutter Pruett's authority and character — not cruel, frightened, and fear has become policy; the rules are reasonable on paper.
- **Establishes:** Selle's voice register — lists, inventories, deferred reckonings; he does not reach conclusions he has not catalogued his way to.
- **Thread: Thesis** — the infected are absent here; they are feared and the rules are designed around that fear. The horror is in the application of reason to human desperation.
- **World invariant:** The infected gradient — Month Two, no infected present. They are only feared. The checkpoint's function is predicated entirely on fear and on a fuel resource the community is protecting.
- **Note to drafter:** Selle's inner life should read like an organized desk: everything in its tray, the tray labeled, the label not questioned.
