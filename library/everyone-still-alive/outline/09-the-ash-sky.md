# Chapter 09 — The Ash Sky
*Vignette 3: The Road South · Month Six · Highway 41 · Days 4–5*

## One-Line Summary
The smelting plant's ash-sky fills Days 4–5; Lenn finds a hand-crank shortwave in a stripped house and hears the Reader of Names while Theo sleeps; the second near-death — a farmhouse shooter whose bullet misses Theo by distance.

## POV and Tense
Lenn ("the man") — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Literary-bleak / McCarthy. The ash-sky is the chapter's dominant sensory fact — it colors everything, including Lenn's interiority. The Reader of Names enters quietly and privately. The second near-death should be sudden and physical, then passed over; Lenn does not dwell; the narration does not dwell.

**Depth-marker:** *Month Six. Day Five.*

## Setting / Time
Highway 41, south of the smelting plant. Days 4–5. The ash-sky from the burned plant: orange-gray, thick, smelling of metals and old fire. A stripped house where they shelter on Day 4 night. A stretch of road with a farmhouse setback on Day 5.

## Chapter Shape
Multi-scene — Day 4 summary and night scene (the radio); Day 5 action (the farmhouse encounter).

## Ending Mode
**Literary-fade** — the chapter ends at the close of Day 5: the ash-sky beginning to thin, Theo asleep in a new shelter, the fish in his hand in sleep.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening (Day 4, compressed):** The ash-sky overhead since yesterday. The man's eyes are gritty. The road is covered in a fine gray film. It has settled on the cars they pass, on the power lines that no longer carry power. The smell is specific: not wood smoke — this is a hotter smell, with metal in it. Theo pulls his shirt collar over his nose.

2. **The road through the ash:** They do not talk much on Day 4. Theo asks once if they are going the right way. The man says south is south. Theo asks how far the ash goes. The man says he doesn't know. These are the two things said. They eat what they have, which is not much since the pack was taken — they found canned soup in the stripped house from Day 3 (half a can each) and Theo found a bag of rice in a cabinet behind the cleaning products. They have that.

3. **The stripped house, Day 4 night:** A split-level on a dead-end, just off the highway. The door locks from inside. Almost everything has been taken — the kitchen stripped to the walls, the bedrooms cleared. But in the basement: a hand-crank shortwave radio. In its case. The crank is stiff.

4. **The radio:** The man takes it upstairs. He cranks it. Static. He adjusts the frequency. Static. He sets it down. Later — after Theo is asleep — he cranks it again, and this time, just past midnight: a voice. Low, measured, of indeterminate gender. Reading names.

5. **The Reader of Names:** The man holds the radio and listens. The names are a list. Some of them sound like towns he has passed through; some of them sound like no one he has known. The voice does not pause between names; it does not say how it knows. It reads until the man's arm is tired from cranking. He stops cranking. The voice goes to static. He does not start again.

6. **What the man thinks:** He does not think it in language. His hands are on the shortwave case. He looks at Theo, asleep. He looks at the fish on the floor beside Theo's hand. He sets the radio down and does not pick it up again. This is all he does about what he heard.

7. **Day 5 — the farmhouse:** Three miles south, set back from the highway, a farmhouse with a porch. They are passing it at a distance — not approaching it, walking the highway — when a shot comes from inside. Distance: forty meters. The bullet hits the pavement to the left of Theo and short of him. The man has Theo by the shoulder and they are running before the sound has finished.

8. **The run:** They run south, off the highway, into the tree line. Another shot, from behind, farther. No pursuit. They keep running, then walking fast, then walking, a long time, before the man slows.

9. **Theo:** He is not injured. He looked back once while running, which the man told him not to do. He does not say he looked back and the man does not ask. They walk.

10. **End of Day 5:** A new shelter — a church annex with a broken lock, south of where the farmhouse was. The ash-sky is thinning as they get farther south. Inside: cold but dry. The man checks Theo — no injury, no shock-symptoms beyond a quickened heart that is already slowing. Theo is holding the fish.

11. **Chapter close:** Outside, the last of the ash-sky makes a pale stripe on the southern horizon. Theo sleeps. The fish is in his palm. The man watches the stripe of ash and does not sleep yet and thinks about the radio and the names and nothing that can be said about them.

## Outcome / Turn
The Reader of Names has entered the man's hands — briefly, privately, not shared with Theo. The second near-death has passed without injury, from a farmhouse shooter acting out of fear or desperation that we are not given access to. The turn: the man's response to the broadcast is entirely physical — he sets the radio down and does not pick it up again. Grief and the names are in the same gesture.

## New Question / Pressure
Who is shooting from abandoned farmhouses and why? Is anyone organizing anything out there, or is the radio voice one person alone? The man does not ask these questions in language.
