# Chapter 07 — The Fever
*Vignette 2: The Line · Month Two · The Narrows Checkpoint, Highway 9*

## One-Line Summary
A woman with two small children arrives; one child has a respiratory fever that triggers the protocol; Selle enforces; she walks away from the checkpoint; Selle writes the entry; the vignette ends on the completed notebook record.

## POV and Tense
Private Daan Selle, 21 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Institutional dread — at its most fully realized here. The worst thing in this chapter is not a violent act. It is a decision made procedurally, recorded accurately, and not revisited. The horror lives in the gap between the entry and what the entry means. Sentences remain medium-length and precise; the chapter does not raise its voice.

## Setting / Time
The Narrows checkpoint. Later in Month Two, several days after Ch 06. A gray mid-afternoon. The river is fully iced at the edges now.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — one event and its aftermath. Compressed time.

## Ending Mode
**Declarative-close** — the chapter ends on Selle's completed notebook entry. The entry is written out on the page. The decision is recorded accurately. The vignette ends on the closed notebook.

## Beats

1. **Opening:** An ordinary early-afternoon shift. Pruett has been at the kiosk since morning and leaves Selle in charge for an hour while he checks the eastern approach. Selle is alone at the kiosk when the woman arrives.

2. **The woman:** Arriving from the east, on foot, carrying a frame pack and a child on her back. The second child — the older one, five or six years old — is walking beside her and holding the pack strap. She has a look that Selle recognizes: the look of someone who has been moving for longer than their body has agreed to.

3. **The intake:** Selle runs the procedure. She states her name and origin (from an evacuation camp to the east — the camp has dissolved; she is not the first person from it). She states her destination: a sister in a town west of the checkpoint. She shows her hands. She is clean. The older child shows their hands. She lifts the younger child — the one on her back — off the pack.

4. **The fever:** The younger child is three. When Selle takes the child's temperature — protocol requires it for anyone under eight — the reading is 38.4°C. Above the threshold. He takes it again. 38.3°C. He notes it.

5. **What Selle does next:** He follows the protocol. He asks the mother if the child has been ill. She says yes — a chest cold from the evacuation camp, three or four days ago. The other children at the camp had it. It is a respiratory illness; the doctors at the camp said so before the camp dissolved. She says this carefully, as if she has rehearsed it for exactly this moment.

6. **Pruett returns:** Selle shows him the temperature reading. Pruett looks at it, then at the child, then at the woman. He is not cruel; this is documented here: he looks at her for a long time. He is frightened, and the rules are what he has instead of a diagnosis.

7. **The decision:** Pruett turns them back. His reasoning, spoken to the woman: the fever reading is above threshold; there is no medical resource at the checkpoint to confirm the diagnosis; the rules require a negative reading or medical attestation for crossings involving fever in children. He does not have attestation. He cannot provide it. He is sorry.

8. **The woman's response:** She does not shout. She looks at Selle, not at Pruett — perhaps because Selle is closer to her age, perhaps because Selle is the one who took the temperature. She says: it is a chest cold. She says: you know that. She says nothing after that. She picks up the younger child. The older child takes hold of the pack strap again.

9. **Selle enforces:** He holds the barrier closed. She walks back east, toward the direction she came from. He watches until she is out of sight — protocol. The road becomes empty. The eastern approach is empty.

10. **What Selle does not think:** Where she goes from here. Whether the evacuation camp is gone. Whether there is shelter on the eastern road. He does not think these things. He adds them to the inventory under *items requiring no follow-up action,* which is a category he does not examine.

11. **The notebook entry:** Selle writes it. Traveler, name, two children, origin (the dissolved camp), destination stated, temperature of younger child: 38.4°C (38.3°C confirmation reading), fever threshold exceeded, cleared protocol: TURNED BACK. He signs it. The entry is accurate. The entry does not say what will happen to her. The entry does not have a field for that.

12. **Chapter close / vignette close:** The entry is complete. Selle closes the notebook. Outside, the river carries ice. The tanker sits in its shadow. Pruett is making something on the propane burner. The vignette ends on the closed notebook.

## Outcome / Turn
The vignette's thesis is complete: the worst thing was not cruelty — it was compliance with a reasonable rule that is being used as license for a decision that may be a death sentence. The turn: the chapter ends on the decision, not its consequence. We do not see the woman again. What happens to her after she turns from the checkpoint is not shown.

## New Question / Pressure
Did the woman and her children survive? (The book refuses to answer.) What happens to Selle over the weeks and months ahead? What did the notebook entry leave out?

## Continuity Notes
- **Continuity fact confirmed:** The woman with children is turned back because of a child's fever; the fever is respiratory illness from an evacuation camp, not the infection. The characters cannot confirm this and do not try.
- **Continuity fact confirmed:** The vignette ends on a decision, not an act of violence. The decision is the worst thing.
- **Continuity fact confirmed:** Selle's compliance — not his objection — is the vignette's final note.
- **Thread: Thesis** — the thesis answer for V2 is delivered: people, using fear as license. The license is procedural; the result is the same.
