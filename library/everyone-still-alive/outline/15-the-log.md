# Chapter 15 — The Log
*Vignette 5: The Raft · Year Two, spring · Lake Ashford, south arm*

## One-Line Summary
Nadia's logbook opens the raft community; daily rhythms establish twenty people living with care and intention; the carved wooden fish is slightly crooked on the central mast; the Reader of Names arrives on the shortwave at night; Mikkel is present and unremarkable.

## POV and Tense
Nadia Ruiz, 62 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Warm communal. Nadia's voice is warm, deliberately calm, precise and affectionate in small observation. Sentences breathe. Beauty is insisted on even amid ruin — the spring light, the tomato seedlings, the children learning. The plural ("the community," "they," "the raft's people") moves alongside Nadia's close third when the scene widens. Logbook-voice paragraphs appear as texture — slightly more formal, as if Nadia is writing rather than observing.

**Hard cut from V4:** From first-person present tense, perpetrator voice, deep winter, to third-person past tense, communal warmth, spring. The floor changes completely — temperature, light, the sound of other people living well. The cut should be felt as a physical relief.

**Depth-marker:** *Year Two, spring.*

## Setting / Time
The raft on Lake Ashford's south arm. Year Two, spring — ice recently out; the light lengthening every day; mud on the floating walkways; cold light off the water. The raft: dock sections and a houseboat hull, the central telephone-pole mast, the greenhouse (spinach, tomatoes, herbs), the shortwave in its weatherproof case, Nadia's logbook in its own case bolted to the mast's base.

## Chapter Shape
Community-rhythm — spans one full day, from Nadia's morning logbook entry through night.

## Ending Mode
**Resolved-beat** — the day ends: the community is present, warm, and intact. Nadia makes the last logbook entry of the day. The chapter closes warmly.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening:** Nadia's morning logbook entry — the date (Year Two, a spring day she has numbered in the logbook), the morning count (twenty people present, the same as yesterday), the weather (overcast, clearing, the smell of lake water with cold still in it). The entry is written in logbook voice: precise, affectionate, slightly formal. This is the chapter's first texture.

2. **The morning count:** The community's first ritual — everyone accounted for at sunrise. Nadia conducts it from the mast end of the raft. She knows everyone: Gus, Petra, Tomás, and sixteen others including the raft's four children. And Mikkel — three weeks on the raft now, having arrived from the eastern shore; he is assigned to the morning count as everyone is; he stands in the usual place.

3. **The fish:** The carved wooden fish is nailed to the mast just above the logbook case, slightly crooked. Nadia has noted it in the logbook before — she notes it again today, in passing, as she opens the logbook case. She does not know its history. She notes only that it is there, slightly crooked, and that she has never moved it because something about the way it sits on the mast makes the mast look like it means something.

4. **The greenhouse:** The tomato seedlings have been transplanted and the spinach is coming in. This chapter contains the greenhouse — Nadia going in to check the progress, the smell of green in cold air, the quality of the light through the mismatched window panes.

5. **Gus:** He is on the solar array — checking the panels, adjusting a connection that has been giving a lower yield since the last hard freeze. He and Nadia exchange words about it: not romantic words, not urgent words, just the words of two people who have been living in close proximity for eight months and have developed a precise shorthand of observation.

6. **Petra and the children:** The children's class — every morning, regardless of weather. Today they are on the raft's main deck because the weather is almost good enough: a waterproof notebook, a piece of salvaged rope for knots, what Petra remembers of fourth grade and then some. The four children are from four different families, three of which are no longer intact. Nadia writes about the children in the logbook.

7. **Tomás:** He is preparing the johnboat for a supply run tomorrow. He is cheerful today — something about the spring light. He sings something while he works, quietly, not for an audience. Nadia hears it from the logbook case and writes: *T. singing while he works. A good sign.*

8. **Mikkel:** He is helping Gus with the solar array — not because he was asked, but because he saw what Gus was doing and has some knowledge (marine electrical systems overlap). Gus is accepting the help without fuss. This is how the raft works: skill offered, skill received. Mikkel is unremarkable. He does not make eye contact with Nadia when she writes about him; she does not make eye contact with him either, but she writes that he helped Gus.

9. **The community rule:** A small friction — a question about how to allocate the week's greenhouse yield when the spinach comes in all at once. This is discussed, briefly, at dinner. The community has a rule: commons first, then distribution by need. The discussion is short and resolves correctly. Someone makes a joke. The tension, if it was tension, is gone.

10. **The shortwave at night:** Nadia takes out the shortwave after the community has settled. She turns to the frequency she has memorized. The Reader of Names: low, measured, indeterminate gender. She listens and writes some names in the logbook. Not names she knows — but she writes them, because someone should. The broadcast ends (or fades); she closes the log.

11. **Chapter close:** The logbook entry closed. The day counted and recorded. The fish slightly crooked on the mast above her. The lake at night is cold and dark and very still. Nadia sits with the closed logbook and the raft makes its small sounds and she is not afraid of anything tonight.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The raft community is established: its people, its rhythms, its warmth, its rules. The fish is present and unremarked. The Reader of Names is heard. Mikkel is present and integrated. This chapter is the book's evidence that the opposite of monstrous is possible — that something good can be built.

## New Question / Pressure
(Light, not urgent.) Can the greenhouse yield keep pace with the community's needs? Who are the names the Reader of Names is reading? No threat is present.

## Continuity Notes
- **Critical continuity fact confirmed:** The carved wooden fish is nailed to the raft's central mast (telephone pole), slightly crooked, above Nadia's logbook case. No community member knows its full history.
- **Continuity fact confirmed:** The raft community has twenty people; Nadia keeps the logbook; Gus maintains systems; Petra teaches children; Tomás rows supply runs. The greenhouse grows spinach, tomatoes, herbs.
- **Continuity fact confirmed:** Mikkel is already on the raft — he arrived three weeks before the Galena appears (which is V6, Ch 18). He is present but unremarkable in this chapter.
- **Thread: Reader of Names** — heard on the raft's shortwave; Nadia transcribes names into the logbook. Third appearance in the book (after Ch 03 and Ch 09).
- **Thread: Fish** — present as a physical object on the mast. The chain of custody is complete (Jesse → Theo → stranger → raft) but no character knows it.
- **Thread: Thesis** — V5's answer to "who is the monster?" begins here: no monster; what humans can build in the absence of fear.
- **Note to drafter:** This chapter is the tonal valley of the book. The warmth must be earned and specific — not sentimental. Nadia's logbook voice should be audible as a distinct texture, slightly more formal than her close third.
