# Chapter 21 — The Canoe
*Vignette 6: The Derelict · Year Two, spring · Lake Ashford · Day 4*

## One-Line Summary
Day 4: Mikkel's canoe is spotted drifting empty to the southeast; no body, no way to know; the community goes inside and does not discuss it; Nadia and Gus's last honest exchange before the ship arrives; by nightfall the Galena fills the eastern sky.

## POV and Tense
Nadia Ruiz, 62 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Slow-dread maritime, sentences now measurably shorter than in V5. The chapter is interiority-inflected — there is less external action; the dread is in observation and what observation cannot tell you. The Galena is present in every east-facing moment. Nadia's logbook entries should now be almost clipped — recording facts without completing thoughts.

**Depth-marker:** *Year Two, spring. Day Four.*

## Setting / Time
The raft. Day 4. The Galena is close enough to see detail — the hull line, the rust at the waterline — from the raft's eastern railing with binoculars.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene / interiority-heavy — one long day on the raft, mostly observation and reckoning, with two key beats: the canoe spotted empty, and Nadia and Gus.

## Ending Mode
**Turn** — by nightfall, the Galena has grown large enough to block the eastern stars when Nadia stands at the eastern railing. This is not an action; it is a fact of scale. The chapter ends on it.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening — Day 4 logbook entry:** Five sentences. The time. The weather. The Galena's updated position. The community's status. Done. This is the shortest opening entry Nadia has written since she started the log. She notes this — one sentence: *I am writing less. I do not know why.*

2. **The Galena at dawn, Day 4:** Now visible in detail through binoculars. Gus estimates it is two to three miles east and still drifting. The hull is dark with rust and age; the name GALENA still legible at the bow in faded paint; the anchor chains are visible hanging at the bow, corroded but present. No lights. No movement. No smoke. No sound.

3. **The community's silence:** The split from the meeting has not resolved. Petra and Tomás are not in conflict — they have not said further words on the subject of the vote — but they occupy different ends of the raft. Nadia observes this. The community is quieter than it has been.

4. **Tomás spots the canoe:** Late morning. He is in the second johnboat, having rowed south for a short supply check along the near shore, when he sees it — Mikkel's canoe, drifting, empty, facing south-southeast. He rows to it. No supplies in it, no paddle; both gone. No blood. No sign of Mikkel. He tows the canoe back to the raft.

5. **The community receives this:** The canoe is tied to the raft's stern. People look at it. No one speaks much. Nadia writes: *The canoe. Tomás found it empty. No sign. We do not know.* She does not finish the sentence.

6. **What they do not say:** No one articulates what the canoe might mean: that Mikkel capsized, that he was pulled in, that he swam to shore, that he left the canoe deliberately. Every possibility exists. None of them are spoken. The community goes back to their tasks.

7. **Gus and Nadia:** He finds her at the mast end, afternoon. He sits with her. He says — not as comfort, but as statement — that he did not vote against Mikkel. She knows this. She writes in the logbook that he said this and that she knows it. He says: *It doesn't change what happened.* She says: *No.* He says: *I helped him with the canoe.* She says: *I know.* He does not say anything after that for a long time. Nadia writes: *I do not have language for what this is. I have never not had language.*

8. **Afternoon — the Galena closer:** Gus measures again: the ship is approximately one mile east. Tomorrow it will be here — or it will be close enough to board, or it will pass directly over the raft's position if the raft stays where it is.

9. **The final decision:** Petra says they should go south. Tomás says they stay. The community looks at Nadia. She is not a commander; she has never been a commander; she keeps the logbook. She says: *Write down what you want to do. All of you. I'll write what the logbook says.* They do not do this. The community decides by not deciding: they stay. The logistics of moving twenty people in two johnboats south, without Mikkel's canoe being available, are too difficult. They stay.

10. **Evening:** The Galena is close enough now that its hull is visible from the raft without binoculars. The superstructure at its stern is a dark shape against the eastern sky. No lights. The east wind is steady. The ship is not stopping.

11. **Chapter close:** Nadia stands at the eastern railing at night. The Galena is large enough, at its current distance, to obscure a section of the eastern stars. She stands there and writes nothing — the logbook is in the shelter. She is just standing. The ship fills the east. The fish is on the mast behind her, slightly crooked. She can hear the lake against the raft's hull. The chapter ends.

## Outcome / Turn
Mikkel's canoe drifts empty — his fate unknown, unknowable with what the raft has. The community has defaulted to staying. The Galena is one day from arrival. The turn: the ship is now large enough to block stars — this physical fact enacts the dread's full arrival.

## New Question / Pressure
What is on the ship? What did the community's decision do? Can Nadia find language for what cannot be undone?

## Continuity Notes
- **Establishes:** Mikkel's canoe found drifting empty, south-southeast of the raft. No sign of Mikkel. His fate is not determined and is not resolved in this book.
- **Establishes:** The community's final decision: to stay (by default — the logistics of moving twenty people in two johnboats without the canoe are too difficult).
- **Continuity confirmed:** Nadia's logbook sentences now at their shortest — almost clipped; the register is "the same house with the walls closing."
- **Thread: Thesis** — the thesis's final instance is complete: the community has done its worst before knowing what the ship carries. The canoe's emptiness is the human cost of that decision, held open and unresolved.
