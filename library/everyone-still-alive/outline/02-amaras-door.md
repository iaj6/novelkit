# Chapter 02 — Amara's Door
*Vignette 1: The Drill · Week One · Day 1, mid-morning through early afternoon*

## One-Line Summary
The art room holds for hours; then Amara hears crying at the stairwell door and opens it; the infected flood the upper floor; Amara does not survive; Jesse and three students retreat to a supply closet.

## POV and Tense
Jesse Okafor, 15 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Fast YA horror. This chapter delivers the vignette's thesis-turn: the worst act is a human choice made from compassion. The register must make Amara's decision feel inevitable and human — not stupid, not villainous. Short sentences, sensory immediacy, Jesse's perception filtering everything.

## Setting / Time
The third-floor art room, then briefly the corridor and stairwell door, then the supply closet off the art room. Day 1, hours after the initial lockdown.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — the long wait in the art room, Amara's decision, the breach, the retreat.

## Ending Mode
**Mid-action-cut** — Jesse and three survivors are barricaded in the supply closet; the sounds outside have changed; the chapter ends inside the emergency without resolving it.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening:** The art room, hours in. The handle no longer moves. No one has opened the door. The nine people have arranged themselves: some are on the floor, some watching windows, Ms. Kwan has put her head down. Jesse has been looking at a broken stool he has not touched. Amara has been listening.

2. Sounds from the corridor have been intermittent and then absent for a long stretch. Jesse tells himself this is good. His body does not believe it.

3. At the stairwell end of the corridor (heard through the art room wall): a sound that is not the wrong-fast sound from before. It is a person crying. Specifically: the sound of a person who has been crying for a long time and is now at the end of it. Amara hears it. She says someone is out there.

4. Jesse says don't. He says it clearly. He has not moved. *He knows, and he knows she knows, and it doesn't matter.* The crying does not sound like anything but a person.

5. The group discussion is not long. Ms. Kwan says they cannot know. The crying continues. Amara has already moved toward the stairwell door — the door at the far end of the corridor that they propped open behind them when they ran in. She goes toward it.

6. Jesse follows. He cannot not follow. He is not brave; he cannot leave a person.

7. **The door:** Amara opens the stairwell door. For a second: a girl — or what was a girl — in the stairwell landing, crumpled, quiet. Then the stairwell is loud with something above them coming down.

8. The corridor fractures. Jesse's perception: fragments, physical, fast. Amara at the door. The sound descending. Jesse's hand on her arm. The stairwell opening. Amara pushed or pulled or— the chapter does not linger on this. Jesse is running back toward the art room with two students he grabbed without planning to grab them.

9. The art room door is closed. Ms. Kwan has a student against it. Jesse hammers. The door opens. They get in. Jesse looks back once.

10. Amara is not there.

11. They cannot hold the art room. The stairwell is open; the corridor has what the corridor has now. Jesse takes three people — the two he grabbed, one more — into the art room's supply closet. Paint cans, rolled canvas. He pulls the door shut.

12. **Chapter close:** The sounds outside the closet door are specific and close and Jesse does not catalog them. He is making no sound. Beside him, someone's phone screen is at 3% battery. It is the only light.

---

## Outcome / Turn
Amara is dead. The thesis is delivered: she opened the door because a person was crying, and it was the most human possible choice, and it cost her life and changed the floor. Jesse is alive and in a supply closet with three other survivors. The infected are now on the third floor.

## New Question / Pressure
How long before the closet is found? What happened to Ms. Kwan and the others?

## Continuity Notes
- **Continuity fact confirmed:** Amara opens the stairwell door; the infected enter the upper floors through this act.
- **Continuity fact confirmed:** Amara does not survive. This happens mid-vignette, not at the end — leaving Jesse to act without her.
- **Establishes:** The supply closet off the art room — small, stacked with paint cans and rolled canvas.
- **Thread: Thesis** — first instance of the central argument: compassion, not failure or cruelty, opens the door. The worst act in the chapter is human and motivated by love.
- **Thread: Fish** — the broken stool Jesse has been looking at is the source of the splinter he will carve in Ch 03. It should be present in this chapter as a background object — present but not yet meaningful.
- **Reader of Names:** foreshadowed — "someone's phone screen is at 3% battery" is the last beat; this is the phone that will pick up the broadcast in Ch 03.
- **Note to drafter:** Do not linger on Amara's death as spectacle. Jesse does not see it happen — he sees her at the door and then he is running. What she cost is felt in the space where she is not, not in a described death.
