# Chapter 06 — Criteria
*Vignette 2: The Line · Month Two · The Narrows Checkpoint, Highway 9*

## One-Line Summary
A harder shift: three crossings, two turned back, including a man with a cough; Pruett tells Selle he is good at this because he does not flinch; Selle writes the entry and does not think about the man after the notebook is closed.

## POV and Tense
Private Daan Selle, 21 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Institutional dread. The chapter deepens the proceduralism: the criteria are applied to harder cases. Selle's inner inventory begins to include items he has not yet totaled. The horror is in the precision of his language — every entry is complete, every entry is accurate, and every entry is also something else.

## Setting / Time
The Narrows checkpoint. A different shift, several days after Ch 05. Cold deepening; ice heavier in the Ashford River.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — one shift. Compressed time.

## Ending Mode
**Declarative-close** — the chapter ends with Selle's log entry for the day: complete, accurate, closed.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening:** Selle arrives at the kiosk to relieve the overnight watch — a man named Devlin, one of two others at the checkpoint. Devlin reports three overnight attempts, none successful. Selle takes over the log. The morning is overcast; the Ashford River moves dark and fast below the bridge.

2. **First crossing of the shift:** A family — mother, teenage son, elderly man (the son's grandfather). They are heading west to the town beyond the checkpoint. The son shows his hands; the grandfather cannot raise his left arm (old injury, the mother explains, farm equipment). Selle checks the arm: no wound visible, no smell, temperature normal. He logs the crossing. He lets them pass. This is the checkpoint working correctly.

3. **Selle's interiority after:** He notes that he made a judgment call on the grandfather's arm — the rules say visible wounds are a flag, but the wound is old and documented by the family's own testimony. He has logged it as "cleared, non-recent injury, family attestation." He has followed the spirit of the rule. He has filed this under *correct decisions* in his internal inventory.

4. **Second crossing:** A man traveling alone, thirties, has a cough. Not persistent — once or twice during the intake procedure. The man says it is dust from the road. His temperature is borderline (37.9°C; the threshold is 38.0°C). He is otherwise unremarkable. He states a destination. He shows his hands. Pruett is watching.

5. **The decision on the man with the cough:** Pruett makes it, after a pause. Not fever — borderline. But the cough. The criteria say: respiratory symptoms combined with elevated temperature constitute a flag requiring secondary review, and secondary review requires a waiting period the checkpoint does not have protocol for. Pruett turns the man back. The man does not argue. He looks at Selle. Selle holds the man's gaze and says nothing. The man turns and walks east.

6. **What Selle does not think:** He does not think about where the man with the cough will go. He does not think about whether the cough was dust or something else. He notes in the inventory: these are decisions the rules require; the rules were written for situations like this. He files this correctly and moves on.

7. **Third crossing:** A woman, alone, approved without incident. She is heading to a settlement Selle has not heard of. She thanks him. He logs her passage. He does not note the thank-you.

8. **Pruett's comment:** In the afternoon, going over the log, Pruett reads the entry on the man with the cough and nods. Then he says — not as assessment, not as criticism, simply as observation — that Selle is good at this because he doesn't flinch. Other people flinch. Flinching makes it harder. Selle doesn't flinch.

9. **Selle's interiority on this:** He adds it to the inventory. Under *assessments received*. He does not examine whether being praised for not flinching is the same as being good at the job. He will examine this later. He has not yet examined it.

10. **End of shift:** Selle makes the log entry. He writes the family (passed, grandfather's arm noted), the man with the cough (turned back, respiratory symptoms, borderline temperature), the woman (passed). He records the CB radio check. He closes the notebook.

11. **Chapter close:** He does not think about the man with the cough. This is not because he has dismissed him — it is because the notebook is closed, the entry is complete, and the entry does not require follow-up. The shift is over. He goes to the tent.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The criteria are applied to harder cases and they hold — procedurally. The turn: Selle has accepted praise for not flinching as if it were a compliment about skill rather than a description of what he is losing. He has not examined this yet. The chapter ends before he does.

## New Question / Pressure
What happened to the man with the cough? (Not asked.) What does "not flinching" cost over time?

## Continuity Notes
- **Establishes:** The checkpoint's secondary-review protocol (or lack of it) — the system creates a gap that will matter in Ch 07.
- **Establishes:** "Don't flinch" as the checkpoint's unofficial virtue — Pruett's standard, Selle's adopted standard.
- **Establishes:** Additional checkpoint personnel: Devlin (overnight watch, unnamed further).
- **Thread: Thesis** — the chapter deepens the argument: people using fear as license — but the license is granted by procedure, and the procedure is reasonable on paper. The horror is the gap between the rule's text and its application.
- **Note to drafter:** The man with the cough should not be made sympathetic in a way that signals to the reader how to feel. He should be specific — his cough, his borderline temperature, his face when Pruett turns him back — and the reader should not know if the cough was the infection or dust. The chapter should refuse to tell them.
