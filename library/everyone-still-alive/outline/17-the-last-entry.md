# Chapter 17 — The Last Entry
*Vignette 5: The Raft · Year Two, spring · Lake Ashford*

## One-Line Summary
A near-perfect day: Petra's children's class on the deck, Tomás singing, the tomato seedlings flourishing, Mikkel woven into the raft's routines; the Reader of Names at night; Nadia closes her logbook; the warmth is at its highest point before the dread begins.

## POV and Tense
Nadia Ruiz, 62 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Warm communal at its peak. This chapter is the book's tonal valley — the warmth must be earned, specific, and present in specific sensory detail, not asserted. The Reader of Names at night should feel like a coda, not a threat. The chapter's last image is warm and quiet; the dread that follows it in Ch 18 should feel like a floor dropping.

**Depth-marker:** *Year Two, spring.* (The last day of V5; the same week as V6.)

## Setting / Time
The raft. A specific spring day — good weather, longer light, the lake at rest. This is the last chapter before the Galena is sighted. **The Galena does not appear in this chapter.** There is no threat, no shadow on the horizon.

## Chapter Shape
Community-rhythm — one full day. Multiple overlapping scenes, all on the raft.

## Ending Mode
**Literary-fade** — Nadia closes her logbook and goes to sleep. The raft is at rest. The light is gone but not threatening. The last sentence is peaceful.

## Beats

1. **Opening:** The morning count, twenty present. Nadia's logbook entry for this morning: she writes about the light — how it comes onto the water now in spring, a specific quality she has tried to describe before and not quite gotten. She tries again today. She gets closer.

2. **Petra's class on the deck:** Good enough weather to take the children outside. Four children and Petra: a rope lesson (practical), then something from the waterproof notebook (a chapter on birds, because one of the children asked about the birds that came back this week). The children identify a bird. The bird's name goes into the logbook.

3. **Tomás:** He is singing while he patches the johnboat's interior paint — not loudly, something with a slow rhythm. Nadia hears it from the mast end of the raft. She writes: *T. singing again. The raft sounds like it has a heartbeat today.*

4. **The greenhouse:** The tomatoes are responding well. Nadia and Mikkel are both in the greenhouse at the same time, each for different reasons — she to check the moisture levels, he to adjust a frame joint that has been pulling. This is the chapter's moment of Mikkel at full integration: he is useful, he is present, he is no different from anyone else on the raft on this day. They work side by side. They discuss the tomatoes. He has opinions about the watering schedule that turn out to be well-informed.

5. **Gus and the solar array:** Good output today — the sun at its best angle. Gus comes to find Nadia mid-afternoon to tell her the array is producing at capacity for the first time this season. He says this without fuss but she hears the satisfaction in it. She writes it down.

6. **The community at dinner:** All twenty eating together — this doesn't always happen, but today it does. Something about the day has gathered people. The food is not extraordinary (beans, the last of the dried greens, a small portion of fresh spinach) but it is eaten with something that resembles contentment.

7. **After dinner, Nadia's logbook:** She writes the day's entry. It is longer than usual. She finds herself writing about the community as *evidence* — using that word, crossing it out, writing it back in. Evidence of what is possible. Evidence that the opposite of the worst things is also possible. She does not write it as a thesis statement; she writes it as observation, as the logbook's job.

8. **The shortwave at night:** The Reader of Names. Nadia has been listening for two years; she knows the broadcast's rhythm — the names arrive at the same pace every night, the voice never changes. Tonight she recognizes one name: a woman from a town two hundred miles south, a name that means something to a community member (not Nadia) who is asleep and doesn't hear it. Nadia writes the name in the logbook.

9. **The logbook's last entry of the day:** She writes about the fish, briefly — looking at it as she closes the case. She writes that the fish is slightly crooked and has always been slightly crooked and she has never moved it and will not.

10. **Chapter close / vignette close:** Nadia puts away the shortwave. She closes the logbook. She goes to sleep in the raft's main shelter. The lake is still; the night is cold but not cruelly cold; the mast is visible through the shelter's small window against the sky. The fish on the mast. The logbook in its case below it. The chapter ends here.
