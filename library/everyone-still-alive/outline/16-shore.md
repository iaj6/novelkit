# Chapter 16 — Shore
*Vignette 5: The Raft · Year Two, spring · Lake Ashford*

## One-Line Summary
Nadia joins Tomás on the supply run to the lakeshore; the shore reveals the larger world's silence; back on the raft a small friction over food allocation is resolved through the community's values; Gus and Nadia share a wordless evening.

## POV and Tense
Nadia Ruiz, 62 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Warm communal — moving out to the lakeshore and back. The shore is not threatening, but it is honest about the world beyond the raft; it carries evidence that the world has gotten darker while the raft has gotten lighter. The chapter does not flinch from this but does not let it capsize the warmth.

**Depth-marker:** *Year Two, spring.* (A few days after Ch 15.)

## Setting / Time
The raft, then the aluminum johnboat on Lake Ashford, then the lakeshore (stripped marina, evidence of fire, silence). Then back to the raft for the afternoon and evening.

## Chapter Shape
Multi-scene — the supply run (raft to shore and back) plus the community friction and evening.

## Ending Mode
**Literary-fade** — the chapter ends with Nadia and Gus on the raft's deck at evening, the lake light fading, the logbook open and not yet written in.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening:** Morning on the raft. Nadia has decided to go with Tomás on the supply run rather than stay. She doesn't do this often — the logbook needs her, the raft needs observation. But the shore is a specific distance away and she wants to have looked at it recently. This is how she thinks about it: *I want to have looked at it.*

2. **The johnboat:** Tomás rows; Nadia sits in the bow. The lake in spring — the water color, the temperature of the air over the water, the way the far shore is sometimes visible and sometimes not. The light is longer now and the cold is in the morning only.

3. **The marina:** Their regular landing point: an old recreation marina, a quarter mile south of the main town. The dock is half-collapsed; they tie up to what holds. The marina has been stripped — the boat storage is empty, the shop is gutted — but there is always something: this time, fishing line (a partial spool in a drawer), a length of marine rope, two anchor shackles. Tomás puts them in the pack.

4. **What the shore shows:** Burn marks on the lakeshore north of the marina — a large fire, months ago; the black is weathered now but specific. Something happened there that Nadia does not know the details of. She notes it in the small notebook she carries when she leaves the logbook on the raft. She does not know if it was a camp, a building, a deliberate fire.

5. **The town's silence:** They walk one block inland to a hardware store they have checked before (the crew was here before them — the shelves are stripped). But the silence of the town in spring is different from the silence of winter: the birds have returned. Nadia notes this. Tomás hears the birds too and says: *Spring birds.* He says it as if it explains something. She thinks maybe it does.

6. **The row back:** The lake is calm; Tomás rows; Nadia watches the raft growing larger on the south arm. She can see the mast from a quarter mile. She can see the fish on the mast — just the shape of it, slightly crooked. She does not think about this except to note it.

7. **The community friction:** Back on the raft: the spinach came in all at once — more than expected, more than the community can eat before it turns. A question about storage and a question about whether to give priority to the children or to distribute equally. Two people have a real disagreement, briefly heated. The community's rule applies: commons first, need second, equality of distribution absent pressing need. The heated part resolves. Someone suggests they eat a lot of spinach tonight. This is laughed at in a specific way — the way people laugh when the community is functioning.

8. **Mikkel's role in the friction:** He offers a practical solution — a way to dry-preserve the spinach using the greenhouse's excess heat that no one had thought to use for this purpose. The suggestion is accepted. This is Mikkel at his best: a skill, offered, received. Nadia writes it in the logbook.

9. **The evening:** After dinner, Gus and Nadia are on the aft deck. He has a tin of something hot — the camp stove's last use of the day. They sit together without sitting close. They do not discuss anything important. The light goes off the lake. The logbook is beside her, open.

10. **Chapter close:** Nadia has not written the evening entry yet. She looks at the logbook, open to a blank page. Gus finishes his tin. He says goodnight in some specific and quiet way. She watches the lake after he goes in. It is dark and still and smells of cold water and the faint green smell of the lake's own spring life beginning. She has not written anything but the blank page is there.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The community is seen from outside (the shore's evidence) and from inside (the spinach friction). Both views confirm it is functioning. The turn is quiet: the blank logbook page at the end of a day that has no crisis to record. The community's goodness is not in absence of conflict but in the way conflict resolves.

## New Question / Pressure
What made those burn marks on the shore? (Not a plot question — a residual note of the world's darkness.) How long can the raft's warmth hold?
