# Chapter 03 — The Night and the Names
*Vignette 1: The Drill · Week One · Day 1 night through Day 2 predawn*

## One-Line Summary
In the supply closet through the long night, Jesse carves the wooden fish from a broken stool splinter while his mind refuses to panic; someone's dying phone picks up the Reader of Names; by predawn Jesse has a plan.

## POV and Tense
Jesse Okafor, 15 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Fast YA horror, but the chapter breathes slightly slower than Ch 01–02 — the night is the pause inside the emergency. Still short declaratives; physical interiority. The horror is present but muffled by walls and exhaustion. The carving is the chapter's structural spine — it gives Jesse's hands something while his mind refuses to reach for what just happened to Amara.

## Setting / Time
The supply closet off the art room, third floor. Day 1 night through Day 2 predawn. October. Cold in the school by now — the heat is off or the building's systems have failed.

## Chapter Shape
Bottle — one room, near-real time, the long wait. Interiority-inflected but physical throughout.

## Ending Mode
**Cliffhanger** — Jesse cracks the closet door at predawn to confirm the corridor is clear enough to move. Something is in the hall. The chapter ends before he knows what.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening:** The supply closet. Paint cans stacked. Rolled canvas against one wall. Four people: Jesse and three students (two boys and a girl — unnamed in text; their names are given once in dialogue, incidentally). The phone screen has gone to 2% and then 1%.

2. The sounds from outside have changed over hours — the fast wrong movement has been replaced by something less directional, more scattered. Jesse does not know if this is better. He decides to treat it as better.

3. Jesse finds the broken stool in the closet (from a stack of art-room surplus junk). He takes a splinter from the broken leg — not a plan, just his hands needing to move. He has a folding knife in his pocket (his father's; not a weapon, just a tool he always carries). He begins to carve. The other students are either sleeping or pretending to sleep.

4. **The fish:** Jesse does not plan it as a fish — it becomes one because of the shape the wood wants. Palm-sized. He runs his thumb over it in the dark. His mind will not go toward Amara; the fish keeps it from going. He knows this is what the fish is for and he does not think about it.

5. The phone screen: 1%. One of the other students (the girl — not named in narration, just "the girl with the phone") holds it up. A signal — weak. Something on the radio app. A voice: low, measured, of indeterminate gender. Reading names. Slowly. Without commentary. Just names.

6. Jesse does not recognize the names. The other students are awake now. They listen. The voice continues. The battery reaches 0%; the screen goes dark mid-name. The name stops half-spoken.

7. Silence. The four students sit in the silence that follows a broadcast cutting out. Jesse's hands are still on the fish.

8. Through the building: no sounds. An hour, maybe two. Jesse establishes in his head what he knows: three people with him. Closet has one entrance. The service exit is in the basement. The loading dock is on the building's south side.

9. **Coach Briggs:** Through the night, once, they hear Briggs's voice from far below — giving orders to someone. They cannot hear what. The cafeteria is in the basement. The loading dock is near the cafeteria. Jesse files this. He does not know yet what Briggs's decisions cost.

10. **By predawn:** Jesse tells the others the plan: they move now, before light, before the temperature in the building changes (he has a half-formed idea that the infected are drawn to heat; he does not know if this is true). Basement. Service exit. The street.

11. Jesse cracks the closet door. The corridor. Dark. Still. He listens. Then: a sound to his left — not the wrong-fast sound, a slower movement. Something in the hall.

12. **Chapter close:** Jesse does not close the door. He waits. The sound does not come closer. It does not go farther. He has a splinter of wood in his hand that is almost a fish.

---

## Outcome / Turn
Jesse has a plan and the will to execute it. The fish is made. The Reader of Names has been heard — the first of the book's connective threads enters, briefly, in the dark. The chapter ends before Jesse moves, with something unknown in the hall.

## New Question / Pressure
Is the hall clear enough to move? What is in the hall?

## Continuity Notes
- **Critical continuity fact established:** Jesse Okafor carves the wooden fish from a broken chair-leg splinter during the school lockdown. The fish is palm-sized. This is the origin of the connective object that will travel the rest of the book.
- **Thread: Reader of Names — first appearance.** Heard on a dying phone (not Jesse's — "the girl with the phone"), battery at 3% → 1% → 0%. The voice is low, measured, indeterminate gender. It reads names without commentary and cuts off mid-name. The question of who is broadcasting is not raised — it is simply heard, and it is the only human voice from outside the building they receive.
- **Thread: Fish** — the fish is present in this chapter as a physical object being made. It is not symbolic in Jesse's hands; it is a tool for keeping his hands busy.
- **Coach Briggs:** Present in the chapter as a distant authoritative voice from below. His decisions (locking the cafeteria, refusing window signals) are not yet fully visible. Something in this chapter should suggest, without confirming, that the cafeteria situation is complicated.
- **World invariant:** The infected may be drawn to heat — Jesse has this half-formed idea. This is character inference, not narrated fact. The narration does not confirm or deny it.
