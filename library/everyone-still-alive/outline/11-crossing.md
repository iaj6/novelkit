# Chapter 11 — Crossing
*Vignette 3: The Road South · Month Six · A river crossing on Highway 41 · Days 11–13*

## One-Line Summary
Lenn and Theo reach a river crossing; at the far bank Theo gives the carved fish to a stranger with a cart heading toward the lake settlements; Lenn watches and does not stop him; they walk south; the fish is gone.

## POV and Tense
Lenn ("the man") — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Literary-bleak / McCarthy. The chapter is the vignette's close and its thesis-moment: Theo's act of generosity is intact; the man's is not. The language should find its fullest McCarthy cadence here — long sentence, cold landscape, the fish moving away in someone else's hands and the road continuing.

**Depth-marker:** *Month Six. Day Thirteen.*

## Setting / Time
Highway 41, approaching and crossing a river (a tributary; unnamed; navigable by bridge). Days 11–13. The snow has eased; the cold has not. The bridge is functional; a small cluster of people at the crossing-point on the far bank, waiting their turn in both directions.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — the river crossing and its aftermath.

## Ending Mode
**Literary-fade** — the chapter ends on the road south of the crossing, the fish gone, the man walking, the boy beside him. No resolution of the larger arc — only the conclusion of the fish's passage through this vignette.

## Beats

1. **Opening (Days 11–12, compressed):** The road narrows approaching the river; the highway runs through flat country and the bridge is visible from a long way off. They have been walking faster since the barn encounter. What they have recovered in two days: a can of beans, a jar of peanut butter found in a car's glove compartment, three crackers from a box under a truck seat. This is not enough. The man calculates anyway.

2. **Day 13 — the bridge:** A concrete span over a tributary of a larger system — not the Ashford, but similar. The bridge is maybe a hundred meters long. On the western (far) side of the bridge: a knot of people. Four or five, their gear visible, waiting or resting. On the eastern (this) side: nothing, just the road and them.

3. **The crossing:** They walk onto the bridge. Halfway across, the man checks behind them: empty. He checks ahead: the people on the far side are watching but not moving toward them. This is what it is now: every approach is a calculation. They reach the far side.

4. **The people on the far bank:** Four, perhaps five. A couple with a cart — the cart is loaded with tools, a sleeping bag, jars, a tarp folded very small. A man alone, older. A younger woman. They nod at each other — the road's version of courtesy.

5. **The woman with the cart:** She is heading northeast — not south. She says it, unprompted: she is going toward the lake settlements. Someone told her about a community on the water. She is going to see if it's real.

6. **Theo and the fish:** Theo has had the fish in his pocket since Day 3. Now, watching the woman adjust the cart's load for the road ahead, he takes it out. He holds it for a moment. He crosses to her and holds it out.

7. **The exchange:** She looks at it, then at him. She takes it without asking what it is. She does not say thank you in so many words — she puts it in her coat pocket and nods. This is the exchange. It is complete.

8. **The man:** He watches. He does not stop Theo, and he does not know, afterward, why he did not stop him. It was not a valuable object — not food, not tool. He watches the fish go into her pocket and does not feel anything he can name. He tells himself this. Theo comes back to his side.

9. **The woman with the cart and the fish** turns north, toward the lake settlements. They do not watch her go for long. The man has the south road in his mind, and the south road does not wait.

10. **What Theo says:** Nothing. What the man says: nothing. They walk south from the bridge. The river falls behind them. The cold is the same cold.

11. **What the man thinks:** He thinks that the boy's generosity is intact. He does not know what to do with this fact. It does not feel like something the road has not taken from him yet; it feels like something the road cannot reach in the boy. This distinction matters and the man will not examine it further.

12. **Chapter close / vignette close:** The road south. The man's footprints. The boy's beside them. The bridge out of sight behind the last tree line. The fish is going toward the lake and they are going south and these are two different directions and they will not meet again on any road either of them will walk.

## Outcome / Turn
The fish departs the vignette in the hands of a woman heading toward the lake settlements — heading toward the raft where it will eventually be nailed to the mast. Theo's trust in generosity is intact; the man's is not; this is the vignette's final moral note. The arc closes: they walked south, they nearly died three times, no infected anywhere, and the worst of what they encountered was other people.

## New Question / Pressure
Where does the fish go? (The reader will learn in V5.) What south means for Lenn and Theo — we are not told; the vignette ends here.

- **Vignette arc complete:** Three near-deaths from other people, no infected, the road's full moral weight rendered. The vignette's thesis answer: people, purely.
- **Thread: Fish** — departs the V3 cast here; next appearance is in V5 (Ch 15), on the mast.
- **Thread: Thesis** — Theo's generosity as the chapter's counterweight: the boy who finds things and gives them away is the vignette's evidence that something survives the road intact.
- **Note to drafter:** The woman with the cart should not be given a name or a backstory. She is a specific physical presence — the cart's load, her coat, her practical nod at Theo — and she departs. The fish goes with her. The reader knows where it is going; the characters do not.
