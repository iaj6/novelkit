# Chapter 19 — The Eastern Shore
*Vignette 6: The Derelict · Year Two, spring · Lake Ashford · Day 2*

## One-Line Summary
Day 2 of the Galena's approach; someone notes Mikkel arrived from the eastern shore; the murmur of connection begins; Petra argues for loading the boats and leaving; Tomás argues for staying; Nadia's logbook sentences grow shorter.

## POV and Tense
Nadia Ruiz, 62 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Slow-dread maritime, tightening. The warm register is present but the Galena is inside it now. Sentences have not yet shortened dramatically — that comes in Ch 20–21 — but they have lost the breath of V5. The logbook entries in this chapter should feel slightly more clipped than in Ch 15–17. The dread is in the observation: Nadia is watching the community the way she watches the eastern horizon.

**Depth-marker:** *Year Two, spring. Day Two.*

## Setting / Time
The raft. Day 2. The Galena is closer — closer enough to be measured against the prior day's position. It is still on the eastern horizon but occupies more of it.

## Chapter Shape
Single scene — a day of observation and talk. The community's temperature changes.

## Ending Mode
**Other (dread-seep)** — the chapter ends without a clean break. The community is still functioning; no event has resolved or crystallized. Nadia's logbook entry is shorter than yesterday's. The temperature in the room has changed, and the chapter ends inside that change.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening — Day 2 logbook entry:** Nadia writes the entry. The time, the bearing, the new estimated distance. The entry is slightly shorter than yesterday's. She notes this without noting that she notes it.

2. **The Galena at dawn:** Closer. Not dramatically — but measurably, against landmarks. Gus measures it: the ship is drifting approximately one-third of a mile per hour on the current east wind. At this rate, it will reach the raft's position in two to three days. He states this plainly to whoever is near him. It is plain. The community receives it plainly.

3. **The morning discussion:** Petra, Tomás, Gus, and four or five others at the mast end of the raft. Petra's position: the small boats can carry all twenty people in relays; they should load essential supplies and go south, now, before the ship arrives. Tomás's position: go south to what? The winter camps to the south are stripped; the lake settlements to the north are more viable but in the direction of the ship. Stay, assess, do not make the decision that uses all your resources before you know what you're deciding about.

4. **The community holds both positions:** No vote yet. Both positions are held by people the community trusts. Nadia writes: *P. wants to move. T. wants to stay. Both are right about something. We have not decided.*

5. **Someone notes Mikkel:** Not dramatically, and it is important that it is not dramatic. One of the unnamed community members — while they are looking east at the Galena through binoculars — says, without accusation: *Mikkel came from the east. From the eastern shore.* This is a fact. It is noted.

6. **The murmur:** The statement is not responded to directly. But it stays in the air. Nadia writes it in the logbook: *The eastern shore. Mikkel came from the eastern shore. This was said today. I am writing that it was said.* This is all she writes on the subject today.

7. **Mikkel:** He is not present for the discussion at the mast end. He is working — on the second johnboat's motor, which has been running roughly. When he comes to the main deck later, no one says anything different to him than they would have said yesterday. But Nadia watches the room and notes that the room has changed by a degree she cannot quantify.

8. **Petra and Mikkel:** Petra says nothing to Mikkel about the ship or about his origin. She is occupied with the children — the morning class happened, shorter than usual because the children want to watch the ship. She redirected them to the bird book. This is Petra functioning. Nadia writes: *Petra is functioning. The children are functioning. This is still enough.*

9. **Gus and Nadia, afternoon:** He is measuring the ship's drift rate again. She stands beside him. He says: the community will have to decide in the next twenty-four hours if they're going to go south. After that, the timing gets harder. She writes this down. He looks at her writing it and does not say anything about the act of writing it down.

10. **Evening — the Galena:** It is visible now without binoculars. Not clearly, but as a mass against the eastern sky as the light goes. It has not appeared on the western horizon; it is not coming for them in any directed sense. It is coming because the wind is coming and the ship is on the wind. Nadia knows this and also knows that the distinction between directed and undirected does not help.

11. **Chapter close:** Nadia's evening logbook entry. It is the shortest entry since she started keeping the log — five or six sentences. She records the position, the temperature, the community's status, and Mikkel's origin being noted. Then she closes the book. The chapter ends on the closed book and the ship on the water.

## Outcome / Turn
The murmur has begun. The community's temperature has shifted. No event has crystallized the suspicion — just a fact stated plainly, and the fact staying in the air. Petra and Tomás have staked positions. No decision has been made.

## New Question / Pressure
When will the community vote? What happens to Mikkel if the murmur becomes accusation?

## Continuity Notes
- **Establishes:** The murmur — the connection between Mikkel's eastern-shore origin and the Galena's eastern approach — noted in the logbook without interpretation.
- **Establishes:** Petra vs. Tomás positions on departure — leave (Petra) vs. stay (Tomás). This split will persist through Ch 20–21.
- **Continuity confirmed:** Nadia's logbook register visibly tightening — this chapter's entries shorter than previous chapters'. The drafter should make this measurable in the text.
- **Thread: Thesis** — the community beginning to turn on Mikkel before they know anything about the ship: the thesis's final instance is in preparation.
- **Note to drafter:** The statement about Mikkel's eastern origin should be made by an unnamed community member, casually, without intent to accuse — it is a fact observed aloud. The horror is in how facts become accusations without any single person choosing to accuse. Nadia's logbook entry recording it should feel like recording evidence she doesn't want to have to use.
