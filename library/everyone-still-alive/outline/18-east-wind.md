# Chapter 18 — East Wind
*Vignette 6: The Derelict · Year Two, spring · Lake Ashford — the same week*

## One-Line Summary
Petra spots a dark shape on the eastern horizon at dawn — a 400-foot freighter, engine-dead, drifting on the east wind; Nadia's logbook entry measures the sighting; Gus and Tomás reconnoitre; the ship is real, dark, and on a course that does not change.

## POV and Tense
Nadia Ruiz, 62 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Slow-dread maritime. The same Nadia voice as V5 — warm, precise, communal — but now the register begins to snag on what is out on the water. The warmth is still present; the Galena is present inside it. Sentences have not yet shortened; the shortening will come over Ch 19–22. For now, the dread is structural (Hitchcock's bomb under the table): the reader knows more than the characters know they know.

**Hard cut within Nadia's POV:** Same consciousness, different movement. The vignette switch is not marked by a POV change but by the register change. The reader should feel the warmth of Ch 17 still present and feel it beginning to catch.

**Depth-marker:** *Year Two, spring. Day One.* (The day counter here is Nadia's — she will mark each day of the Galena's approach in the logbook and the reader follows her count.)

## Setting / Time
The raft on Lake Ashford's south arm. The same week as V5. Day 1 of the Galena's approach. Pre-dawn through late afternoon.

## Chapter Shape
Multi-scene — the sighting (pre-dawn), the community discussion (morning), the reconnaissance (afternoon), the evening observation.

## Ending Mode
**Resolved-beat** (false resolution) — the community has a plan: watch, assess, do not panic. The Galena is still on the eastern horizon, still drifting, still not changing course. They feel in control because they have a plan. The chapter ends on this feeling, which the reader already knows is wrong.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening — Petra at the railing:** Pre-dawn. Nadia is awake early, as usual — the logbook, the count. She hears Petra before she sees her: Petra at the raft's eastern railing, standing still. This is unusual. Petra faces east in the mornings to do breathing exercises; she does not stand still like this. Nadia goes to her.

2. **The Galena:** On the eastern horizon — a dark shape. Not the horizon itself, not a cloud; a shape with the specific mass of something built, something metal. It has no lights. It has the silhouette of a lake freighter. At this distance, details are impressions.

3. **Nadia's logbook entry, Day 1:** She writes it immediately. The discipline of the logbook is that the entry happens before the discussion — record first, then interpret. The entry: the time, the bearing, the estimated distance (far — the far shore of Lake Ashford is twelve to fifteen miles; the shape is at that distance or beyond), the absence of lights. She writes it in logbook voice: precise, measured. The entry is written as if reporting, not as if afraid.

4. **The community wakes:** The sighting becomes communal quickly — Petra has not said anything to anyone except Nadia, but three or four people see Nadia at the railing with the logbook and come to look. By full light, the question is whether it is a ship and whether it is moving. Gus: it is moving. The east wind has been building for two days; the ship is on the east wind. He calculates its drift rate from its apparent motion against a fixed shore point. It is slow. He gives a range of days before it reaches the south arm of the lake: three to five.

5. **The raft's small boats:** Two aluminum johnboats and a canoe. They cannot tow or deflect something four hundred feet long. Gus says this first; others understand it independently.

6. **The reconnaissance:** Gus and Tomás take one of the johnboats toward the Galena. They do not intend to get close — just closer. Nadia watches from the mast with the binoculars. She writes what she sees through them: the ship's lines, the name visible at the bow in flaking paint (GALENA), no movement on the deck, no lights. When Gus and Tomás return, their report: no sound from the ship. No smoke. No visible crew. The portholes on the visible side are dark.

7. **The community discussion — first round:** The community is calm, which Nadia writes as *the community is managing itself, for now.* The primary question: is there value in boarding (supplies, information) and what is the risk (unknown). No conclusion yet. Pruett's instinct — wait. Observe. Don't move toward trouble.

8. **Gus's assessment:** The raft cannot outrun the Galena's drift in the time available, not with two underpowered johnboats and a canoe. Their options are: (a) stay and assess, (b) load the boats and leave south, (c) try to deflect or anchor the ship. He presents these without advocating for one. This is the first moment where the community's decision about what to do before the ship arrives is more important than the ship's contents.

9. **Mikkel:** He is in the group discussion. He listens. He says nothing notable. He is a former marine engine mechanic; he might know something about drifting ships. He doesn't offer this yet, and no one asks yet.

10. **The evening observation:** The Galena is still on the eastern horizon, its position slightly changed — measurably closer. Nadia stands at the eastern railing with the logbook. She writes the evening position. She is aware that the Galena is now present in every angle she casts eastward. It is present in the east-wind sound. It is present in the water's surface to the east.

11. **Chapter close:** Nadia closes the logbook. The community has a plan — they will watch, they will reconnoitre again tomorrow, they will decide together. The Galena is on the horizon. The fish is on the mast. The chapter ends before the plan is tested.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The Galena is established: its presence, its trajectory, the community's initial response. The false resolution: they have a plan and they feel in control. The turn embedded in the chapter: they cannot outrun it, and they cannot deflect it, which means every decision from here concerns what to do *while* the ship approaches — not whether it will arrive.

## New Question / Pressure
What is on the ship? Can the community make good decisions under this kind of slow dread?
