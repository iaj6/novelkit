# Chapter 04 — Out
*Vignette 1: The Drill · Week One · Day 2, predawn through early morning*

## One-Line Summary
Jesse leads the three survivors down the back stairwell and out through the loading-dock service exit; the fish goes into a coat pocket near the door; they run into cold October air and do not stop.

## POV and Tense
Jesse Okafor, 15 — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Fast YA horror — this chapter is action-heavy and moves fast. But the vignette-close requires the action to land with weight: Amara is dead; the fish is left; the survivors are out and changed. Short sentences, physical urgency, and one moment of stillness at the exit where the grief is located without being named.

## Setting / Time
The third-floor supply closet; the back stairwell; the first floor basement; the loading dock / service exit; the school parking lot. Day 2, predawn through early morning. Still dark; the October cold has been building all night.

## Chapter Shape
Action-heavy single scene — the escape.

## Ending Mode
**Resolved-beat** — the immediate arc is complete: they are out of the school. Amara is dead. The fish is left behind. Jesse keeps moving. The resolution is grief-heavy and does not feel like triumph. This is the vignette's final chapter; the arc closes here.

---

## Beats

1. **Opening:** The thing in the hall is a single infected moving slowly — disoriented, not hunting. Jesse watches it through the door crack for long enough to be certain. He signals the three students: *now.*

2. They move in the only way that works: fast, single file, Jesse first, no talking. The hall. The back stairwell — not the north stairwell Amara opened. Jesse knows the building; he has cut class this way before.

3. **The descent:** Two floors. Each landing: Jesse listens before moving. On the second floor landing: sounds from the corridor they passed through yesterday — the infected are still there but not at the stairwell door. On the first floor: the hallway to the cafeteria. Jesse does not go toward it. He hears nothing from the cafeteria. He does not investigate.

4. The basement corridor. The smell: different here — not copper, something older and colder. Loading dock is at the south end. The service exit: a heavy steel door with a push bar.

5. **What Jesse sees on his way through the basement:** Near the service exit, a rack of coats on hooks — winter coats, a few jackets, left by whoever used this exit last. Someone's emergency supply shelf: a can of soup, a first aid kit, a flashlight with dead batteries. Normal things abandoned in a hurry.

6. The push bar. Jesse wraps his hand around it.

7. **The fish:** He is holding it in his other hand. He has been holding it the whole descent without noticing. He looks at it. He cannot carry it — they are moving too fast, they have nothing to carry, they need both hands. He does not think this through explicitly. He tucks the fish into the pocket of the nearest coat on the rack, a gray wool peacoat. He does not know whose it is. He does not look at it again.

8. The push bar moves. Cold air. The parking lot. The October dawn is beginning — gray, not yet light. The pavement is cold through their sneakers. The hospital is three blocks away and its sirens stopped hours ago.

9. They run. Jesse last, the door swinging shut behind him.

10. **The school behind them:** They do not look back. Jesse does not look back. The water tower of Heron Falls is visible above the treeline against the lightening sky.

11. They reach the first corner and turn. Jesse does not stop. He does not stop until they are two blocks away and the others have stopped behind him.

12. **Chapter close:** Jesse looks at the people with him — three students whose names he knows because they went to school together and now knows differently. He counts them. He does not say Amara's name. He does not think it yet. He starts walking. They follow.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The survivors are out of the school. The immediate emergency is resolved. The fish is in a coat pocket at the service exit. Amara is dead. Coach Briggs's decisions — the locked cafeteria, the refused signals — are not resolved in this chapter; they are part of the collateral the vignette leaves in place. Jesse is alive and will not stop.

## New Question / Pressure
What happens to the fish in the coat pocket? (The reader knows; Jesse does not.) What did Coach Briggs do, and how will it settle? Where do four teenagers go from here, in Week One, in Heron Falls?

## Continuity Notes
- **Critical continuity fact confirmed:** The carved wooden fish is placed by Jesse in the pocket of a gray wool coat near the service exit of Heron Falls High School, Day 2 of Week One. He leaves it because they are moving too fast, not as a deliberate gift. This coat and its pocket are the fish's first resting place in its chain of custody.
- **Continuity confirmed:** Jesse gets the surviving students out. He cannot save Amara; she is already gone. He does not look back.
- **Coach Briggs:** His presence in the cafeteria (locked doors, refused window signals) should be present in this chapter as an absence — Jesse hears nothing from the cafeteria, notes the sound, passes. The reader understands that Briggs survived by locking other students in; Jesse does not understand this yet and the narration does not editorialize.
- **Vignette arc complete:** Setup (lockdown, infected real and present) → Escalation (Amara's door, the breach) → Turn (Amara's death, the human cost of compassion) → Close (out, alive, fish left behind). The vignette's thesis: the infected are real and terrible, and the worst thing was a person opening a door out of love.
- **Thread: Fish** — the object leaves Jesse's hands here and enters the chain of custody. Ch 08 is its next appearance.
- **Thread: Thesis** — the thesis is in the structure, not the narration. No sentence should state what Amara's death means.
