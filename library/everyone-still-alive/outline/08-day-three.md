# Chapter 08 — Day Three
*Vignette 3: The Road South · Month Six · Highway 41 · Days 1–3*

## One-Line Summary
Lenn and Theo have been walking Highway 41 south for three days; on Day 3 Theo finds the carved wooden fish in the coat pocket of an abandoned jacket in a roadside house; the first near-death encounter — three armed men who take their food at a road junction.

## POV and Tense
Lenn ("the man") — close third person, past tense.

**Register:** Literary-bleak / McCarthy. Long, unspooling sentences with internal cadence and subordinate rhythms. Sparse dialogue, unattributed (indent only, no tags). Ash-and-cold imagery. No comfort arrived at that is not immediately under threat. The man is "the man" most of the time; "Lenn" appears sparingly. No interior explanation of feeling — feeling lives in what the man does with his hands, what he eats, how fast or slowly he walks. The cold is a character.

**Hard cut from V2:** New world, new body, new dread. The institutional control of the checkpoint gives way to the open road's indifference. The shift should be felt.

**Depth-marker:** *Month Six. Day Three.*

## Setting / Time
Highway 41 southbound corridor. Month Six of the collapse. Days 1–3 of the chapter's arc (compressed; Days 1–2 are summary, Day 3 is rendered). Roadside houses stripped or locked. The smelting plant at the highway's edge approaches on Day 3. Early November: cold, the smell of wet leaves and old smoke, the sky low and gray.

## Chapter Shape
Multi-scene / time-skip — Days 1–2 in compressed narration establishing the rhythm of the road; Day 3 rendered as a full scene.

## Ending Mode
**Literary-fade** — the chapter ends at dark, Lenn and Theo in a ditch or stripped house near the highway, the ash-sky beginning on the horizon behind them. The cold. Theo holding the fish.

## Beats

1. **Opening (Days 1–2, compressed):** The way the road goes: they walk in the morning, rest in the afternoon if shelter is available, walk again until dark. What Theo does on the road: collects things — a watch that stopped, a book about birds, plastic bags because the man says to collect plastic bags. What the man does: looks ahead, looks behind, calculates. The towns they pass through are empty or stripped or locked-door silent.

2. **The road's particulars:** Highway 41 south. Two lanes, intermittently salted (the salting stopped). Road signs that say nothing useful. A gas station with a hole in the roof where something hit it. The smell of wet leaf-rot, old smoke, the specific smell of a building that has been closed for months.

3. **Day 3 — The roadside house:** They stop to search a one-story house set back from the highway. The door is unlocked. Inside: stripped, but not completely. A closet with winter coats. Theo goes through them the way he goes through everything — methodically, without hurry. He finds the fish.

4. **The fish:** Theo holds it out. The man takes it, turns it over. Palm-sized. Carved from something light-colored, a small wood. He does not know what it is — a keepsake, a child's toy, something someone made to pass the time. He hands it back to Theo. Theo puts it in his pocket. The man does not ask him to put it back.

5. **Continuing south:** Day 3 afternoon. The smelting plant appears at the highway's edge — the shell of it, the processing shed, the collapsed east wall. Something about the way the sky has been since noon: thicker, gray-brown rather than gray. The ash-sky is beginning. The man knows what he is smelling now. He does not say it.

6. **The junction:** A T-junction where a county road crosses Highway 41. Three men, a pickup truck with a broken windshield, two rifles visible. The men are not waiting for them — they are there for another reason (a camp, a claim, a fuel situation) — but they see Lenn and Theo and their position becomes a fact.

7. **The encounter:** One man tells them to stop. The man stops. He has Theo behind him. The first man speaks; the man answers; they are brief. The three men want the pack. The man calculates: the pack has their food — four days' worth, a water filter, a sleeping bag liner. He calculates the rifles. He takes the pack off and sets it down.

8. **The men take the pack.** They let Lenn and Theo pass. The man walks Theo past them without looking back. They walk for a long time before Theo says anything. What Theo says: *They took our food.* The man says: *Yes.* Theo says: *But not us.* The man says nothing.

9. **Evening:** A different house, farther south, half a mile off the junction. The door gives. They get inside before dark. The ash-sky overhead is orange-gray now where the last light comes through it.

10. **Chapter close:** Theo is holding the fish. He runs his thumb over it the way anyone would run their thumb over a made thing. The ash-sky at the window is the color of nothing good. The man does not sleep yet.

## Outcome / Turn
The fish arrives. The first near-death encounter passes — they lost the food but not their lives, which is the better calculation. The turn: Theo's question after the encounter (*But not us*) — a nine-year-old's accounting of what matters. Lenn does not answer it because there is no answer that is sufficient.

## New Question / Pressure
What will they eat with no food? Where is the next shelter? And what is in Theo's pocket, and where did it come from?
