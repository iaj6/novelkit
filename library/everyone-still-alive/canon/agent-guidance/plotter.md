# Plotter Guidance — *Everyone Still Alive*

## The central instruction: the structural unit is the vignette

This is a mosaic novel. The outline's primary structure is **six labeled vignette blocks**, each a self-contained short arc with its own setup, escalation, and turn. Chapters are numbered continuously (01–22) but belong to their vignette and obey that vignette's register and genre conventions, not a uniform set of rules applied across the manuscript.

**Do not outline this book as a continuous single-arc story.** There is no protagonist whose arc spans the whole manuscript. The book-level arc is the escalating thesis argument (each vignette answering "who is the monster" differently) and the three connective threads. The vignette is the narrative unit; the chapter is the subdivision.

## POV Count and Rotation

Six POV characters. Rotation is by vignette, not by chapter. Each vignette is single-POV throughout; no chapter mixes POVs. The rotation is sequential — one movement completes before the next begins. Chapters do not braid POVs across vignettes.

Nadia Ruiz is the one exception: she anchors both Vignette 5 and Vignette 6. This is not a rotation — it is the same consciousness in two consecutive movements, with her register visibly shifting between them.

**Do not introduce a multi-POV structure within a vignette.** Even in Vignette 6 (five chapters), all five chapters belong to Nadia.

---

## Vignette-Level Arc Requirements

Each vignette must contain: **(1) setup** (establish the cast and the immediate situation), **(2) escalation** (the vignette's central pressure builds), **(3) a turn** (the moment that recontextualizes or sharpens what the vignette has been building toward), and **(4) a real ending** (that satisfies within the vignette — do not end a vignette on a thread that requires another vignette for resolution).

### Vignette 1 — The Drill
Arc: school lockdown → infected breach upper floors → Jesse gets survivors out. **The turn:** Amara opens the stairwell door (the most human choice; the one that costs). The vignette's answer to "who is the monster?": the infected are real and terrible — but the worst thing is a person's choice made from compassion. The fish is left at the service exit.

### Vignette 2 — The Line
Arc: Selle at the checkpoint → the woman with children → the decision to turn her back. **The turn:** the decision itself (not dramatized as violence — made quietly, procedurally, with full understanding that it may be a death sentence). The vignette's answer: people using fear as license. The vignette ends on Selle's compliance, not on his objection.

### Vignette 3 — The Road South
Arc: Lenn and Theo walking → three near-deaths from other people → the river crossing. **The turn:** Theo giving the fish to the south-bound stranger. What this costs Lenn (the boy's trust in generosity is still intact; Lenn's is not) is the vignette's final note. The vignette's answer: people, purely — no infected anywhere.

### Vignette 4 — The Sweep
Arc: crew sweeping the Rim towns → what "clear a structure" actually means revealed incrementally → the kid's understanding. **The turn:** the kid's confrontation with what the crew does. Cass's narration does not pre-empt his response. The vignette's answer: the narrator is the monster, from inside.

### Vignette 5 — The Raft
Arc: establish the community on Lake Ashford → the raft's warmth and rules → a moment that tests it lightly (a small friction that the community resolves with its values). **No major antagonist; no threat.** This vignette is the book's evidence that the opposite of monstrous is possible. The Galena does not appear until Chapter 18 (Vignette 6, first chapter). Do not introduce the ship in Vignette 5.

### Vignette 6 — The Derelict
Arc: the Galena sighted → community decision-making under dread → what happens to Mikkel → the ship's arrival (or contact). **The turn:** the community's treatment of Mikkel before they know what is on the ship (this is the thesis's final instance). The last image of the book belongs to Chapter 22 and must deliver the thesis one final time. The Reader of Names question is not resolved. The Galena's contents are specific and earned, consistent with the infected gradient.

## Chapter-Shape Preferences

The outline should vary chapter shapes by vignette:

- **Vignettes 1 and 4:** Single-scene chapters dominate. Each chapter covers a tight time window; chapters close before the scene resolves (cliffhanger or mid-action). No bottle chapters, no extended interiority chapters.
- **Vignette 2:** Single-scene or one-continuous-shift chapters. Selle's movements across the checkpoint. Compressed time.
- **Vignette 3:** One chapter per day of walking (approximately). The landscape and the road are structural — each chapter moves through specific physical terrain. Slightly longer than the other vignettes' chapters.
- **Vignette 5:** Community-rhythm chapters — a chapter may span a day or several days of shared life. Allowed to breathe.
- **Vignette 6:** Five chapters escalating from normal-life (Ch 18) to contact (Ch 22). Chapters should shorten as the Galena approaches — same content density, fewer words, tighter sentences.

## Connective Thread Obligations Per Chapter

The plotter must ensure every chapter touches at least one of the following:

1. **The thesis question** — "who is the monster?" advances, complicates, or deepens in every chapter. This is the one thread that touches every chapter. It need not be explicit; it is structural.
2. **The Reader of Names** — present in at least one chapter per vignette that is equipped to hear it (V1: phone; V3: found radio; V5/V6: shortwave). Not required in V2 or V4 where characters lack a working receiver.
3. **The carved wooden fish** — appears in V1 (made), V3 (found and carried), and V5/V6 (on the mast). One chapter per vignette where it appears should contain the fish as a concrete object, not a symbol.
4. **The collapse timeline / gradient** — each vignette's depth-marker and the implicit argument about distance from catastrophe.

The weave check for this mosaic: a chapter deep inside a vignette that advances only that vignette's internal tension is **not** a dead chapter — the vignette arc is its thread. The thesis thread is an exception: it must touch every chapter.

## Genre-Specific Structural Conventions Per Vignette

**Vignette 1 (YA horror):** Monster must appear or be made viscerally present within the first 300 words of Chapter 1. The threat must escalate with each chapter. A major supporting character's death must land in the middle of the vignette (Amara, not the end), leaving Jesse to act without her.

**Vignette 2 (institutional thriller):** The checkpoint's rules must be established in Chapter 5 before they are applied in Chapter 6. The turn (the decision to turn back the woman with children) must land in Chapter 7. The final chapter ends before we see the consequence.

**Vignette 3 (literary bleak):** Three distinct near-death encounters with other people, spread across the four chapters. The fish must arrive early (Ch 8, Day 3). Theo's gift of the fish is the final act of the final chapter (Ch 11).

**Vignette 4 (perpetrator POV):** The nature of what the crew does must be implied in Chapter 12, partially revealed in Chapter 13, and fully visible (though not narrated directly) by Chapter 14. The kid's final decision is Chapter 14's last beat.

**Vignette 5 (warm communal):** No antagonist; no external threat. The raft's rules and rhythms must be established in Chapter 15. Chapters 16 and 17 deepen community rather than threatening it. The Galena does not appear.

**Vignette 6 (slow-dread maritime):** The Galena must be sighted in Chapter 18 and never leave. Mikkel must be identified as a focus of suspicion by Chapter 19. The community's worst decision (regarding Mikkel) must precede the Galena's arrival. Chapter 22 is the last thing the book says.
