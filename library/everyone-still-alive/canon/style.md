# Style

## The Base Contract

*Everyone Still Alive* is written in close third person (past tense) with a single anchor POV per vignette — with one mandatory exception: Vignette 4 (The Sweep) is **first person, present tense**. This exception is non-negotiable and structural to that vignette's perpetrator-narrator effect.

The base contract holds across all six vignettes:

- **Concrete and physical.** Abstraction enters only through specific sensory detail, never through explicit statement.
- **No genre winking, no meta-humor, no fourth-wall acknowledgment.** The book is not self-aware about being a genre object.
- **The catastrophe is never spectacle.** It is rendered through what characters smell, hear, feel underfoot, and are afraid of — not through set-piece description.
- **Dialogue is untagged or minimally tagged.** Attribution is invisible. No interpretive tags ("as if to say," "which meant").
- **The word "zombie" is character speech, not narration.** Characters use it out of fear and rumor. The narration never applies it as a neutral label.
- **The thesis is never stated.** The argument lives in structure, image, and what happens. No sentence in the narration may speak the words "the monster is" as an assertion.

On top of this base, **each vignette tilts hard toward a named register.** The shift must be legible. A reader should feel the floor change underfoot when a new vignette begins. Register averaging — drifting toward a single literary-adult middle — is the manuscript's most serious structural failure mode.

## Per-Vignette Register Map

This is the central content of the style guide. The drafter must execute each tilt fully and the reader/editor must test for it.

### Vignette 1 — The Drill: fast YA horror

Sentence length: short. Declarative by default. Sensory and immediate — what Jesse's body knows before his brain catches up. Dialogue breaks early and often; characters speak because they are frightened, not because they have considered things. Paragraphs close a half-step nearer to danger. The infected are rendered first by motion and sound (something at the end of the corridor moving wrong), then once by close proximity — never by clinical description. Internal thought is brief, physical, and present: *his legs were moving before he decided to move*. Chapters end on cliffhangers or mid-action cuts.

Comp register: Charlie Higson's *The Enemy*, Rick Yancey's *The 5th Wave* (opening chapters).

### Vignette 2 — The Line: institutional dread

Controlled, cold, procedural. Selle observes, categorizes, makes internal lists. The horror lives in bureaucratic language applied to human suffering — the checkpoint rules, the criteria, the log entries. Sentences are medium-length and precise. Dialogue is sparse and formal (orders, procedural queries, the official language of the checkpoint). Long interior passages are permitted but must read like inventory, not meditation. Chapter endings are mid-beat or quietly terrible — no dramatic breaks; the dread seeps through policy.

Comp register: checkpoint chapters in McCarthy's *The Road* + Agustina Bazterrica's *Tender Is the Flesh* (cold procedure applied to the unthinkable).

### Vignette 3 — The Road South: literary-bleak / McCarthy register

Long, unspooling sentences with internal cadence and subordinate rhythms. Sparse dialogue, unattributed (indent only, no tags). Ash-and-cold imagery — the physical world is insistent and indifferent. No comfort: warmth arrived at is immediately under threat of being taken. Lenn is "the man" most of the time; his name appears sparingly. No interior explanation of feeling — feeling lives in what the man does with his hands, what he eats, how fast or slowly he walks. Chapter endings are quiet bleak fades. The cold is a character.

Comp passage: the opening of McCarthy's *The Road* — the drafter must read this exemplar before drafting Vignette 3 and match the cadence of that long first sentence.

### Vignette 4 — The Sweep: cold first-person perpetrator

**First person, present tense — the only vignette in this tense and person.** Cass's voice is flat, precise in physical description, and evasive in moral description. Horror is enacted through what is noted without commentary — the matter-of-fact detail that the reader understands before the narrator acknowledges. Occasional cracks: a short sentence arrives that nearly acknowledges what has been happening; Cass re-covers it immediately. Reader is never given a complete moral account, only the receipts. Sentence length: short to medium. Paragraphs are tight; no lyrical sprawl.

Comp register: Alden Bell's *The Reapers Are the Angels* — lyrical-violent first person; the narrator's beauty and their damage existing simultaneously without resolution.

### Vignette 5 — The Raft: warm communal

Third person, past tense. Nadia's voice is warm, deliberately calm, precise and affectionate in small observation. The community is rendered through shared ritual — the morning count, the logbook entry, the way people rotate cooking duties. Sentences breathe; length varies, with longer sentences permitted when the subject is collective life. Beauty is insisted on even amid ruin. The plural — "the community," "they," "the raft's people" — is used alongside close third of Nadia when the scene widens to include the group. Chapter endings can be resolved or quiet lyrical fades; earned rest is allowed here.

Comp register: Emily St. John Mandel's *Station Eleven* (the Year Twenty Traveling Symphony chapters) — present-tense calm translated into a past-tense warmth; survival as something people build together, not only endure.

### Vignette 6 — The Derelict: slow-dread maritime suspense

Same Nadia voice as Vignette 5 — but the register is the same house with the walls closing. Sentences that were warm become watchful; warmth is still present but keeps snagging on what is out on the water. The Galena must be present in every chapter even when not named — in the angle of Nadia's observations, in what people avoid saying, in the quality of the light off the water at evening. The dread is structural (Hitchcock's bomb under the table): the reader watches characters act without knowing what the reader already suspects, and the horror is in the watching. Chapter endings escalate; the final chapter carries the book's last image.

Comp register: Daphne du Maurier's *The Birds* (ambient dread becoming specific, ordinary life persisting until it cannot) + maritime claustrophobia.

## Failure Modes

The AI-author patterns most tempting in this story's register. The drafter should actively avoid these; the compression pass should cut them.

- **Thesis restatement** — the book has an explicit argument ("the monster is whatever distance from the catastrophe lets people believe they are still good"), and the temptation to state it abstractly after a morally difficult scene is high, especially in Vignettes 2 and 4. Show the receipt; do not write the invoice. If the narration has just made the argument through action, it does not then need to say what the action meant.

- **Register averaging** — the most serious structural failure mode of the manuscript: each vignette drifting toward a single literary-adult middle register instead of holding its assigned tilt. If a paragraph from Vignette 1 could plausibly appear in Vignette 3, the register has failed. The shifts must be legible and they must be maintained.

- **Redundant transition / re-summary** — the drafter will be tempted to open each new vignette with orienting context for the new cast and setting. Do not. Every vignette opens cold — new cast, new place, depth-marker, and immediate situation. No connective-tissue throat-clearing that recaps what came before or introduces who we are about to meet.

- **Post-scene subtext naming** — the moral horror of Vignettes 2 and 4 does not require a paragraph identifying what just happened as horror. The scene has already done that work. Move on.

- **Sentence-after-the-sentence** — an image lands and the next sentence glosses or explains it. Avoid across all vignettes. In Vignette 3, a second sentence after an image should deepen or contradict it, not annotate it.

## Lean-Into Patterns

Patterns the drafter and editors should NOT treat as failures, because this register depends on them.

- **Unattributed dialogue in Vignette 3** — the McCarthy register expects it. Dialogue without tags is correct here; do not flag missing attribution in this vignette.
- **Flat meta-cognition in Vignette 4** — in Cass's voice, brief notes on cognition ("I note this." "I write it in the log.") are structural to the perpetrator-narrator affect. These are not AI-tells in this context; they are the form.
- **Community plural in Vignette 5** — using "they" and "the raft's people" when Nadia's close third widens to include the group is intentional and registers the communal sensibility the vignette is built on. Do not compress to single-POV interiority at the expense of this.
- **Short-sentence clusters in Vignette 1** — three or four short declaratives in a row are the intended rhythm, not a tic to vary away from. The brevity is the register.
- **Logbook-voice paragraphs in Vignettes 5 and 6** — Nadia occasionally writes in a slightly more formal, logbook-entry cadence. This is voice texture, not drift from close third.
