# Pitch

*Everyone Still Alive* is a mosaic novel set across two years of an uneven apocalypse in the cold lake country of the upper Midwest. The infected are real — fast, agile, wall-climbing — where the story first begins, and only a rumor by the time most of the survivors meet. Six separate casts live at six different depths of the collapse: a fifteen-year-old trapped in his school during the first terrible week; a checkpoint guardsman two months in who decides daily who is human enough to cross a bridge; a father and son walking south through month six who never encounter a single infected and are nearly killed three times by other people; a scavenger crew in year one who have stopped pretending they are the good guys; a floating community of twenty on a cold lake in year two who have built something worth protecting; and, drifting toward that fragile raft in the same week, a dead freighter that may or may not be carrying the thing everyone fears most. Three threads connect the six casts: a pirate radio voice that reads the dead over the air each night, a small carved wooden fish that travels hand to hand across two years, and the slowly answered question of whether the thing in the stairwell, the thing on the ship, and the thing that used to be a neighbor are all the same thing. The book argues — across every vignette, in six different registers — that the monster is whatever distance from the actual catastrophe lets people pretend they are still good.
