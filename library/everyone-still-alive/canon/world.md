# World

## The Umbrella World

### The Collapse

The collapse began approximately two years before the book's final scenes. Cause unknown and unresolved — characters speculate, but the narration never adjudicates. What is established in Vignette 1 and must not be contradicted: the infected are fast, agile, heat-seeking, and recognizably wrong in the way a person becomes wrong when some core switch has been thrown. They climb walls and railings. They are drawn by sound and heat. At distance, in bad light, they look like a person running. They are not slow; they are not shuffling; they are not dead in any theatrical sense. Infection spreads by bite and possibly by proximity in confined spaces — no character knows for certain, and the uncertainty is structural.

The collapse has been uneven, geographically and temporally. Urban centers fell first, in weeks. The rural upper lake country emptied more slowly: some towns fell to the infected, some to the panic of quarantine, some simply drained as people fled south. By Year Two, the population of the region is a fraction of what it was. Pockets of functioning community exist alongside territories of total devastation. No government signal has been heard in months; the pirate radio is what passes for public record.

**The infected gradient — a load-bearing structural argument:** The closer a vignette is to the actual infected, in time and physical proximity, the less monstrous the people in it are to each other. The further the vignette from the real monster, the worse people treat each other. This gradient must be kept intact. The narration must never confirm infected activity in vignettes where the infected are only feared.

### Geography

A thinly fictionalized upper-Midwest lake-country corridor — the feel of the Great Lakes border region (northern Michigan, Wisconsin, southern Ontario) without pinning to exact real names. Dominant features: a major inland lake large enough to have carried freighter traffic; a highway network running south; clusters of small and mid-size towns that have largely emptied; deciduous and pine forest; long winters. Cold is structural in every vignette except briefly in The Raft (spring thaw). The smell of wet leaf-rot, smoke, and lake water recurs.

**Named places in use:**

- **Heron Falls** — Mid-size lake-country town, home of Heron Falls High School. Grocery stores, a main street, a water tower visible from the school's upper floors. Setting for Vignette 1.
- **The Narrows** — The bridge carrying Highway 9 over the Ashford River, repurposed as a control checkpoint two months into the collapse. The river runs fast and cold; carries ice by November. Setting for Vignette 2.
- **Ashford River** — The river at the Narrows. Twenty meters below the bridge deck. Not navigable.
- **Highway 41** — The southbound corridor. Two lanes, intermittently plowed, the road Lenn and Theo are walking in Vignette 3. Passes through emptied towns, a smelting plant that burned in Month Four (its ash-sky lingers two days of walking distance), and a river crossing near the vignette's end.
- **The Rim towns** — No single name. A ring of emptied small lake-country towns that the Vignette 4 crew sweeps systematically: a farmhouse, a laundromat, a hardware store, a church. They blur. That is the point.
- **Lake Ashford** — The large inland lake anchoring Vignettes 5 and 6. South arm is where the raft is moored. The lake is wide enough that the far shore is not always visible. Freighter-scale; the Galena was built for it.

### The Three Connective Threads

These are what cross vignettes. Characters do not cross vignettes (with the single noted exception). These do.

**1. The Reader of Names**
A pirate radio broadcast, sent each night, that reads aloud the names of the confirmed dead — slowly, without commentary. The voice is low and measured; gender is indeterminate. Heard on a dying phone in Vignette 1 (a minor character's phone, battery at 3%, the names arriving as Jesse waits in the dark). Heard on a hand-crank shortwave in Vignette 3 (Lenn finds the radio in a stripped house; holds it while Theo sleeps). A working shortwave set on the raft picks it up in Vignettes 5 and 6. Whether the broadcaster is organizing a recovery effort or simply reading into the dark — one person with a list and a transmitter — is never resolved. Whether the broadcaster is connected to the Galena is a question the reader is allowed to carry and the book is allowed to refuse.

**2. The Carved Wooden Fish**
A small fish, palm-sized, carved from a splinter of chair-leg during the school lockdown in Vignette 1. Jesse Okafor makes it because his hands need to be doing something while his mind refuses to panic. He leaves it in the pocket of a coat near the school's service exit when the survivors get out — too fast and too far gone to take anything. It is found by Lenn in Vignette 3 (tucked in the pocket of an abandoned jacket in a roadside house on Day 3). Theo carries it; at the vignette's end, Theo gives it to a stranger heading toward the lake settlements. It arrives at the raft by hands not narrated, and by Vignette 5 it is nailed to the central mast. No character connects the full chain. The reader does. This is the book's proof that all six casts are one humanity across time.

**3. The Thesis Question**
"Who is the monster?" — entered in Vignette 1, complicated and escalated by each subsequent vignette, given its final word in Vignette 6. Each vignette delivers a different answer:
- #1: The infected are real — but the worst act is a human choice made out of compassion
- #2: People using fear as license
- #3: People, purely; no infected anywhere
- #4: From inside — the narrator has become the thing they feared
- #5: No monster; what humans can build in the absence of fear
- #6: Unknown — and the community turning on itself before it knows

No two vignettes may land the same beat on this question.

## Per-Vignette Micro-Settings

### Vignette 1 — The Drill (Heron Falls High School, Week One)

Heron Falls High School: four stories, cinderblock exterior, long central corridor with grey lockers. The gymnasium has a dropped ceiling and emergency lighting that runs three hours before the batteries die. The art room is on the third floor — wide windows, a ledge outside, overlooking the parking lot and the water tower. The cafeteria is in the basement with two exits, one of which is a loading-dock door. The administrative office on the first floor has the building's only hardwired telephone. The school sits three blocks from Heron Falls General Hospital; the sirens carry but no one is responding to them. It is early October. The leaves are mostly down. The pavement is cold enough to ache through sneakers.

### Vignette 2 — The Line (The Narrows Checkpoint, Month Two)

Highway 9 over the Ashford River. The eastern approach is barricaded with Jersey barriers and a plywood checkpoint kiosk. The western end is open but watched from a position on the bridge superstructure. Behind the kiosk: a fuel tanker truck (three-quarters full; the tanker is why the checkpoint matters), a surplus tent, a propane heater, a folding cot, a CB radio with intermittent range. The river is twenty meters below and running fast; it is November and there is ice in the current. The official designation stamped on the sandbags is REGIONAL TRANSIT CONTROL, HIGHWAY 9. The checkpoint's unofficial name is "the Narrows." Sgt. Pruett's rules are written in a spiral notebook; Private Selle knows them by heart because he helped write them.

### Vignette 3 — The Road South (Highway 41 corridor, Month Six)

Lenn and Theo have been walking for six months. Highway 41 goes south toward what they call "below the snow line" — a destination defined by what it is not. Fifty-mile days are impossible; they make eight to ten on good days. The towns they pass are empty or stripped; houses that are unlocked have been cleaned out; some locked houses still have canned goods in the back of a pantry behind the cleaning products. A smelting plant that burned in Month Four sits beside the highway on Day 3; the ash-sky it generates fills the air for two days of walking. The wood is wet from a November rain that has turned to snow by the vignette's final chapter. Three attempts on their lives — all from other people, not infected — punctuate the vignette's arc.

### Vignette 4 — The Sweep (The Rim towns, Year One winter)

A circuit of six or seven emptied lake-country towns, worked systematically by the four-person crew. Farmhouses. A laundromat (the folded clothes still on top of the machines). A hardware store. A church with a locked basement that the crew opens. Deep winter — the hardest winter so far, because the easy supplies were taken in Year One's first months and what remains requires decisions about what you are willing to do. The moral horror is that "clear a structure" has acquired meanings beyond its original one. The Rim towns blur into each other deliberately; there is no single vivid place, only the accumulation of emptied buildings and the crew's efficiency.

### Vignette 5 — The Raft (Lake Ashford, Year Two spring)

A floating platform on the south arm of Lake Ashford, assembled over two winters from salvaged dock sections and a houseboat hull lashed together with marine rope and steel cable. A central mast (a telephone pole, bolted vertical) anchors the canvas shade structures and the solar panels salvaged from a marina. The greenhouse — eight feet by twelve, window panes of varying sizes set in a timber frame — is bolted to the aft section; it grows spinach, tomatoes from saved seed, and herbs. The raft's working shortwave set is kept in a weatherproof case in the main shelter. Nadia's logbook is in a second case, bolted to the base of the mast. The carved wooden fish is nailed above the logbook case, slightly crooked. It is spring — ice only recently out — and the light is longer every day and the community feels it in their bodies. There is mud on the floating walkways and cold light off the water and a feeling of expansion.

### Vignette 6 — The Derelict (Lake Ashford, Year Two, same week as Vignette 5)

The same raft; the same spring week. But the Galena — a 400-foot lake freighter, dark, engine-dead — has appeared on the eastern horizon and has not changed course. It is drifting on the prevailing east wind. At the observed rate, it will reach the raft's position in three to five days. The raft cannot move fast enough to clear the ship's path. The community's small boats (two aluminum johnboats and a canoe) cannot tow or deflect something that size. The central dread is twofold: what is on the ship, and what the community will do to each other before it arrives. The Galena's presence in every chapter of this vignette is structural — it appears in the angle of Nadia's observations, in what people choose not to say, in the weather, in the sound the water makes at night.
