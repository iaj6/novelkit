# Continuity

## Part A — World Invariants

These facts hold across every vignette. No chapter may contradict them.

1. **The collapse timeline** runs from Week One (Vignette 1) through Month Two (V2), Month Six (V3), Year One (V4), to Year Two spring (V5 and V6, simultaneous). Every vignette must open with a depth-marker that is consistent with this spine.

2. **The infected are fast and agile.** They climb walls and railings. They are drawn by sound and heat. At distance in bad light they look like a running person. They are not slow; not shuffling; not unambiguously dead in any theatrical sense. This is established in Vignette 1 and must not be contradicted in any later vignette.

3. **The infected gradient is load-bearing:** Vivid and physical in Vignette 1 only. Absent and only feared in Vignettes 2, 3, 4. Never seen in Vignette 5. Ambiguous — possibly present on the Galena — in Vignette 6. The narration must **never confirm** infected activity in vignettes where the infected are only feared. This gradient is a structural argument: the further from the real monster, the worse people treat each other.

4. **"Zombie" is character vocabulary, not narrative vocabulary.** Characters use the word in fear and rumor. The narration does not use it as a neutral label.

5. **The Reader of Names** is a pirate radio broadcast, transmitted each night, that reads aloud the names of the confirmed dead without commentary. The voice is low, measured, and of indeterminate gender. It is heard in Vignettes 1, 3, 5, and 6. Whether the broadcaster is one person reading into the dark or an organized recovery effort is never resolved. Whether the broadcaster is connected to the Galena is an unanswered question the book deliberately refuses to close.

6. **The carved wooden fish** was made by Jesse Okafor in Vignette 1, from a chair-leg splinter, during the school lockdown. Its chain of custody: left in a coat pocket at the school service exit (V1) → found by Lenn in a roadside house coat pocket on Day 3 (V3) → carried by Theo, given to a south-bound stranger at a river crossing (V3 end) → arrives at the raft by unnarrated hands → nailed to the raft's central mast (V5/V6). No single character knows the full chain. The reader assembles it.

7. **No character is a named cast member in more than one vignette.** The single exception is Nadia Ruiz (POV for Vignettes 5 and 6, which are simultaneous and form one continuous arc).

8. **Named geography** (consistent throughout): Heron Falls (town and high school, V1); the Narrows checkpoint / Highway 9 / Ashford River (V2); Highway 41 southbound corridor (V3); the Rim towns (V4); Lake Ashford and the raft on its south arm (V5/V6).

9. **The Galena** is a 400-foot lake freighter, engine-dead, drifting on the east wind toward the raft in Vignette 6. It is introduced in Vignette 6 only; no earlier vignette may reference a specific derelict ship. What is aboard it is the book's final revelation.

10. **The thesis must advance in every vignette** without being stated in the narration. Each vignette delivers a different answer to "who is the monster?" — and no two vignettes may land the same beat on this question. The answers escalate and complicate; they do not repeat.

## Part B — Per-Vignette Local Facts

*(These facts are scoped to their vignette. Downstream continuity checks enforce Part A book-wide; Part B only within the assigned vignette.)*

### Vignette 1 — The Drill
- Heron Falls High School: four stories, cinderblock, basement cafeteria (two exits including a loading-dock door), third-floor art room (windows overlooking the parking lot), first-floor administrative office (only hardwired phone).
- The infected reach the upper floors because Amara Soyinka opens a locked stairwell door for someone pounding and crying on the other side.
- Amara does not survive the vignette.
- Coach Briggs locks the cafeteria doors with students inside and survives.
- Jesse whittles the fish during the art-room wait and leaves it in a coat pocket at the service exit when the survivors get out.

### Vignette 2 — The Line
- The checkpoint is on Highway 9 at the Narrows (Ashford River bridge). It guards a fuel tanker truck that is three-quarters full — this is why the position matters.
- Sergeant Cutter Pruett commands. Private Daan Selle enforces.
- The woman with children is turned back because of a feverish child; the fever is respiratory illness, not infection. The characters cannot confirm this and do not try.
- The vignette ends on a decision, not an act of violence. The decision is the worst thing in the vignette.

### Vignette 3 — The Road South
- Lenn is a former chemistry teacher; this fact does not surface unless directly relevant.
- Theo is nine. He finds things; he gives the fish to a stranger heading toward the lake settlements at a river crossing near the vignette's end.
- They are six months into the collapse, walking Highway 41 south.
- They never encounter an infected. They are nearly killed three times by other people.
- A smelting plant that burned in Month Four sits on the highway; its ash-sky persists for two days of travel.

### Vignette 4 — The Sweep
- Narrated in first person, present tense. Narrator: Cass (name appears once; gender unspecified and the prose does not invite the question).
- Crew: Cass, Jute (leader), Parish (talkative), and "the kid" (unnamed, seventeen or eighteen).
- The vignette operates in Year One deep winter.
- "Clear a structure" has acquired meanings beyond its original one; this is made clear by midpoint without being stated directly.
- The vignette's final beat: the kid's understanding of what the crew does, and what he does with that understanding. Cass's narration does not pre-empt this.

### Vignette 5 — The Raft
- The community has twenty people on Lake Ashford's south arm; solar panels, shortwave, greenhouse with window panes.
- Nadia Ruiz (62) keeps the logbook. Gus Pereira (54) maintains systems. Petra Halse (27) teaches children. Tomás (35) rows supply runs.
- The carved wooden fish is nailed to the central mast. No community member knows its full history.
- The community rule: no violence; labor shared; new arrivals give something to the commons.
- It is spring. Ice recently out. The community feels the light returning.

### Vignette 6 — The Derelict
- The Galena is sighted on the eastern horizon by Petra Halse.
- Mikkel (recent arrival, three weeks before the Galena appears; former marine engine mechanic; came from the eastern shore) becomes the focal point of community fear despite no evidence connecting him to the ship.
- Nadia's logbook register visibly tightens (shorter sentences, less warmth) as the Galena closes distance.
- The community's decision about what to do before the ship arrives matters more than the ship's contents.
- The last image of the book belongs to this vignette.
