# Threads — *Everyone Still Alive*

> **Note on mosaic structure.** This is a vignette novel, not a single continuous arc. The four threads below are the *connective threads* — the only questions the reader can track across the hard cuts between vignettes. Within each vignette, the local arc itself functions as an additional thread (the reader is also tracking "will Jesse get the survivors out?" or "what will the community do before the ship arrives?"), but those are vignette-level arcs, not book-level threads. The weave check accounts for both: a chapter that advances only its vignette arc is **not** a dead chapter. Every chapter is required to advance at least one connective thread; Thread 3 and Thread 4 cover every chapter in the book.

---

## Thread 1: The Reader of Names

- **Question**: Is the Reader of Names one lonely person reading the dead into the dark, or is someone out there organizing a recovery — and is the broadcaster connected to the Galena?
- **Enters**: Ch 03 (*The Night and the Names*) — a classmate's dying phone (3% battery) picks up the broadcast in the supply closet; the voice reads names without commentary until the screen goes dark mid-name. The question of who is broadcasting is not raised by the characters; it is simply heard.
- **Advanced in**:
  - Ch 09 (*The Ash Sky*) — Lenn finds a hand-crank shortwave in a stripped house and holds it alone while Theo sleeps; the voice reads names until his arm tires; he sets the radio down and does not pick it up again. Still private; not shared with Theo.
  - Ch 15 (*The Log*) — the raft's working shortwave picks it up; Nadia transcribes names into the logbook because "someone should."
  - Ch 17 (*The Last Entry*) — the Reader of Names heard again; Nadia recognizes one name belonging to a community member who is asleep and doesn't hear it.
  - Ch 20 (*The Vote*) — Nadia hears the broadcast in daylight, for the first time; she calls Gus over; someone (unnamed) asks: *Is that coming from the ship?* Nadia writes in the logbook: *The question was asked. I did not answer it because I do not know the answer.* The question is recorded, not resolved.
- **Resolves in**: Ch 22 (*What We Keep*) — **Deliberately unresolved.** Gus and Tomás find a shortwave transmitter in the Galena's radio room; its frequency dial is set to the Reader of Names frequency. Whether it was the broadcast source is not confirmed and the book does not confirm it. The transmitter is present; the broadcast is not tied to it with certainty. Nadia writes in the logbook: *The transmitter. The frequency. I don't know. I have written that I don't know.*
- **What it's about underneath**: This thread is about testimony — about whether anyone is bearing witness to the collapse, and whether bearing witness matters. The Reader of Names is either one person's act of grief (someone who will not let the dead go nameless) or an organized recovery effort; the distinction matters enormously to the survivors who hear it, and the book refuses to tell them which it is. That refusal is the thread's meaning: in a world where institutions have collapsed, reading names into the dark is both the most and least that can be done, and we cannot always know which.

## Thread 2: The Carved Wooden Fish

- **Question**: Can this small made thing — carved in terror, left behind in flight — survive the collapse and reach someone who needs it, and will any character ever know the full chain of its journey?
- **Enters**: Ch 03 (*The Night and the Names*) — Jesse Okafor carves the fish from a broken chair-leg splinter during the supply-closet wait, not as a symbol but because his hands need to be doing something while his mind refuses to panic. It becomes a fish because of the shape the wood wants.
- **Advanced in**:
  - Ch 04 (*Out*) — Jesse leaves the fish in the pocket of a gray wool peacoat near the school's service exit; they are moving too fast to carry it; he does not look back at it.
  - Ch 08 (*Day Three*) — Theo finds the fish in the pocket of an abandoned jacket in a roadside house on Day 3 of the walk south; he carries it from this chapter forward.
  - Ch 09 (*The Ash Sky*) — the fish is in Theo's hand while he sleeps; Lenn sees it beside the shortwave.
  - Ch 10 (*The Barn*) — Theo takes the fish from his pocket after the barn encounter and holds it briefly; the fish is his private logic.
  - Ch 11 (*Crossing*) — Theo gives the fish to a woman with a cart heading toward the lake settlements at the river crossing; he gives it without explaining why; Lenn watches and does not stop him. The fish enters the gap between vignettes — its path to the raft is not narrated.
  - Ch 15 (*The Log*) — the fish is nailed to the raft's central mast, slightly crooked; Nadia notes it as she opens the logbook case; no community member knows its history.
  - Ch 16 (*Shore*) — visible from the lake while Nadia rows back with Tomás; she notes its shape, slightly crooked, at the top of the mast.
  - Ch 17 (*The Last Entry*) — Nadia writes about the fish in the logbook: "slightly crooked and always will be; I have never moved it and will not."
  - Ch 18–21 (*The Derelict*, Days 1–4) — the fish is present in every chapter on the mast, in Nadia's eastward angles, in the logbook; Ch 21 closes with Nadia standing at the eastern railing and the fish on the mast behind her.
- **Resolves in**: Ch 22 (*What We Keep*) — the book's last image: Nadia puts her hand on the fish. The wood under her palm: small, smooth, the grain of it, slightly warm from the morning light. The reader has assembled the full chain (Jesse → coat pocket → Theo → stranger → raft → mast → Nadia's hand); no character has. This asymmetry — reader knowledge versus character ignorance — is the proof of the book's argument about shared humanity.
- **What it's about underneath**: This thread is about the persistence of care across time and disconnection. No one coordinates the fish's journey; no plan sends it forward. It travels because people carry small made objects for a while and set them down. The fish is also the proof that all six casts are one humanity — the reader assembles this; the characters cannot. The fish arriving at the raft is not a triumph or a coincidence; it is just what happens when objects made with attention pass through the hands of people who still notice things.

## Thread 3: The Monster Gradient

- **Question**: Is it true that distance from the actual catastrophe is what turns people into monsters — and if so, who, finally, is the monster?
- **Enters**: Ch 01 (*The Wrong Sound*) — the infected are real, fast, and present; in Week One, with the real monster in the hallway, the worst human act is Amara opening the door out of compassion. The gradient begins: real monster present, human moral failure compassion-driven rather than cruelty-driven.
- **Advanced in**: Every chapter. Each vignette delivers a different answer and places a new data point on the gradient:
  - Chs 01–04 (V1, Week One): infected vivid and physical; worst human act is motivated by love.
  - Chs 05–07 (V2, Month Two): infected absent, only feared; worst human act is procedural compliance — fear licensed as policy.
  - Chs 08–11 (V3, Month Six): infected nowhere; every near-death from other people; fear needs no object now.
  - Chs 12–14 (V4, Year One): infected hundreds of miles away; the narrator has become the thing they feared and knows it and continues.
  - Chs 15–17 (V5, Year Two): the opposite of the gradient — no monster near, and the community is at its best; the counterargument.
  - Chs 18–22 (V6, Year Two): the unknown threat approaching; community turns on Mikkel before the ship arrives; the gradient's final proof.
- **Resolves in**: Ch 22 (*What We Keep*) — the Galena's hold contains a small number of weakened infected, sealed behind hatches dogged from the outside. The community could have assessed this together. They sent Mikkel south in a canoe three days before they knew. The monster was never the ship. The thesis's final word is in the structure, not the narration.
- **What it's about underneath**: This thread is the book's structural argument made visible as a reading experience. The infected gradient (vivid in V1 → absent in V2/V3/V4 → never seen in V5 → ambiguous in V6) is the mechanism; the escalating human cruelty is the proof. What the thread delivers at Ch 22 is not a statement but a completed pattern: the reader has watched six different casts at six different distances from the real threat, and the correlation is legible without being named. The narration must never state this argument — the thread's job is to make the structure speak.

## Thread 4: The Question of Goodness

*[Internal thread — a character question, not a plot question.]*

- **Question**: Has each person we follow managed to remain good — and is goodness something that can be insisted on, or is it what the collapse takes first?
- **Enters**: Ch 01 (*The Wrong Sound*) — Jesse's defining drive is introduced: he cannot leave a person behind even when he should. He knows this about himself and does not think it is a virtue. This is the thread's first instance, in its most innocent form: the collapse is six hours old and a fifteen-year-old is already measuring himself.
- **Advanced in**: Every chapter, with each vignette delivering a different answer and escalating the question:
  - Chs 01–04 (Jesse): **Yes, at cost.** He cannot not follow Amara. He gets the survivors out. He leaves the fish. He does not say her name yet.
  - Chs 05–07 (Daan Selle): **Trying, and losing.** He is praised for not flinching and accepts the praise. The notebook closes. He does not think about the woman after the entry is complete.
  - Chs 08–11 (Lenn): **Uncertain.** He stops the pack from being taken without hurting the other family — but Theo's question ("What were those people afraid of?") goes unanswered because the man knows the answer and knows it is not something Theo should hear confirmed. Theo's generosity at the river crossing is intact; the man's is not; the vignette ends on this distinction.
  - Chs 12–14 (Cass): **No — and continuing.** Cass's flat affect is a survival adaptation that has outlasted any emergency that required it. The crack appears in one short sentence and is sealed. The kid's act is the counterweight: the thread passes through the kid, unnamed, when he places himself between the crew and the basement door.
  - Chs 15–17 (Nadia, V5): **Yes — deliberately, earned, specific.** Nadia insists beauty and care are possible and the logbook is her proof. The chapter calls the community "evidence of what is possible." The answer is yes and it is not naive.
  - Chs 18–22 (Nadia, V6): **Fragile yes, at cost.** The sentences shorten. The warmth snags. She does not speak at the community meeting. She records what she did not say. The canoe drifts back empty. She does not have language for what she cannot undo. And then she puts her hand on the fish.
- **Resolves in**: Ch 22 (*What We Keep*) — elliptically. Nadia's hand on the fish is the book's closest approach to an answer: not that goodness always survives, not that trying is sufficient, but that it can be chosen again — even after the meeting where you did not speak, even with the empty canoe tied to the stern, even with the logbook that cannot hold what cannot be undone. The resolution is specific and physical, not propositional.
- **What it's about underneath**: This thread is the book's emotional arc — the sequence of answers (Jesse's yes, Daan's losing, Lenn's uncertain, Cass's no, Nadia's yes, Nadia's fragile yes) is the proof that the question cannot be settled globally and must be answered fresh by each person in each moment. The thread is internal because its resolution does not depend on any external event; it depends on what each POV character does with what the collapse has given them. The arc from Jesse's yes through Cass's no to Nadia's fragile hand on the fish is the book's argument about what it costs to try and why it is still worth trying.

### Dead-chapter check

No chapter is dead. Threads 3 and 4 advance in every chapter of the book; Threads 1 and 2 carry specific chapters inside V1, V3, V5, and V6 to a higher density. The chapters with the lowest thread-density (Chs 05–07, 12–14, 18–19) are all sustained by both their vignette arc and Threads 3 and 4, which is sufficient — their vignette arcs are themselves substantial short arcs with internal tension the reader is tracking.

### Deliberately-unresolved check

Thread 1 (**Reader of Names**) is explicitly and finally unresolved in Ch 22: the transmitter is present, the frequency matches, and the book closes without confirming the connection. The question "Is anyone out there, organizing?" is the question the collapse makes most urgent, and the book's answer is: *we do not know, and that is the honest answer, and someone should write it down.*

Thread 4 (**Question of goodness**) resolves elliptically rather than being refused — the final image (Nadia's hand on the fish) is the book's closest approach to an answer, but it is an image, not a proposition. This is not a refusal; it is a resolution by specific sensory detail, which is the book's formal register for its deepest arguments.
