# Characters

Characters are grouped by vignette. **No character appears as a named cast member in more than one vignette.** The single exception is Nadia Ruiz, whose POV spans both Vignette 5 (The Raft) and Vignette 6 (The Derelict) — these vignettes are simultaneous and form a continuous arc; she is the book's one returning consciousness. Connective tissue between all other vignettes is carried by objects and ideas, not people.

---

## Vignette 1 — The Drill (Week One)

### Jesse Okafor (POV)
Age 15, sophomore, Heron Falls High School. Compact, athletic, fast. His defining drive: he cannot leave a person behind even when he should. He knows this about himself and does not think it is a virtue. During the lockdown hours in the art room, he whittle a small fish from a broken chair-leg splinter — not to mark time, but because his hands need to be doing something while his mind refuses to panic. He is not brave; he is practical, and the two things look the same from outside. His final act in the vignette: he gets the surviving students out through the service exit and leaves the fish in a coat pocket near the door, because they are moving too fast to carry anything and the fish is only a fish.

*Voice: fast interior, sensory, athletic, afraid and managing it. Thoughts arrive as physical impulses before they arrive as language. "His legs moved before he decided to move."*

### Amara Soyinka
Age 16, Jesse's closest friend. Smart, decisive, accustomed to being the one who acts first and explains second. In Act 2 of the vignette, she opens a locked stairwell door because someone is pounding on it and crying. This is the most human possible choice. It lets the infected into the upper floors. She does not survive the vignette. She is not a villain. She is the thesis's first proof: the catastrophe the infected bring is terrible; what her death costs Jesse is something else.

### Coach Briggs
Age 44, PE teacher and weight-room supervisor. Takes charge in the first hour, projects authority, makes two decisions that cost lives: he locks the cafeteria doors with students inside to "contain" a situation he misreads, and he refuses to signal from the upper windows because "the protocol says no external communication." He survives the vignette. His survival is not presented as a lesson or an irony — it is simply what happens, and that is worse.

## Vignette 2 — The Line (Month Two)

### Private Daan Selle (POV)
Age 21, former second-year ecology student at a state university that no longer functions. He volunteered at the checkpoint in the first weeks because it seemed like the right thing to do, and because the alternative was being alone. He did not expect to still be here two months later with a rifle and a spiral notebook of rules for who may cross and who may not. His defining drive: to remain a reasonable person. He is losing this, one small compliance at a time. His interiority is bureaucratic — he notes, catalogs, postpones conclusions. He is not cruel; he is complicit, which the book argues is its own kind of cruelty.

*Voice: controlled, observational, the prose equivalent of standing very still. His inner life is a list of deferred reckonings.*

### Sergeant Cutter Pruett
Age 39, former county sheriff's deputy. Not cruel by temperament — genuinely frightened, and fear has become policy. The rules he has written for the Narrows checkpoint are reasonable on paper. Their application is not. He trusts Selle precisely because Selle does not challenge the rules, which Pruett reads as Selle understanding. He is wrong about this.

### The Woman with Children (unnamed)
A woman attempting to cross west with two small children, one of whom has a fever. The fever is a respiratory illness contracted in the evacuation camps — not the infection. She cannot prove this; she does not know the distinction matters to the checkpoint. She is turned back. What happens to her after she turns from the checkpoint is not shown. This is its own answer to the vignette's question.

## Vignette 3 — The Road South (Month Six)

### Lenn (POV — close third; often referred to as "the man")
Age 37, former high school chemistry teacher. He does not mention this unless pressed and it has not come up. He has been walking south with Theo for six months. His defining drive: to get the boy to warmth and relative safety, and to not become something Theo will remember as a monster. By the vignette's end, he is not certain he has succeeded at the second part. His inner life surfaces in his actions and silences rather than interior monologue; in the McCarthy register, the man does not explain himself even to himself.

*Voice: sparse, dry, undemonstrative. Feeling lives in what he does with his hands, what he eats, how fast or slowly he walks.*

### Theo
Age 9, Lenn's son. Does not remember much of before. He finds things on the road — a watch that stopped, a book about birds, the carved wooden fish in the pocket of an abandoned jacket in a roadside house on Day 3. He gives the fish to a stranger at a river crossing near the vignette's end: a woman with a loaded cart, heading toward the lake settlements. He does this without explaining why, and Lenn does not stop him. Theo notices what Lenn has stopped noticing.

*Voice: questions. Concrete observation without irony. The fish is the only thing in the vignette that moves in a hopeful direction.*

## Vignette 4 — The Sweep (Year One)

### Cass (POV — first person, present tense)
Age approximately 30. Gender is not specified in the narration; the prose does not invite the question. Cass joined the crew seven months ago and has not thought carefully about before since. Their defining drive: to keep moving, because stopping is when the accounting arrives. The flatness in Cass's voice is not sociopathy — it is a survival adaptation that has outlasted any emergency that required it. They are precise in physical description and evasive in moral description. Cracks arrive in single short sentences; Cass closes them immediately.

*Voice: first person, present tense. Short to medium sentences. Beautiful in the physical world and oblique in the moral one. The horror is in what is noted without comment.*

### Jute
The crew's de facto leader. Compact, efficient, has stopped apologizing and has stopped noticing that he has stopped. He does not explain what the crew does; anyone who needs an explanation is welcome to leave.

### Parish
The crew's third member. Talks constantly in a way that suggests he is keeping some internal counter from reaching zero. His chatter is the most disturbing thing in the vignette because it is cheerful.

### The Kid (unnamed)
Seventeen or eighteen; joined the crew one month before the vignette begins. He has not fully understood what the crew does. By the vignette's end, he does. What he does with that understanding is the vignette's final beat and must not be pre-empted by Cass's narration.

## Vignette 5 — The Raft (Year Two, spring)

### Nadia Ruiz (POV — also POV for Vignette 6)
Age 62, former high school librarian from a town that no longer exists. She has been on the raft for eight months. She keeps the logbook because she cannot not keep it — someone needs to write down that this happened, that people were here. Her defining drive: to insist that beauty and care remain possible, and to catch evidence of this in writing before it passes. She is not naïve; she has seen the burn marks on the lakeshore and knows what made them. In Vignette 5, her warmth is earned and deliberate. In Vignette 6, the same voice tightens into something watchful.

*Voice (Vignette 5): warm, deliberately calm, precise and affectionate in small observation. Sentences breathe. Beauty is insisted on.*
*Voice (Vignette 6): the same register, but the sentences get shorter as the Galena gets closer.*

### Gus Pereira
Age 54. Engineered the raft's structure over two winters, maintains the solar array, keeps the desalination rig going. He and Nadia share a warmth that is never named as romantic but is present in every scene they occupy together — a shorthand of long familiarity, which is remarkable given that they have known each other less than a year. In Vignette 6, under pressure, he begins to propose solutions the community did not think it would need to consider.

### Petra Halse
Age 27. Arrived from a refugee column, alone. She teaches the raft's four children every morning regardless of weather, using a waterproof notebook and whatever materials the raft has. She is the first to spot the Galena on the eastern horizon in Vignette 6. In the ensuing crisis, she becomes the argument for leaving — loading the small boats and clearing the area before the ship arrives.

### Tomás
Age 35, Nadia's logbook assistant and informal second voice. He rows the smaller johnboat on supply runs to the lakeshore. He is the first person in Vignette 6 to say aloud that the community needs to decide what to do before the ship arrives — not after. In the crisis, he becomes the argument for staying and waiting.

## Vignette 6 — The Derelict (Year Two, same week as Vignette 5)

*(The core cast is the same as Vignette 5. One new character enters.)*

### Nadia Ruiz (POV — continued from Vignette 5)
*(See above. Her register tightens with each chapter as the Galena closes the distance.)*

### Mikkel
Age unknown; the raft's most recent arrival, only three weeks before the Galena appears on the horizon. He came from the eastern shore. He has a useful skill (he is a former marine engine mechanic) and was admitted by the community's own rules. Once the Galena is sighted — coming from the east — some community members begin suggesting his arrival and the ship's are not a coincidence. He has no connection to the ship. This does not protect him. He becomes the focal point of the community's fear before the ship is close enough to board, which is the vignette's central moral event.
