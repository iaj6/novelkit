## Premise

The world ended unevenly. Somewhere a sickness turned people into something fast and wrong; somewhere else it was only ever a rumor that came over the radio and emptied the highways. Two years into the collapse, no one alive agrees on what the monster actually is — and that disagreement is the book's subject. Where the infected are real, they are the least of anyone's problems. Where they are only feared, the fear is enough to make ordinary people do the worst things they will ever do.

***Everyone Still Alive*** is a mosaic of **six vignettes**, each a separate cast in a separate corner of the same drowned, cold, lake-country apocalypse, ordered chronologically from Week One to Year Two. Each vignette answers the same question — *who is the monster?* — a different way, and the answers escalate and complicate each other. A schoolful of children overrun by something that climbs the walls. A bridge checkpoint where the men with rifles decide who is human enough to cross. A father and son walking south who never see an infected and are nearly killed three times by the living. A scavenger crew, told from inside, who have stopped pretending. A floating community of twenty people who have built something good out of garbage and water. And, drifting toward that fragile raft on a current it cannot escape, a wrecked ship that may or may not be carrying the thing everyone is most afraid of.

The vignettes do not share characters, but they share a world, a moving timeline, and three quiet objects that travel between them: a pirate-radio voice called **the Reader of Names** that reads the dead over the air each night; a small **carved wooden fish** that passes from hand to hand across two years and six casts; and the slowly answered question of whether the thing on the radio, the thing on the ship, and the thing the children saw in the stairwell are all the same thing.

The book argues — across all six — that the monster is whatever distance from the actual catastrophe lets people pretend they are still good.

## Length and shape

- Target length: ~70,000 words
- Number of chapters: 22 (grouped into 6 vignettes)

This is a **mosaic novel**. The structural unit is the **vignette**, not the chapter. Each vignette is a complete short arc — its own setup, escalation, and turn — with its own cast and its own register. The book-level arc is carried by the **escalating thesis** and the **connective threads**, not by any single character.

Vignette breakdown (chronological by depth into the collapse):

1. **The Drill** — 4 chapters — *Week One* — fast YA horror
2. **The Line** — 3 chapters — *Month Two* — institutional thriller
3. **The Road South** — 4 chapters — *Month Six* — literary-bleak / McCarthy register
4. **The Sweep** — 3 chapters — *Year One* — cold first-person, perpetrator POV
5. **The Raft** — 3 chapters — *Year Two (spring)* — warm, communal register
6. **The Derelict** — 5 chapters — *Year Two (the same week as #5)* — slow-dread maritime survival

Within each vignette, chapters flow continuously (a real short arc). **Between vignettes there is a hard cut** — new cast, new place, a jump forward in the timeline, and a register change. Each vignette opens with a depth-marker (e.g., *"Day 6"*, *"Month Two"*, *"Year Two"*).

## Voice

There is **no single voice.** The defining feature of this book is that the register **shifts per vignette**, deliberately and visibly, and the drafter must execute each shift. A base contract holds underneath (third person except where noted, concrete and physical, no genre winking, the catastrophe rendered through specific sensory detail rather than exposition) — but on top of that base each vignette tilts hard toward a named register:

- **The Drill** — fast, present-danger, dialogue-forward YA horror. Short sentences. Sensory and immediate. Cliffhangers. POV: a 15-year-old student.
- **The Line** — controlled institutional dread. Procedure rendered coldly; the horror is bureaucratic. Restrained, watchful. POV: a young guardsman at the checkpoint.
- **The Road South** — literary-bleak in the McCarthy register: long unspooling sentences, sparse unattributed dialogue, ash-and-cold imagery, no comfort. POV: a father (close third).
- **The Sweep** — cold **first-person**, present tense, a perpetrator who has stopped flinching. Flat affect that occasionally cracks. POV: a member of a scavenger crew.
- **The Raft** — warm, communal, slightly lyrical; the tonal valley of the book where humans are at their best. Plural sensibility (the community as near-character). POV: an older woman who keeps the raft's logbook.
- **The Derelict** — slow-dread maritime suspense, Hitchcock pacing: the threat is a thing approaching across water that cannot be outrun, and the people turning on each other before it arrives. POV: same logbook-keeper as #5, the dread tightening her warm register into something clenched.

**This per-vignette register map is the single most important instruction in this brief.** It is the experiment. Encode it aggressively into `canon/agent-guidance/drafter.md`.

## Audience and register

- **Target audience age range**: adult crossover (the book reads adult; *The Drill* vignette reads upper-YA and is allowed to). Not gated to one age — it's an adult literary-genre apocalypse with a YA-pitched opening movement.
- **Voice pace**: **varies by vignette** — fast (#1, #4), medium (#2, #6), slow (#3, #5). Do not normalize to one pace.
- **Reading-grade target**: ~8th grade for #1; ~10th–college for #3 and #5; medium elsewhere. Per-vignette, not uniform.
- **POV count intent**: **one anchor POV per vignette, six total across the book.** Within a vignette, single POV. Across the book, six discrete POVs that never share a chapter. This is NOT a four-POV ensemble braided per chapter — it is six sequential single-POV movements.
- **Chapter-ending convention**: **mixed by design, and tilted per vignette.** #1 ends chapters on cliffhangers; #3 ends on quiet bleak fades; #6 escalates dread toward its final chapters. Vignette-final chapters may land harder than mid-vignette chapters. Quiet endings welcome where a vignette wants them.

## Exemplar passages

The book lives across a *range* of registers, so these exemplars triangulate the range rather than pin one voice. Each vignette tilts toward one corner of this space; the drafter must be able to move between them.

**The bleak floor — *The Road*, Cormac McCarthy (opening), the register for Vignette 3:**

```
When he woke in the woods in the dark and the cold of the night he'd reach out
to touch the child sleeping beside him. Nights dark beyond darkness and the days
more gray each one than what had gone before. Like the onset of some cold glaucoma
dimming away the world.
```

**The warm communal register — comp: Emily St. John Mandel, *Station Eleven* (the Year-Twenty Traveling Symphony chapters), the register for Vignette 5.** *Cadence sketch (not a quote): present-tense calm, a community rendered through small shared rituals, beauty insisted on amid ruin, sentences that breathe. Survival as something people build together, not only endure.*

**The fast-horror ceiling — comp: Charlie Higson, *The Enemy* / Rick Yancey, *The 5th Wave* openings, the register for Vignette 1.** *Cadence sketch (not a quote): short declaratives, a teenager's sensory present tense, dialogue breaks early and often, the monster rendered by motion and sound before it's named, every paragraph closing a half-step nearer to danger.*

Comps for the remaining vignettes (for register only, no passage needed): **#2 The Line** — the checkpoint scenes of *The Road* + the cold proceduralism of Agustina Bazterrica's *Tender Is the Flesh*. **#4 The Sweep** — the lyrical-violent first person of Alden Bell's *The Reapers Are the Angels*. **#6 The Derelict** — *The Birds* (du Maurier) dread + maritime claustrophobia.

## Constraints

These are the rules that keep the mosaic from collapsing into one continuous story, and that keep the Threads / continuity phases working *with* the structure instead of against it. They are mandatory.

- **Six vignettes, six discrete casts, six POVs.** No character appears as a named cast member in more than one vignette. The connective tissue (below) is what crosses vignettes — not people. A character glimpsed at the edge of two vignettes (the same crew, the same rumor) is allowed and encouraged; a *shared protagonist* is forbidden.
- **One anchor POV per vignette, single-POV within the vignette.** Vignette 4 (*The Sweep*) is **first person, present tense**; Vignette 3 (*The Road South*) is close third in the McCarthy register; the rest are single close third. No chapter mixes POVs. No omniscient drift.
- **Register shifts per vignette and the shift must be legible.** A reader should feel the floor change underfoot when a new vignette begins. The drafter guidance must encode the per-vignette register map from the Voice section as hard direction, and the Reader phase must flag any vignette that has drifted toward the book's overall literary average instead of holding its assigned register.
- **Hard cut between vignettes.** Each vignette opens cold on a new cast, a new place, and a timeline depth-marker. No connective-tissue scene "introducing" the next vignette. The cuts are the form.
- **The thesis escalates and complicates; it does not simply repeat.** Each vignette gives a *different* answer to "who is the monster" (see the per-vignette table in Premise/Structure). #1: real monster, but a person's choice damns the kids. #2: people, using fear as license. #3: people, purely. #4: people, from inside the monster's own head. #5: people building the opposite of monstrous. #6: the unknown — and the community turning on itself before it knows. Do not let two vignettes land the same beat.
- **The infected are real in the world but rendered unevenly on purpose.** Vivid and physical in #1 (fast, agile, climbing walls and railings — the scariest thing the book contains). Absent and only feared in #2, #3, #4. Ambiguous / possibly-real in #6 (the central dread of the derelict ship). #5 never sees one. This gradient — *the closer the real monster, the less monstrous people are; the farther, the worse* — is a load-bearing argument. Keep the gradient intact.
- **Each vignette is a complete arc.** Setup, escalation, turn, and a real ending *inside its own chapters*. A vignette must satisfy on its own even though the book's larger meaning depends on the whole sequence. No vignette ends on a thread that only resolves in another vignette (the connective threads are the exception — see below).
- **No DM-coded or genre-winking prose.** No mechanics, no zombie-movie meta, no fourth wall. The word "zombie" is used by characters in fear and rumor, never by the narration as a label.
- **The ending is the derelict.** Vignette 6 closes the book. What is on the ship is specific and earned, not a generic reveal — and whatever it is, it delivers the thesis one final time. The flotilla's decision (what they do before and as the ship arrives) matters more than the contents. The last image of the book belongs to *The Derelict*.

## Structure — how each phase must handle the mosaic

outline for a mosaic. Treat it as operative direction. -->

**Architect** — build canon for a mosaic, not a single story:
- `canon/world.md` is an **umbrella**: the shared apocalypse (the cold lake country, the uneven collapse, the timeline from Week One to Year Two, the ambiguous nature of the infected) PLUS a short sub-section per vignette for that vignette's specific micro-setting (the school, the bridge, the southbound road, the scavenged towns, the raft, the water).
- `canon/characters.md` is **grouped by vignette**, each group with its POV character first, and an explicit note that casts do not cross vignettes.
- `canon/continuity.md` is **split into two parts**: (A) **World invariants** that hold across every vignette (the timeline, the nature/uneven-distribution of the infected, the three connective objects/threads, the geography) and (B) **per-vignette local facts**. Downstream continuity checks should treat per-vignette facts as scoped to that vignette and only enforce World invariants book-wide.
- `canon/themes.md` carries the thesis ("who is the monster" / distance-from-catastrophe-makes-monsters) as the book's spine.
- `canon/agent-guidance/drafter.md` must encode the **per-vignette register map** as its central content — this is the project's whole reason for existing.

**Plotter** — `outline/00-chapter-map.md` must be **grouped into six labeled vignette blocks**, each block a self-contained arc with its own setup/turn/ending, each chapter tagged with its vignette, its POV, and its register tilt. Number chapters continuously (01–22) but label the vignette boundaries clearly. Vary chapter shape and ending mode *per the vignette's assigned register*, not uniformly across the book.

**Threads** — the **connective threads ARE the book-spanning threads**, and they are what make the weave check pass for a mosaic. Use these (plus any you find):
1. **The Reader of Names** — the pirate-radio voice reading the dead each night. Heard in most vignettes (a kid's dying phone in #1, a wind-up radio in #3, a working set on the raft in #5/#6). Question: is anyone organizing a recovery, or is it one lonely person reading names into the dark — and is the broadcaster connected to the derelict ship? Resolve or deliberately refuse in #6.
2. **The carved wooden fish** — a small object that travels hand-to-hand across two years and six casts (whittled by the boy in #1, found by the father in #3, ends nailed to the raft's mast in #6). The book's proof that all six casts are one humanity across time. Internal/thematic thread.
3. **The thesis question itself** — "who is the monster?" — entered in #1, advanced and complicated by each vignette, given its final word in #6. At least this thread is *internal*, and the book's structure requires it to advance in **every** chapter.
4. **The collapse timeline / the gradient** — the dated spine (Week One → Year Two) and the argument that distance from the real monster is when people turn monstrous. Advanced by every vignette's placement on the timeline.

For the **weave check**, treat each chapter as advancing *either* a connective thread *or* its vignette's local arc — a chapter deep inside a vignette that advances only that vignette's internal tension is **not** a dead chapter (the vignette arc is its thread). At least one connective thread (the thesis, #3 above) must touch every chapter. The Reader-of-Names thread must have its resolution **deliberately refused or only half-answered** — the book should not hand over a clean answer about whether anyone is really out there.

## Genre touchpoints (for register)

A literary-genre mosaic apocalypse. Closest structural comps: David Mitchell's *Cloud Atlas* and Emily St. John Mandel's *Station Eleven* for the mosaic-across-time architecture and the way separate casts rhyme into one argument; Max Brooks's *World War Z* for the "the catastrophe told as discrete human accounts" instinct (but rendered as continuous prose vignettes, not interviews). The per-vignette registers reach from McCarthy's *The Road* (bleak floor) through Mandel's warmth (communal valley) to Higson/Yancey YA-horror pace (fast ceiling). Not grimdark-for-its-own-sake; not a monster-action novel (the infected are sparingly used and load-bearing); not a single-protagonist survival arc. The monster is the thesis, the vignettes are the proof, and the form — the hard cuts, the register shifts, the objects that travel — is the meaning.
