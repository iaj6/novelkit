# Calibration Sample — Iter 1
*Chapter 01: The Wrong Sound · Vignette 1 · Jesse Okafor POV · fast YA horror register*

---

The fire alarm gave two short pulses and stopped.

Jesse looked up from his geometry worksheet. That was the wrong pattern. The drill was three long, one short, three long. Two short meant something else, and for a second nobody in the room moved, because two short wasn't in the handbook and everyone knew it.

The PA crackled. Principal Warburg's voice started and cut off in the middle of a word.

Jesse was already looking at Amara. She was already standing.

"Art room," she said.

He grabbed his backpack by reflex. He wasn't sure why. He wasn't sure he needed it, but his hands needed something to do.

The second-floor corridor was quiet for the three seconds it took them to reach it. Then it wasn't.

Jesse heard the sound before he saw anything. Feet, but wrong — the rhythm too quick and the pace too even, like something that hadn't learned when to slow down. He turned toward the far end of the corridor. The fluorescent lights were doing their usual flicker near the trophy case. The silhouette at corridor's end was moving fast and at an angle that no person moved.

His body turned before he decided to turn.

The air smelled like copper and something warm underneath that.

"North stairs," Amara said. She was already ahead of him.

They ran. Seven other kids fell in behind them — Jesse didn't know who, didn't look back, just heard the sneakers and the breathing. The north stairwell door banged open against the wall. Ms. Kwan — the substitute, the geometry substitute, she'd been writing review problems on the board twenty seconds ago — caught the door on the backswing and dragged it shut.

Third floor. The art room had a reinforced door. Chair-ledges, high windows, a clear view of the parking lot. Jesse had noted all this without knowing he was noting it.

He got the door. Amara got the rolling tool cabinet. Ms. Kwan got her hands on a stack of ceramic stools and her hands were shaking and Jesse didn't say anything about that, just moved the first stool for her and then the second.

Nine people. Inside. Door blocked.

From the parking lot below: ordinary asphalt. A car with the driver's door standing open. Nobody near it.

From somewhere below them: sound. Not a person in distress. Faster than distress, and rhythmic in the wrong way.
