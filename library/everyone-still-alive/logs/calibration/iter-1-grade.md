## Compared sample against brief anchors

**Sample**: Chapter 01 "The Wrong Sound," Vignette 1 — The Drill, Jesse Okafor POV, fast YA horror.

**Anchor comps used**: Charlie Higson's *The Enemy* / Rick Yancey's *The 5th Wave* (opening chapters), as named in the brief's exemplar section. The cadence sketch in the brief specifies: short declaratives, a teenager's sensory present tense, dialogue breaks early and often, the monster rendered by motion and sound before it is named, every paragraph closing a half-step nearer to danger.

**Sentence-length target**: 10–14 word average per the drafter guidance.

The base rhythm is solidly short. The few longer sentences appear as deliberate bursts:

These outliers are not the base rhythm; they are the exceptions. The mean sentence length across the sample is comfortably inside the 10–14 word target.

## What the sample does well

- **Opening is action-first and situational.** "The fire alarm gave two short pulses and stopped." — no abstract observation, no orienting context dump, no interiority. Jesse's body is in the scene from the first word. Honors the chapter-opening convention exactly.
- **Infected rendered correctly by motion, then proximity-sensation, never clinically.** "The rhythm too quick and the pace too even, like something that hadn't learned when to slow down" (motion). "The air smelled like copper and something warm underneath that" (proximity sensation). The word "zombie" is absent. The narration never labels it. Fully correct.
- **Short declarative rhythm is authentic to the comp register.** Runs of 3–4 declaratives throughout — "He got the door. Amara got the rolling tool cabinet." / "Nine people. Inside. Door blocked." — land with Higson-style punchy panic.
- **Dialogue breaks early and is sparse-functional.** "Art room." / "North stairs." Characters speak because they are afraid, not because they have considered things. Exactly as guided.
- **Chapter-ending mode is dread-cliffhanger.** "From somewhere below them: sound. Not a person in distress. Faster than distress, and rhythmic in the wrong way." — the monster is confirmed below the barricaded group and the chapter cuts there. Honoring the cliffhanger / mid-action-cut requirement.

## What the sample drifts on

- **One slightly literary meta-cognition line.** "Jesse had noted all this without knowing he was noting it." — this is a single sentence and sits at the high end of tolerable interiority for this vignette. It works, barely, because it replaces a paragraph of interior observation with one compact move. It is not a drift-level failure, but worth monitoring if it becomes a recurring construction.
- **Two longer sentences in the 29–31-word range.** Explained above — both are contextually earned. Not a rhythm failure, but the drafter should be cautious not to let these become the norm in subsequent chapters.
