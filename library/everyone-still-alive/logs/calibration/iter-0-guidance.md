# Drafter Guidance — *Everyone Still Alive*

## The central instruction: this book has six registers, not one

The single most important instruction in this document is that **each vignette operates a different prose register, and the shift must be legible.** The pipeline's default attractor — literary adult fiction, long sentences, deep interiority, restrained subtext — is correct for one vignette only (#3, The Road South). For the other five, the drafter must actively tilt away from it. The per-vignette register map below is the whole reason this guidance file exists.

When you receive a chapter to draft, identify its vignette first. Then execute the register assigned to that vignette — not the register that feels natural, not a compromise between the vignette's register and literary default, but the assigned register, held.

---

## Per-Vignette Register Map

### Vignette 1 — The Drill (Chapters 1–4): fast YA horror

**Voice:** Jesse Okafor, 15, close third past tense. Athletic, sensory, immediate. His body knows before his brain does.

**Sentence-length target:** Short — averaging 10–14 words. Run of 3–4 short declaratives is the correct rhythm, not a tic to vary away from. A longer sentence is earned; it is not the default.

**Dialogue density:** High. Characters speak because they are afraid, not because they have things to say. Dialogue breaks early in scenes. Tags are minimal.

**The infected:** Rendered first by motion and sound at the end of a corridor. Then by proximate sensation (heat, smell, wrong gait). Never by clinical description. The word "zombie" is allowed in a character's terrified mouth; not in narration.

**Chapter-opening convention:** Action or dialogue, first paragraph. No abstract observation. Situational — where is Jesse's body, what is his body sensing.

**Chapter-ending convention:** Cliffhangers or mid-action cuts. All four chapters. No literary fades in this vignette.

**Anti-defaults for this vignette:** Do not open on extended interiority. Do not let interior observation run more than 2–3 sentences without a concrete sensory event or action. Do not use long subordinate sentences as the base rhythm. The literary attractor will try to deepen and slow this vignette — resist it.

### Vignette 2 — The Line (Chapters 5–7): institutional dread

**Voice:** Private Daan Selle, 21, close third past tense. Controlled, watchful. His interiority is bureaucratic — he notes, catalogs, defers conclusions.

**Sentence-length target:** Medium — averaging 15–22 words. Precision matters more than brevity. Sentences that itemize or enumerate are appropriate.

**Dialogue density:** Sparse and procedural. Checkpoint language — orders, criteria, formal queries. When Selle's inner voice intrudes, it reads like annotation of the official record, not personal meditation.

**The infected:** Absent. They exist as the reason the checkpoint exists, nothing more. Do not invent infected encounters or sightings in this vignette — that would break the gradient.

**Chapter-opening convention:** Procedure. A task Selle is performing, a rule he is applying, a log entry he is writing. The horror is in the ordinary work.

**Chapter-ending convention:** Mid-beat or quietly terrible. The dread seeps; it does not break. The final chapter ends on a decision that has already been made, rendered without drama.

**Anti-defaults for this vignette:** Do not let Selle have an emotional crisis that names its emotions. His complicity must arrive through what he does, not through what he feels or articulates about feeling. Do not inject warmth or community into this vignette — it is designed to be cold.

### Vignette 3 — The Road South (Chapters 8–11): literary-bleak / McCarthy register

**Voice:** Lenn (close third, often "the man"), past tense. Inner life surfaces in action and silence, not interior monologue.

**Sentence-length target:** Long — the base rhythm is the long unspooling sentence with internal subordination and repetition of cadence units. Average 20–35 words per sentence is appropriate. Short sentences appear for emphasis, not as the rhythm.

**Dialogue:** Sparse, unattributed — indent only, no tags. Characters do not respond in ways that feel like they have heard each other. Dialogue is adjacent utterances, not exchange.

**The infected:** Absent. Completely. The man and the boy never encounter one. They are nearly killed three times by other people. Do not introduce the infected into this vignette.

**Imagery:** Ash, cold, wet wood, the indifference of the physical world. The smelting plant's ash-sky on Day 3. November rain turning to snow by the vignette's end. The cold is a character; give it weight.

**Chapter-opening convention:** Sensory — where are they in the world, what does the world smell and feel like. Not interior thought; physical ground.

**Chapter-ending convention:** Quiet bleak fades. The last sentence of every chapter in this vignette should land like a door closing — not with drama, with quietness.

**Anti-defaults for this vignette:** Do not revert to short declaratives — that is Vignette 1's rhythm. Do not give Lenn internal monologue that explains what he is feeling. Do not introduce comfort that is not immediately under threat of being taken. Read the McCarthy exemplar passage in the brief before drafting any chapter of this vignette.

---

### Vignette 4 — The Sweep (Chapters 12–14): cold first-person perpetrator

**Voice:** Cass, **first person, present tense** — the only vignette in this tense and person. Flat, precise in the physical world, evasive in the moral world.

**Sentence-length target:** Short to medium — averaging 12–18 words. Paragraphs are tight. No lyrical sprawl.

**The key register move:** The horror is in what is noted without commentary. A detail that the reader understands as evidence of atrocity is recorded in the same flat register as a note about the weather. Cass does not editorialize. The narration does not editorialize. Occasional cracks — a single short sentence that nearly acknowledges what has been happening — arrive and are immediately re-covered. The reader is never handed the full moral account; only the receipts.

**Gender of Cass:** Unspecified. The prose does not invite the question. Do not assign pronouns beyond "they" used generically, or simply structure sentences to avoid the issue.

**Chapter-opening convention:** Immediate action or observation. The crew is doing something. We join it mid-motion.

**Chapter-ending convention:** Flat, cold, occasionally cracked. One chapter may end on a crack that Cass does not close — but only one.

**Anti-defaults for this vignette:** Do not give Cass a redemptive inner life. Do not let the narration condemn or excuse. Do not let Parish or Jute be cartoonish villains — their ordinariness is the horror. Do not write "I did not feel anything" — that is the sentence-after-the-sentence. Just describe what Cass does.

### Vignette 5 — The Raft (Chapters 15–17): warm communal

**Voice:** Nadia Ruiz, 62, close third past tense. Warm, deliberately calm, precise and affectionate in small observation. The community is a near-character.

**Sentence-length target:** Variable — longer sentences for communal scenes, shorter for Nadia's close observation. No sentence-length rule governs this vignette the way rules govern Vignettes 1 and 3. Rhythm follows content.

**Dialogue density:** Medium-high. Community members talk — about work, about weather, about small plans. Dialogue is characterful and warm without being saccharine.

**The infected:** Never seen; never encountered; mentioned only in the context of what they used to be afraid of. Do not introduce infected encounters in this vignette.

**The plural:** Use "they," "the community," "the raft's people" when Nadia's close third widens to include the group. This is not a POV violation; it is the vignette's communal register at work.

**Chapter-opening convention:** A shared ritual — the morning count, a meal, the logbook entry, the sound of the greenhouse door. The community in the ordinary motion of its day.

**Chapter-ending convention:** Resolved beats or quiet lyrical fades. Earned rest is allowed. This is the book's tonal valley — warmth is correct here.

**Anti-defaults for this vignette:** Do not let this vignette drift into sentimentality (the community is not perfect; it has frictions; Nadia notices them with warmth, not blindness). Do not introduce any dread in this vignette — that is Vignette 6's job. The carved fish is on the mast; Nadia sees it but does not know its history.

---

### Vignette 6 — The Derelict (Chapters 18–22): slow-dread maritime suspense

**Voice:** Nadia Ruiz, same close third — but the register tightens with each chapter as the Galena closes distance.

**The register shift within the vignette:** Vignette 5's Nadia writes warmly and with ease. Vignette 6's Nadia writes the same warmth, but it keeps snagging on what is out on the water. By Chapter 21, the warmth is strained. By Chapter 22, the prose is spare and clenched. This is a gradual shift executed across five chapters, not a sudden switch.

**Sentence-length target:** Begins at Vignette 5 pace; shortens with each chapter as the Galena approaches. By Chapter 22, sentences average 10–16 words.

**The Galena must be present in every chapter** of this vignette, even when not named — in the angle of Nadia's observations, in what people choose not to say, in the quality of light off the eastern water at evening.

**Hitchcock pacing:** The reader understands the threat before the community acts on it. The horror is in watching characters discuss ordinary things with a ship on the horizon. Do not resolve the suspense early. Do not let any character definitively answer what is on the Galena before the final chapter.

**Chapter-opening convention:** Starts at Chapter 18 with a Vignette-5-style communal opening. By Chapter 20, openings are on the Galena — its position, its drift, its silhouette.

**Chapter-ending convention:** Escalating. Chapter 18: a resolved or calm beat. Chapter 19: a note of dread. Chapter 20: the community fracturing. Chapter 21: a turn. Chapter 22: the book's last image.

**The final image:** Must deliver the thesis one final time. The flotilla's decision (what they do to each other before the ship arrives — specifically, what happens to Mikkel) matters more than what is on the ship. Whatever is aboard the Galena must be specific and earned, consistent with everything the infected gradient has established.

**Anti-defaults for this vignette:** Do not resolve the Reader of Names mystery. Do not confirm definitively that the Galena carries infected — leave it in the register of the possible. Do not give the final chapter a comfort ending.

## POV Strategy

One anchor POV per vignette. Six POVs total across the book. No chapter mixes POVs. No omniscient drift. No POV character appears in another vignette's chapters as a named cast member. Vignettes 5 and 6 share Nadia Ruiz; this is the one exception and it is deliberate.

Within each vignette, if the POV character is in a scene with others, free indirect discourse is permitted — but do not slip into another character's interiority within the same chapter.

---

## Anti-Defaults — What the Drafter Must Actively Avoid Across All Vignettes

The literary attractor produces the following failures in this specific manuscript. The drafter must treat these as active warnings:

1. **Thesis articulation in narration.** No sentence beginning "The real monster was" / "What he was becoming" / "This was the thing that the catastrophe had done." Show; do not invoice.

2. **Vignette-opening context dumps.** Each vignette opens cold. Do not write an orienting paragraph that explains who we are, where we are, and what has happened. Drop into situation.

3. **Register averaging.** If a draft passage from Vignette 1 could plausibly be from Vignette 3, the register has averaged. Stop and reset to the assigned register.

4. **Extended interiority in short-register vignettes.** In Vignettes 1 and 4, interior observation runs 2–3 sentences maximum before a sensory event or action interrupts it.

5. **Explanatory tags after infected description.** "Something that used to be a person" — do not follow with a sentence elaborating on how it used to be human. The image does that work.
