# Developmental Editor's Letter — *Everyone Still Alive*

---

## Overall Impression

At its best, *Everyone Still Alive* does what the brief proposed and what very few manuscripts actually achieve: it holds six distinct registers simultaneously and makes the form itself carry the argument. The mosaic architecture — the hard cuts, the tonal range from Higson-fast in Vignette 1 to McCarthy-bleak in Vignette 3 to maritime dread in Vignette 6 — is not merely a structural choice but a proof: the reader assembles the thesis from the fragments the way the book's survivors assemble knowledge from radio signals and road signs. When it works, the hard cut from Vignette 4's "I look at Jute's hands" to Vignette 5's "T. singing while he works. A good sign." is not a tonal pivot but an argument — this is what coexists in the world, these six simultaneous truths about who the monster is. That is a difficult thing for a novel to do, and this one largely does it.

---

## What's Working

**1. The per-vignette register discipline holds across all six vignettes.** This is the book's central formal challenge and its primary success. Vignette 1's short declaratives and body-first interiority ("his legs moved before he decided to move") are consistently distinct from Vignette 3's unspooling subordinate cadences and unattributed dialogue. Vignette 4's first-person present tense never slips; Vignette 5's communal plural is calibrated without losing Nadia's close-third anchor. No vignette averages toward the literary-adult default. A reader will feel the floor change underfoot with each cut — this is the book's whole reason for existing, and it is achieved.

**2. Amara's death in Chapter 2 lands as tragedy, not horror-movie stupidity.** This is the brief's hardest single beat and the thesis's first proof. The sound through the wall — "crying... not hysteria — not fresh, not new" — establishes the moral plausibility of what Amara is about to do before she does it. Jesse's "Don't" carrying "they both understood this" is exactly right: not ignorance on her part, not failure of will on his, but the unavoidable fact that compassion is the right human response in the wrong situation. The vignette's argument rests on this beat holding, and it holds.

**3. The Reader of Names thread enters and exits correctly, and the refusal to resolve it is the correct formal move.** The dying phone at 1% battery cutting off mid-name in Chapter 3 is the thread's defining statement about itself: testimony interrupted, the dead named into an indifferent dark, the question of whether anyone is organizing never raised because the characters have more urgent things to worry about. Chapter 22's transmitter on the correct frequency — present, power dead, connection neither confirmed nor denied — is exactly what this thread requires. "I don't know. I have written that I don't know." The refusal is the meaning, and the book delivers it.

**4. Vignette 4 (*The Sweep*) is the manuscript's most technically precise execution.** Cass's voice does what the Alden Bell register requires: physically precise in the external world, oblique and evasive in the moral one, the horror in what is noted without comment. The street-naming system (Parish's cheerful Hammer Street, Propane, Jam, Receipt) is the cleverest structural move in the vignette — a portrait of someone who has stopped weighing the world, rendered in the act of naming it. The basement chapter (Chapter 14) is the best scene in the book: the padlock sealed from the outside (misdirection and revelation simultaneously), the child's face at the top of the stairs, the sound of the cut shackle finding its seat described as "small and specific," and then "I look at Jute's hands" — stopping exactly where the narration must. Nothing is over-explained; nothing is withheld past the point of functioning.

**5. The carved wooden fish completes its journey cleanly and without sentiment.** The fish enters in Chapter 3 as what Jesse's hands do while his mind refuses to panic; it exits Chapter 4 in a grey wool peacoat pocket without a backward look. Its arrival in Theo's hands in Chapter 8 (the right pocket of the right abandoned jacket on Day Three of the walk) passes without significance to either character. Chapter 11's wordless gift — Theo pressing the pocket once with the flat of the woman's hand, the man watching without stopping him — is the vignette's thematic payoff at the correct pitch. And Chapter 22's final image earns it: the physical inventory of the wood (palm-sized, grain, the asymmetric tail, the narrow eye) earns the warmth of "slightly warm from the morning light" before the seven final words. The reader has assembled the chain from Jesse's supply closet to Nadia's palm; no character has. This asymmetry is intact and is the book's proof of its argument about shared humanity.

**6. The register tightening of Nadia's voice across Vignette 6 is technically precise.** Chapter 17's logbook runs full paragraphs of warmth (the killdeer identified by call and confirmed by notebook, Gus's face at "full capacity," the community written as "evidence"). Chapter 19's entries are measurably clipped. Chapter 20 breaks logbook format entirely. Chapter 21 reduces to five sparse lines and "Done." and then a sentence that cannot complete itself. Chapter 22's formal entry is four lines and a period. This is not announced; it is felt. The close-third and the logbook voices have converged to the same sparseness by the final chapter, which is the formal equivalent of the argument the book has been building for 22 chapters. This tightening is achieved without any loss of Nadia's distinctiveness as a consciousness.

## What Isn't Working

**One factual contradiction in Vignette 2 needs resolution.** In Chapter 5, Selle explicitly tells an anxious traveler "We don't take names" — a specific, policy-level statement delivered as direct response to a man's anxiety about documentation. Two chapters later, in Chapter 7, Selle opens his intake procedure with "Your name, please" and records the woman as "Marta Solak" in the crossing log. No shift in procedure is noted; no explanation is offered. This is a provable factual contradiction within a single vignette between temporally adjacent chapters, and because "Marta Solak" is the only named traveler in the vignette, the departure is conspicuous. Either the "no names" exchange in Chapter 5 must be adjusted, or Chapter 7's intake must return to the established policy. This is the manuscript's one critical-level issue; both fix directions are available, and a human needs to decide which version serves the vignette's argument better — the impersonality of the checkpoint (which favors keeping the no-names policy) or the particular cruelty of recording the woman's name before turning her back (which favors the Chapter 7 version).

**One passage in Chapter 11 runs hotter with explicit interior reasoning than the McCarthy register allows.** In the final section of "Crossing," the man reasons through Theo's act across four paragraphs of articulated inner argument: the thought shaped like a comfort, the setting it down, the wrong-ness of the thought, the conclusion that "the road could not reach what the boy did with his hands at a crossing. Not had not yet. Could not." This is necessary emotional weight at the vignette's close, but it is delivered as sustained explicit reasoning where the McCarthy register keeps interior feeling in actions and silences. The distinction is meaningful and earns its weight — but it is a softening of the register, and a revision that distributes this realization across physical displacement (what the man does with his hands, how he adjusts his pace, what he looks at on the road south) rather than thought-sequence would serve the vignette better.

**A single sentence in Chapter 15 glosses behavior the logbook entries are already demonstrating.** "She was a person who wrote down what she saw" names the behavior the preceding logbook entries have already enacted. This is the style guide's "sentence-after-the-sentence" failure mode in mild form — one sentence, medium concern — but it is the only place in Vignette 5 where the narration steps outside Nadia's practiced restraint to editorialize about itself. The sentence can be cut without loss.

## Pacing Assessment

The book paces correctly for its architecture. Vignette 1 is tight and propulsive. Vignette 2 is correctly slower — the escalating cases (establishing norms, borderline physical and respiratory, definitive fever and family) give the vignette a ratchet structure that earns its three-chapter length. Vignette 3 is accumulative and cold; the slight surface-area problem in Chapter 11 (noted above) is the act's only drag. Vignette 4 escalates cleanly from farmhouse sweep to hardware store to church basement. Vignette 5 breathes deliberately and earns the space it takes — the warmth is specific enough that it does not feel like a break from the book but like the book's argument about what is still possible. Vignette 6 accelerates through compressed daily chapters and ends quickly, correctly: the boarding is efficient, the inventory of the fish is measured, and the final image does not linger.

There is no midbook sag. The act structure does have a tonal valley — Chapter 17 is the book's warmest point and is structurally intended to be — and readers who came for the dread will feel the pause. This is the book's architectural argument, not a pacing failure: the warmth is the counter-evidence before the final proof.

## Character Assessment

**Jesse Okafor** feels alive and specific — the body-knowledge before cognition, the practical courage that looks like bravery from outside, the fish left without looking back. His arc is complete and earned within its four chapters.

**Amara Soyinka** is on the page for one chapter and does the book's hardest work in it. She functions as the thesis's first proof rather than a full character, and this is exactly correct.

**Private Daan Selle** is fully realized through the bureaucratic interiority: he notes, catalogs, defers. The complicity arrives through what he does and what he does not think about. He is the book's most quietly terrible character.

**Lenn** holds the McCarthy register as a character as much as a voice: sparse, undemonstrative, feeling lived in what he does with his hands. Theo's open curiosity and generosity (the fish, the question "But not us") serve as the vignette's moral counterweight, and their contrast is the vignette's argument.

**Cass** is precisely executed — the perpetrator-narrator's flatness is a survival adaptation that has outlasted its emergency, the cracks arrive in single short sentences and are immediately sealed. The unnamed kid's act in the basement is correctly left to action and one specific sound; the narration stops where it must.

**Nadia Ruiz** carries two vignettes and sustains both. The V5 warmth is earned and deliberate; the V6 tightening is measurable chapter by chapter. She is the book's most inhabited consciousness — the logbook is her form and her argument, and both survive intact through Chapter 22. Her "I have never not had language" in Chapter 21's italics is the book's best single sentence.

## Thread Assessment

**Thread 1 (The Reader of Names):** Develops across the correct vignettes, heard privately by the right characters at the right moments (the dying phone, the shortwave alone at midnight, the transcription because "someone should"), and deliberately unresolved in Chapter 22 with the transmitter present and unconfirmed. The refusal is the meaning and it is executed.

**Thread 2 (The Carved Wooden Fish):** The complete chain from Jesse's supply closet through Theo's roadside house through the stranger at the river crossing to the raft's mast to Nadia's hand is assembled by the reader across the 22-chapter span without any character having access to the full chain. The asymmetry — reader knowledge versus character ignorance — is preserved from entry through final image. No over-articulation; no moment where any character approaches recognizing the fish's full history.

**Thread 3 (The Monster Gradient):** The infected gradient is intact across all six vignettes: vivid and physical in V1 only; absent and only feared in V2–4; the counter-argument in V5 (no monster near, community at its best); ambiguous in V6. Chapter 22's sealed holds — "slow. Irregular. Not many — three, perhaps four" — deliver the thesis's final word through structure: the community did its worst before the ship arrived, and the community turned on Mikkel before it knew anything. The monster was never the ship. This is delivered in image and structure, not narration. The word "zombie" does not appear in narration; the thesis is never stated.

**Thread 4 (The Question of Goodness):** The escalating sequence of answers — Jesse's yes at cost; Selle's losing; Lenn's uncertain; Cass's no, continuing; Nadia's earned yes; Nadia's fragile yes with the empty canoe tied to the stern — is the book's emotional arc and it is fully delivered. The final image's answer (the hand on the fish, not a proposition but a specific physical act in a specific morning light) is the correct resolution — elliptical rather than refused, answering with an image what the narration correctly declined to answer with a sentence.

## Voice Assessment

The prose holds its six registers without averaging toward a literary-adult default. This is the manuscript's central formal achievement. The only voice note worth raising is a single long, subordinate-clause-rich sentence in Chapter 2 that performs psychological analysis in a register otherwise committed to body-knowledge before cognition. It is one sentence, it is doing real structural work (the mutual understanding between Jesse and Amara is load-bearing), and it is not a drift — but it sits outside the chapter's sentence register and a revision could render the same understanding more physically.

The "she wrote in the logbook / she wrote in the running account" construction appears at high frequency as a section-transitional gesture across Chapters 19–21. The logbook-voice is explicitly a lean-into pattern in the style guide (see *Style — Lean-Into Patterns*), and the tag is performing necessary structural work — establishing each writing act as an event, tracking the tightening of Nadia's prose control alongside the Galena's approach. This is not a tic to vary away from. Protect it from compression.

One line in Chapter 20's "What I did not say" list approaches thesis-statement territory: *"That fear is a reason and is not the same as a reason."* It is inside the logbook-voice container (first person, italics, private self-accounting), and the lean-into pattern for logbook-voice paragraphs applies. It is at the boundary — the closest the manuscript comes to direct articulation of the structural argument — but it is inside the correct formal container and should be left alone. Any revision pass that touches this section should be aware it is at the boundary.

## Suggested Revisions

**Required:** Resolve the "no names" / "Marta Solak" contradiction between Chapters 5 and 7. Decide which version is canonical and adjust the other accordingly. This is a factual contradiction, not a tonal concern, and it will be visible to any attentive reader of Vignette 2.

**Recommended:** In Chapter 11, redistribute the man's reasoning about Theo's gift from articulated inner argument to physical displacement — what his hands do, how he walks, what he looks at rather than thinks. The conclusion ("the road could not reach what the boy did with his hands at a crossing") can remain, but the four-paragraph reasoning-through that precedes it is too warm for the McCarthy register's emotional method.

**Minor:** Remove or restructure the single sentence in Chapter 15 that glosses the logbook entries ("She was a person who wrote down what she saw"). The logbook entries enact this; the gloss undoes their work.

## What to Leave Alone

**Chapter 4's quiet close.** Jesse not looking back, not saying Amara's name — this is a literary fade at the close of a vignette the brief specifically said could end differently than its mid-vignette chapters. The restraint (the fish in the coat pocket, the street empty and ordinary, the name withheld) is the grief and the grief is the argument. Do not push toward resolution.

**The three consecutive quiet bleak fades ending Chapters 9, 10, and 11.** These are register compliance, not ending-mode uniformity. The McCarthy register specifies quiet bleak fades; three of them in a row is the vignette holding its form, not the drafter defaulting. The imagery is distinct in each — ash-sky/arm-tired/no-crank; smooth-white-road/no-record; walking-south-fish-gone — so there is no repetition of image, only consistency of mode. The brief gave explicit permission for this.

**The basement scene's final line** ("I look at Jute's hands"). The narration stops exactly where it must. The violence is not shown; the horror is in the stopping. Any revision that adds clarification or continuation would undo the chapter's central formal achievement.

**Vignette 5's warmth.** The Raft is the book's counter-evidence and it must earn its warmth, not apologize for it. The spinach dispute resolved at dinner, the killdeer named, Mikkel integrated, Gus's face at "full capacity" — these are not generic comfort; they are the book's argument that the opposite of monstrous is achievable. Do not let any editor read this warmth as tonal slackening. It is the book's case for the defense, and it is specific and particular and correct.

**The last seven words of Chapter 22.** "Her hand on the fish." The physical inventory that precedes it (the grain, the asymmetric tail, the narrow eye, the morning warmth) earns the contact. The sentence declines to summarize what 22 chapters have already proven. It is the correct ending for this book. Protect it.

The manuscript is largely working at a high level; revision priorities should focus on the V2 factual contradiction first and the Ch11 interior-reasoning softening second.*
