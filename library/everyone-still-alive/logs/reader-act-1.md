# Reader Act 1 Assessment — *Everyone Still Alive*

Chapters 01–08: Vignette 1 (*The Drill*, Chs 01–04), Vignette 2 (*The Line*, Chs 05–07), and Vignette 3 opening (Ch 08).

---

## What's Working

**Vignette 1 executes its register cleanly.** The fast YA horror brief is held throughout Chapters 01–04 without drift or softening. Sentence length is genuinely short by default; section breaks within chapters pace spatial transitions rather than time gaps; the infected are rendered exclusively through motion data and atmospheric signal ("wrong-fast, a side-tilting speed, a gait that went at an angle people didn't go at" — Ch 01). There is not a single clinical or explanatory sentence about what the infected are or how the infection works. This discipline is exactly right.

**Amara's death lands with moral weight, not horror-movie stupidity.** The brief's hardest single beat works. The sound through the wall ("crying... not hysteria — not fresh, not new") establishes the human plausibility of what Amara is about to do. Jesse's "Don't" — knowing it won't stop her — is the right note: "they both understood this" (Ch 02). She is the thesis's first proof, and the reader feels it as tragedy rather than error.

**The Reader of Names entrance is precisely calibrated.** The dying phone at 1% battery, the voice reading names without commentary, the battery cutting out mid-name ("Diane Var—") — and then silence (Ch 03). The thread enters as lived experience, not narrative setup. No character asks who is broadcasting; the question isn't raised; the battery simply goes dark. This restraint is important and correct.

**The carved fish enters organically.** Jesse's hands carving the wood in the supply closet ("not toward anything, just moving") is the right scene for the fish's introduction. The fish is a fish "because of the shape the wood wants." Its handoff in Ch 04 — slipped into the grey wool peacoat pocket near the loading dock, not looked at again — lands without sentiment. And Ch 08's retrieval (Theo pulling it from a coat pocket in a stripped roadside house, holding it out to Lenn without saying anything) is exactly right in its quietness.

**The register shift from V1 to V2 is legible.** The "Month Two" depth marker signals the cut; the prose changes underfoot. V2's procedural cold is consistently held across all three chapters. The shift-log structure — Selle's templated entries, the "Notes: none" at end of shift — achieves its horror through accumulation. "There was nothing in it that had told him anything was wrong" (Ch 05 close) is a fine final note: the voice of a man who has organized himself against the knowledge his notes are already carrying.

**Ch 08 establishes the McCarthy register correctly.** The opening paragraph is a genuine register shift — long sentences with internal cadence, subordinate rhythms, indifferent landscape. The unattributed dialogue arrives immediately ("Theo said it was the best thing he had eaten. The man said it was not."). The chapter closes on "But not us" going unanswered in the ash-colored dark, which is the exact note V3 needs to open on.

---

## Concerns

**Continuity error in Vignette 2: the "no names" policy.** In Ch 05, Selle explicitly tells an anxious traveler: "We don't take names." The line is specific, policy-level, and contextually important — it's a direct response to a man's anxiety about documentation. Two chapters later, in Ch 07, Selle opens his intake with "Your name, please" and records the woman as "Marta Solak" in the crossing log entry. No explanation is offered for the departure from stated policy; no shift in procedure is noted. This is a factual contradiction within the same vignette, between two chapters that are temporally close (same checkpoint, same two officers, a few days apart). It's also the only named traveler in the vignette, which makes the departure more visible, not less. This needs resolution — either the "no names" exchange in Ch 05 must be adjusted, or Ch 07's intake procedure must return to the established policy.

**A single analytical reach in Jesse's voice (Ch 02).** "He also knew — the same way his body had turned in the second-floor hallway before his brain caught the shape in the corridor — that saying 'don't' was the only thing he could do, and that it wasn't going to stop her, and that they both understood this." This is a long, subordinate-clause-rich sentence doing psychological analysis — not a body-first impulse, not a short declarative. It sits outside the chapter's sentence register. It's one sentence and it's doing real work (the mutual understanding between Jesse and Amara is load-bearing), but a revision could render this more physically and still carry the same weight.

## Thread Development in Act 1

All four connective threads enter correctly. T1 (Reader of Names) enters at Ch 03; T2 (fish) enters at Ch 03 and advances in Ch 04 and Ch 08; T3 (monster gradient) advances through every chapter and the gradient argument is already visible: V1's compassion-as-worst-act versus V2's procedural compliance versus Ch 08's three armed men with rifles; T4 (question of goodness) enters with Jesse's drive and escalates correctly into Selle's complicity.

---

## Act Pacing

V1 is tight across its four chapters. V2 correctly slows — the escalating cases (Ch 05: establishing norms; Ch 06: borderline physical and respiratory cases; Ch 07: definitive fever, turned back) give the vignette the structure of a ratchet tightening. The transition into Ch 08 is clean. No mid-act sag.

---

## What to Leave Alone

Chapter 04's quiet close — Jesse not looking back, not saying Amara's name — reads as a literary fade rather than a V1 cliffhanger. This is the vignette's final chapter, and the brief gives explicit permission for vignette-close chapters to land differently. The restraint here (the fish in the coat pocket, the street empty and ordinary, the name withheld) is the right move. Do not push this toward dramatic resolution; the silence is the grief and the grief is the argument.

The paragraph in Ch 07 where Selle catalogs the things he "did not think about" regarding the woman and children walks to the edge of over-articulation — but it's doing what the style guide specifies as V2's lean-into: "his inner life is a list of deferred reckonings." The list is Selle's voice, not the narrator's, and the bureaucratic syntax of the non-thinking is the point. Leave it.
