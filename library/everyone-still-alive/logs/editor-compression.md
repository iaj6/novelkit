### Meta-commentary on cognition

**Cut:** `He held onto that.` (re: the art-room door being thick)
The preceding sentence is "It was thick." — the reader grasps at this thin justification alongside Jesse without needing the cognitive label. The label was the gloss.

### Emotional structural analysis

**Cut (three sentences):** `Jesse had thought he knew what it would sound like — a building in distress, an emergency. He'd seen movies. He didn't know.`
These sentences retrospectively annotate the sounds-from-below by explaining Jesse's expectation vs. reality. The sounds had already been rendered in specific sensory terms. The remaining sentence, "This was faster than he'd imagined, and more rhythmic…," is still intelligible: a teenager in a school emergency will have imagined this; the explicit frame is not needed.

This pair diagrams Jesse's emotional arc after hearing Coach Briggs's voice. The preceding passage already conveys both the comfort ("He sounded like someone who believed they would work") and, by the bareness of that single observation, its fragility. The diagram is surplus.

### Redundant transition / re-summary

Inline prose between two of Jesse's dialogue lines. The reader witnessed Jesse check his phone and get no service earlier in the chapter. The dialogue "I tried it in the hallway" already covers when. This was re-summary disguised as attribution. The two dialogue lines were merged into one sentence ("I tried it in the hallway.
