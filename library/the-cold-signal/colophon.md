# colophon

an early test of sustained tension in one place: a scientist alone at an Antarctic station in polar darkness, twelve days before her team returns, holding a forty-seven-second signal that admits only one reading. the brief forbade the usual escapes — no second transmission, no decoded message, the signal never described — so the book lives entirely in what she decides to do with it. a later research re-run of this brief, not this edition, helped prove that a countdown only creates dread when it gates an act that cannot be undone, a lesson the press now writes into its briefs.

it came out disciplined. the best decision arrives on the first pages: the signal's arrival is given to the instruments — a stylus moves at a logged timestamp — and the narration refuses to name what she is watching, just as her training does. the reader's letter finds the restraint structural rather than performed, and calls Elena Hartmann "a scientist who is a scientist." the honest flaw is repetition: one grounding detail, the orange guide rope between huts, recurs in near-identical words through the early chapters until texture becomes tic; the letter judges the register held for about eighty-five percent of the book.
