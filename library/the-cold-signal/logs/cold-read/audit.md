# Panel Audit
*Subject: the seven-lens review panel, not the novel*

---

## 1. IS THE PANEL BEING TOO GENEROUS?

**Modest generosity, unevenly distributed.** The 1.5-point spread (6.0–7.5) looks narrow on a 10-point scale, but it isn't manufactured consensus: propulsion (6.0 / reluctantly) and airport (7.0 / reluctantly) are both genuinely critical and identify the same structural failure in the middle third. The spread is real.

The generosity problem is subtler. The two 7.5 scores (literary and comparative) both come from lenses predisposed to what this novel is doing — they are, explicitly, the readers this book is written for. Literary calls the novel's withholding strategy "the most intelligent formal bet"; comparative reads the protocol as structurally mirroring the narrative's own option-elimination. These are charitable readings. They may be right, but they are not the result of a lens in tension with its own prior. A panel that wanted to stress-test its own generosity would have included a lens constitutively hostile to slow-interiority literary fiction. It didn't.

More specifically: six of seven lenses praise the novel's ending in nearly identical terms without anyone asking whether "Then we have work to do" actually earns its claimed weight, or whether it lands well only because the reader has been trained by 200 pages of restraint to receive a plain sentence as a profound one. That's a question about reader manipulation, not literary achievement. The panel as a body never asked it.

## 2. GROUNDED CLAIMS VS. PADDING

### Grounded (checked against the manuscript)

**Chapter 8 consensus.** All seven lenses call ch08 the book's structural and emotional peak. Checked. The fan repair sequence is in the manuscript as described: thirty-eight minutes of hardware crisis, Elena never considers destroying the data, and she reaches the explicit recognition — "The option had eliminated itself on the night she made the first copy" — by behavioral evidence rather than deliberation. The seven lenses arrive at this conclusion via different routes (behavioral revelation, structural function, earned restraint, psychological accuracy) with different supporting evidence. This is genuine convergence.

**Varga relationship.** Every lens praises the HF radio calls. Checked across chapters 2, 5, 8, 10, 11, 12, 14. The twice-daily format, the welfare-question structure, the compression of eighteen years of friendship into word choice and pause length — all confirmed in the text. Literary's claim that the Varga exchanges "achieve genuine depth through accumulation" and airport's "finest technical achievement" are both grounded observations. The specific line "There is nothing unusual to report" (ch11) — cited by five lenses — is in the manuscript exactly as quoted, and its function (technically true, chosen precisely, received by both speakers as a closed door) is correctly analyzed.

**"She had been, for nine days, protecting herself behind the science."** Quoted by six lenses from ch10. Confirmed in the text. The nine chapters of demonstrated self-deception that precede it are sufficient to earn the naming — the ordinary and authenticity reviewers assess this correctly.

**The chapter 13/14 duplicate morning.** Five lenses flag this production artifact independently. Confirmed: ch13 ends with "At 0600 she opened her eyes. The bunk was warm. Through the insulated wall she could hear the diesels... She lay still for a minute... and then she sat up. The work was done. The LC-130 would land this afternoon." Ch14 opens: "She woke at 0600. The bunk was warm; through the insulated wall the generators held their note. She lay still for a moment..." Same morning, same detail, no shift in perspective, no formal payoff to the repetition. This is a manuscript seam, exactly as diagnosed.

**The day-count error.** Literary is right. Ch03 ends (Day 3) with "Eleven days remained before the LC-130." Ch04 states "Eleven days remaining now — she had shifted past midnight; it was Day 4." But ch13 establishes "Solo operation period, Day 11 of 12" on June 13, placing the LC-130 arrival on Day 12. From Day 3, nine days remain to Day 12. From Day 4, eight days remain. Both ch03 and ch04 say eleven. The error is in both chapters, not one; the literary reviewer correctly cites it.

**The methodology paper date error.** Confirmed across:
- Ch04: "She had published the methodology paper in 1978"
- Ch05: "The methodology paper had been in circulation since 1978"
- Ch10: "The methodology paper in 1979"
- Ch12: "She had designed it that way, in 1979"
- Ch13: "Hartmann, E., 1979, published in *Icarus*, vol. 37"

Four chapters say 1979; two say 1978. The 1979 version is in the official station log entry — the documentary climax — so 1979 is the more authoritative instance. Two of the reviewers who caught this (authenticity, verisimilitude) have the chapter distribution slightly wrong (authenticity says ch05 gives 1979, but it gives 1978; verisimilitude says ch02 gives 1978, but ch02 gives no year at all). The error is real. The chapter-level attribution in those reviews is not.

**The "The tools for finding it were not designed for having found it" dissent.** Authenticity is the sole dissenter on the novel's most-cited sentence, calling it circular. Checked: the sentence is in ch05, written by Elena in her field log at 1544 on Day 5. The sentence is doing genuine institutional work (the protocol covers verification but provides no procedure for post-detection action), and it is also doing emotional work (she was not personally prepared). The authenticity reviewer's critique — "what Elena means is emotional, not methodological" — is overstated but not wrong. The sentence presents itself in the register of an epistemological observation while carrying emotional freight. The synthesis dismisses this as a "real and irresolvable" split between reviewers; it is in fact a checkable question about what the sentence claims to do, and the dissent is more correct than the synthesis grants.

### Padding (unfalsifiable or hedged into meaninglessness)

**Literary:** "That the novel refuses to let its protagonist claim full virtue until she has applied her own protocol to her own motives is an act of literary intelligence that distinguishes this from the merely well-crafted." No comparator named. "Merely well-crafted" is doing no work. This is praise dressed as analysis.

**Comparative:** "Voice and register consistency: the scientist's precise consciousness is the narrative medium throughout, and it never breaks or sentimentalizes." Claimed as one of five strongest elements. This is a description of a technique the novel uses, not evidence that the technique succeeds. "Never breaks" is falsifiable in principle but unverified by the reviewer.

**Comparative:** "This is not diminished by the comparison [to *Contact*] so long as the reader understands it is not competing." This is a diplomatic hedge, not a critical claim.

**Authenticity:** "These are rendered with the authority of someone who has read the genre carefully or lived adjacent to this work." This is unfalsifiable. It's a confidence attribution, not a finding.

**Ordinary:** "'The tools for finding it were not designed for having found it.' The most compressed statement of the book's central problem. It appears in a field log as a single sentence and it says what the novel is about in eleven words." Ordinal superlatives ("most compressed") are not falsifiable without a comparison set.

## 3. DID ANY REVIEWER FAIL TO READ THE WHOLE BOOK?

**Verisimilitude lens: inattentive reading of chapter 02.**

The verification report flagged one misattribution from the verisimilitude lens: "The protocol had done what it was designed to do. It had cornered her." attributed to ch02, actually in ch03. Checked and confirmed.

More significantly, the verisimilitude review states: "Chapter 2 defines [category 12] as a natural transient class identified by a characteristic steep spectral rolloff." Ch02 does not describe category 12's content at all. The full description of category 12 as a "natural transient class" with a "characteristic steep rolloff" appears in ch03 (Elimination Complete), not ch02. The reviewer has slid the chapter 3 description backward into chapter 2. This is the same basic error as the misattributed quote — the reviewer is reconstructing ch02's content from memory of ch03.

**Airport lens: same error, slightly different form.** The airport review states the chapter 2/chapter 12 protocol inconsistency involves category 12, claiming category 12 is described in ch02. The same error applies: ch02 identifies category 12 as requiring an overnight comparison run but does not describe what category 12 *is*. The description appears in ch03.

**No lens read chapter 07 carefully for protocol content.** Every reviewer who examines the protocol inconsistency compares ch02 against ch12. Chapter 07 (The Draft) contains a third, fully incompatible description: Elena writes a twelve-page analysis and in the verification methodology section identifies "Category 12, IMD product hypothesis" — intermodulation distortion, a receiver-electronics artifact that is a fundamentally different phenomenon from either "natural transient class with steep spectral rolloff" (ch03) or "galactic background modulation" (ch12). Three different mechanisms for the same category number across three chapters. No reviewer detected this third version because none read ch07 as a protocol source. The comparative and literary lenses cite ch07 in their claims lists; neither examines what ch07 says category 12 is.

**The methodological-year attributions are wrong in two reviews.** Authenticity says ch05 gives 1979. It gives 1978. Verisimilitude says ch02 gives 1978. Ch02 gives no year. Both reviews have the error's footprint partially wrong.

## 4. SUSPICIOUS SIMILARITIES

**Literary (7.5/yes) and comparative (7.5/yes) are the closest pair.** Both give the highest score, both finish "yes," both deploy the Ishiguro comparison as a structural claim, both praise the fan chapter and the ending in nearly identical terms, and both identify the same three weaknesses (middle section, ch13/14 seam, ch10's retrospective density). They cite largely overlapping quotes. The divergence is real but confined: comparative extends the structural analysis to the "narrative eliminates options as Elena eliminates candidates" argument, which literary does not develop. Literary pays closer attention to sentence-level prose rhythm. These are genuinely different instruments examining the same object; they do not share obscure evidence and reach the same diagnosis from different grounds. Call it convergence.

**The ch13/14 seam is described in nearly identical language by five lenses.** This is not suspicious — it's a textual artifact and any reader who reaches chapter 14 will encounter the same pair of sentences. What the shared description suggests is that this is obvious on the page to any attentive reader, which makes it surprising that the synthesis ranks fixing this second in priority.

**Airport and propulsion are almost identical in diagnosis of the middle section and in their "reluctantly" finish response.** Airport cites chapters 4–9; propulsion cites chapters 5–9. Both identify Option 4 as reader-legible before Elena articulates it. Both name chapter 6 as the most likely stopping point. This is also genuine convergence: two lenses with incompatible priorities (general readership vs. plot mechanics) reaching the same structural diagnosis by independent routes.

## 5. THE MOST DAMAGING TRUE THING THE PANEL IS UNDERWEIGHTING

**The Hartmann Protocol is not a stable document across the novel.**

The synthesis says: "The Hartmann Protocol category inconsistency... Pick one version and make it consistent." That repair prescription is several orders of magnitude too mild for what is actually in the manuscript.

Here is what I found by reading the protocol-deployment chapters:

**Chapter 02 (first verification, categories as explicitly stated):**
- Category 1: local RFI
- Category 2: satellite overpass artifact
- Category 3: aircraft transponder
- Category 4: equipment oscillation/resonance
- Category 5: ionospheric reflection of terrestrial broadcast
- Category 6: solar and stellar natural emission
- Category 7: pulsar or magnetar emission
- Category 8: magnetospheric event
- Category 12: described only as requiring an overnight comparison run (no content given)

**Chapter 03 (category 12 content, the first full description):**
- Category 12: "phenomena not yet fully characterized in the literature... known in a handful of documented cases to produce brief intervals of narrow-band emission... Each had been caught by a specific feature of its spectrum: a characteristic steep rolloff at the upper end of the detection band"

**Chapter 07 (formal analysis, Elena documents the protocol):**
- Category 12: "IMD product hypothesis, eliminated June 5 at 06:04" (intermodulation distortion — a completely different mechanism from ch03's "natural transient class")

**Chapter 12 (second verification, categories as explicitly stated):**
- Category 1: terrestrial interference sources
- Category 2: instrument artifact, self-generated
- Category 3: solar radio burst
- Category 4: Jovian decametric emission
- Category 7: ionospheric reflection
- Category 12: "galactic background modulation"

The protocol in ch12 is not the same protocol with swapped numbers. It is a different document. Categories 2, 3, and 4 in ch02 (satellite overpass, aircraft transponder, equipment oscillation) have no counterpart in ch12 and have been replaced by entirely different phenomena. Category 7 migrates from "pulsar or magnetar emission" (ch02) to "ionospheric reflection" (ch12) while category 5's content (ionospheric reflection, ch02) vanishes. Category 12 has three incompatible identifications across three chapters.

The panel reviewers who caught this (airport, verisimilitude) describe it as "categories 5 and 7 swapped" or "category 7 described differently." Both diagnoses are substantially understated. The actual situation is that the twelve-category protocol appears to have been independently invented on at least three separate occasions during drafting, with none of the versions subsequently reconciled against the others.

**Why this is the most damaging problem for this novel specifically:** The comparative lens correctly identifies the novel's central formal bet: the narrative eliminates options one by one, in the same structural logic as Elena's verification protocol. If the protocol is a coherent, stable, numbered system, this structural parallelism works and earns the Ishiguro comparison at the level of form rather than mood. If the protocol is visibly unstable — if a reader who has internalized categories from ch02 finds them contradicted by ch12 — the formal bet fails. Not as a copyediting error, but as a structural failure. The novel asks the reader to trust the protocol the way Elena trusts it: as a system that corners the truth by exhausting the alternatives. A protocol that reorders and replaces its own categories across the book cannot do this.

The synthesis places this as the first repair to make but describes it as a consistency fix. It is a coherence fix, requiring a canonical category list to be established and then audited across every chapter that describes or uses the protocol: chapters 2, 3, 7, 12, and the station log entry in chapter 13.

**A related error no reviewer caught:** The tape label in ch06 reads "HS-910603-A / BACKUP-1 / VERIFIED 3/6/91." In ch01's labeling scene, Elena writes "HS-910603-A. 21:14:07L. 47s." plus frequency parameters and date — no "BACKUP-1" designation, no "VERIFIED" annotation. In ch10, the tape is described as "HS-910603-A on a strip of adhesive tape she had written in black felt-tip on the first night." In ch13, Elena confirms "It had the date, the time, and the designation she had written in those first hours" — matching ch01 but not ch06. The verisimilitude reviewer catches the "VERIFIED" word but notes only that it "could not yet have had" that word. The deeper problem is that the label has three incompatible versions across the book: ch01 (with parameters), ch06 (with BACKUP-1 and VERIFIED), and ch10 and ch13 (reverting to the simpler original form). The label isn't a label that changed over time; it's a label that the draft rewrote without tracking what it had previously established.

## 6. WHERE THE SYNTHESIS IS WRONG

**On repair priority.** The synthesis ranks "Fix the continuity errors" first, and within that category lists items in a sequence that places the chapter 13/14 seam alongside the protocol inconsistency as comparable problems. They are not comparable. The ch13/14 seam is a production artifact requiring one cut. The protocol inconsistency requires establishing a canonical document and auditing it across multiple chapters. The synthesis's repair ranking makes a single-line copy-edit (remove the duplicate morning) look equivalent in effort and urgency to what is actually a structural overhaul of the novel's central mechanism.

**On the "tools for finding it" sentence.** The synthesis says the literary/authenticity split on this sentence is "real and irresolvable by fact." It is resolvable by reading the sentence in its context. The sentence appears in Elena's field log on Day 5, written at 1544, and the log entry closes immediately after: "She held the pen above the line for a moment... She read it once... and closed the log." The sentence is presented in the narrative as Elena resisting elaboration. The *novel* treats it as a methodological claim; Elena treats it as an emotional limit. The authenticity reviewer's observation that the sentence "presents itself as epistemological" while doing emotional work is a correct formal observation about a mismatch between register and content. The synthesis shortchanges this by framing it as a reader-preference split rather than an observation about the sentence's actual operation.

**On Chapter 10's biographical bolus.** The synthesis says the form/content dispute is "defensible from both sides" and recommends asking whether biographical material can be "refracted through procedure." This is right as a direction, but the synthesis frames the problem as "proportion, not presence." The ordinary reviewer says it reads like "the author explaining the theme rather than dramatizing it." Both things are true, and the ordinary reviewer's version is more diagnostic. The novel has demonstrated at length that it can render insight through action (ch08). Ch10's career accounting is the one chapter that reverts to direct declaration. The synthesis's hedged framing softens what is a real craft failure in one section of an otherwise disciplined chapter.

## 7. WHAT THE PANEL GOT RIGHT THAT DESERVES MORE CREDIT

**Propulsion's observation that Option 4 is reader-legible from chapter 6.** This is the most diagnostically sharp claim in the panel and the synthesis buries it. If Option 4 is visible to an attentive reader after the cache-hut insight in ch06, then chapters 7 through 12 are spent watching Elena catch up to what the reader already knows. The propulsion reviewer articulates this precisely; the airport reviewer confirms it; the synthesis gives it one sentence. This is the accurate account of why the middle section fails — not just that it's repetitive, but that the reader is ahead of the narrative for six consecutive chapters.

**The authenticity reviewer's observation about the signal's "hollow center."** Quoted approvingly by the synthesis only in the context of audience-expectation management. The reviewer says: "the complete refusal to characterize the signal in any positive terms... creates a hollow center. The reader receives Elena's certainty but never its warrant." The synthesis treats this as a jacket-copy problem. It is also a reader-trust problem: the novel asks us to experience Elena's certainty as credible and momentous, but provides no independent access to what defeats twelve categories. The panel, collectively, accepts the certainty on Elena's testimony. That acceptance is the premise the panel inherited from the book's own framing — and it is the claim that was never tested against the text.
