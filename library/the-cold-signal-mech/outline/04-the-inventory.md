# Chapter 04 — The Inventory

**One-line summary:** Elena conducts a systematic inventory of all remaining resources; Protocol Run 3 reveals the first confirmed tape dropout — five seconds, edge of first parametric segment.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — receiver hut (primarily), equipment building, main hab. Day 4.  
**Target words:** ~2,700  
**Chapter shape:** Single scene — one extended activity (systematic inventory + Protocol Run 3), moving through the station's three structures  
**Ending mode:** Resolved beat

---

## Escalation (from Chapter Map)

- **(a) Day 4**
- **(b)** Tape integrity: five-second dropout confirmed in the edge of the first parametric segment during Run 3 — first measured degradation. Instrument run margin: 6 → 5. Power reserve: 67 % → 60 %.
- **(c)** Step closer: the tape is actively degrading at a measurable rate. Elena calculates that at this rate the core record will be compromised in eight to twelve days. She decides to stop scheduling runs until she has resolved the gate question.

---

## Goal

Give Elena — and the reader — a precise, comprehensive picture of what remains and at what rate it is degrading. The chapter is methodical; that is appropriate. The resolved beat at its end is not relief; it is the particular weight of knowing exactly how bad things are.

---

## Obstacle

Elena knows the inventory will show her what she does not want to know. The discipline of doing it anyway — recording it accurately, not rounding up — is the chapter's internal tension.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Elena decides to inventory everything.** Four days in, and she has been managing rather than accounting. She needs to know the exact state of each depleting resource before she can make any decision about the gate. She begins in the receiver hut, which she now treats as the center of the station's work.

2. **Tape housing temperature log.** She reads the minimum-maximum thermometer she mounted in the tape housing rack after Day 2. Overnight minimums: within range, but barely. The 70 % heater setting is holding the hut above spec, though just. She notes this.

3. **Physical tape inspection.** She unreels a larger section of the tape than she did on Day 2 — including the signal segment itself, not just the pre-signal monitoring record. She uses the magnifying glass and a small flashlight held at an angle to catch surface texture. The binder stress she noted on Day 2 has slightly extended — the affected zone is now about three centimeters wider along the reel edge. Not in the signal segment yet.

4. **Printout check.** She retrieves the printout from the hab cabinet, unrolls it fully, examines it column by column. It is intact. The ink has not fractured. She rolls it carefully and returns it. She makes a note: "Printout: no degradation as of Day 4."

5. **Signal analyzer status.** She runs the instrument's self-diagnostic (not a full protocol run — a built-in self-test that costs minimal power). The instrument reports: all subsystems nominal. She notes this.

6. **Generator and fuel inventory.** She checks the fuel gauge and records the level. She checks the voltage regulator's behavior log (the instrument has a simple fault counter). Two additional voltage excursions logged overnight — minor ones, below her alarm threshold. She notes them.

7. **Power reserve calculation.** She runs the arithmetic: fuel remaining, estimated days until the team's return (eight days now), standard daily draw. The estimate she produces: approximately 8 full protocol runs remaining within safe power margin. She writes this estimate down, underlines it, and notes: "Assumes standard burn rate. Verify against actual gauge in 24h." She does not yet know the estimate is wrong.

8. **Protocol Run 3.** She has her resource picture as complete as she can make it without running the instrument. Now she runs it. Warm-up. Tape plays. Signal reads. She watches the analyzer display.

9. **The dropout.** At the beginning of the first parametric segment — the frequency sweep that provides the first set of identifying parameters for the signal — the playback shows a five-second silence. Not the core of the segment, but its opening five seconds. She stops the run. Rewinds. Plays again. Same result: five seconds of blank tape where the binder has already begun to fail.

10. **She finishes the run.** After the dropout, the playback recovers and the rest of the signal reads normally. The core pattern — the structure that makes the signal unambiguous — is intact. The first parametric segment reads completely, minus its opening five seconds, which she can reconstruct from context. But the dropout is real and it was not there in Run 2.

11. **The calculation she does not want to do.** She marks the dropout's position on her tape map and calculates: the binder stress she observed visually on the edge of the reel has now become playback dropout in the signal segment. If the dropout grows at the rate of approximately five seconds per two days, she has eight to twelve days before it reaches the core signature. That might be enough. It might not be.

12. **She decides to stop running the protocol until after the gate.** Each run costs one margin; she has 5 remaining (revised estimate). The gate is in three days. If she uses runs now and decides to hold the data after the gate, she will have fewer runs left to work with. If she uses runs now and decides to send at the gate, the runs are moot. Either way, conservation until the gate decision is the correct move.

13. **End of chapter.** She writes the full inventory in her analysis notebook: tape state, printout state, instrument state, power estimate, run margin, hours to gate. She puts the notebook in the hab. The inventory is complete. She knows exactly what she has. It is less than she had three days ago.

---

## Outcome / Resolved Beat

The resolved beat is the completion of the inventory: Elena now has a precise, documented picture of the resource state. The resolution is not satisfying — the tape is degrading, the power margin is finite — but it is the particular peace of having measured the problem accurately rather than living with the uncertainty of not knowing.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

The dropout is real and growing. Can the tape survive until she decides? And can she decide before she runs out of runs to verify?
