# Chapter 05 — Worse Than Calculated

**One-line summary:** The fuel-burn rate is 15 % above baseline; the original 8-run estimate collapses to 3 remaining; Walczak calls from McMurdo; Elena says nothing.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — equipment building (fuel gauge and generator, then SSB radio console). Day 5.  
**Target words:** ~2,000  
**Chapter shape:** Two-beat — (1) generator/fuel discovery; (2) Walczak's radio call from McMurdo  
**Ending mode:** Cliffhanger

---

## Escalation (from Chapter Map)

- **(a) Day 5**
- **(b)** Power reserve: 60 % → 51 % (fuel burn 15 % above baseline; cold diesel viscosity forcing compensatory draw). Instrument run margin: revised downward from estimated 5 remaining → confirmed 3 remaining (original 8-run estimate was wrong; true budget is ~6 total; with 3 runs done, 3 remain). Hours to gate: ~72 h → ~48 h.
- **(c)** Step closer: instrument margin is two runs lower than Elena believed yesterday. Two options she was holding open are now foreclosed by the revised math.

---

## Goal

Deliver the first concrete collapse of a prior calculation. Elena's confidence in her resource estimate — carefully built in Chapter 4 — is wrong. The world is worse than she measured it. This chapter is short because compression is the right shape for this discovery.

---

## Obstacle

The calculation is not wrong because Elena was careless. It is wrong because the cold has done something she did not account for: diesel fuel thickens below a certain temperature threshold, increasing flow resistance, and the generator has been compensating by drawing slightly more fuel per hour than its standard rate. This is a known cold-weather effect she should have factored in and did not. The chapter should not let her (or the reader) off the hook for this.

---

## Action Beats

**Beat 1: The fuel gauge**

1. Elena checks the fuel gauge as part of her morning generator routine — she has been doing this every day. Today she reads the gauge carefully and compares it to her log entry from Day 4.

2. The consumption since yesterday is 15 % higher than her baseline estimate. She checks the figure twice. She reads the temperature logs. The diesel temperature has been lower than standard spec because the fuel tank is in the equipment building's exterior-facing wall, which runs colder than the interior. At these temperatures, diesel viscosity increases and the generator's pump is working harder.

3. She does the revised calculation. If fuel burn is 15 % above baseline for the remaining days (and it will be — the cold is not changing), the generator's total power output capacity before the team's return is proportionally reduced. She works through the arithmetic twice to make sure she has it.

4. Revised instrument run margin: approximately 6 total runs in the power budget (not 8 as estimated). Three runs have already been used (Runs 1, 2, 3). Three runs remain — not five.

5. She sits with this number for a moment. She writes it in the notebook with a different pen, the one she uses for corrections. "Revised run budget: 6 total. 3 used. 3 remaining." She underlines the "3" twice.

6. Below the calculation: "Gate: 48 hours. After gate: unknown outcome. 3 remaining runs must be managed for whatever outcome applies."

**Beat 2: Walczak's call**

7. At approximately 1100, the SSB radio opens with Walczak's call sign. He calls in on schedule from McMurdo — the check-in she had been expecting for two days.

8. Walczak's voice through the SSB: careful, specific, slightly tired from McMurdo's noise and crowds. He asks about the equipment. He is an instrument engineer; his questions are technical and exact. How is the voltage regulator? How many fault events has it logged? How is the fuel holding?

9. Elena answers every question accurately — except the one question he is not asking. She tells him about the voltage regulator log, the fuel revision (she says: "burn rate is running about 15 percent above baseline; I've adjusted the heating draw"). She does not tell him why her heating draw is in question.

10. Walczak says: he has a new regulator component at McMurdo — he found a compatible part in the McMurdo maintenance stores. He will bring it back on Day 12. She says: good.

11. He asks how she is doing. This is a real question; he and Elena have wintered-over together twice before. She says: fine. He says: you sound tired. She says: I am.

12. He tells her the flight is still on schedule. Day 12. He will check in once more before then. He signs off with the formal close.

13. After the radio goes quiet: Elena does not move for a moment. She is aware of what she just did and did not say. She calculates how this will look on Day 12, when Walczak sees what she has been running on the signal analyzer. She has no answer for this calculation. She writes the date and "Walczak check-in: routine. Nothing transmitted." in the log.

---

## Outcome / Cliffhanger

Two discoveries in one short chapter: the power budget is worse than calculated, cutting her remaining run margin from 5 to 3. And she has heard Walczak's voice and said nothing. The gate is 48 hours away. She has 3 runs for whatever comes after it. The chapter ends on the weight of the closed channel — the radio that is now quiet again.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

With only 3 runs remaining after the gate regardless of what she decides, the cost of using runs prematurely is now clear and steep. And: Walczak asked the right questions about the equipment. If she had told him, this chapter would have been different. She didn't. Why not? The chapter should not answer this; the question should accumulate pressure.

## Continuity Notes

- The **revised instrument run margin** (3 remaining, not 5) is a hard continuity fact from this chapter forward. All subsequent chapters must use 3 as the correct number of remaining runs at the gate.
- Walczak's offer of a **new voltage regulator component** arriving on Day 12 is a new fact introduced here. It has no effect on the current crisis (Elena can't install it now), but it establishes that the generator's fault could be fixed — just not in time to matter.
- The fuel calculation in this chapter should be presented as Elena's corrected math, not as a new fault or failure of the generator. The generator is running normally; the error was in Elena's baseline assumption about fuel viscosity at these temperatures.
- Power reserve: ~51 % after this chapter. Hours to gate: ~48 h (two days).
- The chapter is deliberately short (~2,000 words) because compression is the right texture for the moment the resource picture collapses.
