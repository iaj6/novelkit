# Chapter 14 — The Last Run

**One-line summary:** Protocol Run 6 — the final run; tape dropout at twenty-three seconds; instrument margin reaches zero; power reserve at critical threshold; Elena picks up the radio handset.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — main hab (pre-run morning), receiver hut (Run 6), equipment building (radio). Day 12, 0530–0700.  
**Target words:** ~2,100  
**Chapter shape:** Single scene — one extended activity (Protocol Run 6 and its immediate aftermath)  
**Ending mode:** Mid-action cut

---

- **(a) Day 12, morning (pre-arrival)**
- **(b)** Tape dropout: **23 seconds** (Run 6; up from 20 on Day 11, consistent with the ~2.7 seconds/day rate). Instrument run margin: 1 → **0**. Power reserve: 10 % → ~3 % (at life-support threshold). Days to foreclosure: **0** (this is Day 12).
- **(c)** **PERMANENTLY FORECLOSED:** all further instrument verification. The record is what it is. The core signature is still intact. The instrument is done. Elena picks up the radio handset.

---

## Goal

The final run, and then the handset. The chapter is short because everything that can be measured has been measured; what remains is action. The mid-action cut must land before the reader knows what Elena is going to say into the radio — or who she is calling.

---

## Obstacle

The power reserve is at 10 % entering this chapter. The stabilization procedure for the generator's voltage variance and the run itself will bring it to approximately 3 % — barely above life-support threshold. If the stabilization fails to hold and she has a brownout during the run, she will have used her last margin on a corrupt run. She runs it anyway.

---

## Action Beats

**0530: Pre-run**

1. Elena is awake at 0530. She has not slept more than a few hours. She stands in the galley in the partial dark — the station's interior lights on their winter-night setting, amber and low. The page she set aside last night is still on the table, face down. She does not turn it over.

2. She makes coffee. She is aware that this is the last morning she is the only person in this station. In six hours, the hab will have other people in it, with their voices and their equipment and their clean sight lines on everything she has been doing.

3. She brings the tape housing from the equipment cabinet and carries it to the receiver hut. The walkway between them is at its coldest of the morning — the exterior temperature is below minus forty. She can feel the walkway flexing slightly under the wind load.

4. She sets the housing down and runs the space heater in the receiver hut for ten minutes to bring the instrument above operating temperature. She watches the thermometer.

**Stabilization procedure**

5. She goes to the equipment building for the stabilization procedure. The generator's morning fault log: six overnight excursions. The variance is at +/- 9 % at the cold start. Worse than yesterday. She runs the stabilization: heater load shed, generator settling at reduced draw. The variance drops slowly — it takes twenty-two minutes this time, not fifteen. She is patient.

6. At +/- 5.5 % — not as clean as she has had it, but within the acceptable range for a run — she restores the instrument load. She watches for a further excursion. The generator holds.

7. Power reserve after stabilization: ~7 %. The run itself will cost another 4–5 %. She will be at approximately 2–3 % afterward. This is enough to sustain life support and the hab heater through the team's arrival at 1300. It is not a margin she would have chosen to operate within.

**Protocol Run 6**

8. She initiates the run. The last time. The analyzer's warm-up cycle. The tape loads.

9. **Opening segment:** silence at the top — now approximately seven to eight seconds, consistent with the progression she has been tracking. The same silence she has heard in every run since Run 3. It is familiar now, the way the opening gap of a record you've played many times becomes part of the music.

10. **First parametric segment:** the signal reads. The frequency distribution is intact. The primary anomalous parameters — the ones that are the reason for all of this — are present and unchanged. The signal is still there.

11. Elena sits very still while this part of the run plays. She has heard it five times. It is the same every time. The signal does not change because tapes degrade; the signal is preserved in what the binder has not yet taken. This is what she has.

12. **The gap:** twenty-three seconds this time, by the analyzer's measurement. The second parametric segment: gone, same as in Run 4 and Run 5. The calibration tail: intact. Forty-seven seconds total. End of signal.

13. **Run complete.** The analyzer's display settles to its post-run idle state. Instrument margin: 0.

14. Power reserve: ~3 %.

**After the run**

15. Elena sits in the work chair for a moment. She looks at the run log as it prints out — the last protocol log she will produce at this instrument, at this station, in this role. She pulls the log from the printer and holds it.

16. She writes in the analysis notebook: "Run 6 complete. Day 12, 0640. Dropout: 23s (consistent with progression). Core signature: intact. First parametric segment: complete. Second parametric segment: absent (as of Run 4). Instrument margin: 0. Power reserve: approximately 3%. This is the final run."

17. She closes the signal analyzer. She files the run log in the protocol folder. She puts the folder with the tape housing, the wrapped printout, and the analysis notebook. She carries all of it — the full evidence package — to the main hab and puts it on the equipment cabinet.

18. She picks up the face-down page from the table. She reads it — the reader does not see what it says. She folds it once and puts it in the analysis notebook between the last used page and the back cover.

**The handset**

19. She goes to the equipment building and sits at the SSB radio console. She picks up the handset. The radio is on standby; the red indicator light shifts as she warms it up for transmission.

20. The chapter ends here.

## Craft Notes for the Drafter

- The **mid-action cut** is the chapter's structural requirement. Do not allow Elena to speak into the radio in this chapter. Do not allow her to dial or key the transmitter. The chapter ends at the moment the radio is warm and the handset is in her hand. The action is initiated but not completed; that is the cut.
- The **face-down page**: she reads it and folds it into the notebook. This is the chapter's moment of quiet resolution for that object — what she decided about what was on the page is implied by the fact that she keeps it rather than destroying it. But the drafter should not state this.
- The **final run sequence** should be written with the same patience as every other run — not rushed because it is the last. Elena is deliberate. She does not know how to be anything else.
- **No retrospective emotion** about the runs being over. No "this is the last time she would." The prose does not editorialize. If there is weight in the last run, it is in the physical details and the closing of the analyzer's housing, not in the narration's commentary on it.
