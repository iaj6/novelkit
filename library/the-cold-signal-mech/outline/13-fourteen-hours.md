# Chapter 13 — Fourteen Hours

**One-line summary:** McMurdo confirms the LC-130 on schedule; Elena organizes her evidence package; she brings the tape housing into the hab for the night; fourteen hours remain.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — equipment building (SSB radio), receiver hut, main hab. Day 11, evening.  
**Target words:** ~2,000  
**Chapter shape:** Two-beat — (1) McMurdo Ops weather brief and flight confirmation; (2) physical organization of the evidence package  
**Ending mode:** Cliffhanger

---

## Escalation (from Chapter Map)

- **(a) Day 11, evening**
- **(b)** Days to foreclosure: under 24 hours. Power reserve: 13 % → 10 % (evening passive draw). Generator voltage variance: further widening noted in afternoon fault log (approaching the threshold where stabilization procedures may not hold).
- **(c)** Step closer: McMurdo has confirmed the LC-130 departs McMurdo at 0700, arrives Winslow approximately 1300. The timeline is fixed. Fourteen hours remain in which Elena holds the choice and the tape.

---

## Goal

Compression and preparation. The chapter is short because the situation no longer has room for expansion. Elena is organizing, not discovering — except that organizing forces the question she has not yet answered: what does she say to Walczak? The chapter ends with the question still unanswered and the tape in the hab.

---

## Obstacle

Elena has been deferring the Walczak question for eleven days. In this chapter, the deferral ends by default: in fourteen hours, Walczak will walk through the door. She can organize the evidence, but organizing it does not answer the question of what she does when he arrives.

---

## Action Beats

**Beat 1: McMurdo weather brief**

1. Evening. McMurdo Ops opens the scheduled weather brief at 1800. The operator's voice: professional, slightly bored, carrying the institutional tone that has been the background note of the whole novel. Weather at Winslow: conditions acceptable for flight. No storms anticipated on the transit corridor.

2. LC-130 departure: McMurdo 0700. Estimated arrival Winslow: 1300, depending on winds. Call sign: [standard VXE-6 callsign]. Crew: [standard]. Passengers: six (she knows who they are without being told). Cargo: the new voltage regulator component, plus standard resupply.

3. Elena acknowledges. She thanks McMurdo Ops with the formal radio close. She notes the ETA: 1300.

4. She does the subtraction: it is approximately 1900 now. Fourteen hours. She will have the morning — if she runs the protocol at 0600, she will have the final run result by 0700 and seven hours before the plane arrives. Time she has not had to think about what she is going to say, and too little of it for the thinking to help.

**Beat 2: Preparing the evidence package**

5. Elena goes to the receiver hut. She does not run the protocol (the morning is the right time for that, when the generator is most stable). She checks the tape one more time by visual inspection — no change visible since the morning run. The housing temperature is within range.

6. She makes a decision: she is not leaving the tape in the receiver hut overnight. The hut is the coldest space in the station; the temperature fluctuates as the heater cycles. She will bring the tape to the main hab for the night, where the temperature is higher and more stable. She has never done this before; the tape's assigned storage is the receiver hut. She lifts the housing and carries it through the covered walkway.

7. In the main hab: she clears a space on the equipment cabinet shelf — moves the thermal blanket and the printout, makes room. She puts the tape housing on the shelf. She turns on the small space heater near the cabinet. The hab is warmer than the hut. The tape is safer here tonight.

8. She takes out the analysis notebook and reads the evidence summary she wrote this morning. She does not change anything. She makes one addition: "Day 11 evening: tape transferred to main hab for overnight storage. Run 6 scheduled for Day 12 morning, pre-flight."

9. She takes out a sheet of blank paper from the supply shelf and begins writing — not the notebook, not the log, a separate page. She is drafting something; what, exactly, the chapter does not show the text of. The reader sees her writing, pausing, writing again.

10. She stops. She does not finish the page. She sets it aside — face down on the table. She does not destroy it; she sets it aside.

11. She considers using the last instrument run tonight. The generator has been stable this afternoon; the voltage variance stabilization procedure worked this morning. But the power reserve is at 10 % and she has calculated the margin carefully: one run tomorrow morning, with the stabilization procedure, will bring the reserve to approximately 2–3 %, which is above the life-support threshold but only just. If she runs tonight AND tomorrow, she falls below safe threshold before the team lands — and Walczak would arrive to a station running on its reserves with no explanation for why.

12. She decides to hold the run for tomorrow morning. She writes this in the log: "Run 6: scheduled for Day 12, 0600."

13. **End of chapter.** She goes to her bunk. The sleeping bag is still unzipped and folded back the way it has been for eleven days. She turns off the galley light. The red standby light of the SSB radio is on. The tape housing is in the cabinet across the room. Fourteen hours.

---

## Outcome / Cliffhanger

The cliffhanger: fourteen hours, one run, no answer to the Walczak question. She has the tape in the hab and a page she started writing and didn't finish. The chapter ends before anything is resolved. The morning is coming whether she sleeps or not.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

What was she writing on that page? What will she say to Walczak? And: will the final run show what she needs it to show?

---

## Craft Notes for the Drafter

- The **page she sets aside face-down** is one of the novel's most loaded objects. The chapter should not explain what it is; the reader should feel its weight without reading it. It might be the beginning of a letter to Walczak. It might be the cover statement she would submit if she were submitting. It might be a resignation, a justification, a protocol note. The drafter should not resolve this; it should remain ambiguous enough that multiple interpretations are consistent.
- The **tape housing in the hab** is a meaningful physical act — the first time in the novel the tape leaves the receiver hut for a warmer space. The drafter should let this carry weight without explaining why it does.
- This chapter is short (~2,000 words) because compression is appropriate for the eve of the final day.

---

## Continuity Notes

- The tape is now in the **main hab's equipment cabinet** overnight — not the receiver hut. This must be consistent in Chapter 14 (Elena fetches it back to the receiver hut for Run 6 in the morning).
- Power reserve: ~10 % after this chapter.
- Days to foreclosure: under 24 hours.
- LC-130 ETA: 1300 on Day 12. This is canonical from this chapter forward; subsequent chapters must use this arrival time.
- Generator voltage variance: approaching the threshold where the stabilization procedure may not reliably hold. Chapter 14 will show whether the morning stabilization succeeds or requires extra care.
- The **page set aside face-down**: the drafter may leave it on the galley table through Chapter 14's morning sequence, visible but not opened. Its fate (destroyed, given to Walczak, discarded) can be implied in Chapter 15.
