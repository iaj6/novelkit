# Chapter 09 — Core Dropout

**One-line summary:** Protocol Run 4 reveals a twelve-second dropout in the second parametric segment; full parametric verification is permanently foreclosed; what remains is the first segment and the core signature.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — receiver hut (Run 4), then main hab (analysis). Day 8.  
**Target words:** ~2,800  
**Chapter shape:** Single scene — one extended activity (Protocol Run 4 and its aftermath)  
**Ending mode:** Turn

---

## Escalation (from Chapter Map)

- **(a) Day 8**
- **(b)** Tape integrity: Run 4 reveals a **twelve-second dropout in the core record** — the second parametric segment is gone. The dropout is no longer edge-only. Instrument run margin: 3 → 2. Power reserve: 38 % → 30 %. Days to foreclosure: 4.
- **(c)** **PERMANENTLY FORECLOSED:** full parametric verification. The complete parameter set Elena's protocol was designed to prove is no longer achievable with this tape. What remains is the first parametric segment (minus its opening five-to-eight seconds, now lost) and the core signature — sufficient for a provisional claim but not a peer-reviewed one.

---

## Goal

Deliver the chapter's turn cleanly: the tape is worse than Elena's visual estimates indicated, and the damage is now in the part of the record that matters most for the precision of her claim. This chapter is about what it costs to have a career founded on the standard of proof she insisted on — and to discover that the instrument is now definitively below that standard. What remains is still real. It is just not what she built her career to produce.

---

## Obstacle

The stabilization procedure required before the run (due to the widened voltage variance after yesterday's fault) costs fifteen minutes of generator draw. Elena cannot skip it. She has learned from the chapter 8 fault that the voltage variance is widening; running the instrument without stabilization would risk data corruption in the run, making it worse than not running at all.

---

## Action Beats

**Preparation**

1. Elena wakes on Day 8 with a clear intention: she will run the protocol today. She has not run it since Day 4. The tape has been degrading for four days in between. She needs to know the current state.

2. The stabilization procedure: she runs the generator at reduced instrument load for fifteen minutes before starting the analyzer, letting the voltage variance settle. She watches the output gauge. The variance is wider than it was before Day 7's fault — running at +/- 7 % instead of the +/- 5 % she had before. Not catastrophic, but she notes it.

3. She checks the tape housing temperature. Within range. She takes the reel out of the housing and does a brief visual inspection before the run: the binder stress zone is visibly larger than Day 4, though still concentrated at the reel edge.

**Protocol Run 4**

4. She initiates Run 4. The warm-up cycle. The analyzer's amber numerals cycling through their self-test. The tape loads into the playback head.

5. **First parametric segment.** The tape plays. She watches the analyzer's output display as the frequency sweep runs through the first parametric segment. The opening five to eight seconds: silence. The binder failure she documented in Chapter 4, now slightly wider. Then: the rest of the first segment plays cleanly. The frequency distribution is intact. The first parametric profile reads.

6. **The gap.** Between the first and second parametric segments, there is a brief silence that is normal — a transition in the signal's structure. She knows this part of the recording; she has run this protocol three times before. The transition ends and the second parametric segment should begin.

7. **It does not begin.** The analyzer's display shows: no signal. Not noise, not degraded signal — silence. She watches for ten seconds. Fifteen. The analyzer is not receiving anything from the tape in this region.

8. She stops the run. Rewinds to the beginning of the second parametric segment. Plays it again. Same result: twelve seconds of silence where the second segment should be, followed — at twelve seconds — by the tape recovering and the signal resuming in the third segment (a shorter calibration tail that is not parametric but confirms the event's duration).

9. She plays the twelve-second gap three times. It is not recoverable. The binder has failed in the core of the record, not just the edge. The second parametric segment — the one that extends and completes the parametric profile, the one that gives the signal's full characterization — is gone.

**Completion of the run**

10. She lets the run complete. Everything after the twelve-second gap reads: the calibration tail, the post-event baseline. The core signature — the structure that makes the signal non-terrestrial and non-natural — is intact in the first parametric segment. That is not nothing. But the full parametric profile her protocol was designed to establish requires both segments, and the second segment is gone.

11. Power reserve after the run: ~30 %. Instrument margin: 2 remaining.

**Analysis**

12. She sits in the work chair with the run log printout in her hand. She goes through it slowly, in the way she has gone through data for thirty years: assuming first that she has made an error, checking each step, eliminating the possibility of error one element at a time.

13. She has not made an error. The second parametric segment is unreadable. The tape binder has failed in the core of the signal record.

14. She goes through what remains: the first parametric segment (minus its opening five-to-eight seconds, now also lost to binder failure) is sufficient to establish the signal as anomalous, non-terrestrial, non-natural. It is not sufficient — alone — for the full parametric characterization her protocol was designed to produce. What she can claim, on this basis, is: a signal was received, its primary anomalous parameters are X, Y, and Z (from the first segment), its duration was forty-seven seconds, it did not repeat. What she cannot claim, on this basis, is the complete parametric profile that would allow a peer reviewer to say: "This is definitively [X category] and not [Y category] within the established classification framework."

15. She writes this down in the analysis notebook. Carefully, in the precise language she would use in a paper. She is not writing a draft of a paper; she is writing an honest accounting of what the evidence now supports and does not support.

16. She takes the run log back to the receiver hut and files it in the protocol folder she has been maintaining since Day 1.

**End of chapter**

17. She stands in the hut for a moment with the folder in her hands. The tape is in its housing. The signal analyzer is back in standby. The amber numerals have gone quiet.

18. What she has: the core signature of the signal, readable in the first parametric segment. The signal's duration and primary anomalous parameters. Her three prior runs confirming the signal's presence. The printout (still intact as of Day 4; she will check it again). The analysis notebook. Two runs remaining.

19. What she does not have: the full parametric profile. The second segment. The chain of evidence that would survive the peer review she had designed her protocol to pass.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The turn recontextualizes everything: Elena spent ten days (in her career) building a protocol that produces a level of proof she can no longer achieve with the tape she has. She can still make a claim. But not the claim she built her career to make. The tape has decided for her what kind of claim this will be.

## New Question / Pressure Raised

What kind of claim does the remaining evidence support, and is Elena willing to make it? Is a provisional claim — one that states the signal's primary anomalous parameters without the full profile — something she can stake her name on, given that her name stands for the other thing?
