# Chapter 07 — The Window

**One-line summary:** The relay window opens at 0800 and closes at 0848; Elena does not transmit; the institutional channel closes permanently.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — receiver hut, fax terminal (equipment building), back to receiver hut. Day 7, 0500–0848.  
**Target words:** ~3,100  
**Chapter shape:** Bottle — single time-compressed sequence, near real-time from pre-dawn to 0848  
**Ending mode:** Mid-action cut

---

## Escalation (from Chapter Map)

- **(a) Day 7, 0800–0848**
- **(b)** Hours to gate: 0. Days to foreclosure: 5 (new operative quantity). Power reserve: 47 % → 41 % (terminal active during window + warm-up cycles).
- **(c)** **PERMANENTLY FORECLOSED:** the institutional relay transmission channel. The window will not recur before Day 12. Elena now holds the data and must decide what to do with it in five days.

---

## Goal

This is the book's hardest chapter and its structural hinge. The relay window opens and closes. Something is decided — by action or by inaction, both are choices — and after this chapter, the path not taken is gone. The chapter must be long because it is the book's pivot; because the forty-eight minutes of the window must be felt; because Elena's non-transmission must be shown, not told, without any suggestion that the decision was made before this moment.

**The decision must be genuine until it is done.** The drafter must not signal, foreshadow, or retroactively imply that Elena "knew all along" she wouldn't send. She is in the room, she has the data packet on the terminal, the carrier signal is live, and she is choosing. The chapter ends at the moment the carrier dies, not in Elena's reflection about the choice.

---

## Obstacle

Time. The window is 48 minutes. Every minute of inaction is a minute of decision. And: the tape shows new degradation (eight-second dropout measured this morning before the window), which could push her toward sending — or toward realizing the tape is dying and she needs to verify once more before she commits to a transmission that frames the data without its last clean verification.

---

## Action Beats

**0500–0700: Pre-dawn**

1. Elena wakes before she intends to. She is not rested. She makes coffee in the galley, standing at the cold stove. The mug warms more slowly than it should because the corridor hatch seal is cracked.

2. She goes to the receiver hut at 0530, an hour and a half before the window. She checks the tape. She holds the housing and looks at the reel through its inspection panel. She takes out the timing-mark tool and measures the dropout against the marks: eight seconds confirmed, maybe eight and a half. It has grown overnight.

3. She does not run the protocol. She cannot — the power reserve is at 47 %, and a stabilization procedure plus a protocol run before the window would push the reserve low enough that the terminal draw during the window would risk a brownout. She has to choose between a run and a clean transmission window. She chooses the clean window.

4. She returns to the equipment building. The fax terminal screen is dark (sleep mode). She wakes it. The data packet is still there — she did not close it last night. The cursor blinks.

5. She reads through the cover statement again. She makes one small change to a phrase. The change is not improvement; it is postponement.

6. **0630.** She sits at the terminal and reads through the data packet's metadata summary. She is not checking it for errors; she checked it yesterday. She is reading it the way you read a document you have already decided to sign.

**0700–0800: The hour before the window**

7. The relay carrier signal indicator on the terminal is dark. The window has not opened. She checks the clock.

8. She goes to the receiver hut again. She stands in front of the signal analyzer and looks at the amber numerals of the analyzer's standby display. She thinks about the signal as it arrived — the moment on Day 1 when the numerals changed. She thinks about what the data packet's compressed metadata will look like in the USAP relay buffer, eight hours from now, in McMurdo.

9. She thinks about who will read it first. Someone in the McMurdo operations center, passing it up the chain. The chain is not abstract to Elena; she knows the chain. She knows the names, the institutional dynamics, the grant structures. She knows what happens when a signal of this kind moves through those structures without a scientist's hand on it.

10. She checks the clock again: 0750.

11. She returns to the terminal. She sits down.

**0800–0848: The window**

12. **0800.** The carrier signal indicator on the terminal flashes green. The relay satellite has come over the horizon; the uplink window is open. She has 48 minutes.

13. She does not move immediately. She looks at the indicator. The terminal is showing the packet queue: one item, addressed and ready. The initiation command is a single keystroke.

14. She picks up a pen and writes a timestamp in her notebook: "0800 — window open." She does not know why she is doing this; it is the action of someone who needs something to do with her hands.

15. **0812.** She is still sitting at the terminal. The cursor blinks. She has used twelve minutes.

16. She stands. She goes to the equipment building's small window — the only exterior window in the building — and looks out at the Antarctic dark. She can see nothing. She comes back to the terminal.

17. **0822.** She has the initiation command ready. She does not execute it.

18. She leaves the terminal and goes, one more time, to the receiver hut. This is not analysis. This is the kind of movement that happens when a body cannot sit still at the moment it most needs to sit still.

19. In the receiver hut: the tape housing, the analyzer, the printout gone to the hab cabinet. She puts her hand on the tape housing. She is thinking about Lars, about the data filed as noise at Mather. If she transmits, the agencies will have one record — hers, partially dropout-affected, with a degrading second parametric segment. If she holds, she has three runs and five days, and the tape will degrade further, and on Day 12 the choice is different but not better.

20. **0838.** She returns to the terminal.

21. She sits down. The window closes in ten minutes.

22. She reads the cover statement one final time. She has not changed anything. Her hand is near the keyboard.

23. **0848.** The carrier signal indicator goes dark. The satellite has moved past the transmission window. The indicator shows: CARRIER LOST.

24. The data packet is still on the terminal. The window is gone.

25. Elena sits for a long moment. Her hand is no longer near the keyboard. The packet queue shows: one item, unsent.

---

## Outcome / Mid-Action Cut

**End chapter here — at the carrier loss, not after.**

The mid-action cut lands at 0848 when the CARRIER LOST indicator appears. The drafter must not write Elena's reflection, her relief or regret, or any naming of what she has done. The chapter's last beat is the indicator going dark, full stop. The aftermath is Chapter 8's work.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

She has five days. She has three runs. She has a degrading tape. She chose not to send — or she did not choose to send, which is not the same thing, though it has the same result. What does she do now?

---

## Craft Notes for the Drafter

- The 48-minute window must be **felt as 48 minutes**, not summarized. Use the timestamps. Let the chapter breathe around them. The elapsed time is the chapter's mechanism.
- The **decision must be genuine throughout.** The reader should believe until 0848 that Elena might send. She might have. She didn't. Both must be true simultaneously.
- The **mid-action cut** on the carrier-loss indicator is the chapter's ending. No reaction. No summary. No Elena's face, her hands, her breathing, her thought. The carrier dies; the chapter ends. This is the only acceptable ending mode for this chapter.
- Physical movement through the chapter — terminal to receiver hut to window to terminal — is expressive of Elena's inability to sit still at the moment of decision. But the movements should not be so numerous that they begin to feel like stalling; they should feel like the way a careful person paces before an irreversible act.
- Do not use "she decided" at any point in this chapter. The prose must show the decision happening (or not happening) without naming it.
