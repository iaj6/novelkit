# Chapter 03 — Anomalous

**One-line summary:** Scheduled SSB contact with Lars Engström; he mentions in passing an anomalous reading at Mather that he filed as artifact; Elena recognizes the implication and says nothing.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — equipment building (SSB radio console), with a brief morning visit to the receiver hut. Day 3.  
**Target words:** ~2,800  
**Chapter shape:** Two-beat — (1) morning check and equipment log; (2) SSB contact with Lars Engström  
**Ending mode:** Turn

---

## Escalation (from Chapter Map)

- **(a) Day 3**
- **(b)** Hours to gate: ~144 h → ~96 h (four days remaining). Power reserve: 71 % → 67 % (overnight passive draw; no instrument run today).
- **(c)** Step closer: Lars Engström's mention of an anomalous reading at Mather (~one minute in duration, filed as instrument artifact) means the signal may not be entirely Elena's to control. Independent detection may already exist sixty kilometers east.

## Goal

Introduce Lars Engström as a voice — careful, precise, slightly formal, not pressing. Establish the SSB contact rhythm. Then deliver the chapter's turn: the signal that Elena believes is hers alone may already have been detected by someone who doesn't know what he detected.

---

## Obstacle

Elena cannot ask the questions she wants to ask without revealing what she has. The SSB protocol (call signs, turn-based speaking, the formal register of Antarctic radio traffic) constrains what she can say. She can probe only obliquely.

---

## Action Beats

**Beat 1: Morning — equipment building and receiver hut**

1. Elena begins the day with the generator check — fuel gauge, voltage regulator status, output readings. The generator has behaved overnight. She records its output in the maintenance log, notes the voltage variance (within normal range today, but she has learned to note it every morning).

2. She goes to the receiver hut. She does not run the protocol. She checks the tape housing temperature (within the range she is now managing with the 70% heater setting), confirms the printout is still in the hab cabinet. She writes in her personal log — not the station log, but her fieldwork notebook — a brief entry. She does not write what the entry says. The chapter should not transcribe it; the act of writing is enough.

3. She returns to the equipment building and sits at the communications console. The SSB net with Lars Engström is scheduled for 1400 local. She has two hours.

4. She considers: should she tell Lars today? She thinks through what it would mean to tell him. She reaches no conclusion. She makes tea.

**Beat 2: SSB contact with Lars Engström**

5. The contact opens at 1400. Lars identifies by call sign; Elena responds. The voice comes through with the slight flutter of long-distance HF — not poor quality, but distinctly distant. Lars speaks English with precise, formal syntax; he is Norwegian in his mid-forties and speaks like someone who learned the language from scientific papers.

6. Routine exchange: station status, weather (Mather has had a minor wind event; Elena's conditions have been stable), the ice conditions, any maintenance issues. Lars asks about Elena's generator (she has mentioned the voltage regulator fault on a previous contact); she says it is stable today.

7. Lars mentions his atmospheric chemistry data collection — he is about seven weeks into his winterover and his results have been routine, which he says with something that might be satisfaction or might be resignation. Elena knows which it is, but doesn't say so.

8. Near the end of the contact — as a closing remark, the kind of thing you mention because the silence needs filling — Lars says: he had some anomalous instrument readings last week. "Probably instrument artifact," he says. "My diagnostics didn't flag anything, so I filed it and moved on." He says it without emphasis, the way a careful scientist reports something he has decided is not significant.

9. Elena asks: what kind of anomaly? She keeps her voice neutral. Lars says: a short-duration transient event in a side band he wasn't monitoring closely. A UHF frequency he was not targeting. He ran his diagnostics; nothing wrong with the instrument. He suspects RFI from the Mather Station's own equipment during a maintenance cycle that week.

10. Elena asks: roughly how long did it last? She is aware of the question, of how specifically she is asking it. Lars says: "About a minute, perhaps slightly less. It was not something I spent time on."

11. Elena says: "I've been seeing some side-band anomalies too. Probably the ionosphere." She signs off with the formal traffic close.

12. She sits at the console after the radio goes quiet. She does not move for a while.

13. Lars caught something approximately a minute in duration (slightly less — closer to 47 seconds is consistent with "about a minute") in a UHF side band he was not monitoring. He attributed it to his own station's RFI during a maintenance cycle. He filed it. He moved on.

14. She does the timeline in her head: when was Mather's maintenance cycle last week? She doesn't know exactly, but the day he named is consistent with the night she received the signal. The propagation delay from a distant source to Mather, sixty kilometers east, before reaching Winslow, is consistent with independent reception of the same event.

15. She has not confirmed this. She cannot confirm it without asking Lars for more information than she can ask for without revealing what she has. She goes to make another cup of tea, holds the mug, and does not pick up the radio again.

---

## Outcome / Turn

The turn is the recognition: Lars Engström may have the only independent corroboration of the signal, filed as instrument noise, inaccessible unless Elena tells him what to look for. The signal is no longer entirely contained within Winslow Station. Whether this is a problem or a resource depends entirely on what she decides to do — and she has not decided anything.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

If Lars has corroborating data and doesn't know it, what does that mean for Elena's ability to frame the announcement? If she tells him and he confirms — she has support, but she loses sole custody. If she doesn't tell him and the signal is announced later, his data is filed as noise forever. And: what exactly were the parameters of Mather's anomaly?
