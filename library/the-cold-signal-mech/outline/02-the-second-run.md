# Chapter 02 — The Second Run

**One-line summary:** First cold morning after; tape shows binder stress; heating versus instrument power must be rationed; second run confirms the signal.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — receiver hut, then main hab. Day 2, morning and afternoon.  
**Target words:** ~2,600  
**Chapter shape:** Single scene — one extended activity (tape inspection + Protocol Run 2), one location  
**Ending mode:** Literary fade

---

## Escalation (from Chapter Map)

- **(a) Day 2**
- **(b)** Tape integrity: first binder stress observed at reel edge (temperature threshold breached overnight; heater cycled low). Instrument run margin: 7 → 6 (Run 2). Power reserve: 78 % → 71 %.
- **(c)** Step closer: heater and instruments cannot run simultaneously at full capacity from this point. One must be rationed every day forward.

---

## Goal

Show Elena's first morning-after response, which is methodical rather than ecstatic or panicked. She begins to understand the physical fragility of what she is holding. The chapter is about the discovery that the tape and the power and the temperature are not neutral — they are already in motion against her.

---

## Obstacle

The receiver hut was colder than spec overnight because the space heater cycled to its minimum-draw mode during the night (a known behavior, but one she has not had to account for while also worrying about the tape). The tape binder has been exposed to temperatures below the acceptable threshold. She does not yet know if the tape is damaged; she has to look.

---

## Action Beats

1. **Elena has not slept well.** She returns to the receiver hut in the early morning. The covered walkway between the hab and the hut is colder than the night before — a pressure-drop. She does not note this yet; she notes the temperature on the hut's wall thermometer: three degrees below the minimum she has been holding.

2. **Physical inspection of the tape.** She unreels a test section of the tape — not the signal segment, but the fifteen minutes of monitoring record immediately before it — and examines it under the magnifying glass she uses for instrument repair work. What she is looking for: visual binder stress, flaking, any change in the tape's surface texture. What she finds: the outermost layer of the reel (the section wound on last, representing the most recent monitoring time) shows faint stress marks along the edge. This is the binder beginning to fail under cold exposure.

3. **The heating decision.** Elena goes through the calculation she has been avoiding: the space heater and the signal analyzer draw from the same generator circuit. Running both at full capacity in the same hour pushes the generator's output close to the voltage regulator's fault zone. She has two options: run the heater lower and accept a colder hut (and a colder tape); or run the heater at target temperature and accept that instrument runs require load-shedding the heater temporarily. She settles on a third course: heater at 70 % continuously, full instrument draw only during runs. The hut will be marginally warmer than last night; the tape will not be at full spec temperature, but it will not degrade faster than it is already.

4. **She runs Protocol Run 2.** Set-up: same procedure as Run 1, same parameters. Before she starts, she notes the power gauge. The generator is at its morning baseline. She initiates the run. This time: no brownout. The tape plays back cleanly. The signal reads.

5. **She reads the result carefully.** Same pattern. Same 47 seconds. Same parametric structure. The core signature is intact; the binder stress she found visually has not yet produced dropout in the playback. She makes a careful note: "Run 2: signal intact. Visual binder stress present at reel edge. Does not affect playback at current state. Monitor."

6. **Post-run inventory.** Power reserve at ~71 %. Instrument margin: 6 runs remaining. She makes a small note next to her estimated budget: "Edge stress noted. Handle with minimum unreeling."

7. **The printout.** She takes the printout from the work surface where she left it overnight and checks it in better light. The paper looks intact. The ink is sharp. She wraps it in the station's spare thermal blanket — the gray one from the equipment building's supply shelf — and puts it in the main hab's equipment cabinet, the warmest accessible enclosed space in the station.

8. **End-of-chapter beat.** She adjusts the heater one more notch, records the morning temperature in the hut log, and does not look at the tape again for the rest of the afternoon. She eats something in the galley. The mug of coffee goes colder than it should because the corridor seal is cracked and the cold bleeds through.

---

## Outcome

The second run confirms the signal. The tape is not yet critically damaged, but Elena now knows it is not stable. The heating tradeoff has been defined and she has made a choice she cannot unmade: 70 % heat, full instruments only during runs.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

At what rate is the binder degrading? How much time does she have before the stress becomes dropout? She does not yet know how to answer this, and she has no way to measure it except to wait and run another protocol.
