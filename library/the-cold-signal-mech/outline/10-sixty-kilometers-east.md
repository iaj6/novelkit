# Chapter 10 — Sixty Kilometers East

**One-line summary:** Generator voltage variance widens; Lars Engström reviews his monthly log and reveals that Mather Station recorded a forty-seven-second anomalous event fourteen minutes before Elena's detection; she does not tell him what he had.

**POV:** Dr. Elena Hartmann  
**Tense:** Third-person limited past  
**Setting:** Winslow Station — equipment building (generator check, then SSB radio console). Day 9.  
**Target words:** ~2,800  
**Chapter shape:** Two-beat — (1) morning generator assessment; (2) SSB contact with Lars Engström  
**Ending mode:** Cliffhanger

---

## Escalation (from Chapter Map)

- **(a) Day 9**
- **(b)** Generator voltage variance: +/- 8 % (was +/- 7 % after Day 7 fault; widening). Each remaining run now requires a pre-run stabilization procedure costing fifteen minutes of generator draw and additional power reserve. Days to foreclosure: 3. Power reserve: 30 % → 24 %.
- **(c)** Step closer: Lars's monthly log review reveals Mather Station recorded a forty-seven-second anomalous event in the UHF secondary band, fourteen minutes before Elena's detection at Winslow — independent corroboration. The option to be the sole holder of knowledge about the signal is foreclosed. But the corroboration is inaccessible as evidence unless Elena tells Lars what he had, which would mean telling Lars.

---

## Goal

Two interlocking discoveries in one chapter: the generator is getting worse (making each remaining run more expensive), and there is corroborating data sixty kilometers away that Elena cannot use without revealing her hand. This chapter is about a form of abundance — corroboration she didn't have yesterday — that cannot actually help her, and may make her situation worse.

---

## Obstacle

The SSB contact with Lars is a scheduled exchange governed by radio protocol. Elena cannot ask directly; she can only listen. What Lars reveals, he reveals because he is being thorough, not because he knows what Elena is sitting on. The harder Lars's revelation is to hear, the more disciplined Elena must be in not responding to it.

---

## Action Beats

**Beat 1: Morning generator assessment**

1. Elena begins Day 9 with the generator check. She has been doing this every morning; today the numbers are worse than yesterday. The voltage variance has widened to +/- 8 % — a significant jump from the +/- 7 % she recorded after the Day 7 fault. The generator is not failing, but it is degrading toward a failure state that the new regulator component (arriving on Day 12 with Walczak) would fix.

2. She runs the math she has been avoiding: if each remaining instrument run now requires a fifteen-minute stabilization procedure to bring the voltage variance into safe range, and the stabilization procedure costs approximately 3–4 % of the power reserve on top of the run itself, then each of her two remaining runs will cost roughly 12–14 % of remaining reserve (stabilization + run draw). At current reserve (~28 % heading into today), she has room for two runs, but barely — and only if nothing else goes wrong.

3. She records the fault data in the maintenance log, in the same columns she has been filling since Day 1. The columns are almost full.

4. She does not run the protocol today. She cannot afford to spend a run without a clear purpose, and today's purpose is the Lars contact.

**Beat 2: SSB contact with Lars Engström**

5. The scheduled contact opens at 1400. Lars identifies by call sign. He is preparing his monthly report to the Norwegian Polar Institute and has been going through his instrument logs more carefully than usual.

6. Routine exchange first: weather (a wind event at Mather, moderate, has passed), station status, Lars's chemistry data. Lars mentions that his data collection has been going well; his winterover work is producing the results he expected.

7. Then Lars says: he went back to look at that anomalous reading he mentioned on Day 3. His monthly report requires him to either document or formally discard anomalous readings, so he has been more careful with it.

8. He gives the parameters. Duration: **forty-seven seconds.** He is precise because he has now timed it from his log trace. Frequency: UHF secondary band (he names the specific frequency). Timestamp: he gives the date and time.

9. Elena asks him to repeat the duration. She keeps her voice level.

10. Lars repeats it: forty-seven seconds. He adds: "The timing precision is a little low — the logging interval on that band is two seconds, so I should say forty-six to forty-eight seconds. But forty-seven is the center."

11. Elena does the calculation in her head. The timestamp Lars has given is fourteen minutes before her own detection at Winslow. The propagation delay from a source at interstellar distance, received first at Mather (which sits sixty kilometers east on a bearing consistent with the sky region Elena was monitoring), and fourteen minutes later at Winslow — it is consistent. Not proof, but consistent.

12. She asks: has he filed this in his formal station log? Lars says: "I've noted it as unresolved instrument artifact. That's all it seems to be — my diagnostics don't flag any instrument fault, but I can't rule out RFI from our maintenance cycle. I'll note it in the monthly report as an unresolved anomaly and move on."

13. Elena says nothing for a moment that is one beat too long for comfortable radio protocol. Then: "What frequency exactly?" Lars gives the precise frequency. She writes it down.

14. She says: "I had something similar here. I think you're right about RFI." This is the first direct lie Elena has told in this chapter. It is the only lie she has told in the novel. She does not make it dramatic; she says it in the same voice she uses for everything on this radio.

15. Lars says: "Good to know it's not just us. Strange month." He signs off.

16. After the radio goes quiet, Elena holds the handset for a moment before setting it down. Lars has the corroborating data. It sits in his log as an unresolved anomaly — documented but not analyzed. If he knew what he was looking at, his record would be independent confirmation: a different station, a different frequency band, a different instrument, an earlier detection time consistent with the propagation geometry.

17. He does not know what he is looking at. She has just told him it is probably RFI.

18. She cannot un-tell him that. She could call back. She could say: "Lars, I need you to look at that reading again." She does not.

19. Days to foreclosure: 3. She writes in the notebook: "Mather Station: 47s event, UHF [frequency], T-14 min relative to Winslow reception. Lars Engström. Filed as unresolved artifact." She stares at what she has written. Then she closes the notebook.

---

## Outcome / Cliffhanger

The cliffhanger is the gap between what the corroboration could do and what Elena can now do with it. She has independent confirmation of the signal from an independent instrument at an independent location — and it is filed as noise in Lars's monthly report, increasingly unusable because she has just told him she thinks it is RFI. The option to deploy this corroboration without revealing everything she has withheld for nine days is effectively closed. Three days to foreclosure.

---

## New Question / Pressure Raised

She has just told Lars it is probably RFI. Can she un-tell him? If she calls back, what does she say? And: does she want the corroboration enough to reveal what she has been sitting on for nine days to the one person who could confirm it?
