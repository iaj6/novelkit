# Plotter Guidance — The Cold Signal

## POV Count and Rotation

Single POV: Dr. Elena Hartmann. Every chapter. No rotation, no deviation, no secondary-character perspective inserts. The novel's epistemological premise depends on total confinement to Elena's subjectivity — the reader knows only what she knows, when she knows it.

## Chapter-Shape Preferences

This is a **chamber piece with a depleting-resource engine**. Chapter shapes should reflect the structure of Elena's solitude:

- **Dominant shape:** Single-location, single-extended-activity (a protocol run, a radio contact, a physical inspection of the tape, a generator check). The chapter follows the activity to its conclusion, and the conclusion reveals the worsened state of at least one named quantity.
- **Permitted secondary shape:** Two-beat chapters — a procedure followed by a radio contact, or a discovery followed by a decision point — used when the second beat produces new information that recontextualizes the first.
- **Rare shape:** Interiority-heavy chapters (Elena is not moving through a procedure, just thinking). Use sparingly — maximum 2 of 15 chapters — and only at the book's pivotal hinge moments (the night before the gate, the night after). Even then, anchor the interiority to a physical object or activity.
- **Never:** Multi-location chapters (Elena does not leave the station except for brief exterior checks), dialogue-heavy chapters (radio contacts should be contained, not expansive).

## Episode-vs-Arc Structure

**Continuous arc, not episodic.** Each chapter is one day (or the latter part of one day, for Chapter 1). The chapters flow into each other as a single continuous deterioration. This is not a structure where a chapter can be read in isolation and feel complete; each chapter is intelligible only in the context of the escalating pressure.

The arc has **two irreversible gates**:
1. **Mid-book gate (≈ Chapter 7–8):** The Day 6–7 relay window passes — either with the data sent or without. Whichever Elena does, the other option is permanently foreclosed. This is the book's hardest structural moment and must be its hardest dramatic moment. Do not soften it.
2. **Closing gate (Chapter 15):** The LC-130 lands. Whatever Elena has done with the data by now is the story. The final chapter contains the team's arrival and Elena's first contact with her returning colleagues.

## Escalation Ladder (mandatory for `outline/00-chapter-map.md`)

For every chapter, the outline must specify:
- **(a) The day** (Day 1 through Day 12, with Chapter 1 = Day 1, Chapter 15 = Day 12)
- **(b) The named quantity that worsened** since the prior chapter, and by roughly how much (e.g., "power reserve down 8% from instrument run; instrument margin now 4 runs remaining")
- **(c) What option is permanently foreclosed or one concrete step closer to foreclosure**

A chapter entry without (b) and (c) is a structural failure. The Plotter must fill all three fields for all fifteen chapters before the outline is complete.

**Named quantities to track across the ladder:**
- Power reserve (kilowatt-hours above safety threshold; decreases each instrument run and each cold day)
- Tape integrity (a qualitative + quantitative measure of the magnetic tape's readability; degrades with cold and handling)
- Instrument run margin (number of full verification-protocol runs remaining; each run costs power; this is finite and should reach zero or near-zero by Day 10–11)
- Hours to gate (counts down to zero at Day 6–7; once the gate passes, this quantity becomes "days to foreclosure")
- Days to foreclosure (counts down to zero on Day 12; once the LC-130 lands, the data is no longer Elena's to decide)

Each chapter should worsen at least one of these; the mid-book chapters (Days 4–6) should worsen two or three simultaneously.

## Ending-Mode Distribution (target for 15 chapters)

| Mode | Target count | Notes |
|------|-------------|-------|
| Cliffhanger | 2–3 | Reserve for generator crisis, bad tape read, discovery that Mather may have caught something |
| Mid-action cut | 1–2 | Use for the gate chapter itself — end in the moment of decision, not after |
| Literary fade | 3–4 | Atmospheric close, but weighted by the narrower window |
| Resolved beat | 2–3 | Completion of a protocol run, decision about Walczak, etc. — but larger pressure unresolved |
| Turn | 2–3 | New information that recontextualizes (Walczak's call, the tape read result, the weather report) |
| Elliptical | 1–2 | The night before the gate; the final chapter |
| Coda | 0–1 | Only if the final chapter earns it by resolving the day without resolving the thread |

Do not run the same ending mode for three consecutive chapters.

## Chapter Target Words

Average chapter length: ~2,600 words (15 chapters × 2,600 ≈ 39,000 words, within target of ~40,000).

Variance is permitted and encouraged:
- **Shorter chapters (1,800–2,200 words):** The approach to the gate (Days 5–6); the day after the gate; the final chapter. Compression signals pressure.
- **Standard chapters (2,400–2,800 words):** The working days in the middle.
- **Longer chapters (3,000–3,200 words):** Chapter 1 (the night of the signal — needs to establish Elena, the equipment, the protocol, and the discovery), and the gate chapter (the hardest fork in the book).

Do not allow any chapter to fall below 1,600 words; below that length the escalation ladder cannot be shown, only told.

## Genre-Specific Structural Conventions

This is **literary speculative fiction with a hard-clock structure**. The conventions are:

1. **The clock is not decorative.** The relay gate (Day 6–7) must foreclose one path permanently — not delay it, not complicate it, foreclose it. If Elena does not send the data up the gate, the "send it before the team lands" option disappears. If she does send it, the "keep it private" option disappears. The hinge is real.

2. **The discovery chapter (Chapter 1) must carry both the signal and the clock.** The reader should finish Chapter 1 knowing: what Elena found, why it is unambiguous, why the situation is already urgent, and what the three depleting quantities are. Do not defer the clock to Chapter 2.

3. **The middle does not sag because the window closes.** The plotter's job is to make each chapter's pressure demonstrably higher than the prior chapter's. The escalation ladder is the mechanism. A chapter that is "just a quiet day" is a sag; fill it with a concrete worsening.

4. **The final chapter is not a coda.** The LC-130 arrives, the team lands, Elena has her first physical contact with another human in twelve days. Whatever she has done with the data, the team's arrival is the moment the decision's consequences become real and irreversible in a different register — social, professional, institutional. At least one thread must remain unresolved at the end of this chapter.
