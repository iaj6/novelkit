# Reader Guidance — The Cold Signal

## Intended Audience

Adult literary fiction readers — specifically readers who enjoy quiet, psychologically precise fiction in the tradition of Ishiguro's *The Remains of the Day*, Richard Powers, or Ursula Le Guin's more restrained speculative work. The book is not genre science fiction; it is a chamber piece that uses a speculative premise as a pressure mechanism for a character study. Readers who want alien encounters, decoded messages, or genre-thriller pacing are not this book's audience. The target reader wants a sustained, precise interiority and is willing to sit with a decision that never becomes obvious.

## Brief-Adherence Checks to Perform

The Reader must answer these questions in addition to craft review:

1. **Does each chapter advance at least one named measurable quantity?** (Power reserve, tape integrity, instrument run margin, hours-to-gate, days-to-foreclosure.) If a chapter cannot name the quantity that worsened, it is marking time — flag it as a structural failure, not a pacing choice.
2. **Is the decision genuinely live throughout?** Has the manuscript introduced any moment that implies Elena already knows what she will do, or that the days of deliberation were "just" deliberation with a foregone conclusion? If yes, flag as a critical brief violation.
3. **Is the signal's content withheld?** Any passage that describes what the signal encodes, what message it might carry, or what interpretation is most likely is a brief violation. Flag as critical.
4. **Does the mid-book gate (Day 6–7) function as a true hinge?** Does it permanently foreclose one option? Does the pressure increase rather than relieve after the gate? If the manuscript treats the gate as a delay rather than an irreversible fork, flag as structural failure.
5. **Is extra time rendered as pressure, not relief?** Any weather delay, equipment interruption, or scheduling change that gives Elena "more time" must make her situation worse, not easier. Flag any passage where delay reads as reprieve.
6. **Does the final chapter contain the LC-130 arrival and end without a coda?** At least one thread should be unresolved on the final page. If the manuscript resolves everything or adds reflection beyond the day of arrival, flag as brief violation.
7. **Is Elena alone (no physical human presence) for the entire story except Chapter 15?** Radio contacts only; no other person enters the station before the team lands.

## What "Good" Looks Like for This Book

A good chapter in this novel:
- Opens mid-activity, in Elena's hands and attention, not in her retrospective summary.
- Surfaces the depleting quantity organically through what Elena is doing (she checks the generator gauge because she needs to know if she can run the protocol again; the gauge tells her the number is worse).
- Contains at least one moment where the prose precision earns a physical detail — the temperature of the tape housing, the SSB static pattern, the smell of the receiver hut — that adds weight without decoration.
- Ends with the felt sense that the window is narrower than when it opened.
- Never names what Elena is feeling; shows the shape of the feeling through what she does and does not do.

A good draft of the whole manuscript:
- Reads as a single sustained interiority across 40,000 words, not fifteen separate episodes.
- Has a mid-book gate that the reader feels as a real fork — the moment when the book's tension spikes, not relaxes.
- Respects the signal's opacity: the reader finishes the book knowing the signal was real and knowing nothing about its content, and finds that the opacity was the correct choice.

## What to Flag Explicitly if the Manuscript Drifts

The draft is almost certainly being generated by a model with a default literary fiction register — for this book, that is largely correct. But watch for these specific drift patterns:

- **The decision gets pre-decided.** The most dangerous drift for this brief. Any passage that implies Elena "knew on night one" what she would do, or that frames her deliberation as costume rather than real decision, violates the central contract. Flag at the highest severity regardless of how good the prose is.
- **The signal gets content.** Any description of what the signal might mean, what it could be saying, what kind of intelligence could have sent it — even as speculation in Elena's interior — is a brief violation. The opacity of the signal is the story's spine; breaking it is structural damage.
- **The middle sags (quantity does not worsen).** If two consecutive chapters pass without naming a quantity that has measurably worsened, the escalation ladder has broken. Flag both chapters.
- **The gate softens.** If the Day 6–7 relay window passes and the pressure drops or holds rather than increasing, the structural hinge has failed. The whole brief exists to prevent this specific failure — call it out explicitly.
- **Extra time reads as relief.** Watch for passages that allow a delay, a brownout, a missed schedule to give Elena breathing room. The brief is explicit: extra time tightens, never relieves.
- **The narration names Elena's emotional state.** "She was afraid." "She felt the weight of the decision." These are brief violations in this register — the feeling is shown through action and attention, not labeled. Flag any direct emotion-naming.

## What to Applaud

Defend these in the "what to leave alone" section if the manuscript achieves them:

1. **Scientific proceduralism as interiority.** If Elena's verification protocol is rendered as a full step-by-step sequence that doubles as a portrait of her character (her rigor, her fear of being wrong, her inability to stop running it), do not cut the technical detail for pacing. The procedure IS the character.
2. **The SSB radio constraint as expressiveness.** If the dialogue in radio contact scenes feels constrained, formal, and unable to carry what Elena actually needs to say — good. That is the correct register. Do not flag it as "flat dialogue." The flatness is structural.
3. **The unresolved ending.** If the final chapter leaves the reader without a clean answer about what Elena did with the data or what it means for the world, that is the brief working as intended. Resist any pressure to clarify or resolve. The open thread is not a failure; it is the final structural choice.
