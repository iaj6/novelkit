# Threads

*Three to seven named tensions the reader tracks across chapters. These wrap around the spine
("what will Elena do with the signal?") and give the novel its sense of braid. Each thread is
distinct in its target — relational, epistemic, institutional, material — so that a quiet chapter
that does not advance the main plot question can still advance two or three threads.*

---

## Thread 1: The Walczak Question

- **Question**: Will Elena tell Piotr Walczak about the signal before the team lands, and if
  she does, how will she say a thing that SSB radio protocol does not allow her to say?
- **Enters**: Ch01 — Walczak flew out this morning; Elena does not pick up the radio. The team
  is twelve days away and she has said nothing.
- **Advanced in**:
  - Ch05 — Walczak calls from McMurdo; his questions are technically exact; she answers
    everything accurately except the question he isn't asking; he says *you sound tired* and she
    says *I am*.
  - Ch12 — Elena writes the evidence summary and notes in her own handwriting that she must
    decide how to present this to Walczak. The note is the first time she names him as the
    recipient.
  - Ch13 — She organizes the evidence package for tomorrow; fourteen hours; the face-down page
    is set aside on the table.
  - Ch14 — She picks up the SSB handset after Run 6. The chapter ends before she speaks.
- **Resolves in**: Ch15 — Elena brings Walczak to the equipment building in the first private
  moment after the team's arrival; she opens the analysis notebook to Run 1 and says "I need
  you to read this." The chapter ends before Walczak looks up.
- **What it's about underneath**: The cost of being right alone. Elena and Walczak have
  wintered-over together twice; he is the only person at the station who could read the
  instrument logs and understand what they mean. Her silence across twelve days is not
  concealment — it is the inability to say this thing over a radio in the SSB register, with
  call signs and formal closings, to someone who deserves to see the printout in his hands.
  Her resolution — showing him the notebook rather than telling him anything — is the most
  intimate act in the novel.

## Thread 2: The Mather Corroboration

- **Question**: Is there independent confirmation of the signal at Mather Station sixty
  kilometers east — and can Elena do anything with it, given what she has withheld from the
  man who holds it?
- **Enters**: Ch03 — Lars Engström mentions in passing a short-duration anomalous reading he
  filed as instrument artifact. Elena asks how long it lasted. "About a minute, perhaps slightly
  less." She says: I've been seeing some side-band anomalies too. Probably the ionosphere. She
  signs off.
- **Advanced in**:
  - Ch06 — Elena thinks about Lars while staring at the cursor on the data packet terminal.
    She does not pick up the radio.
  - Ch08 — In the Antarctic dark outside, after the gate has closed: *Lars — the thought
    arrives and leaves quickly. There is something there. She doesn't follow it.*
  - Ch10 — Lars reviews his monthly log and gives Elena the precise parameters: forty-seven
    seconds, UHF secondary band, fourteen minutes before Winslow's detection — consistent
    propagation geometry from a common source. Elena tells him it was probably RFI. This is
    the only lie she tells in the novel.
  - Ch12 — Elena writes the evidence summary. Under "corroborating data" she formally records
    Lars's reading: *Mather Station (Lars Engström), forty-seven-second event in UHF secondary
    band, received fourteen minutes before Winslow detection. Filed as unresolved artifact. Not
    independently verified; not yet communicable without revealing the signal's existence to
    Engström.*
- **Resolves in**: Deliberately unresolved. The notebook — containing Elena's record of Lars's
  data — goes to Walczak in Ch15. Whether Lars is ever contacted, whether the Mather
  instrument logs are ever formally examined, and what the independent detection means for the
  claim's credibility are all past the novel's close. The corroboration exists, in a monthly
  report filed as noise, sixty kilometers east.
- **What it's about underneath**: The accidental nature of discovery, and the specific cost of
  Elena's single lie. Lars caught the same signal fourteen minutes earlier on a different
  instrument in a different band. He is independent confirmation. He does not know this. Elena
  knows this and has told him it was probably interference from his own equipment. She cannot
  un-tell him. The corroboration that would most strengthen the claim — two stations,
  two instruments, two frequency bands, consistent geometry — is unreachable precisely because
  Elena has withheld the signal's existence from the one person who could confirm it.

## Thread 3: The Verification Ceiling

- **Question**: Will Elena be able to prove the signal to herself — completely, by her own
  protocol — before the instrument margin runs out?
- **Enters**: Ch01 — Protocol Run 1 includes a brownout in the calibration segment. Elena
  judges the run valid. She counts eight runs in her power budget. She does not celebrate; she
  calculates.
- **Advanced in**:
  - Ch02 — Run 2; first binder stress at the reel edge; from this point the heater and
    instruments compete for every draw.
  - Ch04 — Run 3; a five-second edge dropout is measured — the first direct evidence that the
    tape is actively degrading.
  - Ch05 — Revised fuel arithmetic: the original 8-run estimate was wrong; true budget is six
    total, three used, three remaining. Two options Elena was holding open are foreclosed by the
    corrected math.
  - Ch09 — Run 4; a twelve-second dropout confirmed in the *core* record — the second
    parametric segment is gone. Full parametric verification is permanently foreclosed from
    this run forward.
  - Ch12 — Run 5; twenty-second dropout; core signature intact. Elena writes: *What can be
    claimed: a provisional statement ... What cannot be claimed: a peer-reviewed complete
    parametric characterization from this tape.*
  - Ch14 — Run 6; twenty-three-second dropout; instrument margin reaches zero. The core
    signature is still there.
- **Resolves in**: Ch14 — Partial and bittersweet. The most critical evidence — the primary
  anomalous parameters of the first parametric segment — is confirmed on the final run. The
  complete parametric profile her protocol was designed to produce is gone since Run 4. Elena
  cannot prove the signal completely by her own standard; she can prove the most important
  part of it.
- **What it's about underneath**: A scientist's relationship to the protocols she invented.
  Elena spent thirty years building a standard of evidence designed so that when she called
  something real, no one could argue with her. The one signal that is real has degraded past
  the point where the full protocol can be satisfied. The question of whether the incomplete
  verification is sufficient — whether the core signature is "enough" — is the novel's deepest
  epistemic problem, and it is left to Walczak (and ultimately the institutions) to decide.

## Thread 4: Who Holds the Frame

- **Question**: Will Elena be able to hand the signal over on her own terms — in her own
  framing, to a person who can read what it actually says — before the institutional machinery
  takes it from her?
- **Enters**: Ch01 — Elena calculates the relay window: six days. She does not pick up the
  radio. Already she is thinking about what it means if the agencies get the summary before
  anyone gets the data.
- **Advanced in**:
  - Ch03 — Lars's unexamined reading means Elena may not be the sole custodian of the
    signal's footprint; the question of who controls the knowledge is already complicated.
  - Ch05 — Walczak calls; Elena stays silent; the institutional timeline is unaffected by
    her silence, but she is holding the frame by not letting the first description of the
    signal be his.
  - Ch06 — Elena writes the cover statement at the fax terminal, aware that it will be the
    first frame anyone outside Winslow reads. She revises it twice. She does not send it.
  - Ch07 — The relay window opens at 0800 and closes at 0848. Elena does not transmit. The
    institutional relay channel closes permanently. She has won the first gate, but at the
    cost of five more days alone with a degrading tape.
  - Ch08 — Post-gate arithmetic: the exits now are hand-to-team, destroy, or publish. None of
    the three is clean. She has kept the frame; she has not yet decided what to do with it.
  - Ch11 — The printout's chain-of-custody timestamp is partially illegible. The
    institutional paper trail is degrading along with the tape; the documentation that would
    support the formal claim is weakening.
  - Ch12 — Elena writes the evidence summary in the language of a paper, not of shorthand.
    She is authoring the frame that Walczak will read.
  - Ch13 — Evidence package assembled; the face-down page set aside for tomorrow.
- **Resolves in**: Ch15 — Partial resolution. Elena brings Walczak to the equipment building
  before the rest of the team has unpacked, and she opens the notebook to Run 1. She has
  controlled the first handoff — scientist to scientist, before the institutional chain takes
  hold. What the agencies and press offices do with it from there is past the chapter's close
  and past the novel's reach.
- **What it's about underneath**: Who owns a discovery you make alone. The relay would have
  put the data in the hands of people who did not write the protocols; McMurdo Ops would have
  been a source, not an author. Elena's twelve days are the only window in which the framing
  belongs to her. She does not keep it forever — she gives it to Walczak, who will give it to
  the field — but she gives it in her own time, in her own language, to the one person who
  can read it before the machinery runs with it.

## Thread 5: The Tape

- **Question**: Will the physical record — the sole magnetic tape and the printout — survive
  the cold long enough to remain usable as evidence?
- **Enters**: Ch01 — The tape is the only copy. The binder is already in a hut sliding toward
  minus-forty. The generator's voltage regulator is already faulting. The clock on the record
  started the moment it was made.
- **Advanced in**:
  - Ch02 — Binder stress confirmed at the reel edge overnight; from this point the heater
    and instruments cannot run simultaneously at full capacity.
  - Ch04 — Five-second edge dropout measured on Run 3. The tape is degrading at a
    quantifiable rate. Elena calculates: at this rate the core record will be compromised in
    eight to twelve days.
  - Ch06 — Eight-second dropout estimated by visual measurement (no run burned). The dropout
    is extending at a visible rate.
  - Ch09 — Run 4 confirms a twelve-second dropout in the *core* record. The second parametric
    segment is gone from the tape permanently.
  - Ch11 — Visual estimate: eighteen seconds. The printout's chain-of-custody timestamp
    column and calibration reference column are partially ink-degraded from cold contraction.
    Three data points are unreadable.
  - Ch12 — Run 5 measures twenty seconds precisely. The core signature is intact. Elena
    writes: *The evidence is fixed. It will not improve.*
  - Ch13 — Elena brings the tape housing into the main hab (warmer than the receiver hut) for
    its final night.
- **Resolves in**: Ch14 — Run 6 measures twenty-three seconds of dropout. The core signature
  is still readable. The tape has survived — partially, barely, exactly enough. The record
  that exists is what will be handed over.
- **What it's about underneath**: The specific vulnerability of the unique object. If the tape
  dies completely — if the dropout reaches the core signature before the team arrives — there
  is nothing to decide about, nothing to hand over, nothing to verify. Its survival is the
  precondition for every other thread. That it survives at all is a close call that the
  Antarctic winter nearly won.

### Dead-chapter check

No chapter advances zero threads. The thinnest chapter in the weave is **Ch02** (T3 + T5 only),
which is structurally appropriate: it is the first cold morning after, a day of consolidation before
the weave thickens. **Ch07** (T4 + T5 only) is also thin, but correctly so: the gate chapter's
entire mass goes into the forty-eight minutes of the window; the thematic compression is the point.

### Deliberate non-resolution confirmation

Per `continuity.md §11`: at least one thread must remain deliberately unresolved in the final chapter.
**Thread 2 (The Mather Corroboration)** fulfills this requirement. Lars Engström's forty-seven-second
reading, filed as unresolved artifact in a Norwegian Polar Institute monthly report, is documented in
Elena's notebook but never acted on within the novel's scope. Whether it is ever examined is past the
chapter's close and past the novel's reach.
