# Style

## POV and Tense

Third-person limited past, bound tightly to Elena Hartmann throughout. No chapter shifts POV. The narration lives inside Elena's perceptual and epistemic world; it does not know what she does not know, does not observe what she does not observe. When the narration names Elena's emotional state, it names only what Elena would concede to herself.

## Prose Register

Patient and exacting — the rhythm of a scientist examining a problem. Sentences follow the grain of Elena's thinking: hypothesis, evidence, inference, provisional conclusion. Not austere: there is room for the weight of small physical observations, for the felt texture of thirty years. But the prose does not reach for beauty it hasn't earned; it earns it by precision, not by ornament.

Narrative distance is close. We are not behind Elena's shoulder; we are in her hand, the one holding the printout.

## Sentence Rhythm

Sentences are middle-length by default — 16–25 words — with deliberate variation. Elena's close attention to procedure produces careful compound sentences. Her anxiety — which she does not name — produces short declaratives when the prose bears down.

The rhythm is not spare. It is measured. There is a difference: spare prose withholds; measured prose allocates. Elena allocates her attention carefully, and the prose reflects that.

## Technical Detail

Technical content enters the prose because Elena is doing science, not because the narrator wants to explain it. Instrument names, protocol steps, power-reserve calculations appear in the flow of Elena's work. They are never lectured; they are always enacted. The reader need not understand Antarctic radio astronomy to feel the weight of what Elena is doing — but the detail must be specific enough that a reader who does know something about it will not wince.

## Dialogue

Sparse. Elena is alone. The radio contacts are her only dialogue; they are constrained by SSB protocol (people speak in turn, identify by call sign at the start, sign off formally). The constraint is expressive: there is a register available to Elena in face-to-face conversation that SSB radio protocol does not permit. She cannot ask the question she wants to ask over the radio. This is not a limitation; it is the story's texture.

Dialogue tags are spare. "She said" is the base case; variation only when it carries content the tag itself does not already have.

## What the Narration Does Not Do

- It does not describe the signal's content. Shape, duration, what makes it unambiguous — yes. Content — never.
- It does not resolve the decision in advance. Elena's choice is unmade until she makes it; the narration does not hint, foreshadow a "right" answer, or frame one option as obviously correct.
- It does not editorialize on the ethics. The story respects the difficulty without naming it as a dilemma.

## Failure Modes

The AI-author patterns most tempting in this story's register. The drafter should actively avoid these; the compression pass should cut them.

- **Sentence-after-the-sentence** — Elena's observations are precise; the next sentence glossing the observation undoes the precision by converting it to statement. An image that lands once should not be explained.
- **Meta-commentary on cognition** — "She noted." "She registered." "She found herself thinking." The narration already shows the thought; labeling it is redundant and distances us from Elena's interiority at the moment we need to be inside it.
- **Post-scene subtext naming** — Elena finishes a protocol run, stares at the result, and then the narration writes a sentence naming what this moment "really" cost her. Cut the naming sentence; the procedure already said it.
- **Emotional structural analysis** — She dramatizes a feeling (the cold in the tape housing, the hiss of a bad contact), then the prose explains the architecture of that feeling. The feeling is in the texture; the analysis is usually the worst sentence in the paragraph.
- **Thesis restatement** — The story's argument (who owns a discovery, what verification costs) must never be stated. Not by Elena, not by the narration. The argument lives in the event, not in its summary.
- **Redundant transition / re-summary** — Each chapter is a day. The temptation is to open a new chapter by re-establishing yesterday's state before advancing today's. Do not. The reader knows what day it is. Begin in motion.

## Lean-Into Patterns

Patterns the drafter and editors should NOT treat as failures, because this register depends on them.

- **The "exactly as it always had" fold** — Elena's world is built on procedure, on the repetition of verification. A sentence that names something by saying it is what it has always been — and then implies it is no longer — is a structural move this novel earns. Use sparingly, as Robinson-style cadence.
- **Free indirect interiority through scientific vocabulary** — Elena's inner voice speaks in her professional language. A sentence in the narration that sounds like a hypothesis statement or a measurement log IS interiority, not exposition. Trust it.
