# Characters

## Dr. Elena Hartmann (POV)

**Age:** 64  
**Role:** Astrobiologist; winterover science lead at Winslow Station  
**Institution:** University of Wisconsin–Madison, contracted through USAP

### Appearance and physical presence
Short, dense, deliberate in movement. Gray hair cropped close. Hands that run cold even in the hab. She moves through the station with the economical confidence of someone who has wintered-over before and knows exactly which corridor hatch sticks.

### Career
Thirty years. Her early work was on extremophile biology in hydrothermal vents; she shifted toward the radio-biosignature question in the early 1980s, when SETI was something you studied quietly, without naming it on your grant applications. She has spent her career at the edge of a claim she was not willing to make — a career defined by not saying it until it was true. The institutional joke ("if Hartmann calls it, it's real") is what happens when rigor becomes reputation: it raises the stakes for the one moment when she might actually be right.

### Inner drives
Elena wants to call the signal real and cannot — not yet. Not without verification. And verification is what her protocol is for. Her professional reflex is to repeat the test, narrow the error bars, eliminate the mundane. But there are a finite number of tests left, and she does not know if she can prove it to herself one more time after the instrument margin runs out.

She also wants the discovery to be handled correctly — framed by someone who understands what the data actually says, not by press officers or agency spokespeople reading a summary. She has watched wrong announcements destroy the careers of better scientists than herself. She will not do that to this.

What she has not fully admitted to herself: she also wants it to be hers. Not for credit — she is sixty-four, her career is nearly done — but because she wrote the protocols and she understands the data and she is the correct person to frame this, and the machinery is not.

### Voice and register
Patient. Precise. Sardonic in small doses about bureaucratic culture ("the committee will note that the committee exists"). Not given to sentiment, but not cold — the sentiment is there in her attention to small things, to procedure, to the way she maintains her equipment with what looks like but is not quite affection. When she is frightened, she runs the protocol again.

### Relationships (voice/radio only; no physical presence until Day 12)
- **Dr. Piotr Walczak** — Winslow's instrument engineer, Elena's closest colleague. He flew out with the team. She has not told him about the signal. The decision about whether to tell him over the radio is part of the pressure.
- **Lars Engström** — researcher at Mather Station, SSB radio contact three times a week. Careful, methodical, slightly formal. Elena trusts him more than she lets on. He does not know what she is sitting on.
- **McMurdo Operations (radio voice)** — routine medical check-ins, weather traffic. Institutional presence, not a person.
- **The team** (Walczak, plus four others) — they land on Day 12. Their arrival is the final foreclosure. We meet them only in the final chapter.

## Secondary Characters (Voice / Radio Only)

### Lars Engström
Winterover researcher, Mather Station. Norwegian, mid-forties, studies ice-core atmospheric chemistry. Speaks English with precise formality; occasionally warms into dry humor. He contacts Elena on the SSB net on a schedule. He becomes, involuntarily, a sounding board — not for the signal (Elena does not tell him directly) but for the general questions she begins to float in their conversations.

### Piotr Walczak
Winslow Station instrument engineer, Polish-American, late forties. He and Elena have wintered-over together twice before. He is the only person at Winslow who could read her instrument logs and understand what they mean. He calls in a standard check-in from McMurdo partway through the story; the question of what to tell him and when is one of the story's live pressures.

### McMurdo Ops (voice only)
Unnamed radio operator. Professional, routine, slightly bored. Represents the institutional machinery Elena is trying to stay ahead of — not malevolent, just indifferent to what it does not know.
