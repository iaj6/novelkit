# World

## Setting

**Winslow Station**, a small United States Antarctic Program (USAP) outpost on the edge of the Weddell Sea quadrant, June 1991. The station houses a skeleton crew of six during summer operations; in the polar winter, the standard configuration is two winterover staff. When the story begins, Elena's five colleagues have flown out to McMurdo for a twelve-day maintenance rotation, leaving her as the sole occupant.

The station consists of three joined structures: the main hab module (bunks, galley, a short corridor), the equipment building (instrument racks, the failing generator, the fax terminal, the HF radio console), and the receiver hut — a separate outbuilding connected by a covered walkway, housing the antenna feeds, the magnetic tape receivers, the signal-processing rack, and the single rattling space heater Elena cannot afford to run continuously.

**The sister station** is Mather Station, a Norwegian-affiliated outpost sixty kilometers east, staffed by two winterover researchers. Elena contacts them via SSB net three times a week. They are a voice in the dark, not a presence; Mather is not accessible overland in winter.

## Period (June 1991)

The Cold War has just ended. SETI is institutionally marginal — NASA's billion-channel spectrometer project hasn't launched yet, and the Drake equation is what gets you laughed out of grant committees. No satellite internet. No e-mail. Communications infrastructure at Winslow:

- **HF single-sideband (SSB) radio** for voice contact with Mather Station, McMurdo, and the USAP network.
- **A scheduled uplink window** via a relay satellite (a narrow, weather-dependent, once-per-fortnight slot) for transmitting data packets and faxes.
- **Fax machine** (facsimile thermal paper, unreliable in extreme cold).
- **VHF radio** for short-range / emergency use.
- A **cassette-based logging system** and reel-to-reel magnetic tape for the primary receiver.

The scheduled mid-winter SSB net (the relay window) falls around Day 6–7 of the story. It is the only channel with bandwidth to carry the full dataset to McMurdo before the LC-130 arrives. After it passes, the next opportunity is the aircraft itself.

## The Cold and the Machine

Minus-forty Celsius outside, minus-fifteen in the receiver hut when the space heater runs on full draw — which it cannot do continuously without pulling the generator below safe margin. The cold does specific things to Elena's equipment: magnetic tape binders degrade faster than the spec assumes; the printout paper contracts and the ink becomes brittle; the instrument racks need warm-up cycles that cost power.

The **generator** is the real antagonist. It is a twelve-kilowatt diesel unit, old by 1991 Antarctic standards, running on a tank whose resupply was scheduled with the team's return. It has developed a fault in the voltage regulator that causes intermittent brownouts. Every instrument run risks a surge; every brownout costs warm-up time and power reserve. The generator's fuel supply and electrical stability are the resource that governs everything else.

## Sensory Texture

The receiver hut at night: the smell of diesel exhaust seeping from the umbilical conduit, the tick and contraction of the metal roof at thirty below, the amber numerals of the signal analyzer glowing in the dark. The galley mug cooling faster than it should because the corridor hatch seal is cracked. The sky outside — when Elena steps out — entirely, perfectly black and littered with stars so dense they look like a texture rather than points. No aurora in June this far east; just the wind and the dark.

The main hab: the particular silence of a space built for six when one person is in it. Elena's bunk with the sleeping bag unzipped and folded back because she is no longer sleeping well. The supply shelf with its labeled bins. The SSB radio's red standby light, the only color in the dark.

## Technology Constraints

- Magnetic tape degrades in cold; the record on the tape is not infinitely stable.
- The signal analyzer is a proprietary instrument: its verification protocol cannot be run on backup hardware; there is no backup instrument.
- There is no mechanism to digitally transmit the full dataset in real time; the relay window can carry a compressed data packet and the printout scan, not a verified-instrument readout.
- The LC-130 Hercules aircraft (operated by U.S. Navy VXE-6) carries passengers and cargo but no onboard analysis capability. Once the team lands, the chain of custody is broken.
