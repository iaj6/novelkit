# Glossary

Alphabetical. Later phases may append entries; do not delete existing entries.

---

**Astrobiologist** — A scientist studying the potential for life elsewhere in the universe, including biosignature detection. In 1991, this is a marginal field; Elena's SETI-adjacent work is formally described in grant applications as "radio biosignature monitoring."

**Brownout** — A partial voltage drop from the generator's faulty voltage regulator, causing instrument racks to lose power momentarily. Each brownout risks data corruption and requires a warm-up cycle, burning additional power reserve.

**Chain of custody** — The formal record of who has handled data and in what sequence. Once the LC-130 lands, the chain of custody for the signal data passes from Elena to the institutional machinery.

**Fax (facsimile)** — Thermal-paper document transmission over telephone/radio lines. The station has a fax terminal that can transmit over the relay window. Cold makes the thermal paper brittle and unreliable.

**The gate** — Elena's shorthand (and the narrative's) for the Day 6–7 communications window: the SSB net to Mather Station coinciding with the relay uplink window. The gate is the book's structural hinge — after it passes, no equivalent transmission channel exists before Day 12.

**Generator** — The station's 12-kilowatt diesel unit, primary power source for all instruments and heating. Its voltage regulator is faulty. Fuel supply is sufficient to last until the team's return, but instrument draws are drawing down usable margin.

**HF radio (high-frequency radio)** — The primary long-distance voice communication medium at the station. Operates in the 3–30 MHz band; range and clarity depend on ionospheric conditions, which vary with solar activity and season.

**Instrument margin** — The remaining number of full verification-protocol runs the signal analyzer can complete before power reserves fall below safe threshold. A finite, decreasing quantity.

**LC-130 Hercules** — The ski-equipped version of the Lockheed C-130 transport aircraft operated by U.S. Navy Antarctic Development Squadron Six (VXE-6). The workhorse of USAP logistics. Capable of landing on snow/ice; carries cargo and personnel. The team's return vehicle.

**Mather Station** — A Norwegian-affiliated Antarctic outpost approximately sixty kilometers east of Winslow Station. Staffed by two winterover researchers. Elena's closest SSB radio contact outside the USAP network.

**McMurdo Station** — The largest U.S. Antarctic base, located on Ross Island. Hub of USAP logistics. Elena's team flew to McMurdo for the twelve-day maintenance rotation. The institutional relay point for communications out of the continent.

**Magnetic tape** — Reel-to-reel magnetic recording medium used to capture the receiver output. The sole physical record of the signal. Cold causes binder degradation; the tape's integrity is a finite, decreasing quantity.

**Power reserve** — The usable kilowatt-hours remaining above the threshold needed to sustain heating and life support. Instrument runs and the space heater compete for this margin; it is the story's primary depleting resource.

**Printout** — A paper record of the signal's analyzed waveform and data, produced by the signal analyzer at the time of receipt. Physically fragile in cold; the ink becomes brittle. The only human-readable copy of the data aside from the tape.

**Protocol** — Elena's verification procedure for a candidate signal: a multi-step analytical sequence that rules out terrestrial sources, natural phenomena, and instrument artifacts in sequence. Running the protocol requires the signal analyzer and costs power.

**Receiver hut** — The outbuilding housing the antenna feeds, magnetic tape receivers, signal-processing rack, and signal analyzer. Separate from the main hab by a covered walkway. The site of the signal detection and the center of the story's action.

**Relay** — The scheduled satellite uplink window, occurring approximately once per fortnight. Capable of transmitting a compressed data packet and fax scan. The Day 6–7 occurrence is the only one before the LC-130 arrives.

**SETI (Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence)** — The scientific program seeking evidence of technological civilizations beyond Earth, primarily via radio telescope monitoring. Institutionally marginal in 1991; NASA's High Resolution Microwave Survey has not yet launched.

**Signal analyzer** — The proprietary instrument in the receiver hut used to process, filter, and verify candidate signals. Its verification protocol is not replicable on any backup hardware at the station.

**Single-sideband radio (SSB)** — A form of amplitude modulation used for long-range voice communications. The standard medium for Antarctic station-to-station and station-to-McMurdo traffic. Protocol requires call-sign identification and turn-based speaking.

**USAP (United States Antarctic Program)** — The national program managing U.S. scientific presence in Antarctica, administered by the National Science Foundation. Operates Winslow Station, McMurdo, South Pole, and Palmer stations, plus remote field camps.

**VXE-6** — U.S. Navy Antarctic Development Squadron Six, the unit that operates LC-130 Hercules aircraft in support of USAP logistics. Colloquially: the people who fly the plane.

**Winterover** — A researcher or technician who remains at a station through the Antarctic winter (approximately March–October), when flights are suspended or severely restricted. Elena has wintered-over at Winslow before; this is not her first isolation.

**Winslow Station** — The small, isolated USAP outpost where the story takes place. Weddell Sea quadrant. Peak summer crew: six. Current winterover occupant: Elena Hartmann (sole occupant during the story's events). Three conjoined structures: main hab, equipment building, and receiver hut.

**Protocol Run** — A full execution of Elena's verification protocol against the signal tape, using the signal analyzer. Each run takes approximately 20 minutes and costs power reserve. Elena estimated 8 total available at the start of the story; after Run 1 this became 7 (provisional, subject to revision in Ch05). Protocol runs are numbered sequentially (Run 1 through Run 6 in the story's arc).

**Instrument run margin** — The estimated number of full protocol runs remaining before the generator's power reserve falls below safe threshold. Decreases by at least one per run; subject to downward revision as fuel arithmetic is refined. Introduced in Ch01 at 7 remaining (revised from initial estimate of 8 after Run 1 brownout).

**Upper UHF monitoring band** — The non-standard-allocated frequency band in which Elena's protocol is designed to monitor, chosen specifically because standard-allocation frequencies are where interference concentrates. The 47-second signal was detected here.

**Calibration segment** — The pre-signal warm-up portion of a protocol run that establishes baseline parameters. A brownout or degradation event in the calibration segment does not necessarily invalidate the run if the signal's core parametric data is unaffected.

**Green-enameled mug** — A green-enameled steel mug in the Winslow Station main hab galley, of unknown prior ownership. Used by Elena as the galley mug during the story. Goes lukewarm faster than it should due to the cracked corridor hatch seal.

**Tension mechanism (tape transport)** — The mechanism on the reel-to-reel tape transport that maintains tension on the tape as it feeds through the capture head. Elena noted slight resistance four days before Day 1 and again during Run 1 setup. A potential tape-degradation precursor.

**Winslow Station** — Small USAP outpost on the edge of the Weddell Sea quadrant, Antarctica. Three joined structures: main hab module, equipment building, and receiver hut. Elena's location for the entire story. Houses a twelve-kilowatt diesel generator with a faulty voltage regulator.

**Receiver Hut** — Separate outbuilding at Winslow Station connected to the main hab by a covered 31-pace walkway. Contains the antenna feeds, reel-to-reel tape receivers, signal-processing rack (signal analyzer), and a single rattling space heater. Smells of diesel exhaust from the umbilical conduit.

**The Signal** — A forty-seven-second anomalous radio event received at 22:21:52 on night three of polar darkness (Day 1), in the upper UHF monitoring channel. Non-terrestrial, non-natural, structurally non-random. Will not repeat. Sole record: the magnetic tape and the printout.

**Elena's Verification Protocol** — Signal-verification procedure written by Elena Hartmann in 1983, revised 1986, 1989, 1990, and March 1991. Designed to reject all but one-in-a-billion false positives. Each full run costs generator power; Elena estimates 7 runs remaining after Run 1.

**Protocol Run 1** — First execution of Elena's verification protocol against the signal tape. Run 22:49–23:11 on Day 1. Generator brownout at minute 11 (calibration segment only); core data unaffected. Judged valid. Power cost: ~14% of baseline reserve.

**Signal Tape** — Reel-to-reel magnetic tape containing the sole recording of the signal. Stored in receiver hut housing with a small heating element. No backup exists. Housing temperature dipped 2 degrees below spec during the Day 1 brownout — one degradation event logged.

**Winslow Generator** — Twelve-kilowatt diesel generator at Winslow Station. Voltage regulator is faulty, causing intermittent brownouts. The limiting resource: governs power reserve, instrument runs, tape stability, and heating. At 78% of baseline after Protocol Run 1.

**Relay Window** — The scheduled mid-winter uplink slot — approximately Day 7, approximately 48 minutes in duration — by which Winslow can transmit a compressed data packet and fax scan to McMurdo via satellite relay. The only data-transmission opportunity before the LC-130 arrives on Day 12.

**Dr. Elena Hartmann** — Protagonist. Sixty-four years old, astrobiologist, University of Wisconsin–Madison / USAP. Winterover science lead at Winslow Station, June 1991. Thirty years of career defined by refusing to announce prematurely. Institutional shorthand: 'if Hartmann calls it, it’s real.'

**Dr. Piotr Walczak** — Winslow Station instrument engineer, Polish-American, late forties. Flew out to McMurdo with the team on Day 1 morning. Elena's closest colleague; the only person at Winslow who could read the instrument logs and understand them. Elena does not call him after the signal.

**Lars Engström** — Winterover researcher at Mather Station, Norwegian, mid-forties. Ice-core atmospheric chemist. Elena's SSB radio contact three times a week. Careful and methodical. Will later reveal (Ch10) that he recorded the same signal fourteen minutes earlier than Winslow.

**Mather Station** — Norwegian-affiliated Antarctic outpost 60 km east of Winslow Station. Staffed by two winterover researchers including Lars Engström. Not accessible overland in winter. Elena contacts Mather via SSB net three times a week.

**SSB Radio** — HF single-sideband radio — Winslow Station's primary voice-communication link. Protocol-constrained: call signs, turn-taking, formal signing off. Elena signed off at 2101 on Day 1 and did not call Walczak after the signal. Red standby light is on in the equipment alcove off the galley.

**LC-130** — U.S. Navy VXE-6 Hercules cargo/passenger aircraft; the resupply and rotation transport. Scheduled to arrive at Winslow on Day 12 with Elena's returning team. Its landing is the final foreclosure: after it, the data, framing, and credit are no longer Elena's to decide.

**Protocol Run 2** — Second execution of Elena's signal verification protocol, Day 2 morning, 09:47–10:04. No brownout. Signal confirmed intact. Power cost: 7 percentage points (clean-run baseline). Instrument margin after: 6 runs.

**Binder Stress (tape)** — First observed Day 2 morning on the signal tape's outermost reel layer (monitoring-record section, pre-signal): faint surface roughening at the tape edge, ~1 inch extent, 2–3mm width, visible under 10x magnification. Does not affect playback at current state. Progression rate unknown.

**Gray Thermal Blanket** — Standard-issue Antarctic emergency thermal blanket, flat-folded on equipment building supply shelf (second shelf). Retrieved Day 2 and used to protect the signal printouts, stored in main hab equipment cabinet.

**Hab Equipment Cabinet** — Equipment cabinet on the interior wall of the main hab at Winslow Station; draws from the hab's base heating circuit; the warmest consistently temperate enclosed space in the station. Used from Day 2 onward to store the signal printouts.

**Heating Protocol (70/100)** — Elena's Day 2 operational decision: space heater at 70% capacity running continuously; full analyzer draw only during protocol runs. Combined load within generator fault threshold. Permanent operating condition from Day 2 forward.

**Ten-power loupe** — Magnifying loupe (10x) in Elena's instrument repair kit; handle worn smooth on one side from thirty years of use. Purchased Madison 1961. Used for tape binder surface inspection.

**Lars Engström's Anomalous Reading** — A short-duration UHF transient detected by Lars Engström's secondary background logger at Mather Station, approximately one week before Day 3 of the story. Duration approximately one minute (slightly less), UHF secondary band, detected via shift-review software flag. Lars attributed it to RFI from Mather's own maintenance cycle and filed it as instrument artifact. As of Day 3, Elena believes this may be independent corroboration of the Day 1 signal but cannot confirm without disclosing what she has. Precise parameters (date, time, frequency, event duration) not yet known to Elena. See Thread 2: The Mather Corroboration.

**Elena's Fieldwork Notebook** — Elena Hartmann's personal spiral-bound notebook, separate from the official USAP station log. Carried through thirty years of field work; handwriting evolves across its pages. Used for non-administrative personal field notes. Elena writes in it during the Day 3 receiver hut visit; she also uses it to write the evidence summary in Chapter 12. The notebook ultimately goes to Walczak in Chapter 15.

**Shift-review software** — Automated logging software used by Lars Engström at Mather Station that flags events above a preset threshold in background channels for manual review at end of shift. The Mather transient (Lars's anomalous reading) was detected via this mechanism, not by a primary instrument alarm.

**Secondary background logger** — Lars Engström's passive reference-channel instrument at Mather Station, running continuously to log UHF frequency data as a reference rather than a primary science program. Not actively monitored during operations; reviewed via shift-review software flags.

**Protocol Run 3** — Third playback of the signal tape through Elena's verification protocol, conducted on Day 4 during the systematic inventory. First run to reveal a dropout event: five seconds of blank oxide at the start of the first parametric segment. Validity judgment: conditional. Power reserve drops to 60% after this run. Instrument margin: 5 runs remaining.

**tape dropout** — An absence of recorded signal on playback: blank oxide reading, not static or noise, produced when the tape binder has failed and the oxide layer has detached. In the signal tape, the first dropout (Run 3, Day 4) is five seconds at the beginning of the first parametric segment — sharp onset, sharp recovery. Distinct from the binder stress zone, which is a visual indicator; the dropout is a playback indicator.

**first parametric segment** — The initial frequency-sweep section of the signal data on the tape, immediately following the transition marker (two-second tonal calibration reference) at the monitoring-record boundary. The segment provides the first set of identifying parameters for the signal. Its opening five seconds are lost to dropout as of Run 3 (Day 4). The core signature is located approximately 40 seconds into this segment.

**tape housing inspection card** — A strip of card stock from Elena's instrument repair kit, clipped to the back of the signal tape's housing, used as a dimensional log for binder stress measurements across Days 2 and 4. Transferred to the back cover of Elena's fieldwork notebook at end of Day 4.

**tape map** — A sketch maintained in Elena's fieldwork notebook from Day 2, showing the layout of the signal reel: monitoring-record section, signal segment start, parametric segment positions, and dimensional annotations (binder stress boundary, dropout position). Used to cross-reference physical inspection measurements against playback events.

**minimum-maximum thermometer** — A field thermometer mounted by Elena on the lower shelf of the tape housing rack on Day 2 to monitor overnight temperature lows at housing level. By Day 4 it has recorded a low of minus fourteen point two Celsius — fractionally below the minus-fourteen protocol threshold. The instrument has a measurement error of approximately plus or minus two tenths Celsius at these temperatures.

**Corrections pen (red mechanical pencil)** — Fine-point red mechanical pencil kept by Elena in the back-cover pocket of her fieldwork notebook. Used exclusively for corrections and revisions to prior notebook entries, distinguishable from her regular blue ballpoint at a glance. First used in Ch05 to overwrite the Day 4 fuel-based run estimate (8 total runs → 6 total runs → 3 remaining).

**Voltage regulator replacement component** — Compatible replacement part for the Winslow Station generator's faulty voltage regulator, found by Walczak in McMurdo Station maintenance stores. Part number confirmed against the Winslow instrument manual. Walczak will bring it to Winslow when the team returns on Day 12; it cannot be installed before then.

**Fault interval (voltage regulator)** — The measured time between consecutive voltage regulator sub-alarm dip events. Elena begins logging fault intervals on Day 5 at Walczak's implicit instruction. First datum: 63 minutes between the 0307 and 0410 overnight events (Day 4-5). A single data point; trend direction unknown.

**Data packet (USAP data transmission protocol)** — Elena's formatted three-section transmission document built at the fax terminal on Day 6: header metadata (frequency band, timestamp, duration), primary data record (amplitude profile, parametric segment summaries, verification protocol notes for Runs 1–3), and a cover statement. Addressed to the USAP relay buffer. Not transmitted by end of Day 6.

**Fax terminal** — The facsimile/data terminal in the equipment building alcove beside the HF radio console at Winslow Station. Used for transmitting data packets via the relay satellite window when the carrier signal indicator shows green. Interface: text input field, packet-formatting utility, relay carrier signal indicator.

**Timing marks (tape housing)** — Pencil lines Elena drew on the interior of the tape housing on Day 2 to bracket key reel segment positions (monitoring record start, monitoring record end, signal start, parametric divisions) for visual inspection without running the instrument. Used in Day 4 and Day 6 physical inspections to estimate dropout extent by comparing binder stress position against segment boundaries.

**USAP relay buffer** — The satellite relay data buffer that Elena's formatted data packet is addressed to. When the relay carrier window opens and the initiation command is entered, the packet would route to McMurdo Operations and then to the agency's data management unit in Washington.

**USAP relay buffer** — The data processing queue at McMurdo Operations that receives instrument returns and data packets transmitted via the scheduled relay satellite window. Non-routine returns are flagged per USAP protocol 7.3.1 and routed up the program officer chain. Elena knows the names of the people who process this queue from her attendance at Antarctic program reviews in 1987 and 1989.

**F5 (initiation command)** — The F5 function key on the fax terminal at Winslow Station's equipment building — the single keystroke required to execute the packet queue and transmit the data packet through the relay uplink. The key cap is worn through at the center from years of use. Elena did not press it during the Day 7 relay window.

**cold-weather kit** — Elena's full exterior kit at Winslow Station: insulated overtrousers, parka, balaclava, goggles, gauntlets. Stored on rack beside the equipment building exterior door. Fixed 7-minute dressing sequence. Used in Ch08 when Elena goes outside to check the generator exhaust pipe — the only exterior scene in the novel.

**generator exhaust pipe** — South-wall exhaust terminus of the Winslow Station generator. Requires post-fault visual check for frost accumulation or ice blockage. Confirmed clear in Ch08 Day 7 evening check.

**voltage regulator fault light** — Amber indicator on the Winslow generator output panel. Amber = monitored deviation (not emergency). Red = instrument emergency. Active (amber) during the Day 7 afternoon fault; extinguished after stabilization procedure.

**Protocol Run 4** — Fourth full verification protocol run, conducted on Day 8. Confirmed 8-second opening dropout in first parametric segment (consistent with prior estimates) and a 12-second blank oxide dropout at the second parametric segment position — a core dropout, not an edge dropout. Permanently forecloses full parametric verification. Instrument margin reduced to 2 remaining; power reserve to 30%.

**432.275 MHz** — The specific frequency of Lars Engström's secondary background logger channel at Mather Station on which the 47-second anomalous reading was detected on approximately June 1, 2208 local. A UHF secondary passive reference channel, distinct from Elena's upper UHF primary monitoring channel. Canonical; must not vary in later chapters.

**Mather corroboration** — The independent detection of a 47-second anomalous event at Mather Station (Lars Engström's passive background logger, 432.275 MHz) approximately 14 minutes before Elena's Winslow detection on June 1. Geometry is consistent with a common source. Rendered inaccessible as evidence after Elena told Lars on Day 9 it was 'probably RFI.' Documented in Elena's analysis notebook and later in the Ch12 evidence summary.

**stabilization procedure** — A 15-minute reduced-draw generator warm-up procedure required before each protocol run from Day 8 forward, to compensate for voltage variance widened by the Day 7 fault. Costs approximately 3-4% power reserve above the run itself, bringing per-run total to approximately 11-12% at +/-8% variance.

**Station Camera** — 35mm SLR camera in the field documentation kit, second shelf of the equipment building at Winslow Station. 50mm lens, manual controls. Used for site documentation (antenna alignment, equipment configuration) and introduced as an evidence-preservation tool on Day 10 when Elena photographs the damaged printout. Cold condition by Day 10: aperture ring stiffened, winding lever has increased resistance. See also: Roll F.

**Roll F** — 35mm film roll in the station camera as of Day 10. Before Elena's photography session: 18 exposures remaining. Session uses 12 frames (full-width printout documentation plus close-up frames of degraded columns). 6 exposures remaining after Day 10. Cannot be developed at Winslow (no darkroom); will go to McMurdo for development when the team arrives Day 12.

**Examination Card** — Laminated reference card Elena made when designing the verification protocol (1989 revision); lists all 14 printout columns by number, label, units, and expected value range. Used for the systematic column-by-column examination of the printout. Filed in the protocol folder side pocket.

**Two-Pass Method** — Elena's standard technique for examining a printout column: first pass through all 23 data rows checking legibility and completeness; second pass on the 8 flagged rows carrying peak signal values. Used in Chapter 11's printout examination.

**Protocol Run 5** — Fifth execution of Elena's verification protocol against the signal tape; Day 11 morning; confirms 20-second transition gap dropout (up from 12s on Run 4); reel-edge opening interval approximately 9 seconds; core signature (first parametric segment) intact and confirmed across all five runs; signal duration 47 seconds confirmed; power reserve reduced to approximately 13%; instrument run margin reduced to 1 remaining.

**Evidence Summary (Day 11)** — Complete evidentiary summary written by Elena Hartmann in her analysis notebook on Day 11, in paper-register language. Covers what physical items exist (tape, printout, notebook, photographs, run logs); what instrument runs established (47-second duration, primary anomalous parameters, non-terrestrial/non-natural character within protocol scope); what was not established (complete parametric profile; second segment unrecoverable); corroborating data at Mather Station (unverified, inaccessible without disclosure); what can be claimed (provisional statement); what cannot be claimed (peer-reviewed complete parametric characterization). The canonical text is registered as record 'evidence-summary-day11'.

**Signal Tape** — The reel-to-reel magnetic tape holding the sole recording of the forty-seven-second signal received at Winslow Station on June 1, 1991. Degrading in cold throughout the investigation. As of Day 11 (Run 5): reel-edge opening interval approximately 9 seconds; transition gap dropout 20 seconds; core signature (first parametric segment) intact; second parametric segment unrecoverable since Run 4.

**LC-130 Hercules** — U.S. Navy VXE-6 turboprop aircraft used for Antarctic resupply. The LC-130 carrying Elena's returning team of six departed McMurdo at 0700 Day 12, with estimated arrival at Winslow Station approximately 1300. Carries the new voltage regulator component plus standard cargo.

**face-down page** — A sheet of plain blank stock paper Elena wrote approximately six lines on (in a register between instrument log and evidence summary) and set face-down on the galley table corner on Day 11 evening, stopping without completing a sentence. Its content is never shown to the reader; its fate (given to Walczak, discarded, destroyed) is implied in Chapter 15.

**auxiliary space heater** — Small emergency heater unit kept in the supply crate for instrument-rack warming when the main heater requires servicing. Positioned below the main hab equipment cabinet on Day 11 night to maintain a warmer ambient for the tape housing.

**Protocol Run 6** — The sixth and final verification run of the signal protocol, conducted at 0640 on Day 12 (the morning of the LC-130's arrival). Confirms core signature (first parametric segment) intact at a 23-second dropout. Second parametric segment absent as of Run 4. Instrument margin after this run: 0. Power reserve after this run: approximately 3%. This is the final instrument measurement of the investigation.

**Face-Down Page** — A sheet of blank station stock written by Elena on Day 11 evening — six lines in a register between the instrument log and the evidence summary, stopping mid-sentence. Set face-down on the galley table overnight. On Day 12 morning Elena read it, folded it once, and inserted it into the analysis notebook. Its content is not disclosed to the reader. That she kept rather than destroyed it is implied by the action.

**Winslow Evidence Package** — The assembled physical record of Elena Hartmann's twelve-day investigation: (1) the tape housing (magnetic reel-to-reel tape; 23-second dropout; core signature intact); (2) the wrapped printout roll (partially ink-degraded, three data points unreadable in calibration column); (3) the protocol folder (Runs 1–6 complete, one run per slot); (4) the analysis notebook (detection entry, run logs, housing inspection cards, Run 4 honest accounting, Mather corroboration entry, evidence summary, and the folded face-down page between last entry and back cover). Transferred to Piotr Walczak in the equipment building on Day 12, approximately 1315.

**LC-130 arrival (Day 12)** — The Hercules LC-130 ski-equipped aircraft operated by U.S. Navy VXE-6 landed at Winslow Station on Day 12 at approximately 1300. Standard landing procedure: one low pass to check the strip, then second approach with ski struts extended. Brought Walczak and four other team members. Walczak's arrival ended Elena's twelve-day solo occupation of the station and began the transfer of the signal evidence.
