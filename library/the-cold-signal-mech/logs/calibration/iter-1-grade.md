# Calibration grade — iteration 1

## Compared sample against brief anchors

The brief specifies: **literary adult fiction**, third-person limited past, Elena as sole POV, patient/exacting scientific register, with Kazuo Ishiguro's *The Remains of the Day* as the closest cadence exemplar — specifically, a character who withholds from herself methodologically rather than psychologically, who applies her verification reflex to her own emotional states. The drafter guidance translates this into: procedural openings, 18–24 word average sentence length, 40-word hard maximum, short declarative clusters at high-stakes moments, and a strict list of anti-defaults.

---

## What the sample does well

- **Register is fundamentally correct.** This reads as literary adult fiction in a precise scientific idiom. The opening paragraph uses the "exactly as it always had" fold explicitly endorsed by the lean-into section: "exactly as it had begun every night for the past six weeks" — and then implies the normality is about to rupture. That structural move earns its place.
- **Methodological self-withholding is fully rendered.** "She wrote it in the log with the date and said nothing about it, even to herself." This is the Ishiguro-without-repression move the guidance asks for — she notes the anomaly (power drop), logs it, and the prose withholds the emotional weight without labeling the withholding.
- **Technical detail enters as action, not lecture.** The voltage regulator fault, input attenuation confirmation, the strip-paper reminder above the rack — these appear because Elena is doing the protocol, not because the narration wants to explain a system. The self-taped paper note (*Confirm input attenuation before initiating. Confirm again.*) is excellent: it externalizes her rigor as object.
- **Short declarative clusters appear exactly where they should.** "Not an alarm. Not a spike." / "Elena did not move. She watched the display and counted." — the pressure-bearing short-sentence mode lands at the right moment, then the chapter closes before the prose over-explains.
- **Anti-defaults are clean.** Signal content is not described. Emotional state is not named. No foreshadowing of the decision. No prior-chapter summary (chapter 1, so not applicable, but the sample treats itself as mid-stream correctly). No post-scene subtext naming after the signal appears.

---

## What the sample drifts on

- **Two sentences exceed the 40-word maximum significantly.** The opening sentence runs ~43 words (borderline). More importantly, the second paragraph contains a single sentence of approximately 63 words: *"Now the space heater had knocked the ambient temperature up to something workable — her hands were no longer stiff — and the hut held the smell it always held: diesel exhaust bleeding through the umbilical conduit from the generator shed, and beneath that the dry-cold smell of electronics running in sub-zero air that she had stopped noticing sometime in the second week."* This is more than 50% over the stated 40-word maximum. It is doing genuine descriptive work — it is not reaching for ornament — but it is long enough that if this pattern persists over 40,000 words, the prose will feel heavier than the guidance intends.
- **The climactic signal sentence also slightly overshoots:** *"…the kind of clean frequency line that does not occur in background noise because background noise does not have edges"* — this is ~45 words, again procedural and well-earned, but above limit.

Neither of these drifts breaks the register. The sentences are complex procedural description (which the guidance allows at the ceiling), and the content justifies the length. But the drafter should be aware that 60-word sentences in the sensory setup and 45-word sentences in the signal description are already at the high end of what the register permits.
