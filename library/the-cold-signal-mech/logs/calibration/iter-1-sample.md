# Calibration Sample — Iter 1
## Chapter 1 opening, ~400 words

---

The scan began at twenty-two hundred, exactly as it had begun every night for the past six weeks, and Elena watched the signal analyzer's amber numerals settle into their baseline pattern with the incurious attention of someone checking a familiar face for change.

The receiver hut was nine by four meters, poorly insulated, and when she had pushed through from the covered walkway twenty minutes ago her breath had come out in a fine cloud above the strip fluorescents. Now the space heater had knocked the ambient temperature up to something workable — her hands were no longer stiff — and the hut held the smell it always held: diesel exhaust bleeding through the umbilical conduit from the generator shed, and beneath that the dry-cold smell of electronics running in sub-zero air that she had stopped noticing sometime in the second week.

She logged the power reserve at the start of the run. Sixty-three percent. The voltage regulator fault — Piotr had flagged it before the team flew out — meant that baseline draws were running slightly higher than calculated, and the baseline read at the start of last night's scan had been sixty-eight. A five-point drop across twenty-four hours was consistent with the fault's documented behavior, not yet a crisis, but not the number she had estimated before the LC-130 left. She wrote it in the log with the date and said nothing about it, even to herself.

The antenna feed processor was running cleanly. The reel-to-reel advanced at its standard speed, the tape a fresh spool — she had loaded it yesterday and had less than forty minutes of run on it. She had taped a strip of paper to the equipment rack above the analyzer three weeks ago and written on it, in her own hand: *Confirm input attenuation before initiating. Confirm again.* She confirmed it.

At twenty-two seventeen the analyzer's display changed.

Not an alarm. Not a spike. The amber numerals held, and then a secondary parameter moved out of its expected range — a narrow-band event at twenty-one hundred megahertz, the kind of clean frequency line that does not occur in background noise because background noise does not have edges.

Elena did not move. She watched the display and counted.
