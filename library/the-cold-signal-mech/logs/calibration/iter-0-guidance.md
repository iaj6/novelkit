# Drafter Guidance — The Cold Signal

## Voice Register

This is literary adult fiction in a precise, scientific register — the default pipeline voice is largely correct, but it needs to be anchored to Elena's professional idiom. The closest exemplar register is late Kazuo Ishiguro (*The Remains of the Day*): close third-person limited with a narrator who partially withholds from herself; a character who speaks in professional/formal language even in interior thought; a slow-burn revelation of what the protagonist cannot quite admit. The difference is that Elena is not repressed in Ishiguro's sense — she is rigorous. Her self-withholding is methodological, not psychological. She applies her verification reflex to her own states: she notes a symptom before she names the feeling, the same way she notes an anomaly before she names a detection.

## Sentence-Length Expectations

- **Average sentence length:** 18–24 words in reflective/procedural passages; 10–14 words when the prose bears down during high-stakes moments.
- **Maximum acceptable sentence length:** 40 words (and only in complex procedural description). Longer than 40 words is a warning sign that the narration is reaching for eloquence rather than enacting thought.
- **Short-sentence clusters:** 3–4 short declaratives in a row are the register's signal of pressure. Do not sustain them longer than that; they should break back into the measured middle-length rhythm.

## Chapter-Opening Conventions

Do NOT open a chapter on abstract reflection or summary of the prior day's state. Begin in motion: Elena is doing something (running the protocol, making coffee, checking the generator gauge) or perceiving something specific (the sound of the pipes, the temperature differential between the hut and the walkway). The reading-order experience should be: *we are back with Elena mid-action on a new day.* The named quantity that worsened since last chapter should surface organically within the first 300 words — not as a summary statement ("The power reserve was now at X") but as the consequence of what Elena is doing in the opening.

## Chapter-Ending Conventions

This is literary fiction; endings should not be uniformly cliffhanger. But the book's structural contract (the window closing, the depleting resource) means the ending of each chapter should leave the reader with the felt sense that the situation is now irreversibly worse than when the chapter opened.

Target distribution across 15 chapters:
- **Resolved beat (within chapter):** 2–3 chapters (moments when Elena completes something — a verification run, a decision about whether to tell Walczak — but the larger pressure is unresolved)
- **Literary fade:** 3–4 chapters (sensory/atmospheric close, but always with the weight of the narrowed window in the final image)
- **Cliffhanger or mid-action:** 3–4 chapters (generator fault, bad tape read, the relay window imminent)
- **Turn (new information or recontextualization):** 2–3 chapters (the revelation of what the gate will cost; the moment Walczak calls)
- **Elliptical:** 2 chapters (the chapter where Elena decides, and the final chapter — both should resist clean closure)

Do NOT cluster the same ending mode in consecutive chapters. Vary deliberately.

## POV Strategy

Single POV throughout: Elena Hartmann, every chapter. No exceptions. The story's epistemological premise (she is the only person who knows, and we are inside that aloneness) requires that the reader never escapes her subjectivity. When radio contacts occur, we hear the other voice only as Elena hears it — through the tinny SSB speaker, often clipped, sometimes garbled.

## Dialogue Density

Low. Elena is alone; most chapters have no dialogue at all, or only a single radio contact. When dialogue occurs, it is SSB-protocol constrained: call signs, turn-taking, formal signing off. Do not use dialogue to advance exposition the radio would not actually carry (SSB audio quality is poor; long technical explanations are implausible). Dialogue tags are "she said / he said" — no glossing tags ("she said, as if explaining"). The constraint is part of the story's texture: Elena cannot ask the real question over the radio.

## Technical Detail

Enter technical content only when Elena is doing it. If the protocol has a step, Elena does the step; the step's name and what it rules out emerges from the action. Never: "The signal analyzer, which worked by [explanation]." Always: "She initialized the filter sweep. If the source was terrestrial — a satellite, a radar bounce, the base's own HF transmitter — it would show here."

## Comic Timing and Tonal Balance

No comedy in the conventional sense. The register's version of tonal relief is: dry understatement, procedural irony (the gap between the magnitude of what Elena has and the smallness of what she does about it — makes another cup of coffee, checks the generator, files a routine log entry). Do not reach for warmth or humor that the situation does not organically produce. Sardonic observations about institutional culture are permitted, briefly, as character.

## Anti-Defaults — What the Drafter Must Actively Avoid

- **Do not open any chapter on extended interiority.** Elena reflects while doing, not before doing.
- **Do not name Elena's emotional state directly.** She does not tell herself she is frightened; she runs the protocol again. The reader infers from action, not from the narration's label.
- **Do not write the signal's content.** If a passage drifts toward describing what the signal encodes or what it might mean in terms of message, cut it immediately. The constraint is absolute.
- **Do not hint at what Elena will decide.** No "she would later understand that this was the moment," no structural foreshadowing of the choice's resolution. The decision must be live.
- **Do not resolve the ethics.** The narration does not editorialize. One option is not framed as obviously correct. If any passage reads as advocacy for a particular choice, that is a drift from brief.
- **Do not let any chapter "mark time."** Every chapter must name, organically, the quantity that has worsened since the last. A chapter that does not tighten the window is a structural failure, not a quiet beat.
- **Do not summarize the prior chapter at the open.** Begin today. The reader remembers yesterday.
