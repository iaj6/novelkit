The gale had blown three days and the harbor showed it. Trap line was tangled in the first channel marker — blue and orange braid she would need to clear before the boats came out. A skiff lay against the near mooring piling, half-submerged, its gunwale stove in where it had beaten through the night. She noted these in the order she saw them. The channel was closed until she said otherwise.

Dawn was not quite up. The sky above the eastern headland had gone pale grey without color. Cold — the damp kind, water still in the air from the retreating storm. The harbor smelled of low tide and engine oil and rope. She had Owen's coat on, the peacoat he'd worn until the lining gave, and she'd had the lining replaced but kept the coat. It was too big through the shoulders. It did the work.

She walked the breakwater from the shack end toward the channel mouth. Standard procedure after a blow: the full length, seaward face, note any shift in the footing and any debris the gale had driven in. After three days of northeast wind the seaward face collected everything the sea had been carrying — trap buoys, planking, line, now and then a barrel or a crate. You looked at what the gale brought in.

The granite was wet and had been covered on the high tide. She set each foot before lifting the other. On wet granite you did not hurry. Eight winters she had done this walk. She did it the same way each time. The light came up slowly to the east, grey and without warmth.

At roughly two-thirds of the way out she saw the shape against the seaward face.

She knew what it was before she was close enough to confirm it. She continued walking at the same pace. The last thirty feet she picked her footing around a coil of warp and the splintered frame of a lobster trap. Then she crouched.

Male. Fifty, perhaps older. Wool trousers, a heavy coat, leather shoes — a man dressed for dry land, not this coast. No oilskin. No rubber boots. His face was partly turned to the stone and the skin had gone the color of the stone. She did not touch it. She did not recognize him.

She looked at the leather shoes. Good leather, still intact. Not a fisherman's shoes.
