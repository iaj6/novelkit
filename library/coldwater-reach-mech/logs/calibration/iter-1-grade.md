## What the sample does well

- **Voice cadence is unmistakably Eira.** The procedural register is alive from the first sentence. "The channel was closed until she said otherwise." — this is the harbormaster speaking in her authority register, exactly as the brief asks.
- **No emotional glossing, anywhere.** Owen's coat is introduced in three sentences and dropped: "It did the work." The prose does not reach for meaning. The coat carries grief and stops there; no sentence after it names what the coat is "about."
- **The body inventory is clinical without being cold.** "Male. Fifty, perhaps older. Wool trousers, a heavy coat, leather shoes — a man dressed for dry land, not this coast." Each sentence is reportable fact. The emotional weight is entirely in what is not said.
- **The chapter-opening honors the convention.** Physical situation first (the harbor after a gale, itemized), then the character's body moving through it. No abstraction, no memory as the entry point.
- **Short declaratives cluster at freighted moments.** "She knew what it was before she was close enough to confirm it. She continued walking at the same pace." The prose withholds exactly as instructed — the character knows; the reader is not told; she walks toward it anyway.

## What the sample drifts on

- **"She noted these in the order she saw them."** This sentence is borderline meta-cognitive — it labels the mental act (noting) rather than showing the noting directly. The guidance names "meta-commentary on cognition" as a failure mode ("She registered this." "He noted."). In isolation this is minor and likely defensible given the procedural register; noting *is* Eira's job and the sentence has more authority-claim than hedge. But it is the closest the sample comes to the listed failure mode. If the drafter repeats this construction in subsequent chapters, it becomes a tic.
- **Harbor log (Eira's primary displacement object) is absent.** The guidance specifies: "Eira's primary object: the harbor log — specifically a given entry or blank space in it." Owen's coat appears as a secondary displacement object; the shoes gesture toward the mystery object. The log is the chapter's end-destination (when she returns to the shack, the reporting decision will land) and would not appear here if this is the opening scene of the chapter. This absence is contextually appropriate for a discovery beat, not a structural failure in a full chapter. Noting for the drafter: the log must appear before the chapter ends.
