# Story Arc

One tight line per drafted chapter, in chronological order.

- **01-the-breakwater** — Eira finds an unidentified man's body on the breakwater, logs the finding precisely, and leaves the October 2, 2300 entry blank — the novel's first act of will.
- **02-the-fragment** — A naval officer brings Marcus a water-damaged paper fragment inscribed "Lutz, J."; Marcus recognizes the name, translates nothing further, and puts the fragment in his desk drawer unreturned.
- **03-allgau** — Hannah hears Karl speak Allgäu German in the barn, answers without thinking, and receives a folded note she reads alone that night — it ends with a sentence about a man on a rain-soaked road she does not yet understand.
- **04-the-commander** — Commander Willard reviews the log, closes the case as Unknown Man — the blank at October 2, 2300 survives official scrutiny and acquires legal weight.
- **05-the-name** — Marcus works through the fragment with his magnifying glass, confirms the name as Josef Lutz — a man he knew at Sherbrooke — drafts a letter to his Montreal contact, burns it, writes a second, and does not send it.
- **06-the-lining** — Hannah watches Werner watch Karl on the crew's last full workday, then moves Karl's note from her coat pocket into the sewn lining of her old school bag — passive keeping becomes active concealment.
- **07-owens-pages** — Eira, alone on a January ice watch, reads Owen's meticulous 1933 log, attempts to fill the October blank, crosses it out correctly — and understands the blank is now permanent, and visible.
- **08-montreal** — Ehrlich's Montreal reply confirms Josef Lutz's departure date and disappearance; Marcus places the letter in the drawer with the fragment and grades Latin exercises in silence.
- **09-the-road** — Karl tells Hannah he saw Josef alive and walking toward the harbor on the coastal road — Hannah writes his account on the back of his note, making the two-sided document the midpoint's recontextualization.
- **10-no-new-information** — Eira fills out the Rockland follow-up form, writes "None," mails it, walks to the breakwater's end — the reader receives the clearest inventory yet of what she saw on the dock.
- **11-the-crow-table** — At the Crow dinner table, Marcus discloses that he thinks he knew the dead man; Henry nods once and says "a man gets far from home and farther still, and then he runs out of road" — the burden shifts from one keeper to two.
- **12-not-yet** — Werner searches Hannah's room for tobacco and misses the note by inches; that evening Hannah holds it to the lamp flame, watches the margin yellow, and pulls it back — keeping it, now, by explicit choice.
- **13-the-man-at-atwoods** — Eira recognizes Werner Pfeiffer's back and shoulders in the Atwood's queue and that evening writes a complete log entry that does not contain his name.
- **14-one-year** — On the one-year anniversary of the body's discovery, Marcus walks to the Prospect Street mailbox and sends a letter to Josef Lutz's sister in Zürich — minimal, irreversible, the secret's first outward movement.
- **15-the-crew-moves** — Karl's POW crew is reassigned to a Steuben cannery; he and Hannah exchange only "Good luck to you" before he leaves without looking back, and Hannah reads the note alone one final time before returning it to the lining.
- **16-the-old-volume** — Eira files the old log volume after writing one line of weather into the October blank — the first true entry in fifteen months — and cannot write what follows.
- **17-what-henry-knows** — Henry tells Marcus that Werner Pfeiffer sheltered Josef Lutz one night before the gale; Marcus goes upstairs, writes the final entry in his private notebook, and assembles the complete picture of Josef's last journey — privately, imperfectly, sufficiently.
- **18-the-reach** — Before dawn on a morning in late April 1945, Eira writes the minimum dock entry into the old log volume and shelves it beside Owen's; the war ends on the constable's radio across the road; she walks the breakwater, checks the mooring lines, and closes the new volume.
