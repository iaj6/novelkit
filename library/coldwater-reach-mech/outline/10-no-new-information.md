# Chapter 10 — No New Information

**One-line summary**: Rockland's follow-up inquiry form arrives; Eira fills it out, checks "no new information," mails it — and in doing so inventories to herself, for the first time, exactly what she saw on the dock.

**POV**: Eira Bowman
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: Harbormaster's shack; the post office; the breakwater. An afternoon in April 1944.
**Chapter shape**: Multi-scene (the shack for the form; the post office to mail it; the breakwater end; back to the shack)
**Ending mode**: Literary-fade
**Target words**: ~2,300

---

## Displacement Object

**The routine follow-up inquiry form** — a boilerplate Rockland document asking for any new information on the October 1943 case. Eira fills it out. Where it asks for new witnesses or information, she checks "none." She seals it. She mails it. The form is now a second legal record of her omission, delivered voluntarily. She did not have to mail it today; she chose to.

**Fact introduced**: Eira makes a second legal omission — the inquiry form joins the log blank as an official document in which she has affirmed "no new information." More importantly: the act of filling out the form forces Eira to articulate to herself, for the first time, the full inventory of what she saw on October 2. The reader gets the clearest view yet. What she saw: a man alive on the dock at approximately 2300 hours; he was not alone; there was a second figure, someone she believed at the time was local. She did not speak to either person. She did not log it. By morning the man was dead.

**Named fact changed/recontextualized**: There are now two official documents in which Eira has said "nothing." The blank in the log was a passive omission; the inquiry form is an active one. The distinction is not lost on her. Post-ch9's midpoint revelation (Josef walked to the dock), the reader now holds Eira's "second figure" against that fact: Josef walked there alive, to meet — or because of — someone. That someone is the blank's content.

**Object rhyme**: Eira's mailed inquiry form ↔ Marcus's unsent-then-sent Montreal letter (ch8): both are minimal acts of communication that commit the writer to a position. Marcus's letter said almost nothing but established contact; Eira's form says nothing and establishes a record. Both are irreversible once sent.

---

## Goal

Complete the Rockland follow-up form on time (she has had it for a week; it is due) and return to work.

## Obstacle

The form is a single page. It takes ten minutes to fill out. The obstacle is not the form itself but what filling it out requires: she has to read each question and answer each one, which means she has to hold, in sequence, everything she knows and does not report. The form is a test she is passing by lying to it.

## Action Beats

1. **The form arrives.** The form came in the mail a week ago — a standard Rockland boilerplate, sent to all parties who filed reports on the October 1943 case (Eira; the county examiner's office; Constable Tibbetts). One page. Date. Location of original finding. Cause of death (as determined by county examiner: drowned). Any new witnesses, physical evidence, or information bearing on identity or cause of death. A signature line. Return by April 15.

2. **Eira fills it out at the desk.** April 13. She has two days. She fills in the date, the location, the cause of death — all of these are already on record; she copies from the log. Then: new witnesses. She writes: None. New physical evidence. None. New information bearing on identity or cause of death: None.

3. **The inventory.** She writes "None" and puts the pen down. She doesn't read it back. She reads it back. As she does, she moves through what she knows — the way she moves through a channel checklist. Man on the dock at approximately 2300 hours on October 2. Alive. Not in distress that she could see; moving, not slumped. He was not alone. A second figure — standing near him or with him, a person she took at the time for a local, someone of a certain height and posture. She did not speak to either. She went back to the shack. By dawn he was dead against the breakwater.

4. **The form is not changed.** She reads back what she has written. "None." She seals the envelope.

5. **The walk to the post office.** April afternoon — the harbor is waking up again after winter, a few boats already out on the grounds. She walks the breakwater to the access road, then up the main street to the post office. She passes Atwood's. She sees no one she needs to see. She drops the form in the mail slot at the post office counter and walks back.

6. **The breakwater.** On the way back, she walks to the breakwater's end — the place where the body came in. She doesn't do this often; she has been avoiding it since October. Today she walks to the end and stands and looks at the water. April water: grey-green, running hard with the spring tidal flush. The seaward face of the granite. The body was in the angle where storm-wreckage collects, held against the stone by the backwash. She stands and looks for a while. Then she turns.

7. **Back at the shack.** She writes the afternoon log entry: tide height, wind, one vessel movement. Weather improving. She is precise and complete. She closes the log.

---

## Outcome

Two official documents now record Eira's silence: the log's blank, and the inquiry form. The form is in the mail; it is irreversible. The investigation will stay officially closed.

## New Question or Pressure Raised

What does Eira feel, standing at the breakwater's end? The novel does not name it. But the reader — who now knows, from ch9, that Josef walked to that dock alive and with purpose — holds Eira's "second figure" as the remaining open question of the braid's first half.

---

## Continuity Notes

- The inquiry form: a routine Rockland follow-up. It asks for new information; it does not ask Eira to revisit the log or explain its contents. She is not re-interviewed; this is a paper process.
- Eira's inventory of what she saw: this is the clearest the novel gets about her knowledge until ch13 (when she identifies Werner). The drafter should render this inventory with Eira's spare precision — not a description of feelings but a list of facts she holds. "A man on the dock. Alive. Not alone. A second figure." It reads like a log entry she has never written.
- The breakwater scene: Eira goes to the exact location of the body's discovery. She does not do this often. The chapter does not tell the reader why she does it now. The literary-fade ending holds the moment without explaining it.
- The post office: a real exchange is not required. She drops the form in the slot. The postmistress may be present but the chapter does not need her.

---

## Craft Notes for the Drafter

- **Literary-fade ending**: The chapter closes on a procedural action (writing the afternoon's log entry) — not on the breakwater moment, which is earlier. The ending is the ordinary work resuming after the charged walk. The last image: the closed log on the desk, the shack, late afternoon light through the small window. Quiet return to routine.
- **The inventory is the chapter's center.** When Eira writes "None" and reads back what she's written, the prose should slow — not to name her feelings but to move through her inventory with her, factually. Short declarative sentences. The reader assembles the meaning. The prose does not.
- **Eira does not dramatize the walk to the post office or the breakwater.** She goes; she stands; she turns. Her interiority at the breakwater's end is brief — perhaps one or two observations about the water's condition. What she feels is in what she does not name.
- **The form is a legal document.** Its officialness matters. Eira has signed her name to "None." This is a formal act, not a private one.
- **Pacing**: shorter chapter, but not rushed. The walk to the post office and the breakwater visit should have some air — the April harbor, the returning boats, the cold and light. The season's change is part of the texture: the world is not standing still just because Eira is.
