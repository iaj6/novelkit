## Displacement Object Master Key

Three objects, each "a thing known and not sent," staggered across threads and rhymed:

| Thread | Primary Object | What It Withholds |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Eira** | The harbor log — the entry she makes, leaves blank, or writes over | What she saw on the dock the night of October 2, 1943 |
| **Hannah** | Karl's note — folded paper, received in November 1943, annotated, hidden in a school bag lining | A relationship with a POW that crosses legal bounds; Karl's testimony about the dead man |
| **Marcus** | The water-damaged document fragment + the Montreal reply letter | The dead man's identity, which Marcus recognizes from Quebec |

**Rhyme logic**: All three are records that should be reported, filed, or surrendered and are not. When one thread's object advances, the next chapter in the other thread advances its object too — the reader assembles one pattern, not three.

## The Body's Identity — Reader's Assembly Across the Braid

The dead man is **Josef Lutz**, a stateless German-Jewish refugee who was interned with Marcus in Quebec (Sherbrooke), released in late 1942, and traveled illegally through Maine in autumn 1943 trying to reach a cousin in New York. He had no valid documents. He was sheltered one night by Werner Pfeiffer — who knew him, or knew of him, through Bavaria — and was guided to the Coldwater Reach dock to find a boat south. He walked to the dock on the night of October 2 under his own power (Karl's testimony). Something happened on the dock — he went into the water in the NE gale. Whether Werner was present when Josef fell, whether it was an accident or something else, is **deliberately not resolved**. Josef is buried in the town cemetery under a stone that reads "Unknown Man, October 1943."

**What each character knows / withholds:**

| Character | What they know | What they withhold |
|-----------|---------------|--------------------|
| Eira | Saw a man alive on the dock at 2300 with a second figure (Werner Pfeiffer, though she's not certain until ch13); did not log it; the man was dead by morning | Werner's name; what happened after they left the dock |
| Hannah | Holds Karl's written account of Josef walking toward the harbor; knows the note connects Karl (a POW) to the body's final hours | The note's existence; the connection to the investigation |
| Marcus | Knows Josef's name, refugee history, and (by ch17) that Werner sheltered him | Everything — filed nothing, reported nothing |
| Werner Pfeiffer | Helped Josef; was on the dock; knows what happened | Has told no one |

**What remains unresolved at the novel's close**: Whether Josef fell or was helped into the water; whether Werner could have saved him; what Werner told Josef about the boat he would find. Hannah's note — and by extension Karl's testimony — remains hidden and may stay so. Werner Pfeiffer's name will not appear in the harbor log.
