# Chapter 4 — The Commander

**One-line summary**: The District Commander from Rockland visits the shack to take Eira's formal report, reads through the log, and closes the case as far as Rockland is concerned — the blank survives his inspection.

**POV**: Eira Bowman
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: Harbormaster's shack, Coldwater Reach breakwater. A Tuesday in mid-November 1943.
**Chapter shape**: Single scene (the Commander's visit, beginning to end; one location)
**Ending mode**: Resolved-beat
**Target words**: ~1,800 (compression chapter)

---

## Displacement Object

**The harbor log** — Eira opens it to show the Commander her October 3 entry on the body. The blank at October 2, 2300 hours is on the same page or the facing page. The Commander reads the entry Eira wrote; he does not ask about the blank because he has no reason to suspect one should be there.

**Fact introduced**: Official ruling — the body will be interred in Coldwater Reach cemetery as "Unknown Man." The case is closed from Rockland's perspective unless new information comes in. Eira is asked to report anything that surfaces.

**Named fact changed/recontextualized**: The official record now treats the body as a closed matter. Eira's blank is preserved inside a formally completed investigation — which means it is now harder, not easier, to fill in. The blank has acquired legal weight.

**Object rhyme**: The log shown to authority with the blank preserved ↔ Marcus's unreturned fragment: both records have been withheld from the people who should have received them. The reader has now seen two instances of the same act.

---

## Goal

Give the Commander a satisfactory, professional, accurate account of the October 3 finding and the subsequent notification chain. Be exactly what she has always been: the dependable harbormaster.

## Obstacle

The Commander is thorough without being suspicious. He goes through the log with the care of a man who learned in the Navy that details matter. He asks one question that comes close: "Any vessel traffic the night before?"

## Action Beats

1. **Preparation.** Eira has made coffee — the good coffee, the actual grounds, not the ersatz. She has put the chairs right. She does these things as she would for any official visit. She does not rehearse what she will say; she knows what she will say.

2. **The Commander arrives.** Commander Horace Willard, Naval District, Rockland. Sixty, approximate — a man who has been doing this longer than the war. He is respectful without being deferential; he has met enough hardmasters to know which ones know their harbor. He shakes her hand. He sits.

3. **The report.** Eira gives the account: the three-day gale, the channel-closed status, the dawn walk of the breakwater on October 3, the discovery, the call to Rockland, the county examiner. She has it all in the log; she reads the entries aloud when he asks. He takes notes in a small notebook.

4. **The photograph.** He produces a photograph — the county photographer's image of the body, taken before removal. He asks if she recognizes the man. Eira looks carefully. She does not recognize him. (This is true: she saw a living man at distance and at night, briefly; the photograph shows the dead; she cannot say they are the same person, though she suspects they are. Her answer is technically honest.)

5. **The close question.** He asks: "Any vessel traffic the night before the storm ended? Night of the second?" Eira says: "Gale conditions all through the second. Channel was flagged. No vessel traffic." This is true — she logged no vessel traffic. She saw a man on the dock. She did not log him as vessel traffic; she did not log him at all. The Commander accepts this.

6. **The log is reviewed.** He reads through her October entries — first the second's entry (weather, channel), then the third's (the finding). He is satisfied with their precision. He does not ask why there is no 2300-hour note on the second — because nothing in the log suggests he should look for one.

7. **The official disposition.** He tells her: they've run the physical description against merchant marine rosters, Allied and German naval registries, civilian reports of missing persons. No match. The man will be buried in the town cemetery. If anyone comes forward with information — a missing report, a letter, anything — she should contact Rockland immediately.

8. **He leaves.** Eira walks him to the door of the shack. She watches his car turn on the breakwater access road. She goes back to the desk. Opens the log. The blank at October 2, 2300 is still there — above the Commander's now-reviewed and officially satisfied entries. She touches the blank with her thumb. She closes the log.

---

## Outcome

The investigation is formally closed from Rockland's perspective. The blank has survived scrutiny. The body will be buried as Unknown. Eira is the sole official custodian of the log, and the blank is inside it.

## New Question or Pressure Raised

Now that the official weight has settled on the log as complete — now that the Commander has read it and found it satisfactory — what would it take to correct the record? And does Eira still want to?

## Continuity Notes

- Commander Willard: Naval District, Rockland. Fictional. About sixty. Efficient and respectful — he is not a villain or an antagonist; he is the procedure.
- The body will be interred in the Coldwater Reach town cemetery. The stone will read (per the novel's ending) "Unknown Man, October 1943." This is established here.
- Constable Tibbetts coordinated with the county on the body's removal; the Commander's visit is the naval/coast guard formality.
- The photograph: the county photographer's image of the dead man on the breakwater. Eira looks at it carefully and does not recognize him — this is technically true and should be written with that precision.
- Eira's answer about vessel traffic: "No vessel traffic" is technically accurate (she logged none). The blank she's protecting is not about a vessel but about a person on the dock. The drafter should make this distinction visible through precision, not through commentary.

---

## Craft Notes for the Drafter

- **This is a procedural chapter.** The Commander is a professional; Eira is a professional. The tension is below the surface — in the gap between what is said and what the reader knows is unsaid. Do not dramatize this externally.
- **The compression target is deliberate.** This chapter should feel shorter — it moves efficiently through a procedural event that serves one purpose: locking in the blank's survival. Under 2,000 words is right.
- **Resolved-beat ending**: The official pressure has passed. The immediate danger is over. But the reader knows the blank is still there, and now it has survived the one review that might have caught it. The resolution is relief with a cost embedded in it.
- **The photograph moment is the chapter's hinge.** Eira looks at the dead man's face and says she doesn't recognize him. The drafter should make this moment slow — not melodramatic, but given weight. The reader knows she may have seen this man alive. The chapter does not say she did.
- **Eira's voice**: short declarative sentences, procedural vocabulary, no editorializing. The Commander's questions and her answers should feel like a log — exact, no more than required.
