# Chapter 15 — The Crew Moves

**One-line summary**: On the POW crew's last day at the Pfeiffer farm, Hannah and Karl exchange a few guarded words in the barn, and then he leaves — and she goes upstairs and reads the note alone for the last time.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: Pfeiffer farm barn and farmhouse. A Friday in late October 1944.
**Chapter shape**: Single scene (the last barn morning; the truck's departure; Hannah's room)
**Ending mode**: Elliptical
**Target words**: ~2,400

---

## Displacement Object

**Karl's note** — taken from the lining after Karl leaves; read one final time; both sides; returned to the lining. It is now the only record of Karl's testimony about Josef Lutz. Karl is gone. The note is what remains.

**Fact introduced**: The POW crew is being reassigned to a cannery in Steuben for the winter — labor redistribution, a government rotation order. Last day is a Friday in late October. Karl leaves without looking back. Hannah and Karl exchange a few words in the barn in the morning, with Oakes watching at the far wall. They do not speak about the note, about the man on the road, about anything that matters. They speak about the cattle.

**Named fact changed/recontextualized**: Karl is gone. His connection to Coldwater Reach — and to the evidence in the note — is severed. The note's status changes from the documentary record of a living witness to the documentary record of someone Hannah may never see again. This is Hannah's thread at its close: suspended, unresolved, the note in the lining and Karl somewhere else.

**Object rhyme**: Hannah reading the note alone after Karl's departure ↔ Eira returning to the blank alone after the Commander's visit (ch4): both characters return to their displacement objects after the loss of human connection associated with them. The pattern of holding the object alone, after the person who could change things is gone, runs across the two threads.

---

## Goal

Get through the last day. Not say anything to Karl that can't be said in front of Oakes.

## Obstacle

There is nothing to say in front of Oakes. There is not enough time to say anything without Oakes. The morning passes.

## Action Beats

1. **The notification.** The Army notified the Pfeiffer farm a week ago: the POW work crew is being redistributed. A cannery in Steuben is short-staffed for the fall and winter run. The Pfeiffer crew will be replaced by a different crew in spring, if the program continues. Werner received the notification. He mentioned it at dinner, once. Hannah said nothing.

2. **Last day: the morning.** The truck arrives at seven-thirty — thirty minutes early, for some reason. Hannah is in the barn when she hears it. She keeps working — she is forking hay from the left-side mow. Karl comes in with the others; they are assigned the final tasks of the season, things that can be completed in half a day. Fence wire. Manure hauling. The barn gutter.

3. **Oakes watching.** Oakes is at the barn's end, not dozing — he seems aware that it's the last day and has put on some performance of attention. He watches the men work. He watches Hannah. She doesn't look at him.

4. **In the barn corridor.** Mid-morning. Karl is near her — they are both moving hay from the mow. Oakes is at the far end. Karl says, in English, quietly, without preamble: "The cattle here are good." She says: "They are." He says: "The Braunvieh at home are a little smaller." She says: "I know." A pause. He says: "Good luck to you." In English. She says: "You too." That is the whole of it.

5. **She does not say:** I still have the note. I wrote down everything you told me. I don't know what it means or what I'll do with it. I don't know if you're safe where you're going. She does not say any of this.

6. **Three o'clock.** The truck comes at three instead of four — the dispatcher moved the schedule. The men put down their tools. They line up by the truck. Carvell Oakes counts heads and makes a mark in his roster. The truck turns down the farm road. Karl does not look back.

7. **Hannah does not watch from the window.** She goes back into the barn and finishes the hay work herself. She is in the barn for another hour. Werner comes in at four and doesn't say anything about the crew being gone. They are just the two of them for a while, working near each other, not speaking.

8. **After supper.** Hannah goes upstairs. The farmhouse is quiet — Marta has gone to bed early; Werner is at the radio, listening to the evening news about the Western Front. Hannah takes the school bag down from the hook.

9. **The note.** She opens the lining. Unfolds the note — carefully, as always; the fold-lines are deep by now. She reads Karl's side first: his name, his town, the cattle, the man on the road. *Den Mann auf dem Weg habe ich noch in Erinnerung.* Then she turns it over and reads her own side: the testimony she transcribed in March, in the kitchen, after he told her. The time, the location, the description. *Bewegte sich Richtung Wasser. Sah sich um. Lief weiter.*

10. **She folds it.** She puts it back in the lining. She presses the seam. She hangs the school bag on the hook. She goes to her window. The farm road is dark. The barn light is off; Werner must have gone out and turned it off. Below, the radio murmur: the Western Front, the Allied advance, the names of cities being taken.

11. **She doesn't know.** She doesn't know where Karl is being sent tonight — the camp, the cannery — or whether he will come back to this farm in the spring, or whether the war will end before spring. She doesn't know whether what she's holding matters to anyone but her. She doesn't know what the man on the road had ahead of him. She knows what Karl said: *Er schien einen langen Weg vor sich zu haben.* She knows he didn't get there.

---

## Outcome

Karl is gone. Hannah's thread ends here — suspended in the October dark of the farm, the note in the lining, the war still on the radio. This is the last chapter in Hannah's POV in the novel.

## New Question or Pressure Raised

None is answered. This is deliberate. Hannah still has the note. Karl is gone. The man on the road is in the cemetery with a stone that says "Unknown Man, October 1943." Werner is at the radio. The thread ends without resolution — the unresolved withholding the brief requires.

---

## Continuity Notes

- Steuben: a real coastal Maine town where canneries operated. A POW labor reassignment to a seasonal cannery is historically accurate.
- The replacement crew in spring: the outline mentions it but it is not guaranteed — the war's ending may make it moot. This ambiguity is appropriate; Hannah doesn't know if she will see Karl again, and the novel does not resolve it.
- Karl's last words: "Good luck to you" / "You too" — in English, quietly. This is the register of what can be said in front of Oakes. The drafter should honor this restraint.
- Werner at the radio: the Western Front news in October 1944 — the Allied advance into Germany, battles on the Belgian-German border (the Hurtgen Forest fighting; the approach to Aachen). This is historically accurate background for late October 1944.
- Hannah's thread ends at chapter 15. She will not appear in chapters 16-18. Her absence is the deliberate unresolved withholding — the reader's last image of her is the farm road, dark, and the note in the lining.

---

## Craft Notes for the Drafter

- **The goodbye must not be sentimentalized.** Karl and Hannah say nothing meaningful in front of Oakes. "Good luck to you." "You too." That's what gets said. The chapter earns its weight from what is not said, not from a final charged exchange.
- **Elliptical ending**: The chapter ends with Hannah at the window and the radio's murmur and the last sentence about the man on the road — *Er schien einen langen Weg vor sich zu haben* — repeated without comment. The thread ends on that sentence. It is the note's last line. It is also true.
- **Hannah's interiority: feeling before naming.** The paragraph of "she doesn't know" should feel like Hannah at the edge of her comprehension, not like a narrator listing what remains open. She is seventeen; she feels the shape of not-knowing before she can name what she doesn't know.
- **Karl does not look back.** This is not a Hollywood detail — it is a fact of the scene. He faces forward because looking back would be a gesture, and gestures draw attention, and he has spent a year learning not to make gestures. The drafter should note this briefly, without sentimentality.
- **The note is read once more, in full.** Both sides. The drafter should render Karl's side in German and Hannah's side in German — the two hands, the two moments. The fold-lines are deep now; the paper is soft from handling.
