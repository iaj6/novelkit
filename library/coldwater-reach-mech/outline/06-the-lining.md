# Chapter 6 — The Lining

**One-line summary**: On the POW crew's last day before the winter reduction, Hannah watches Werner watch Karl, and hides the note inside her school bag's lining.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: Pfeiffer farm yard and farmhouse. An early December day — the POW crew's final full workday before winter reduction to once-a-week.
**Chapter shape**: Multi-scene (farm yard; barn; farmhouse kitchen; Hannah's room)
**Ending mode**: Elliptical
**Target words**: ~2,300

---

## Displacement Object

**Karl's note** — now moved from her apron pocket to the lining of her old school bag, sewn into the seam with a needle and a partial stitch. The note's physical situation has changed: it is now hidden rather than carried. The act of hiding it is the chapter's central action.

**Fact introduced**: The note's geographical detail — Karl mentioned "near Wilder's landing, the coastal road" (implied from his transit route) — begins to map onto what Hannah heard around town about where the body was found. She doesn't yet know what to do with this connection, but it is forming.

**Named fact changed/recontextualized**: The note is no longer a letter someone gave her; it is a thing she has actively concealed. The distinction between *having* and *hiding* is the accretion.

**Object rhyme**: Hannah's note hidden in the lining ↔ Marcus's documents in the bottom drawer (ch5): both characters have taken their displacement objects from open possession to deliberate concealment within the chapter sequence. The pattern of active hiding — not passive retention but chosen obscurement — runs across both threads simultaneously.

---

## Goal

Get through the crew's last day; not let anything show; keep the day ordinary for Werner and Marta.

## Obstacle

Werner comes out to the yard and watches Karl work. Werner's watching — not hostile, not approving, something in between — makes Hannah understand that the note cannot stay in her coat pocket. She needs it somewhere her father will not find it if he searches her things.

## Action Beats

1. **Morning: the crew arrives.** The Army truck comes later than usual — Werner is already out in the yard when it pulls up. Marta is at the kitchen window. It is cold and grey, and the fields have a skim of ice on the drainage channels. The crew descends — Karl, Brauer, and two others. Oakes climbs down carefully from the cab, holds his rifle without attention.

2. **Werner in the yard.** Werner is ostensibly checking a fence post. But Hannah, crossing from the house to the barn with the feed bucket, can see that he is watching Karl — specifically Karl, who is carrying fence wire from the truck bed. Werner watches him the way you watch something you recognize and don't want to recognize. His expression is closed and tight. Not hate; something more complicated. Recognition and the cost of it.

3. **The work.** Hannah works through the morning. She and Karl are not near each other — she keeps the barn interior; he is on the fence line with Brauer. Oakes drifts to the wagon shed. The day passes. There is no private conversation; Werner is in and out of the yard all morning.

4. **Karl leaves without a look.** The truck comes at three — earlier than usual. The men line up without ceremony. Karl doesn't look toward the farmhouse window as the truck turns. Hannah is watching from the barn door. She notes the not-looking.

5. **The farmhouse: evening.** Werner at the radio. Marta setting the table. Hannah takes off her coat. She reaches into the pocket — the note is there, folded, where it has been for three weeks. She looks at her coat on the hook. If her father were to go through her coat, looking for something —

6. **The decision.** She goes upstairs. She takes out the note and reads it again. The line about the man on the road: *"near the water... moving fast..."* She has heard things at school, overheard things at Atwood's — the body found at the breakwater, the direction of the storm, a man who'd come from somewhere inland. The geography lines up. She doesn't know what to do with this yet. She folds the note.

7. **The lining.** She gets her old school bag — the one she used before the farm work took up her school hours, the canvas bag with the worn strap. She gets a needle and thread from her mother's sewing box (without asking). She opens a seam at the inside bottom of the bag, works the note in flat against the canvas, and stitches the edge partially back — enough to hold, not enough to look deliberate. She checks it with her hand. Nothing visible.

8. **Downstairs.** She hangs the school bag on the hook by the stairs. She goes to the kitchen. Her father is not listening to the radio; he is looking at the table where his hands are. *Was denkst du, Vater?* she thinks, and says nothing. She sets the water glasses on the table.

---

## Outcome

The note is hidden in the lining. Hannah has not told anyone about it — not about the note, not about what Karl said, not about the way the geographical detail maps onto the body's location. The act of hiding is now an active choice, not a passive one.

## New Question or Pressure Raised

What does Werner see in Karl that costs him something? And what will Hannah do with the connection she's beginning to trace between the note and the body on the breakwater?

---

## Continuity Notes

- Wilder's landing: a coastal road feature (a landing or cove) that serves as a geographical reference point. The POW transport from Bangor would have passed it — it is on the coastal road between Bangor and Coldwater Reach. This is a fictional place name consistent with the Maine coast setting.
- The note's current content (carried forward from ch3): Karl's name, town, conscription account, a line about the cattle, and the sentence about the man on the road.
- Werner's behavior: he watches Karl. He says nothing to Hannah. He does not search her things in this chapter — that comes in ch12. His watching is its own disclosure.
- Marta's performance: she sets the table with the American flag placemats. She is trying very hard. Hannah notices this without naming it.
- Oakes: increasingly incidental — a body in the frame, barely functional as a guard.

---

## Craft Notes for the Drafter

- **The chapter is about watching and being watched.** Werner watches Karl; Hannah watches Werner; Oakes isn't watching anyone. The geometry of surveillance matters — render it physically, not analytically.
- **German surfaces in Hannah's interiority.** When she thinks about her father — *Was denkst du, Vater?* — it is in German, the language of private things. No translation needed. The English reader feels the register.
- **The hiding scene is tactile.** The needle, the thread, the canvas of the old school bag. The note going flat against the bottom lining. Make this physical and specific — the needle is blunt; she has to push; the thread is the wrong color but it doesn't matter because no one will look.
- **Elliptical ending**: The chapter closes on the school bag on the hook and the father who is not listening to the radio. The open question (Werner's silence; the note's significance) is not named. The last image — Werner looking at his hands — is held without comment.
- **Hannah's register**: medium-length, more associative sentences. German surfaces. The most porous POV — she reads mood before she can name it. Werner's watching registers on her as weather before she can articulate it as meaning.
