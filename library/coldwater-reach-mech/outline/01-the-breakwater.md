# Chapter 1 — The Breakwater

**One-line summary**: Eira Bowman finds an unidentified man's body on the breakwater after a three-day blow, logs what she must, and leaves blank what she cannot.

**POV**: Eira Bowman
**Tense**: Third-person limited past
**Setting**: Harbormaster's shack; the breakwater; the harbor. Dawn, October 3, 1943 — the morning after the storm ends.
**Chapter shape**: Multi-scene (the walk, the finding, the log, the reporting, the waiting)
**Ending mode**: Elliptical
**Target words**: ~2,500

---

## Displacement Object

**The harbor log** — specifically the entries Eira writes for October 3 (the finding) and the deliberate blank where the entry for October 2 at 2300 hours should be. The blank is not an accident; it is the first act of will in the novel.

**Fact introduced**: An unidentified male adult, no papers, possible European features, found drowned on the seaward face of the breakwater after the three-day NE gale. This is the spine question.

**Named fact changed/recontextualized since prior chapter**: First chapter — establishes the baseline. The body exists; Eira's blank exists.

**Object rhyme**: None yet — this is the first displacement object. The blank will rhyme forward with Marcus's unreturned fragment (ch2) and Hannah's kept note (ch3).

---

## Goal

Perform her duty: find the body, establish its condition, log the finding, notify the constable and District at Rockland. Maintain the log as the legal record it must be.

## Obstacle

The correct procedure requires logging everything she observed — including what she saw on the dock at approximately 2300 hours on October 2. She cannot write that. She does not yet know why she cannot; she knows only that she cannot.

## Action Beats

1. **Dawn walk, post-storm.** Eira leaves the cottage before full light. The gale has blown three days; the channel is littered with broken trap line and a skiff that came off its mooring. She is checking the channel markers — standard procedure after a blow. The light is grey and horizontal and very cold. She wears Owen's old coat.

2. **The finding.** She walks the breakwater from the shack-end toward the channel. At roughly two-thirds of the way out, against the seaward face where wreckage collects after northerlies, she sees the shape. She knows what it is before she is close enough to confirm it. She moves carefully — the granite is wet and angled — and crouches to look. Male. Perhaps fifty, perhaps older. Not a fisherman's clothing — wool trousers, a heavy coat, no oilskin, leather shoes inappropriate for this coast. No identifying papers in the coat pockets or the trouser pockets. His features, in what she can observe: not a face she recognizes. Possibly European — she cannot say more. The sea has had him three days or more.

3. **The return to the shack.** She does not touch him further. She walks back to the shack — the same deliberate pace she uses for everything on the breakwater, because the granite is always dangerous. She does not run. Inside the shack she hangs up Owen's coat. She opens the log.

4. **The log entry.** She writes the October 3 entry: 0615 hours, discovery of body, seaward face of breakwater at approximately 210 feet from the pier head, male adult, estimated 50–60 years, no papers or identification found on the body, death by drowning presumed, condition consistent with several days' exposure. She writes this clearly, precisely, completely. Then she looks at the October 2 entry above it. The October 2 entry covers the morning tide, the gale conditions, the channel-closed notice. At 2300 there is a blank. She picks up the pen. She looks at the blank. She does not write what she saw at 2300. She writes: **"Weather: NE gale, 35 knots decreasing at first light. Channel: temporarily impaired."** That is all.

5. **Notification.** She radios the District Commander's office in Rockland — reports the finding as required. She telephones (or sends the harbormaster's boy) for the town constable, a man named Aldous Tibbetts. Tibbetts comes. He looks at the body. He says, "Jesus." He does not touch it either. They wait for the county examiner.

6. **The body is removed.** By early afternoon, a truck from the county seat takes the body. The constable has made a report. Eira has made hers. The channel is still closed for the debris.

7. **Closing the log.** She is alone in the shack. She opens the log again — reads back through October 2. The blank at 2300 sits there. She reads it. Then she closes the log and sets it on the desk.

---

## Outcome

The body has been officially reported; the procedure has been followed; the log records the finding precisely. What the log does not record is the observation Eira made on the dock the previous night — a man alive on the dock, not alone. That blank is now a legal document.

## New Question or Pressure Raised

What did she see at 2300 on October 2? Why has she left the blank rather than filled it?

## Continuity Notes

- Body: male adult, approximately 50–60 years, no papers, no identification, possible European features, clothing inappropriate for a Maine coast fisherman. Wool trousers, heavy coat, leather shoes. Drowned.
- The body is found against the seaward face of the breakwater at approximately 210 feet from the pier head.
- Eira does not recognize the body.
- The log's blank is at October 2, 2300 hours.
- Owen's coat is kept in the cottage; Eira wears it for cold weather walks. (Continuity anchor: Owen is present in objects.)
- Constable Aldous Tibbetts is the town law. He is not antagonistic; he is limited.
- The body is removed by the county examiner's office. Its disposition (burial) will be handled by the district/county.

## Craft Notes for the Drafter

- **Open on procedure, not feeling.** The first paragraph should be the walk along the breakwater — tide conditions, the channel marker status, the smell of the post-gale harbor. The body should be discovered about one-third of the way into the chapter. Do not open on the body.
- **The log entry is the chapter's emotional center.** The physical act of writing, the specific pen, the specific columns — make it tactile. The blank is not announced; the reader feels it in what is not written, not in a sentence naming the absence.
- **Eira does not explain herself, even to herself.** She does not think "she could not write what she had seen." She writes the weather notation and moves on. Her interiority is very short and factual. The chapter earns its meaning through what is not said.
- **The chapter ends on the closed log.** The last image: the log on the desk, the shack, Eira standing. Elliptical — open-ended without announcing its openness. The blank is the last thing the reader felt; the chapter does not remind them of it.
- **Period-accurate procedure**: Rockland District Commander is the reporting chain. Constable Tibbetts for local law. County examiner for the body. These are facts of the world, not explanations.
