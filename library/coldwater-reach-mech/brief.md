## Premise

A small Maine fishing town, autumn 1943 to spring 1945. Three lives intersect across the second half of
America's war.

**Eira Bowman**, fifty-two, harbormaster of Coldwater Reach for eight years — since her husband Owen, the
previous harbormaster, drowned in the November storm of 1935. The only woman in the post on the Maine
coast; she holds it because no man wanted it after Pearl Harbor took the able-bodied. In October 1943 a
man's body washes up on the breakwater after a three-day blow. He is not from Coldwater. He carries no
papers. Eira knows what she saw on the dock the night before he came in.

**Hannah Pfeiffer**, seventeen, on her family's dairy farm three miles inland — parents who left Bavaria
for Maine in 1922 and have not been the same since the war started. Under the federal POW labor program,
four German prisoners work the Pfeiffer fields under an aging Coastal Patrol guard. The youngest, Karl
Reinmar, nineteen, is from a town fifty kilometers from where Hannah's father was born.

**Dr. Marcus Klein**, forty-seven, a Viennese displaced scholar by way of Paris, London, and a Quebec
internment camp he does not discuss; he teaches German and Latin at the town high school and boards with
Henry Crow, the school's Black custodian, and his wife Lillian — the only household where Marcus is
treated as guest rather than specimen.

The body on the breakwater is the inciting object the three of them, separately, end up carrying. None
planned to. None sets it down before the war ends.

## Length and shape

- Target length: ~45,000 words
- Number of chapters: 18 (≈6 per POV)

Chapters rotate POV across the three, in narrative-time order (not a fixed cycle — longer or shorter runs
in a single POV when the story requires). The braid is the engine: rotation and contrast keep momentum.
The ending lands in spring 1945, just before VE Day; resolutions are smaller and older than the war's end.

## Voice

Third-person limited past, bound tightly to whichever POV the chapter belongs to; each voice has its own
register, felt within the first paragraph.

- **Eira**: spare, procedural, a working harbormaster's observation. The sea is a workplace, not a
  metaphor. Even her grief comes to her in the language of tide, weather, draft, and load.
- **Hannah**: closer to adolescent interior consciousness; German phrases threaded where her father's
  voice has reached her. The most porous of the three.
- **Marcus**: precise, slightly formal, the displaced scholar weighing every word for what it betrays.

Across all three: restrained period prose, action and observation over reflection, the story trusting the
reader to feel what is not said. Real-world historical accuracy (the Maine POW labor program, harbormaster
regulations, rationing, V-Mail, casualty telegrams) rendered as lived texture, never set decoration. No
spy-thriller plot; the war is in the radio, the letters, the missing men, the prisoners — not in plot
mechanics. Race and class are facts of the town, not subject lectures. Names of real places may appear;
real people may not.

## Constraints

- The body on the breakwater is the spine question. None of the three sees the whole; the reader assembles
  it across the book. At least one thread stays deliberately unresolved.
- Each POV has its own internal arc not subordinate to the central mystery; the three never meet as a group.
- **EVERY chapter built on withholding must give the withheld thing a concrete PHYSICAL OBJECT that
  performs it** — a thing the POV character handles, keeps, hides, fails to write, returns to, or destroys.
  Eira's withholding lives in the log entry she does or does not make (and what she enters instead);
  Hannah's in a kept object that passed from Karl; Marcus's in a document, a letter, a photograph he cannot
  file. **FORBIDDEN: a withholding chapter that stays abstract** — "a thing she could not name," a feeling
  circled with no object to hold it. An objectless withholding chapter is the author stalling; it is the
  single failure this variant exists to prevent.
- **The objects are STAGGERED and RHYMED across the three threads.** All three are "things known and not
  said," made physical; when one thread's object recurs, it should rhyme with another's, so the reader
  assembles one pattern across the braid rather than three separate moods.
- **The braid must ACCRETE, not orbit.** Every chapter must change a NAMED fact about the breakwater body,
  OR recontextualize a fact the reader already holds, OR advance an object's meaning. A chapter that merely
  re-establishes an atmosphere the reader already has is a failure, however well-written.

## Structure — how the braid must accrete (operative direction)

**Architect / Plotter** — for EACH chapter, fix in `outline/00-chapter-map.md`: (a) the POV; (b) the
concrete displacement OBJECT that chapter handles; (c) the named fact it changes or recontextualizes since
the prior chapter. No chapter may be "atmosphere only." Stagger the objects across the three threads so they
rhyme; track the recurring objects as book-spanning threads.

**Reader / pacing discipline** — flag any chapter that cannot name both a handled object and a changed or
recontextualized fact as **"orbiting"** — the book's central failure mode, the thing this variant is built
to eliminate.
