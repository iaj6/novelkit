# Plotter guidance — Coldwater Reach

## POV count and rotation

Three POV characters — Eira Bowman, Hannah Pfeiffer, Marcus Klein — each with approximately 6 chapters across an 18-chapter manuscript. The rotation is **motivated, not strictly cyclical**: a run of two consecutive chapters in one POV is allowed; three consecutive chapters in one POV requires explicit justification (a sustained crisis in that thread). No POV may go more than three chapters without appearing.

The rotation's primary purpose is contrast: Eira → Hannah = spare to porous; Hannah → Marcus = feeling to taxonomy; Marcus → Eira = controlled formality to procedural plainness. Design the sequence so each rotation provides that relief.

## Chapter-shape preferences and the accretion requirement

**This is the single most important structural constraint in this manuscript:**

Every chapter must carry (a) a concrete displacement object that the POV character physically handles, and (b) a change to a named fact OR a recontextualization of a fact the reader already holds. Fix both items in the chapter map (`outline/00-chapter-map.md`) before any prose is drafted.

The chapter map must include, for each chapter:
- POV character
- The displacement object handled in this chapter
- The named fact changed or recontextualized since the prior chapter in this thread
- A one-line note on how this chapter's object rhymes with another thread's object (if applicable)

A chapter that cannot name both a handled object and a changed/recontextualized fact is an **"orbiting" chapter** and must be revised or cut before it goes to the drafter.

## The displacement objects — stagger and rhyme

The three threads' primary objects must be staggered and rhymed across the braid so the reader assembles one pattern, not three separate moods. The rhyme logic:

- All three objects are **"things known and not sent"** — a log entry that should be filed and is not; an object received from Karl that should be surrendered and is not; a document Marcus should have reported and has not.
- When a chapter introduces a new stage of an object's life (hidden, moved, nearly discovered, partially destroyed), it should land in the reader's sequence near the next beat in a different thread's object arc, so the rhyme accumulates across the braid rather than within a single thread.
- Object arcs do not need to resolve simultaneously. One thread's object may be destroyed; another may remain unresolved at the novel's end.

## Episode-vs-arc structure

This is a **continuous braid**, not episodic. Chapters flow into each other; the reader is always mid-accumulation. The structure is not: "each chapter is a self-contained encounter." The structure is: "each chapter is a step further into the situation, and the braid makes each step recontextualize the last."

There are three distinct sub-arcs (one per thread), each with its own shape. The braid interleaves them. The three sub-arcs share the breakwater body as a common referent but are not causally locked to each other — Eira's choices do not automatically determine Hannah's, and Marcus's document does not automatically intersect with Eira's log. The connections are lateral and accreting: each thread illuminates the others without being subordinate to them.

## Ending-mode distribution (18 chapters)

Target distribution across the manuscript:
- **literary-fade**: 5 (quiet image/sensory/object close; spread across all three threads)
- **turn**: 4 (new fact or recontextualization in final beat; more common in later half)
- **elliptical**: 4 (deliberate unresolved gesture; appropriate for withholding chapters)
- **resolved-beat**: 3 (sub-arc beat closes; use to give the reader air before next pressure)
- **coda**: 2 (aftermath; use after the highest-pressure scenes in each thread)

Do not cluster the same ending mode. Two literary-fades in sequence is the braid going slack; flag it and revise.

## Chapter target words

- Standard chapter: 2,200–2,800 words
- High-pressure scene chapters (at most 3): may expand to 3,200 words
- Compression/transition chapters (at most 2): may contract to 1,600 words
- Target total: approximately 45,000 words

Chapters do not need to be the same length; the braid benefits from some variation. But chapters shorter than 1,500 words or longer than 3,500 words are structural anomalies that need justification.

## Genre-specific structural conventions

This is quiet literary WWII home-front fiction with a mystery braid. The structural conventions:

- **The inciting object (the body) must appear in chapter 1 or by the end of chapter 2**, as a fact or as its physical presence in Eira's POV. The reader must know from early that the body is the spine question.
- **Each thread's primary displacement object must be introduced in the thread's first two chapters.** The reader needs the object before the withholding around it can accumulate.
- **The midpoint (approximately chapters 9–10) should deliver a recontextualization** — something that makes the reader reassemble what they understood about the body, or about one character's situation, up to that point.
- **The final quarter (chapters 14–18) accretes rapidly** — two or three turns in quick succession as threads that have been running in parallel begin to intersect or reflect each other.
- **The ending (chapter 18) resolves the three threads' sub-arcs at different levels of closure.** At least one thread ends in deliberate unresolved withholding. The body's full identity may remain partial. The war ends (VE Day, May 1945) but the story does not depend on it for resolution.
- **The three characters never all meet.** Pairwise intersection is permitted where motivated. Do not engineer coincidences that require the full cast to gather.

## The chapter map requirement

The plotter must produce `outline/00-chapter-map.md` with every chapter's POV, displacement object, and fact-changed entry filled in before handing to the drafter. This is not optional for this manuscript — the accretion discipline is the whole mechanism. An empty cell in the "fact changed/recontextualized" column is a red flag requiring revision.
