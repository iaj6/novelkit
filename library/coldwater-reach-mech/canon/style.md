# Style — Coldwater Reach

## POV and tense

Third-person limited past tense, tightly bound to whichever POV the chapter belongs to. The narrator does not editorialize; it sees only what the character can see and renders only what the character would notice. Shifts of POV happen between chapters only, never within a chapter. The three POVs are distinguishable from the first paragraph of each chapter — register carries the POV signal, not chapter headers alone.

## The three registers

**Eira**: Short sentences, declarative syntax, the vocabulary of harbor work. Observation before reflection; action before feeling. The sea is a workplace — its conditions are facts to be recorded, not moods to be entered. When emotion surfaces it arrives in the language of her work: a displaced cargo, a failed mooring, a log entry left incomplete. Interior monologue is brief and rarely named as such. Average sentence 10–16 words. No rhetorical flourish.

**Hannah**: Medium-length sentences, more associative structure, more incomplete gestures. German words and phrases surface naturally — not italicized exoticism but the texture of bilingual thought; they appear without translation when their meaning can be felt from context. The most porous of the three registers; she receives mood before she can name it. Her interiority is longer than Eira's but never analytical. She is the only POV that runs toward feeling; the discipline is in not letting that run explain itself.

**Marcus**: Precise, slightly formal, the syntax of a man trained to be exact. He thinks in complete subordinate-clause sentences. His vocabulary is wider than any other character's and he is wary of displaying it. His interiority is taxonomic — he names the structure of things, the category of what he sees — but this naming is often a way of avoiding the feeling underneath it. He is the most controlled narrator and also the most frightened. Average sentence 14–22 words; occasional longer periodic sentences for a moment of suppressed intensity.

## Dialogue density

Low overall. This is a novel of what is not said. Dialogue is freighted — each exchange carries more than its surface content. Dialogue tags are spare (said, asked; occasionally a qualifying adverb only when it changes the meaning entirely). No glossing of dialogue after the fact ("which meant," "as if to say"). Silence and non-response are dialogue moves.

## Narrative distance

Intimate but not confessional. The narrator is lodged behind the POV character's eyes without becoming self-dramatizing. The reader is trusted to feel what the prose does not name. The hardest thing about this register is holding back the explanatory sentence — the sentence that tells the reader what the scene just meant. That sentence must not appear.

## Pacing

Chapters are scenes with a shape — they begin in a specific physical moment and end in one, with a change in the situation or the reader's knowledge between. The pace is not slow; it is precise. A chapter can cover one hour or one month, but it must carry a concrete object and change a named fact (see the Displacement Object constraint in continuity). Chapters that do only atmosphere are failures; atmosphere is the texture, not the load.

## Period vocabulary

Use period-accurate vocabulary without explanation when the context carries it. V-Mail, ration book, PW armband, District Commander, Coastal Patrol, the Eastern Sea Frontier, coal oil, the draft board, Form W-2 (war production). Real place names in Maine are permitted. Real historical persons may not appear. Real historical procedures (POW labor, harbormaster reporting, Geneva Convention POW rights) appear as lived fact.

## Failure modes

The AI-author patterns most tempting in this story's register. The drafter should actively avoid these; the compression pass should cut them.

- **Sentence-after-the-sentence** — an image lands, then the next sentence glosses or explains it. This prose runs on images that land without comment; the gloss destroys the effect. Cut the second sentence.
- **Post-scene subtext naming** — after a scene between characters, a paragraph (often the last) names what the scene "was really about." The braid depends on subtext staying subtext. If the reader can read it on the page, they do not need to assemble it; the assembly is the experience.
- **Meta-commentary on cognition** — "She registered this." "He noted." "She found herself thinking." Labels for mental acts the prose has already shown. Especially tempting in Marcus's POV, where the scholarly voice makes it feel precise. It is not precision; it is a hedge.
- **Emotional structural analysis** — a feeling is dramatized and then its architecture is explained ("she understood now that what she had felt was not grief but the particular anticipatory grief of someone who—"). Marcus's scholarly interiority is the high-risk site. The analysis must stop one step before the explanation.
- **Thesis restatement** — a sentence that names the war, or the silence, or the cost of keeping, in abstract terms. The themes are load-bearing precisely because they are never stated. The moment the prose says "the war made ordinary things impossible" the novel becomes smaller.

## Lean-into patterns

Patterns the drafter and editors should NOT treat as failures, because this register depends on them.

- **The procedural anchor** — Eira's chapters (and, by influence, moments in the other two) earn their emotional weight by grounding in exact physical work. A boat's draft, a log's column, the procedure for reporting an unusual finding. This is not throat-clearing; it is the register. Cut around it but not through it.
- **The unfinished sentence or withheld clause** — in Hannah's and Marcus's chapters especially, a sentence that does not arrive at its expected conclusion (whether in syntax or in thought) performs the novel's central act. Not every paragraph, but deliberately placed.
- **German without translation** — in Hannah's chapters, German words and phrases that require no translation because feeling carries them. Do not add an English gloss. Trust the reader.
