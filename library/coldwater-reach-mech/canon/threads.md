# Threads — Coldwater Reach

Five threads braid the novel. Three are POV-centered (the three displacement objects); one is the moral question that runs beneath all three threads and refuses resolution; one is the internal relational thread that ends in deliberate suspension. The spine question — who was the man on the breakwater? — is not a thread; it is the rod the threads are wound around. The threads are what the reader is tracking about the living.

---

## Thread 1: Eira's Blank

- **Question**: Will Eira write into the harbor log what she saw on the dock, and what does her sustained refusal make of her?
- **Enters**: `01-the-breakwater` — Eira makes the October 3 entry precisely and completely, then leaves the October 2 entry at 2300 blank. The blank is deliberate from the first sentence it isn't written.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `04-the-commander` — the blank survives Commander Willard's review of the log; officially, the case closes around it
  - `07-owens-pages` — Eira reads Owen's meticulous 1933 entry and understands, for the first time, that her blank is no longer an omission she will someday correct; she begins to write it and stops; the crossed-out attempt is now *in* the legal record
  - `10-no-new-information` — she fills out the Rockland follow-up form and checks "none," converting a passive blank into an active lie-on-paper; walking to the breakwater's end, she inventories what she saw in the clearest internal account the reader has yet received
  - `13-the-man-at-atwoods` — she identifies Werner Pfeiffer as the second figure and that evening writes a log entry that does not name him; the blank now has a name inside it that the log will never hold
  - `16-the-old-volume` — she writes one line of weather into the blank for the first time in fifteen months and cannot go further; the blank is partially filled, which is different from absent
  - `18-the-reach` — she writes the minimum dock entry ("one vessel docking at approximately 2300. One person at the pier.") and puts the pen down; Werner's name is absent from the final record
- **Resolves in**: `18-the-reach` — minimally and permanently; the blank is filled with the smallest true thing the log can hold; Werner's name is not in it and will not be
- **What it's about underneath**: Eira *is* the log. Eight years of harbormaster work have made procedural completeness her identity; when Owen kept the record, she held it alongside him, and after he drowned she kept it for both of them. The blank is the first time she has split from her own definition of integrity, and every chapter in her thread is the cost of that split, accumulated. The question is never whether the blank gets filled; it is whether Eira can live with what filling it completely would cost the man she saw on the dock — and whether her procedural self and her protective self can ever fully occupy the same page.

## Thread 2: Karl's Note

- **Question**: What will Hannah do with the note in the school bag lining — the only documentary record of the last known sighting of the man who died without a name?
- **Enters**: `03-allgau` — Karl presses a folded note into Hannah's palm before Carvell Oakes circles back; Hannah reads it that evening; it contains a line about a man on the coastal road in the rain that she doesn't yet understand
- **Advanced in**:
  - `06-the-lining` — Hannah moves the note from her coat pocket to the sewn lining of her school bag after watching Werner watch Karl; the act of hiding changes the note's status from *received* to *concealed*
  - `09-the-road` — Hannah asks Karl directly what he saw; his oral testimony establishes that Josef walked to the dock alive under his own power; Hannah transcribes it on the reverse of the note in her own hand; the note is now a two-sided document in two hands, carrying the only witness account of Josef's last hours
  - `12-not-yet` — Werner searches Hannah's room for tobacco and his hand passes near the school bag; that evening Hannah holds the note to the lamp flame and pulls it back; the keeping is now explicit, irreversible in meaning, an act of will rather than a default
  - `15-the-crew-moves` — Karl is reassigned; Hannah reads the note one final time, both sides, and puts it back in the lining; the note is now the only record of a testimony from a witness Hannah will not see again
- **Resolves in**: Deliberately unresolved — Hannah's thread ends at `15-the-crew-moves`; her absence from chapters 16–18 is the novel's required unresolved withholding; the note is in the lining of the school bag and may stay there
- **What it's about underneath**: Hannah receives the note because Karl recognized her as someone who could hold it — the shared dialect was a form of identification, not seduction. She keeps it because destroying it would mean destroying the only account of the last moments of a man who is buried as Unknown. The thread pressurizes what loyalty means across lines the official world draws: between enemy and neighbor, between evidence and person, between the German of private things and the German of the record. Hannah is the most porous of the three keepers, and she is the one who keeps the most irresolvable thing.

## Thread 3: Josef Lutz's Name

- **Question**: Will Marcus disclose what he knows about the dead man's identity before his knowing destroys him?
- **Enters**: `02-the-fragment` — a naval officer leaves a water-damaged document fragment with Marcus for translation; Marcus recognizes the name "J. Lutz" and does not return it
- **Advanced in**:
  - `05-the-name` — working alone on a Saturday, Marcus confirms the name as Josef Lutz, a German-Jewish refugee he knew from the Sherbrooke internment camp; he drafts a letter to his Montreal contact, burns the first draft, writes a second, and does not send it
  - `08-montreal` — the Montreal reply confirms Josef's identity, route, and disappearance; Marcus puts the letter in the drawer with the fragment; he is now the sole keeper of a confirmed identity in a town where the stone says "Unknown Man"
  - `11-the-crow-table` — at dinner, Marcus tells Henry Crow he thinks he knew who the man on the breakwater was; Henry nods without asking more; the burden shifts from one keeper to two; Marcus is no longer alone with it
  - `14-one-year` — on the first anniversary of the body's discovery, Marcus walks to the Prospect Street mailbox and sends a letter to Josef's sister in Zürich: one minimal truth, signed *ein Freund aus Sherbrooke*; the secret has moved outward for the first time
  - `17-what-henry-knows` — Henry tells Marcus that Werner Pfeiffer sheltered Josef one night before the gale; Marcus goes upstairs, spreads all three documents on the desk, and writes the final entry in his private notebook; the reconstruction is complete
- **Resolves in**: `17-what-henry-knows` — privately, imperfectly, sufficiently; Marcus has assembled Josef's story, shared minimal truth with Henry and with the sister, and built a record no official hand will ever read; the sub-arc closes with Marcus eating supper while the advance on Berlin plays on the radio
- **What it's about underneath**: Marcus survived the internment camps by being useful — the translator, the language teacher, the man you bring your unusual problem to. Josef Lutz's fragment makes him useful again in a way that requires explaining how he could be useful, which requires explaining things he cannot explain without losing the place he has built. The thread pressurizes what usefulness costs when the knowledge you hold would identify you as the kind of person who has complicated knowledge — precisely the category Marcus has spent two years escaping. The Crow house is the only safe space he has, and Henry's "that happens in a war" is the only validation the novel gives him.

## Thread 4: Werner Pfeiffer on the Dock

- **Question**: What did Werner Pfeiffer do on the night of October 2, 1943 — and will anyone ever establish whether he caused, witnessed, or failed to prevent Josef Lutz's death?
- **Enters**: `01-the-breakwater` — Eira sees a man alive on the dock at 2300 and does not log him; she saw two figures; one she "believed at the time was local"; the second figure is the thread's subject
- **Advanced in**:
  - `04-the-commander` — the official case closes; the second figure is unrecorded; the frame for official accountability is locked shut
  - `06-the-lining` — Werner watches Karl work with an expression Hannah reads as recognition and its cost; his connection to the situation is foreshadowed without being named
  - `07-owens-pages` — Eira commits permanently to the blank; the second figure she is protecting accumulates weight she cannot yet name
  - `09-the-road` — Karl's testimony that Josef walked to the dock alive collapses the disposal theory; the dock was a meeting or a destination; someone was waiting there, or had been; the crime question reopens
  - `10-no-new-information` — Eira's internal inventory of the dock gives the reader the clearest view yet: a man alive, not alone, a second figure she took for local
  - `13-the-man-at-atwoods` — MAJOR TURN: Eira recognizes Werner Pfeiffer's back and shoulders at the Atwood's counter; that evening the log entry does not name him; the thread and Eira's thread converge
  - `17-what-henry-knows` — Henry tells Marcus that Werner sheltered Josef and drove him to the harbor; Marcus writes Werner's name in his private notebook for the first time; Werner is named in two private records (Eira's knowledge, Marcus's notebook) and in neither official one
- **Resolves in**: Deliberately and structurally refused — `18-the-reach` closes the novel with Werner Pfeiffer's name absent from the harbor log; the cemetery stone still reads "Unknown Man, October 1943"; what happened on the dock between Josef's arrival and his death is never established; Karl's testimony (in Hannah's note) and Eira's identification (in her log) and Marcus's reconstruction (in his notebook) together name Werner but do not indict him; what they cannot establish is what the novel withholds
- **What it's about underneath**: Werner Pfeiffer sheltered a man fleeing Europe and guided him toward a harbor where a boat might save him. This is either the action of a decent man doing the only thing available, or the action of a man whose guidance led Josef to a dock where something went wrong. The novel refuses to distinguish between these because the refusing is the argument: moral accountability in extremity cannot be discharged by a verdict the surviving witnesses are not positioned to deliver. The thread exists to hold that refusal in place for the reader while the other threads resolve around it.

## Thread 5: Hannah and Karl

- **Question**: What exists between Hannah and Karl across the war's divide, and what of it survives when the war re-sorts them?
- **Enters**: `03-allgau` — Karl speaks to another POW in the Allgäu dialect, the German of Hannah's father's kitchen; Hannah answers before she can stop herself; the connection is established through sound before it is established through any other fact
- **Advanced in**:
  - `06-the-lining` — Werner watches Karl work with a look Hannah reads as recognition and its cost; she understands that the connection is visible to her father even before she understands what the connection is; she hides the note
  - `09-the-road` — Karl trusts Hannah with what he saw on the coastal road; the bond is shown to be real and mutual; the conversation is conducted in German, in the Allgäu dialect, in a window of fifteen minutes before Oakes returns
  - `12-not-yet` — Hannah's choice to keep the note over the flame is also, without being named as such, a form of fidelity to what Karl gave her
  - `15-the-crew-moves` — the crew is reassigned; Hannah and Karl say "good luck to you" in English with Oakes watching; Karl does not look back from the truck; Hannah goes upstairs and reads the note alone
- **Resolves in**: Deliberately suspended at `15-the-crew-moves` — the human connection is severed; the note is all that remains of it; Hannah's thread ends here without resolution or reconciliation; Karl's fate is not established; we do not learn whether they meet again
- **What it's about underneath**: Hannah and Karl share a dialect — not German in general, but the specific vowel-shaping of the Allgäu, the German of a kitchen fifty kilometers from both their origins. What passes between them is not romance and not information-exchange and not collaboration in any official sense; it is recognition. The thread pressurizes what remains when two people find each other in a precise and personal way across a line that insists they are enemies, and then the administrative machinery of the war re-sorts them into their assigned categories. What Karl saw on the road, he told Hannah because she was the only person he could tell. The note is what language leaves behind when the speakers are separated.

**Coverage**: All 18 chapters advance at least one thread. No dead chapters.

**Distribution note**: The Marcus chapters (02, 05, 08, 11, 14, 17) each advance Thread 3 and occasionally Thread 4; their single-thread chapters (05, 08, 11, 14) are not dead — they advance the novel's most sustained accretion arc. The Eira chapters (01, 04, 07, 10, 13, 16, 18) always advance both Thread 1 and Thread 4, which is by design: Thread 4's question lives inside Thread 1's displacement object. The Hannah chapters (03, 06, 09, 12, 15) always advance both Thread 2 and Thread 5, with Thread 4 also advancing in the chapters where Werner's connection to the situation is in frame (06, 09).

**Resolution shape**:
- Thread 1 (Eira's Blank): **Resolved minimally** — the dock entry is written; Werner's name is absent
- Thread 2 (Karl's Note): **Deliberately unresolved** — the note is still in the lining; Hannah disappears from the novel
- Thread 3 (Josef's Name): **Resolved privately** — Marcus holds the complete picture; no official record acknowledges it
- Thread 4 (Werner on the Dock): **Deliberately refused** — the moral question is structurally unanswerable; the novel withholds it as argument
- Thread 5 (Hannah and Karl): **Suspended elliptically** — Karl is gone; Hannah is gone; the language they shared is in a piece of paper in a school bag
