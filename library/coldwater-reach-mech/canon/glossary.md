# Glossary — Coldwater Reach

Alphabetical. Expand as the draft proceeds.

---

**Atwood's** — The general store in Coldwater Reach. Keeps ration books and a ledger. One of the few communal gathering points in town.

**breakwater** — The granite seawall extending three hundred feet into the channel off Coldwater Reach harbor. The body washes up against its seaward face in October 1943. Also the site of the harbormaster's shack.

**Carvell Oakes** — Coastal Patrol guard assigned to the Pfeiffer farm POW work detail. Age 61, arthritic, carries a rifle he has not cleaned in months. Not cruel; not alert.

**Coldwater Reach** — The town; also the name of the channel the harbor sits on. A small Maine fishing and lobstering community, population approximately 400 in 1943.

**Crow, Henry** — School custodian at Coldwater Reach High School. Black, Maine-born, approximately 60. Marcus Klein's landlord. Discreet, observant, and kind.

**Crow, Lillian** — Henry Crow's wife. Runs the household. Knows what she has not been told.

**District Commander (Rockland)** — The naval/Coast Guard authority to whom the Coldwater Reach harbormaster reports unusual findings. Rockland is the nearest city with district-level authority, approximately 30 miles away.

**displacement object** — Term of art for this manuscript: the concrete physical object that performs a POV character's withholding in a given chapter. Each of the three threads has its own recurring objects; they are staggered and rhyme across the braid.

**Eastern Sea Frontier** — The U.S. Navy's Atlantic coastal command, responsible for waters off the Maine coast. Relevant to the breakwater body's possible origin and reporting chain.

**Geneva Convention** — The 1929 international agreement governing treatment of prisoners of war. Governs Karl Reinmar's legal status on the Pfeiffer farm: work conditions, pay rate, treatment standards.

**harbormaster's log** — The legal record Eira Bowman keeps of all vessel arrivals, departures, and unusual findings. Entries are in Eira's hand; Owen's earlier entries remain in the earlier pages. The log is Eira's primary displacement object.

**harbormaster's shack** — The weatherboard structure nailed to the breakwater pier. One oil lamp; the log on the desk.

**Karl Reinmar** — German POW, age 19. From a town approximately 50 km from the Pfeiffer family's Bavarian home village. Assigned to the Pfeiffer farm work crew.

**Klein, Dr. Marcus** — Teacher of German and Latin at Coldwater Reach High School. Age 47. Born Vienna; interned in Quebec; arrived Coldwater Reach September 1942.

**Murphett's** — The diner on the main street in Coldwater Reach. Closes at 2 p.m. due to wartime food shortages.

**Oakes, Carvell** — *See Carvell Oakes.*

**Pfeiffer, Marta** — Hannah's mother. Immigrant from Bavaria (arrived 1922). Copes through relentless performance of American normalcy.

**Pfeiffer, Werner** — Hannah's father. Immigrant from Bavaria (arrived 1922). Simultaneously ashamed of his German origins and privately attached to them; the war has made both impulses stronger.

**Pfeiffer farm** — Dairy farm three miles inland from Coldwater Reach on a gravel road. White clapboard farmhouse, unpainted barn. The German POW work crew operates here under Carvell Oakes.

**Prospect Street** — The street in Coldwater Reach where Henry and Lillian Crow's house stands, near the school.

**PW armband** — The arm marking worn by German prisoners of war in U.S. custody. "PW" (prisoner of war) in black letters on white background.

**ration book** — The government-issued booklet of rationing coupons required for purchasing sugar, meat, gasoline, and other controlled goods. Tracked at Atwood's.

**Reinmar, Karl** — *See Karl Reinmar.*

**V-Mail** — Victory Mail: the system by which letters to and from overseas servicemen were photographed onto microfilm, transported by air, and re-enlarged at the destination. The resulting letter is slightly smaller than the original, the ink faintly ghostly.

**Western Union boy** — The bicycle messenger who delivers casualty telegrams. His appearance on a road is a communal event; people go still.

**Tibbetts, Aldous** — Town constable of Coldwater Reach. Quiet, not given to speech when nothing is decided. Limited by jurisdiction. First words on seeing the breakwater body: 'Jesus.' Does not touch the body.

**Agnes Louise** — Vessel (probable Bellamy family) that came off her mooring in the October 1943 gale and went aground on the shingle at the channel's edge. Found aground by Eira on her October 3 dawn walk.

**Eira's cottage** — Eira Bowman's residence adjacent to the harbormaster's shack. Owen's coat hangs on the back of the door. Connected to the pier by a track.

**county examiner** — The official from the county seat responsible for receiving, documenting, and transporting bodies. Traveled to Coldwater Reach on October 3, 1943 at 1:30 p.m. to collect the breakwater body. Filed probable-drowning report.

**Bellamy family** — Local family, likely owners of the vessel Agnes Louise (provisional). Eira noted to contact them after the Agnes Louise went aground in the October 1943 gale.

**Dr. Marcus Klein** — German and Latin teacher at Coldwater Reach High School. Viennese Jewish refugee, age 47. Displaced via Paris, London, Quebec (Sherbrooke internment camp, 14 months under Canada's War Measures Act); placed in Coldwater Reach September 1942 by a Quaker relief organization. Third-POV character. Boards with Henry and Lillian Crow on Prospect Street. His displacement object: the water-damaged fragment. He thinks taxonomically; interiority names structure, not feeling.

**Josef Lutz** — German-Jewish refugee from Vienna, approximately 39–41 years old. Interned with Marcus at Sherbrooke, Quebec. Quiet, polyglot (four languages fluent; a fifth poorly), habit of watching doors. Released from Sherbrooke late 1942 or early 1943; had no valid Canadian papers, no US visa; was trying to reach a distant cousin in New York. His fate after release is unknown. His partial name — 'Lutz, J.' — appears in the water-damaged fragment from the breakwater body's clothing.

**Lieutenant Graves** — Young naval officer (approximately 25–26) from the Rockland district office. Brings the water-damaged fragment to Marcus in late October 1943 for translation. Leaves the fragment with Marcus for further examination. Not antagonistic; he is an errand. His phone number is with the school secretary.

**Henry Crow** — School custodian at Coldwater Reach High School. Black, Maine-born, approximately sixty. Marcus's landlord on Prospect Street. Observant and discreet; treats Marcus as a person. He asked no questions when Marcus arrived in 1942 and has asked none since.

**Lillian Crow** — Henry Crow's wife. Runs the Crow house on Prospect Street. Sharp, warm, observant. She knows things she has not been told. Does not perform not knowing them.

**The Crow house** — Two-story frame house on Prospect Street, Coldwater Reach, near the high school. Home of Henry and Lillian Crow; Marcus boards in the front room on the second story. Stairs creak on the third and seventh steps. The only place in town Marcus is treated as a guest rather than a specimen.

**Sherbrooke internment camp** — Fictional internment facility in Quebec, Canada, operating under Canada's War Measures Act during WWII. Held German Jews, German nationals, Italian fishermen, and others without strong administrative distinction. Marcus Klein was interned there for fourteen months (released late summer 1942). Josef Lutz was also interned there, released late 1942 or early 1943.

**The water-damaged fragment** — A piece of sodden paper (~3x2 inches, torn edges, ink mostly dissolved to brown shadow) recovered from the clothing of the breakwater body. Legible text: partial salutation arc; 'kalt' (cold); 'ankommen' (to arrive); and in the heading/address block: '...Lutz, J...' The rest is gone. Brought to Marcus by Lt. Graves for translation. Marcus recognized the name, said nothing of it, and kept the fragment in his desk's bottom drawer unreturned. Marcus's primary displacement object (Thread 3: Josef's Name).

**Allgäu** — Region of Bavaria in southern Germany, known for dairy farming; the home region of both the Pfeiffer family (near Kempten) and Karl Reinmar (near Marktoberdorf). The specific dialect spoken there — a vowel-rounding, consonant-softening form of Bavarian German — is the sound Hannah recognizes when Karl speaks to Brauer in ch03.

**Marktoberdorf** — Town in the Allgäu region of Bavaria; Karl Reinmar's origin. Approximately 50 kilometers from Kempten, the Pfeiffer family's home region.

**Kempten** — City in the Allgäu region of Bavaria; the region nearest the Pfeiffer family's village of origin. Werner and Marta Pfeiffer emigrated from near Kempten to Maine in 1922.

**Karl's note** — Hannah Pfeiffer's primary displacement object (Thread 2). A small piece of ruled workbook paper, folded four times, written in pencil by Karl Reinmar in Allgäu German. Content: his name and town; three sentences on his conscription (he had wanted to be a Schreiner); a line about the cattle reminding him of home; the closing two sentences about a man seen on the coastal road in rain in mid-October. Registered verbatim as record 'karls-note-text'.

**Brauer** — German POW on the Pfeiffer farm work crew. Older, wide-shouldered, rarely speaks. Present in the barn corridor during Karl and Hannah's first exchange in ch03; looked at the wall.

**Carvell Oakes** — Aging Coastal Patrol guard assigned to the Pfeiffer farm POW crew. 61, arthritic. Makes his patrol circuit approximately every 20–25 minutes; Hannah has mapped his patterns. His rifle strap is slack; he is not attentive.

**Pfeiffer dairy farm** — Werner and Marta Pfeiffer's dairy farm, three miles inland from Coldwater Reach on a gravel road. White clapboard farmhouse, unpainted barn. The POW work crew arrives by Army truck each weekday morning.

**Schreiner** — German: carpenter. Karl Reinmar's intended trade before his conscription. Hannah knows the word from her father's usage.

**Gell?** — Allgäu/Bavarian dialect particle; roughly equivalent to 'isn't it?' or 'right?' The word Hannah says involuntarily when she hears Karl speaking the Allgäu dialect in the barn corridor — the moment that opens Thread 5 (Hannah and Karl).

**Commander Horace Willard** — Commander, Naval District, Rockland, Maine. Approximately sixty. Efficient, respectful, procedural. Visited the Coldwater Reach harbormaster's shack in mid-November 1943 to take the formal report on the October 3 body; officially closed the case. Not an antagonist — he is the procedure.

**Unknown Man, October 1943** — The text of the cemetery stone for the unidentified body found on the Coldwater Reach breakwater. Official disposition established in ch04; interment in the Coldwater Reach town cemetery. Reproduces exactly in ch18.

**Coldwater Reach town cemetery** — Cemetery in Coldwater Reach, Maine. Interment site of the unidentified body from the breakwater; stone reads 'Unknown Man, October 1943.'

**body photograph** — Black-and-white photograph taken by the county photographer on October 3, 1943, showing the unidentified body on the seaward face of the breakwater. Produced by Commander Willard during his November 1943 visit. Eira was shown it then for the first time; she said she did not recognize the man.

**Simon Ehrlich** — German-Jewish refugee. Formerly at the Sherbrooke internment camp (8 months). Now in Montreal, Côte-des-Neiges neighborhood, running a refugee-aid clearinghouse under cover of a book distribution concern. Knew both Marcus Klein and Josef Lutz at Sherbrooke. Marcus's address for him is memorized, not written down. First appears in ch05; will confirm Josef Lutz's identity in ch08.

**Unsent letter to Ehrlich** — A folded letter Marcus wrote in ch05 — second draft, first burned. Disguised as an academic inquiry about German-language classroom texts, with a personal aside asking after Josef Lutz. Not addressed, not sent. Kept in the bottom drawer of Marcus's desk at the Crow house, beside the fragment. Marcus's displacement object for ch05 (combined with the fragment).

**Marcus's magnifying glass** — Small German-made lens with a brass casing worn smooth along the thumb-rest. Carried in Marcus's left coat pocket since Vienna, through Paris, London, Quebec, and Coldwater Reach. A scholar's tool. Used in ch05 to examine the fragment under oblique lamplight, revealing date fragment, city fragment, and the phrase *nicht mehr warten*.

**nicht mehr warten** — German phrase (can wait no longer). Found near the bottom of the water-damaged fragment in ch05; ink smeared before drying. Establishes that the writer was in a state of urgency at the time of writing — no longer able to wait for whatever they had been waiting for.

**Josefsgasse** — Address in Vienna of the archive where Josef Lutz was training as an archivist before he had to stop. Josef described it to Marcus at Sherbrooke: provenance-organized card catalog, leather-bound books in the basement stacks with a particular smell of damp. A marker of Josef's professional identity and of what he left behind.

**Wilder's Landing** — A named point on the coastal road between Bangor and Coldwater Reach, Maine (fictional). The POW transport from Bangor passes it heading south into town. Hannah traces Karl's road-sighting geographically through this point toward the harbor.

**Hannah's school bag** — Old canvas bag, tan, worn strap — Hannah's school bag from before farm labor took her away from school. As of ch06, Karl's note is hidden in its inside bottom seam, stitched closed with black thread. The bag hangs on the hook by the Pfeiffer farmhouse stairs.

**Jess Atwood** — Minor character — member of the Atwood family (Atwood's general store). Hannah overheard her at the counter in October 1943 relaying Constable Tibbetts's view that the breakwater body had come from inland, based on the smooth-soled shoes.

**American flag placemats** — Flag placemats with a small gold eagle in the corner, ordered by Marta Pfeiffer from Atwood's in spring 1943. She sets them out at each table place every evening — an act Hannah reads as effort at American normalcy.

**Jared Cates** — Lobsterman, Coldwater Reach; owner of an approximately 34-foot vessel. Appears in Owen Bowman's November 19, 1933 harbor log: logged for running an unlicensed vessel outside his licensed grounds during a nor'easter. Reported to Rockland; received a formal warning and lost one week's hauling rights. Accepted the penalty without appeal. He does not appear in the main narrative — he is a figure in the record, used by Eira to measure Owen's standard of logging against her own.

**Old Harbor Log Volume** — The harbor log volume started February 1941, closed early November 1943 when the last column filled. Contains Owen Bowman's entries from the 1930s (including November 1933) and Eira's from 1936 onward, including the October 1943 blank. Stored on the shelf below the log stand in the harbormaster's shack. In January 1944, Eira attempted to write a corrective entry at October 2 2300, stopped, crossed it out (single line, initialed E.B.), and wrote a correction notice and ice report below — the crossed-out attempt is now permanently in the record.

**Number-Two Marker** — Channel marker in Coldwater Reach harbor; five seasons old at the time of the January 1944 chapter. Susceptible to ice pressure during hard freezes; requires overnight monitoring. Bell rings when ice shifts against it. Holds through the January 1944 overnight ice watch.

**Ehrlich's Montreal letter (February 1944)** — Simon Ehrlich's coded reply to Marcus Klein, postmarked Montreal P.Q., February 26, 1944. Written in English on commercial stationery, using an academic-inquiry cover (Diesterweg readers, Langenscheidt series). Confirms Josef Lutz's November 1942 release from Sherbrooke, his departure from Montreal in early September 1943, Ehrlich's assistance with 'certain practical details,' and that Ehrlich's network expected word by November 1943 and received none. Closing: 'I had hoped he had simply lost touch.' Canonical text registered as record 'ehrlich-letter-feb1944'. Currently held in the bottom drawer of Marcus's desk with the fragment, under the manila folder.

**Diesterweg graded readers** — German-language pedagogical readers published through the late 1930s, named in Ehrlich's letter as the cover for Marcus's academic inquiry. Used as fictional academic content to disguise the letter's real purpose.

**Sherbrooke lending arrangement** — Informal system by which B-block inmates at the Sherbrooke internment camp circulated reading material — tracked in a notebook, distributed and returned. Josef Lutz participated actively, borrowing all available German-language texts.

**Wilder's Landing** — Fictional named point on the coastal road (Küstenstraße) between Bangor and Coldwater Reach, Maine. A wide curve where white pines overhang the verge; the POW transport from Bangor passed through it heading south. Karl Reinmar saw the man on the road here on an October night in 1943 — the last known sighting of the breakwater body alive.

**Küstenstraße** — Hannah Pfeiffer's German word for the coastal road between Bangor and Coldwater Reach. Used in her written account on the reverse of Karl's note.

**Two-sided note (Karl's note, second state)** — After ch09, Karl's note is a two-sided document: Karl Reinmar's original writing (November 1943) on one side; Hannah Pfeiffer's written transcription of Karl's oral testimony (April 1944) on the other side. Both sides in pencil, both in German. It is the only known written account of the last sighting of the breakwater body.

**Rockland Follow-Up Inquiry Form (CLD-43-081)** — Boilerplate form from the Office of the District Commander, First Naval District, Rockland, Maine, sent to all parties who filed reports on the October 1943 Coldwater Reach case. One page; asks for date, location, cause of death, and any new witnesses/evidence/information. Eira received it April 6, 1944; mailed it April 13 with 'None' in the last section. Due April 15, 1944. The second official document in which she has affirmed no new information.

**Mrs. Welliver** — Postmistress of the Coldwater Reach post office on the main street (brick building, two doors past the Congregational church). Appears in ch10 when Eira drops the Rockland inquiry form for the outgoing mail.

**CLD-43-081** — Case number assigned by Rockland District to the October 1943 Coldwater Reach unidentified body case (CLD = Coldwater, 43 = 1943, 081 = sequential case number). Referenced on the follow-up inquiry form and in Eira's log reference column.

**Crow table** — The dining table in the Crow house at Prospect Street — the setting for the ch11 disclosure scene. Four chairs; west window; sideboard with trivet. Marcus has eaten here for approximately twenty months by May 1944.

**Carroll** — A student in Marcus Klein's second-year German class at Coldwater Reach High School (named ch11). Has difficulty with subordinate clause constructions in extended writing; composed an essay on 'eine Reise' containing a sequence-of-tenses error (Zeitenfolge).

**Dunbar (neighbor)** — A Prospect Street neighbor three houses up from the Crow house (named ch11). Mrs. Dunbar traded a non-laying hen to Lillian Crow in May 1944, providing the chicken for the dinner scene.

**Elsa (Heger side)** — Hannah Pfeiffer's cousin, on the Heger side of the family; lives in Vermont; receives occasional ordinary letters from Hannah. She writes back in careful English. First named in ch12.

**Werner's barn coat** — Werner Pfeiffer's old barn coat, hung in Hannah's bedroom closet. He keeps pipe tobacco in its pockets to avoid Marta's disapproval. His search of this coat in June 1944 brings his boot within inches of the school bag and the hidden note — the chapter's near-discovery event.

**Hanna (German form)** — Werner Pfeiffer's private name for his daughter Hannah — the German form, without the English H at the end. He uses it without thinking inside the house; it is the language of the family and the same register as Karl's note.

**Atwood's General Store** — The general store on Coldwater Reach's main street. Handles ration books, kerosene orders, dry goods, and twine. Run by Atwood's son since March 1944 when the senior Atwood's hip gave out. Rationing tickets kept in a ledger under the counter.

**HELEN J.** — Trap boat (lobster), owner Noss, home port Coldwater Reach. First logged August 12, 1944.

**REENIE D.** — Trap boat (lobster), owner Farrar, home port Coldwater Reach. First logged August 12, 1944. Farrar reported fouled ground gear on the eastern ledge.

**Noss** — Coldwater Reach lobsterman; owner of trap boat HELEN J.

**Farrar** — Coldwater Reach lobsterman; owner of trap boat REENIE D.; reported fouled buoy on the eastern ledge (approximately 46°22'N) in August 1944.

**Form DD-4** — Federal POW labor requisition form authorizing German prisoners to work on farms under Coastal Patrol guard. Eira filed the Pfeiffer farm's DD-4 authorization in January 1944.

**working certainty** — Eira's term (implicit) for the kind of identification she makes from posture and proportion in poor conditions — not forensic certainty, not guessing; the methodology she uses to identify vessels by waterline or running light pattern before reading the name board. She applies it to Werner Pfeiffer in ch13.

**Phyllis Bowen** — Senior student in Marcus Klein's fourth-period Latin class at Coldwater Reach High School. Working through Vergil's Georgics in October 1944. Introduced in ch14.

**Letter to Lutz Sister (letter-to-lutz-sister)** — Marcus Klein's fourth-draft letter to Josef Lutz's unnamed sister, sent October 3, 1944 from the Prospect Street mailbox. Signed 'Ein Freund aus Sherbrooke.' Registered verbatim record. Later chapters must read_record before re-quoting.

**Lutz's Sister** — Josef Lutz's unnamed sister; last-known address in Zürich as of 1944; name known to Marcus from two occasions at Sherbrooke camp. Her fate and whether the letter reached her is deliberately unresolved.

**Ein Freund aus Sherbrooke** — German: 'A friend from Sherbrooke.' The signature Marcus uses on the letter to Josef Lutz's sister — identifying himself to anyone who knew the Quebec internment camp system without naming himself.

**Steuben** — Real coastal Maine town; site of the cannery to which the Pfeiffer farm POW crew is reassigned in late October 1944 for the fall/winter run. Seasonal canneries operated in Steuben historically.

**Braunvieh** — A breed of cattle native to the Alpine/Allgäu region of Bavaria and Switzerland; Karl Reinmar mentions them in his final exchange with Hannah as a point of comparison with the Pfeiffer herd. Slightly smaller and more delicate than the Pfeiffer farm stock.

**Er schien einen langen Weg vor sich zu haben** — German: 'He seemed to have a long way ahead of him.' The last line of Karl's side of the two-sided note, referring to the man Karl saw on the coastal road in October 1943. Repeated as the final line of chapter 15 and of Hannah's thread in the novel.

**Bewegte sich Richtung Wasser. Sah sich um. Lief weiter.** — German: 'Moved toward the water. Looked around. Walked on.' Hannah's transcription of Karl's road-sighting testimony, on the reverse side of Karl's note. The documentary core of the only eyewitness account of Josef Lutz's last hours.

**Log shelf (harbormaster's shack)** — Cedar plank shelf on iron brackets, built by Owen Bowman in 1933, running the length of the back wall of the harbormaster's shack. Holds the complete series of harbor log volumes in chronological order: Owen's 1933 volume at the left end through the current active volume at the right. Harbor record runs back to 1893.

**Eastern ledge channel marker** — Navigational aid at the eastern ledge of Coldwater Reach harbor. Maintained by Eira. Developed a slight list to the northwest in November 1944 from the mooring cable working in the current; appeared correct as of January 1945 from the breakwater end.

**Harbor log (old volume)** — The log volume covering November 1942 through December 1944, retired to the shelf on January 14, 1945. Contains the October 2, 1943 page with its original 2300 blank, the January 1944 crossed-out attempt, the correction notice, the ice report, and the January 1945 partial correction entry (weather only).

**Deacon Pratt** — Deacon at the Coldwater Reach Congregational Church. Part of Henry Crow's informal African American and working-class community information network. Learned of Werner Pfeiffer's sheltering of Josef Lutz from Edna Wills and passed it to Henry Crow at a men's meeting in February 1945.

**Edna Wills** — Laundress for several farm families out past Route 1. Heard the account of a foreign man (Josef Lutz) spending a night at the Pfeiffer farm from a woman at the farm in December or January 1944/45. Passed the account to Deacon Pratt.

**Marcus's Reconstruction Notebook** — Marcus Klein's private German-language working document, a narrow exercise book in small compressed hand, begun February 1944 after Ehrlich's Montreal reply confirmed Josef Lutz's identity. Contains 25 pages of entries reconstructing Josef's journey from Sherbrooke to Coldwater Reach. Final entry written March 24, 1945, names Werner Pfeiffer. Kept at the back of the bottom drawer of Marcus's desk at the Crow house. Never shown to anyone.

**Annalisa K.** — Lobster vessel belonging to the McCready family, operating out of Coldwater Reach harbor. Appears in ch18 as the morning's first vessel departure (0430 for the grounds). The McCready family has operated it for at least fifteen years before ch18.

**McCready family** — Coldwater Reach fishing family; operate the lobster vessel Annalisa K. and a relief boat. The McCready brothers work the pre-dawn lobster run. Referenced in ch18 (the old volume also mentions 'the McCready relief boat').
