# Characters — Coldwater Reach

## POV Characters

---

### Eira Bowman — *harbormaster, primary POV*

**Age**: 52  
**Role**: Harbormaster of Coldwater Reach; the only woman in this post on the Maine coast  
**Background**: Married Owen Bowman, previous harbormaster, for twenty-two years. Owen drowned in the November storm of 1935. Eira took the post the following January because no one else would, and has held it for eight years. She knows this harbor more precisely than anyone alive.

**Drives**: Order and accountability — the log must be accurate, the channel must be marked, the anomaly must be logged. But in October 1943 she knows something she cannot put in the log. Her arc is built around the question of what a person of absolute procedural integrity does when the procedure itself becomes dangerous.

**Voice**: Spare, declarative, procedural. She observes the world in the vocabulary of her work — tide, draft, load, weather, heading, bearing. Even grief came to her that way: when Owen died she could not find words for it except that the mooring held and the mooring held and then it did not. No metaphors for the sea; it is a workplace. Interiority is short and factual.

**Displacement object**: The harbor log — specifically the entries she writes, leaves blank, or writes over. What the log records and what it omits is Eira's primary act of will. A single altered entry is a consequential act.

**Relationships**: Widowed; no children. Respectful professional relations with the District Commander at Rockland. Known in town as dependable, unreachable, and right. Does not board at anyone's house; she lives in the cottage adjacent to the shack.

---

### Hannah Pfeiffer — *farm daughter, second POV*

**Age**: 17  
**Role**: Daughter of Bavarian immigrant dairy farmers; student at Coldwater Reach High School in theory, increasingly absent in practice as farm labor demands increase  
**Background**: Parents, Werner and Marta Pfeiffer, emigrated from Bavaria in 1922. They have not been easy since the war started — Werner is suspicious and ashamed by turns; Marta performs American normalcy too hard. Hannah was born in Maine, has American English and her father's German in roughly equal measure. German is the language of private things: argument, prayer, the names of foods, endearments.

**Drives**: Hannah is the most open of the three — she has not yet learned the full discipline of concealment. She is drawn to Karl not out of naivety but out of recognition: he is from fifty kilometers from her father's birthplace, and his German is the German she grew up hearing. What passes between them is not quite romance and not quite espionage and not quite friendship; it is a form of language she does not have anywhere else.

**Voice**: More interior than Eira's, more associative. German phrases surface where English fails her — not italicized exoticism but the natural grain of a bilingual consciousness. The most porous POV: she feels the mood of a room before she can name it. Sentences run a little longer and more connected than Eira's; they are less finished.

**Displacement object**: An object received from Karl — what it is, and what happens to it, is one of the braid's slow reveals. She keeps it, hides it, returns to it; its meaning accretes across the novel.

**Relationships**: Karl Reinmar (POW, complicated); Werner Pfeiffer (father, complicated loyalty); Marta Pfeiffer (mother, exhausting performance of normalcy); Carvell Oakes (the Coastal Patrol guard — she has learned to read his schedule).

---

### Dr. Marcus Klein — *displaced scholar, third POV*

**Age**: 47  
**Role**: Teacher of German and Latin at Coldwater Reach High School; resident alien; former internee  
**Background**: Born Vienna; fled in the late 1930s by way of Paris, then London, then Quebec — where he spent fourteen months in an internment camp under the War Measures Act, along with German Jews, German nationals, and Italian fishermen, sorted together without much distinction. He does not discuss the camp. He arrived in Coldwater Reach in September 1942 through a faculty placement arranged by a Quaker relief organization; his credentials were real but his references were two dead men and a dissolved institution.

**Drives**: Marcus is trying to be invisible in a way that does not cost him his integrity. He has survived by being useful — the language teacher, the translator, the man you bring your unusual problem to. His arc is what happens when being useful pulls him into a situation where usefulness itself becomes compromising.

**Voice**: Precise, slightly formal, measured. The displaced scholar weighing every word for what it betrays. He speaks and thinks in complete sentences. He is wary of his own expertise because expertise draws attention. His interiority is analytical — he thinks in analogies, taxonomy, the structure of things — but the analysis is often a way of not feeling what he cannot afford to feel.

**Displacement object**: A document, photograph, or letter he cannot put anywhere safe — cannot file it, cannot destroy it, cannot return it. What it is and where it came from is the mystery Marcus carries. It arrives early; what it reveals comes late.

**Relationships**: Henry Crow (landlord, custodian — the man who asked no questions when Marcus arrived and has asked none since; the steadiest relationship Marcus has); Lillian Crow (Henry's wife — sharp, warm, observant; she feeds Marcus and declines to perform ignorance of his situation); the students (he is careful with them; they are his cover and his reason).

## Supporting Characters

### Owen Bowman *(deceased)*
Eira's husband, previous harbormaster. Drowned in the November storm of 1935. He is present throughout the novel in the gap he left — in the log's earlier entries in his handwriting, in the equipment he chose, in Eira's procedural habits formed alongside his.

### Karl Reinmar
Age 19. German POW. Assigned to the Pfeiffer farm work crew. Conscripted out of a small Bavarian town roughly fifty kilometers from the Pfeiffers' home village. Not an ideologue; he had no more choice than Hannah's dead uncles did. He speaks careful English and excellent German. He is careful with Hannah in a way that comes either from decency or from fear, and Hannah cannot always tell which.

### Henry Crow
School custodian; Marcus's landlord. Black, Maine-born, possibly sixty. He is observant and discreet with the economy of someone who has spent a lifetime reading rooms accurately and not speaking it. He treats Marcus as a paying guest who happens to be a refugee and happens to be Jewish — which is to say: as a person. This makes the Crow house the only safe space Marcus has.

### Lillian Crow
Henry's wife. Runs the house. She knows things she has not been told. She does not perform not knowing them.

### Carvell Oakes
The aging Coastal Patrol guard assigned to the Pfeiffer farm. Sixty-one, arthritic, carries a rifle he has not cleaned in months. Not cruel; not alert. Hannah has mapped his patterns.

### Werner Pfeiffer
Hannah's father. Bavarian immigrant, dairy farmer, in his mid-fifties. The war has made him simultaneously more German (in grief, in private) and more desperately American (in public). He does not know what he feels about the POW crew. He does not ask.

### Marta Pfeiffer
Hannah's mother. Her coping strategy is the relentless performance of American normalcy — the right flag, the right rhetoric, the right expressions of patriotism. It costs her something the novel can see even if she cannot.
