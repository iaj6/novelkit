# Pitch — Coldwater Reach

A small Maine fishing town called Coldwater Reach, autumn 1943 to spring 1945. Three lives are braided around a single unsolved question: the body of an unidentified man that washes in off the breakwater after a three-day blow. Eira Bowman, the harbormaster, knows what she saw on the dock the night before he came in. Hannah Pfeiffer, seventeen, on her family's inland dairy farm, begins receiving what passes between her and the young German POW working the fields. Dr. Marcus Klein, a Viennese displaced scholar boarding with the school custodian's family, finds documents he cannot put anywhere safe. None of the three tells what they know. None of them plans to carry it. None of them sets it down before the war ends. The novel assembles the body's story across the braid, but the deeper question is what each of the three does with their piece of it — and what keeping it costs them, over the long duration of a war that does not show up in the plot, only in the radio, the letters, the missing men, and the silences between neighbors.
