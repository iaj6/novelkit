
## 01-the-strong-room
**Ending mode:** declarative-close
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Set-off document quotation as scene event: R1 and R2 stand as indented blockquotes; surrounding prose resumes at the same narrative level with no gloss sentence.
- Physical anchor substituting for summary: Garside's hat still on as proof of recent arrival — inference given through observed detail, never stated.
- Register entry in italics as a distinct visual and tonal register within the chapter, differentiated from both prose narration and blockquoted documents.
- Displacement of discovery onto the ledger: Prescott reading the September entry and finding his own initials (A.P.) performs the book's Garside-grief thread in its earliest form without naming it.
- Short declarative 'No codicil.' as its own paragraph — the chapter's weight-moment, landed by isolation and brevity after a list.
- Period-legal vocabulary without gloss throughout: 'substitutional form,' 'letters of administration with the will annexed,' 'Section thirty-three of the Wills Act,' 'engrossed' — used as furniture of the world, not as explained terms.
**Recurring constructions:**
- `the file was in order`
- `instrument read entire`
**POV register:**
- **arthur-prescott:** Dry, exact, professionally restrained from first sentence. Interior life surfaces through attention: he notices what Garside has done (the hat, the pre-pulled box) rather than responding to it. One remembered image of Walter (arms at his sides, listening) is a single sentence with no elaboration. The closing paragraph's weight comes from restraint in everything before it.
**Craft notes:** The chapter's sole texture-risk was the Garside executor-point speech: the drafter must note 'or her issue' as the professionally correct form without flagging the 'or/and' distinction that is the book's engine. Solution: Garside explains the s.33 problem (she is collateral, not a descendant, so the substitutional form is required) without ever invoking its contrast with 'and.' The fair-play plant lives entirely in R2's printed text, unmarked. The chapter is deliberately compact (~1,150 words) because the scene's work is procedural: two documents read, two records checked, two doors cracked open. The ending declarative-close signals the book's rhythm: doors closed are the chapter's payoff, and the file's order is not comfort — it is what the case has so far.

## 02-the-second-codicil
**Ending mode:** elliptical
**Opening texture:** routine-procedure
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Two-scene structure separated by a horizontal rule — the letter reading and the attendance; each has its own energy without a stated transition.
- R3 read aloud by Garside as set-off passage, no gloss following: the document ends and Prescott names only the date, not any word of the text.
- R5 introduced via the letter-book carbon as a routine file-check moment, not highlighted — its later significance (gave Walter the date) is planted here unremarked.
- Walter's misquotation embedded mid-dialogue in a subordinate clause whose topic is Josiah's intentions, not the codicil's language: 'the arrangement for Margaret and her issue wouldn't stand as he'd left it.'
- Post-Walter scene beat — Prescott re-reads the letter-book alone, confirming by procedure what has been said: letter-book as silent corroborator of what did and did not leave the office.
- Elliptical close on a physical image (the light already gone) rather than a named question: the open state is shown, not told.
**Recurring constructions:**
- `nothing beyond it`
- `That is all we sent`
**POV register:**
- **arthur-prescott:** Dry and procedural throughout; his suspicion of the second codicil is visible only in what he attends to (the timeline, the witnesses' addresses, the letter-book), never in interior statement. The one moment of interiority is displaced onto the file's contents in the closing paragraph. No evaluation of Walter's character beyond external description: 'thoroughly certain about all of it.'
**Craft notes:** The chapter's main technical challenge was landing Walter's misquotation without flagging it — solved by burying it inside a clause whose grammatical subject is Josiah's intentions, not the codicil's wording, so a first-time reader hears a statement of motive while the attentive reader sees the planted error. R5 appears as letter-book routine rather than evidence-checking, which is correct for Prescott at this stage (he hasn't yet reasoned about what the date means to Walter). The ending deliberately avoids the declarative-close of Ch 1: same evening-departure beat but without any statement about what the file contains, ending instead on 'the light was already gone' — an image that holds the open question without naming it. Two-consecutive routine-procedure openings noted; Ch 3 should vary the opening texture.

## 03-the-habit
**Ending mode:** coda
**Opening texture:** observation
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Observation opening on a physical artifact (the index) before the work begins — Prescott registering the scope of the object rather than mid-task; deliberate break from consecutive routine-procedure openings in Ch 1 and Ch 2.
- Set-off attendance note as document event: Garside's 1924 note stands as an indented blockquote; the prose after resumes without gloss, per the document-insert convention.
- Displacement onto professional practice: Josiah's April 1924 thank-you letter filed in a small folded card rather than flat in the packet — 'The file was well kept' is the full interior sentence; no elaboration.
- Garside's single dialogue line ('Josiah was not a man who did things unofficially') carrying the chapter's finding in the tone of a fact about a person, not an argument about a case — the weight delivered by register, not by weight of words.
- Temporal compression in the coda: the afternoon light failing at the windows and the index lying at the top of the file establish end-of-day without a stated ellipsis; the last three sentences are the single discovery restated at decreasing register (file, working note, Booth's tray).
**Recurring constructions:**
- `in the same hand as`
- `Garside's hand from`
**POV register:**
- **arthur-prescott:** Dry, exact, fully impersonal in the review passages — the correspondence described as 'one legal purpose per letter' without evaluation of its content. One interior sentence ('The file was well kept') performs the Garside-grief thread through displacement; no second sentence follows it. The report to Garside is spare and sequential: three facts, no opinion. Prescott's suspicion of Walter never stated, only implied by the thoroughness of the review.
**Craft notes:** The chapter's central challenge was pacing a document-review scene so that the review feels like discovery rather than inventory. Solution: each document grouping closes with a negative finding ('nothing to suggest...') that itself narrows the second codicil's cover story, so the absence of private drafting evidence is the active event. The attendance note quote is the chapter's one set-off passage (R1-R3 not re-printed per the outline's continuity note), and 'Or her issue: collateral; substitutional form required' in Garside's own hand plants the corroborating exhibit without narration calling attention to it. No calendar countdown appears (days to hearing not yet set per outline) — the first chapter without it; subsequent chapters must restore the calendar pressure. The coda's last two sentences ('The working note said that. The file would say it.') are the book's compounding-mechanism rhythm stated plainly: what is written does not forget.

## 04-bank-parade
**Ending mode:** coda
**Opening texture:** transit
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Transit opening placing Prescott in movement through Preston geography — Fishergate, Avenham ridge, the Ribble valley — before the professional encounter; breaks the routine-procedure run from Ch 1–2 and the observation opening of Ch 3.
- Explanation-as-indirect-discourse: Prescott's explanation of the action is rendered in third-person paraphrase rather than quoted dialogue ('He said: a second codicil...'), preserving the professional register without breaking into formal speech tags.
- Displacement of the visit's emotional weight onto the physical detail of the schoolmaster's hall (exercise books, Latin primer, ranks of coats) — interiority by inventory, not by statement.
- Josiah's letter to Margaret as set-off italic passage — document event, no gloss; prose before it ends with 'Prescott opened it' and resumes after with a held pause ('He read it once and then held it in his hands a moment'), performed rather than named.
- Coda structure split into three visual sections: the professional visit, the walk back, the return to the office — each separated by a horizontal rule; the emotional close lives in the middle section (the heron, the upstairs lamp), not the last.
- Plain-sentence displacement close: 'The file had always had an address in it — Bank Parade, Preston — but it had been a legal address.' — the interior beat is shown through what the address has become, not through what Prescott feels about it.
**Recurring constructions:**
- (none)
**POV register:**
- **arthur-prescott:** Dry and procedural throughout, but fractionally warmer in register than Ch 1–3 — the warmth lives in the sentences about the Fairhurst household ('getting on with things for a year and a half since something necessary to them was taken away') and is immediately returned to professional framing. One interior displacement sentence at the close; no follow-up sentence elaborating it. No meta-commentary on cognition. Dialogue tags spare throughout: 'he said,' 'Fairhurst said,' 'she said.' Nora's composure is described in a third-person observation sentence that is close enough to focalised interiority to do the work without naming Prescott's feeling about it.
**Craft notes:** This is the human-stake chapter — the moment the file gets faces. The technical challenge was giving Nora a genuine voice (the outline demands she have opinions and not be decorative) without giving her lines that reveal anything she would not plausibly know or say. Her two questions — what was our mother to have had, and how long will it take — are precisely what a nineteen-year-old in her position would ask, and they serve the reader as much as the character: they establish the estate's composition and the timeline without Prescott volunteering a speech. Josiah's letter is the chapter's single document event; its key phrase ('I did not act in a soft mood that I expected to revise') is planted without annotation against R4's claim, which is not yet in the record. The coda's three-section structure (parlour / walk / office) is deliberate: the emotional resonance lives in the middle section, the George-at-his-desk inference (the upstairs lamp) carrying more weight than a stated reflection would. The second consecutive coda ending is noted; Ch 5 must use a different mode.

## 05-the-hinge
**Ending mode:** turn
**Opening texture:** arrival
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Arrival-into-crisis opening: Prescott returns from an off-page errand (court) and enters a space already changed — the discovery is managed by what Booth is doing (standing in the passage), not by action or dialogue from Prescott.
- Plain-sentence weight without gloss: 'He was thirty-eight. He had worked beside this man for sixteen years.' — two declaratives, no follow-up, no elaboration; the weight is produced by the restraint of everything around them.
- Strong-room register as set-off italicised passage: the entry is rendered as a period-ledger document, visually distinct, with no prose annotation before or after it explaining what the entry means.
- Garside-displacement through professional observation: 'Instrument read entire, including date. That phrase was not in any requirement of law or practice. It was Garside's.' — the grief is entirely in the noticing of what Garside chose to write, not in any direct feeling.
- Turn ending on a plain number: 'Two hundred and ninety days.' used twice — once as the arithmetic conclusion, once as the final line the chapter ends on; the structural hinge of the novel stated as a count, not a sentiment.
- Compression of the days-following into one paragraph by making the absence of change (Mr. Booth's habits unchanged) carry the weight of 'the firm continues' — no scene dramatises the aftermath; the elision is the affect.
**Recurring constructions:**
- (none)
**POV register:**
- **arthur-prescott:** Driest register of any chapter so far — no interior sentences about Garside except the two-declarative weight beat and the displacement beat on the register entry. Prescott's professional vocabulary does all the emotional work: 'the pen lay on the page, still uncapped' and 'Instrument read entire, including date. It was Garside's.' No meta-commentary; no naming of grief. The register-reading section is almost entirely external description (what the book looks like, what the entry says), with interiority only surfacing in the observation of Garside's standard being 'not the level a register strictly required, but the level of a man who did not distinguish between what required a record and what deserved one.'
**Craft notes:** The central challenge was the Garside-death scene: the brief requires brevity and restraint, but the chapter is also the structural hinge of the novel (Thread 2 fully enters here). The solution was compression into two paragraphs then a plain weight-sentence, with no scene inside the death itself — the uncapped pen and the unfinished annotation carry the displacement, not any description of Garside's condition. The register-reading section carries the chapter's real weight and required the Garside standard to be described in its own terms (what the register invariably includes) before the September entry appears as a set-off passage, so the entry reads as an instance of that standard rather than as a piece of evidence — Prescott is not yet reading for evidence; he is reading to know what he has. The repeated 'Two hundred and ninety days' at the close was deliberate: first as the count he arrives at, then as the thing he takes with him into the December dark, the turn made literal by carrying the number out of the office. Ch 6 must continue the countdown monotonically from below 290.

## 08-the-gazette
**Ending mode:** resolved-beat
**Opening texture:** dialogue
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Dialogue-led opening: Booth presents the list and proposes additions before Prescott speaks — the reader arrives as an institutional exchange is already underway, establishing Booth's institutional authority without commentary.
- Montage-accumulation structure: replies rendered in brief clusters (four then six; by third week thirty-one) so the passage of weeks is felt through rhythm rather than narrated — the slowing rate of replies in the third week is structural, not described.
- Booth's punctuation correction to the Gazette notice: a single small moment that characterises Booth (precision, quiet competence) without any attributed feeling — done in one sentence.
- Absence-as-active-event: the chapter's substance is what did not arrive — no exception firm, no Gazette reply, no invisible solicitor — made active by rendering the absence in each section as an affirmative finding rather than a non-finding.
- The exception Prescott was looking for, named and not found: 'the firm that answered with care, saying it had a file and that the matter was not without complication' — naming the exception by its absence gives the negative substance.
- Light Garside displacement embedded in the final reflection: 'the standard Prescott had grown up in, which supposed that you could not always know, at the time you were building the record, exactly what the record would later be required to show' — one clause, not a paragraph; the Thread 2 beat is present but not heavy.
- Two-sentence close as resolved-beat: 'The profession had not done this. The door was locked.' — short declaratives after a longer analytical paragraph; weight produced by the contrast and the stop.
**Recurring constructions:**
- (none)
**POV register:**
- **arthur-prescott:** Driest register of any chapter since Ch 6 — the montage sections are almost entirely external description (dates, volumes, presence or absence of content in replies) with interiority surfacing only twice: the named-exception passage ('He was looking, throughout, for the exception') and the final reflection on the profession's standard. No meta-commentary on cognition; no named feeling. Prescott's professional vocabulary does all the interior work: 'entirely immaterial,' 'improper form,' 'record item of a different sort.' The displacement observation is one clause in one sentence, not a separate paragraph, keeping it lighter than Ch 7's heavy beat.
**Craft notes:** The chapter's central problem was making a purely institutional negative — forty-six letters sent, forty-six replies received, all of them empty — register as a chapter with substance rather than a chapter of absences. The solution was threefold: naming the exception Prescott was seeking (so the absence of that exception is felt as a specific non-arrival, not a diffuse nothing); using Booth's punctuation correction and his Clitheroe-held-back reveal as two moments of institutional texture that carry his character without authorial assistance; and making the final reflection do the foreclosure work explicitly ('the cover story required... there was no such solicitor') so the resolved-beat landing is earned rather than stated. Ch 9 must open differently from dialogue and should avoid resolved-beat ending to prevent two-chapter repetition. The light Garside displacement in the final reflection must not be revisited at the same intensity before Ch 17.

## 09-vale-mill
**Ending mode:** literary-fade
**Opening texture:** transit
**Heavy stylistic moves:**
- Transit-as-context: the September deed-box journey recalled in one paragraph as displacement, anchored in physical specifics (the box, the drawing room, Garside's voice), giving 'He had not been attending to the right thing' its weight without naming what the right thing was.
- Positive observation replacing the accumulating-negative: where Ch 06-08 built from absences, Ch 09 builds from presences — the machine is there, the daybook entry is there, Walter is there; each is a finding rather than a foreclosure.
- Pocket-book as the record-within-narration: Prescott writes his observational note twice (typewriter, daybook) in a form that signals the record being built in real time; the two entries vary in their interiority — the typewriter entry is pure description, the daybook entry has the suppressed inference sentence immediately following it.
- Suppressed inference as characterisation: 'The second codicil was dated the fourteenth of August 1926. Three months after the old man had last been in this building. He did not write that second thought. The pocket-book was a record, not a commentary.' — Prescott's professional discipline shown as the act of not writing, not as stated restraint.
- Walter-glimpse as moral counterweight: Walter is observed without conversation, described through physical competence and institutional ease, closing on two men crouching over a shared problem — the mill is humanised; Walter is humanised; the narrative offers no editorial frame.
- Literary-fade close through layered sensory recession: the looms audible, then the light failing, then the mill gone behind a fold — three stages of withdrawal before 'He drove west'; the scene ends in motion without resolution.
**Recurring constructions:**
- `the right thing`
**POV register:**
- **arthur-prescott:** Most observational chapter to date — Prescott almost entirely in external perception mode, with two brief interior moments: the September memory (displacement, sensory-anchored) and the suppressed daybook inference ('He did not write that second thought'). The professional vocabulary is present throughout (grant of representation, fixed charges, administrator, mill daybook) but foregrounded in the counting-house scenes rather than in the transit and exterior passages, where the prose becomes more directly sensory. No meta-commentary on cognition; the two pocket-book entries are the chapter's version of interiority — what Prescott attends to is shown through what he records.
**Craft notes:** The chapter's central challenge was being observation-heavy without becoming description-heavy: every physical detail needed to be evidence (the silent weaving shed = short time = economic pressure; the typewriter = the machine thread; the daybook = Josiah's presence thread; Walter in the yard = humanised antagonist). The Garside displacement beat is deliberately the lightest in the book so far — one sentence ('Prescott had seen it ten thousand times at least') — since the heavy Garside beat was Ch 07 and the heaviest is reserved for Ch 17. The next chapter (Ch 10, the document examiner) should not open in transit or with an observation texture, and should vary from literary-fade by using resolved-beat or turn to close — the machine thread needs a decisive closure. The suppressed-inference construction ('He did not write that second thought') should not recur verbatim; it belongs to this chapter's reading of Prescott's discipline.
