# Editor — Pacing Pass
## Macro arc assessment

**Overall shape.** *The Probate of Eccleston* is properly sized for its story — the planned ~51,000 words against a 70,000-word ceiling is the right call, and the compounding mechanism (nine named doors, a monotonically falling calendar) gives every chapter a specific structural task. The three-movement proportion (11,500 / 26,000 / 13,500 words) is sound: setup is lean, middle is the procedural body it needs to be, and the final quarter has enough room to land the discovery, the exposure, and the coda without rushing any of them. No chapter in the weave advances zero threads; the outline delivers on that promise.

**Midbook sag risk.** The anti-sag engine is genuinely working — the calendar pressure and the door-closure discipline prevent the middle movement from becoming inert. However, there is a pressure-texture sag in the sub-sequence of Chapters 6–8 (The Letter-Book, The File, The Gazette). All three are Prescott-alone / documents / door-closed chapters: resolved-beat, coda, resolved-beat in succession, and all three occurring before the first external human contact since Chapter 4 (Bank Parade). That is five chapters (5–9) without a living witness in the room, and three of those five are procedurally identical in texture. The doors they close are genuinely distinct, and the calendar is running — but for a reader the texture of these chapters is the same note played three times. Chapter 9 (Vale Mill, literary-fade) and Chapter 13 (The Fairhursts, coda) are the planned relief valves; they work as designed. The concern is whether the 6–8 cluster, which may run to 6,500–7,000 words in practice, will sustain a reader before Chapter 9 arrives. Flagging for per-chapter review: Chapters 7 and 8 in particular, to confirm they are differentiated by texture and not merely by which door they close.

**Escalation failures.** None structurally. The calendar reversal at Chapter 14 (commission refused, 140 days) is well-placed — the one moment pressure moves against Prescott, landing just past the midpoint of Movement II, creating the escalation step that prevents the door-closing sequence from feeling mechanical. Chapters 16–19 build cleanly through the discovery to the exposure.

**Chapters 16–17 adjacency risk.** Both chapters involve brief assembly and both work the recital variance: Chapter 16 establishes the date asymmetry analytically; Chapter 17 performs the direct visual comparison. The distinction is well-theorised in the craft notes (analytic knowledge vs. experiential encounter), but two chapters in a row working adjacent angles on the same evidence creates a pacing risk if the reader does not experience a genuine surprise at Chapter 17. If "knowing analytically" in Chapter 16 has already done the variance's work, Chapter 17's discovery beats more quietly than the book's central payoff warrants. Per-chapter review recommended for Chapter 17 specifically.

**Discovery chapter weight.** The Chapter 17 craft notes state that the chapter runs to "roughly 900 words of prose" (excluding set-off documents), explicitly called "the shortest chapter yet." The chapter map planned it at 3,500 words. Sparse delivery of the discovery is defensible — even appropriate to Prescott's register — but there is a proportionality question: the book has built nine doors and three fair-play misquotations across 30,000+ words, and if the payoff chapter's prose is shorter than any single door-closing chapter in Movement II, the climactic weight may be misallocated. This does not need restructuring; it needs confirmation that the discovery's compression feels earned rather than underwritten.

**Climax/coda balance.** Healthy. Chapter 19 (Solemn Form, turn, 3,500 words) is the correct weight for the exposure. Chapter 20 (The Estate, coda, 2,000 words) compresses all settlements to their necessary weight without overstaying. No problem here.

**Repeated beats.** Garside displacement appears in seven of twenty chapters (5, 7, 9, 15, 17, 18, 20). This is the book's internal thread, and each instance is calibrated differently — displacement through a different object, at a different stage of the argument. The resolution at Chapter 20 is earned. The risk is mechanical repetition in the 15–18 cluster (four of seven displacement beats in the final movement), not in the pattern overall. Watch for diminishing returns on this beat in the brief-preparation chapters.

**Structural issue requiring resolution before further passes.** The story-arc log and chapter-craft notes both record two competing drafts for what the chapter map designates as Chapter 11/12 (the sale contract). Both `draft/11-the-sale-contract.md` and `draft/12-the-sale-contract.md` exist; the story-arc log shows duplicate entries for both chapter titles. This is an unresolved production conflict: a consolidation pass selecting one draft is needed before per-chapter pacing or repair passes can work this segment reliably.

## 14-the-commission.md

**Small fix applied.** The chapter's final paragraph — a recapitulation of what the brief must establish — was cut. The excised text read:

> *What the brief must establish, in eight weeks, was what the second codicil's recital could and could not have been drawn from: that the executed first codicil had never left the strong-room; that no copy of any kind had existed outside this office; that no one anywhere in the county had been furnished with its language; and that whatever the recital had been composed from, it was not the instrument. The machine evidence placed the typing at the mill office. The text would establish what the recital had and had not seen.*

This paragraph advanced no thread and restated information the reader already holds from the record-building chapters (7, 9, 10). Its quadruple *that…that…that…and that* construction also introduced a brief-summary quality at odds with the controlled register the chapter had sustained throughout. More critically, it caused the chapter to end twice: once cleanly at the declarative pivot — **"What remained was the text."** — and again, softly, via the Hadfield figures displacement beat.

With the cut the chapter closes on two short beats that earn their weight: the figure written in the margin of Mitchell's letter (a physical action consistent with Prescott's habit of marking the calendar in the file), followed by the Hadfield figures — opened the chapter displaced by the letter's arrival, returned to at close. The structural echo is preserved; the recap is gone.


## 16-the-date.md

**Thread check: clean.** Advances Thread 4 (date asymmetry: R5 gave Walter the date in writing; no source gave him the operative text) and Thread 6 (the variance is brought into analytic focus in the record; Chapter 17's discovery follows). The chapter is not dead weight; it earns its place.

**Pacing issue: working note redundancy (~150 words).**

The chapter's structure is: prose analysis → formal document quotation → working note → close. The prose analysis section (before the working note) already establishes, clearly and in sequence:
1. R5 gave the date of the first codicil, not its operative text.
2. The recital in R3 uses the date exactly as given in R5.
3. The recital's operative words ("and her issue") do not match the executed first codicil ("or her issue").
4. The "and her issue" version is documented three times in Walter's hand or voice (R4, attendance note).

Working note paragraphs 1 and 2 restate all four of these points in exhibit-citation form, adding approximately 150 words without advancing the reader's understanding. Paragraphs 3 and 4 are the note's actual work: paragraph 3 synthesizes the date/text asymmetry in a single tight formulation; paragraph 4 places the finding within the nine-door framework and draws the authorship inference ("a person who had the date from R5, had the operative words from memory of a spoken account, and had no access to the executed first codicil at any point"). The first two paragraphs serve the note's formal completeness as a professional document; the last two paragraphs are what the chapter needed to establish.

**Why this matters here specifically.** The macro assessment flags the 16–17 adjacency risk: Chapter 16 establishes the date asymmetry analytically; Chapter 17 performs the direct visual comparison. If Chapter 16 over-explains its own analysis — the working note's restatement paragraphs do exactly this — it further compresses the room available for Chapter 17's discovery to land with surprise. A tighter working note (paragraphs 3–4 only, or paragraphs 1–2 compressed to a single brief citation sentence) would reduce the analytic saturation entering Chapter 17.

**Why this is documented rather than cut.** The fix sits at the boundary between pacing and register. In Prescott's professional idiom, a working note filed for later use would ordinarily carry its own exhibit citations — cutting paragraphs 1–2 entirely would leave the synthesis paragraphs formally unmoored as a standalone document, which is a voice/register call this pass should not make unilaterally. The recommended action is: compress working note paragraphs 1–2 into a single tight citation sentence ("R5 (exhibit 23) gave the date of the first codicil in writing and no operative text; R3's recital reproduces the date exactly and gives the operative words in the form documented three times as Walter Eccleston's own — R4 (exhibit 14) and attendance note (exhibit 21)"), then let paragraphs 3–4 stand unchanged. This saves approximately 110–120 words, eliminates the restatement loop, and preserves the note's formal citation structure. Total chapter impact: modest but meaningful given the chapter's function as Chapter 17's analytical predecessor.

## 18-counsels-brief.md

**Overall: clean, no restructuring required.** Thread 1 and Thread 2 both advance genuinely. The Thurston & Marsh section is the chapter's best-paced movement — a taut three-beat exchange (their very carefully written letter / Booth's "they know something is coming" / Prescott's exact, offering-nothing reply) that builds pressure into Ch 19 without over-signalling. The close ("Sixty days to the hearing. / …took out the next correspondence.") is controlled and correct. The Garside displacement beat (exhibit-schedule section) is the most restrained of the four-in-the-final-movement cluster — three sentences, then "moved on to the next exhibit" — no diminishing-returns problem.

**Modest pacing drag: opening-section restatement (~150 words).**

The chapter's first substantive movement (paragraphs 4–6) describes what the brief says about the variance and the nine-section elimination structure. Paragraph 4 ("The recital of the propounded second codicil quoted the first codicil's gift clause as giving the residue to Margaret Fairhurst *and her issue*…") restates the variance the reader already holds from Chapters 16 and 17. The authorship-section paragraph (para 6) recapitulates the date asymmetry from Chapter 16 before reaching its new work — the conclusion sentence ("*Whatever else remains uncertain…the source of the word in its recital is not in question*"), which is genuinely new and earns its place.

These paragraphs are framed as showing the brief taking shape rather than simply replaying known facts, and that framing carries some narrative weight — this is the investigative record crystallized into a legal argument, which is a different textural register from the investigation itself. But from a pacing standpoint, they back up slightly before the chapter finds its energy (the handwriting section, then the Thurston & Marsh letter). At roughly 1,000 words total, a 150-word restatement in the first third is a non-trivial proportion.

**Suggested action (not a cut, a compression).** Paragraphs 4 and 5 together cover the brief's structure adequately. Paragraph 4's specific restatement of the variance ("The recital of…the question before the court was not whether the recital was wrong; it was where it had been taken from instead") could be compressed to a single sentence setting up the nine-section logic, since the variance itself does not need re-explaining at this point in the book. This would save approximately 60–80 words and let the nine-section description (which is organizationally new — framing the nine doors as an elimination argument rather than a sequence of investigations) do its work without the recap. The authorship-section paragraph (para 6) is doing more new work and should stay largely intact; its conclusion sentence is the chapter's intellectual high point before the Thurston & Marsh exchange.

**Why documented rather than cut.** The restatement is framed (intentionally or not) as brief-as-document; cutting it risks making the brief feel underarticulated at the moment the reader most wants to see the argument assembled. A voice/register pass is better placed to judge whether Prescott's professional idiom permits a terser frame-in of the variance at this stage. This is a borderline pacing/register call.
