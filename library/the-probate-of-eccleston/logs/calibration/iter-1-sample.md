The deed box was already on the table.

Prescott had come in through Winckley Square at half past nine to find it sitting in the centre of the examining table, the Eccleston label in Garside's hand, the lock unchecked. He set down his briefcase and stood for a moment looking at it. The fire had been made up; Mr. Booth had taken his coat. Nothing in the room required comment.

Garside had heard the news the previous evening — Josiah Eccleston, eighty-one years old, cotton spinner and magistrate of this county, dead at Eccleston Hall on the morning of the seventeenth. Every instrument for thirty years had come through this office. There were not many clients of thirty years' standing, and this was not a morning to count what remained of them.

He opened the lid.

The instruments lay in order: the will first, in its deed paper, tied with green tape; the first codicil behind it, similarly dressed; the firm's letter-book carbon of the acknowledgement after execution. Beneath all of it lay the attendance notes, the correspondence, the working file of a man's dispositions across seven years. The custody had been Garside's from the beginning. In the strong-room register the entries ran in Garside's careful hand from 1919: the will deposited, the will verified, the codicil added in March of twenty-four. The last entry was September of this year, the date in the margin column, the purpose set down in the body of the entry in a hand that had by then begun to run thin at the descenders. Garside had noted the occasion fully — the instrument fetched, the family assembled at the Hall, the names of those present recorded in the margin alongside the date.

Prescott's own name was among them.

He had been in this room long enough to know that a file built with more care than occasion required was not an accident. Garside built files the way he argued cases: on the assumption that everything might someday need to be produced. Two decades had made the assumption Prescott's own. He lifted the will from its wrapper, untied the tape, and smoothed the document flat.

The date was the third day of June, nineteen hundred and nineteen.

"We should read it properly," Garside said from the door.

Prescott did not look up. "Yes," he said. "We should."
