## 01-the-strong-room.md

Chapter is largely clean — period vocabulary, displacement interiority, short declaratives at weight-bearing moments, document quotations unglossed. Three items need writer judgment; none is a clear enough word-level violation to fix unilaterally.

---

**1. "the October light was doing what it did" (para 4)**

> The coal fire was going. Outside on the square the October light was doing what it did.

The style guide requires prose that is "mid-range and exact." "Doing what it did" specifies nothing — it is definitionally empty where the register favours precision. Two readings compete:

- *Intentional laconic aside* — Prescott's attention is entirely on the deed box; the outside world doesn't register. The self-referential phrasing IS the dismissal. This is consistent with displacement interiority.
- *Accidental imprecision* — The register calls for Prescott to notice specific things. "The October light was flat" or "the October light sat low on the square" would give the dismissal the same texture with an actual observation behind it.

If the imprecision is intentional, leave it. If it reads as a placeholder that survived drafting, tighten to a concrete adjective.

---

**2. Garside's s.33 exposition (para after first codicil block)**

> "Clause four revoked entire," Garside said. "Gift and executorship both — he intended a complete revision. There is, at present, no executor." He set the codicil alongside the will. "Section thirty-three of the Wills Act does nothing for a niece against lapse. She is collateral, not a descendant. He used the substitutional form accordingly."

Prescott is a qualified solicitor of twelve years who also helped draft this codicil in 1924. He knows s.33. The explanation reads slightly expository — Garside lecturing a junior who already knows the law. This is more structural than a voice slip (Garside thinking aloud is defensible), but the line "She is collateral, not a descendant" in particular has the cadence of a teacher addressing a student, not two professionals confirming what both know. Writer to judge whether Garside's register here should be more muttered-confirmation than instruction.

---

**3. Closing sentence**

> What Walter Eccleston would make of those terms was a question the record had not yet been required to answer.

The phrase "was a question the record had not yet been required to answer" is abstract where the rest of the chapter is concrete. The first half of the closing — "The file was in order: the will of the third of June nineteen-nineteen, the codicil of the fifteenth of March nineteen-twenty-four, the strong-room register exact about both" — is pitch-perfect foreclosure. The second sentence hovers just above the failure mode of "naming the question the chapter poses." The legal construction ("required to answer") saves it from pure subtext-naming, but it sits at the edge. Writer to judge whether this is the register the chapter earns, or whether the closing door should slam on the first sentence alone.

### Ruminating clause in the firm-succession passage

**Location:** The paragraph beginning "It was Prescott's firm now…"

**Text:**
> It was Prescott's firm now, in the way that things come to be yours: not as a gift but as what remains when the person who built them is gone.

**Issue:** The style guide's interiority rule — "noticing and deciding, not ruminating" — and its failure mode for emotional structural analysis ("any passage that begins to anatomize Prescott's feelings about Garside's death must be cut back to the displacement form"). "In the way that things come to be yours: not as a gift but as what remains when the person who built them is gone" is a philosophical generalisation about the nature of succession, not a piece of noticing or a professional observation. It explains the emotional register of the plain statement before it ("It was Prescott's firm now"), which is precisely what the sentence-after-the-sentence failure mode prohibits.

The plain sentence already lands. The explanatory clause follows it and names what it means.

**Why not fixed here:** The clause cannot be repaired at the word level — it would need to be cut entirely or replaced with a concrete displacement object, both of which are compression decisions. The compression pass has already run; this is flagged for the writer's judgment.

**Possible direction (not prescriptive):** Cut the clause after "now" and let the sentence stand alone, letting the following paragraph ("He had known it since December, since the file and the action and the strong-room register had all come to him at once") carry the weight — which it does, in the correct displacement register.
