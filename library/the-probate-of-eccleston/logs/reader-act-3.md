# Reader Assessment — Act 3 (Chapters 14–20)

## Overall

Act 3 is the book's strongest movement. The discovery chapter (Ch 17) is the book at its best; the hearing chapter (Ch 19) is staged with exactly the right calibration of restraint and precision; the resolution (Ch 20) closes every account cleanly and earns its final image. Minor issues in two chapters — an over-long inference passage in Ch 14 and a slightly premature Garside beat in Ch 18 — do not break the act. All threads resolve as promised.

---

## What's working

**The discovery (Ch 17) is exactly right.** The brief required that Prescott find the variance through routine file discipline, not intuition — "a clerk's reflex, a check run late at night." That is precisely what Ch 17 delivers. The pretext is the citation check before the brief goes to London; the descent to the strong-room is habitual; the two documents laid side by side is procedure. The weight lands on a single sentence — "He sat with both documents in front of him and said nothing, because there was no one in the room" — and it lands because everything before it has been controlled. The displacement close (the strong-room register in Garside's hand, somewhere below) does not name grief; it finds it in the geometry of the building. This is the register at its clearest. The retrieval of R4 from the exhibit cabinet — a document already filed, already in the record, not new evidence but a re-reading — honours the fair-play contract and the brief's "the discovery re-reads what exists" requirement.

**Ch 19's exposure is staged correctly.** Walter's response to reading "or" for the first time is observed purely from the outside: the slowing, the going back, the coming to rest. "Something in the set of his shoulders was different from a moment before." This is the right approach — behavioural precision without interior access, which would violate the single-POV rule and flatten the character. Prescott's register at that moment is procedural and almost apologetic, as required: "He kept his face composed and his hands at his sides. There was nothing to say." The judge's closing observation — "ordinary documents do not accommodate themselves to what comes after" — delivers the book's thesis from a procedural aside, never as announced theme. Structurally elegant.

**Ch 20 resolves every thread and gets the calibration right.** Nora is "not exultant, not aggrieved, the way people are when something long-awaited arrives and proves to be not quite what was imagined" — this is the brief's requirement that the children receive what the law gives them, not what they hoped for, and it is precise and true. George asking what happens to the men is the moment that costs, and Prescott's response — "He said it correctly. He was not certain it had been the right thing to say" — is the book's warmth surfacing in a cracked sentence, then held. The final Garside close ("More precise than the occasion required. He could not have known it; he had made it anyway") is the definitive statement of Thread 2, earned by months of accumulation. The closing image — Prescott sitting down to open the next file — is exactly right: continuance, not catharsis.

**Ch 15 (The Hall) uses double-corroboration structurally.** The chapter closes door (b) by aligning the household journal at Eccleston Hall and the strong-room register in Preston against the same date "from opposite ends of twelve miles." Two independent records, each unknown to the other's keeper, locking the same fact. This is the compounding mechanism made visible as method — prior chapters' records tightening the current chapter's finding — and it's the most elegant structural beat of the act outside the discovery.

**Thread resolutions are all earned.** Thread 5 (the witness trap) is the act's most formally interesting close: the witnesses never testify, and Ch 14's refusal, which first reads as a defeat, becomes by Ch 19 a legible piece of the design. The judgment that the text carries the whole weight without a single living witness to due execution is not a rescue — it was the book's structural commitment from the outset, and Ch 14 pays it off correctly.

---

## What isn't working

**Ch 14's inference passage is the act's weakest section.** After Prescott processes the commission refusal, the chapter develops at length a thought about the "shape to the situation" — the inference that the witnesses' dual unavailability in the same autumn maps to deliberate selection. The passage is carefully handled (the thought is labeled inadmissible, the pen drawn through it, the reasoning appropriately withheld from the attendance book), but it runs for nearly six paragraphs and covers ground that was already implied in Ch 12. The chapter's register here approaches the failure mode of "a solicitor recapping what he knows" — the thought exists to tell the reader something about Prescott's developing understanding of the design, but it is the chapter's wordiest moment and slightly dilutes the otherwise effective brevity with which the refusal itself lands. The foreclosure (witnesses permanently beyond reach) is real and earned; the inference padding around it is not.

**Ch 18 anticipates Ch 20's Garside close, diluting it.** Mid-chapter, while Prescott checks the exhibit schedule against the strong-room register, the narrative delivers this: "More precise than a routine visit required. It had not been a routine visit, and Garside had written it as though he understood that... He had written it that way because it was his habit to write it that way, and the habit was now the proof." This is a strong sentence. But it is essentially the same discovery Ch 17 reached through displacement (the register in Garside's hand, its implicit meaning) and the same observation Ch 20 will make more fully and more finally. Three statements of the same Garside insight across three chapters — Ch 17 as displacement image, Ch 18 as explicit statement, Ch 20 as definitive resolution — is one too many. Ch 18's explicit version in particular anticipates and slightly flattens Ch 20's close. Trimming or softening the Ch 18 occurrence would sharpen the final chapter's weight.

**Ch 16's working note restates the chapter's own argument.** The formal two-page working note at the end of Ch 16 lays out the date asymmetry in explicit logical form — correctly, precisely, in procedural register. The problem is that the preceding narrative has already covered the same ground in Prescott's narrated noticing. The chapter states the argument twice: once through Prescott reading the documents, and once in the formal note. The working note adds the legal formulation the narrative doesn't fully achieve, so it isn't redundant in kind, but it is redundant in conclusion. This is low severity; the style does permit formal document inserts, and a solicitor building a brief would produce exactly this note. But a reader will feel the chapter double back on itself.

## Voice check

The voice holds consistently across all seven chapters. No gloss paragraphs after document inserts. No direct naming of Prescott's feelings. No passages where the narration explains what a document "really meant." The legal vocabulary (letters of administration with the will annexed, the recital, due execution, solemn form) is used throughout without explanatory glossing, as the register requires.

One minor note: Ch 14 contains the act's longest stretch of Prescott reasoning in a quasi-summary mode — the inference about the witnesses' selection. The passage is controlled, but it approaches the "So far Prescott had established" tone the style sheet flags. It does not cross into explicit recapitulation, but it is worth watching.

## Brief-adherence checks (Act 3)

- **Fair-play contract**: R2, R3, and R4 all appeared verbatim before the discovery (Chs 1, 2, and 11 respectively). Ch 17's re-reading of R4 is a discovery beat, not a new planting — the exhibit is already in the record. ✓
- **Walter's misquotation occasions**: Three occasions fell across the prior acts (R4 in Ch 11; Walter to Prescott in Ch 2; Walter to the children in Ch 13). No new occasions appear in Act 3; the discovery chapter correctly re-reads R4 as an existing exhibit rather than a fresh planting. Count is exactly three. ✓
- **Single POV discipline**: Maintained throughout. The description of Walter reading the codicil in Ch 19 is behavioural exterior observation, not interior access — Prescott observes the body, not the mind. The parenthetical "He had been wrong since March 1924 and had not known it" in Ch 19 is Prescott stating established fact from the record, not accessing Walter's consciousness. Borderline but defensible. ✓
- **Compounding mechanism**: Ch 14 closes the witness door permanently; Ch 15 closes door (b) (Josiah's presence); Ch 16 closes door (i) (date asymmetry); Ch 17 assembles the proof; Ch 18 formalises and dispatches the brief; Ch 19 and Ch 20 resolve the action and all threads. No door is re-walked. ✓
- **Calendar monotonicity**: 140 days (Ch 14), 125 (Ch 15), 90 (Ch 16), 88 (Ch 17), 60 (Ch 18), then the hearing arrives in Ch 19 with no days count given. The count's absence from Ch 19 is technically a departure from the brief's requirement ("named in every chapter"), but the hearing is the terminal event and the absence is licensed by arrival. Low finding. ✓ with note.
- **Ending completeness**: All six required questions are answered in Chs 19–20. Nothing withheld. ✓
- **Document fidelity**: R3's recital, R2's operative clause, and R4 in Ch 17 match the canonical texts as registered. R5 is cited in Ch 16 by fragment only, which is correct after first appearance. ✓
- **Quotation ceiling**: R2 and R3 each appear in full at most twice before the discovery; R4 twice (Ch 11 and Ch 17 at discovery). Within ceiling. ✓

## Summary

Act 3 delivers the book. The discovery, the hearing, and the resolution are all handled at the level the brief required. The principal concerns — Ch 14's over-long inference passage and Ch 18's premature Garside beat — are craft-level adjustments, not structural failures. Both are fixable in a light revision pass without touching the book's architecture. Every thread is closed. The fair-play contract is honoured. The ending is clean, complete, and earned.
