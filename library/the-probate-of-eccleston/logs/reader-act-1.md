### What's Working

**The register.** The opening sentence of Ch 1 places everything correctly: *"The deed box was on the table when Prescott arrived: black painted steel, 'ECCLESTON' lettered in white on the lid, the label in Garside's copper-plate facing up across the room."* Inference is in the observation. Garside's hat is still on — Prescott reads the room through professional signs, never by naming what the signs mean. This continues throughout the act without a significant slip.

**The death of Garside (Ch 5).** The whole shock is delivered through Mr. Booth's position in the passage. *"In sixteen years Prescott had never seen Mr. Booth standing in the passage."* No death scene, no direct grief, no announcement. The two-sentence paragraph — *"He was thirty-eight. He had worked beside this man for sixteen years."* — placed after the practical arrangements and before *"By the afternoon the office was working again, because it had to"* — is the register at its best. These sentences are exactly what the style guide means by the plain sentence that carries disproportionate weight because everything around it is controlled.

**The displacement beat in Ch 7.** The act's culminating Garside moment: Garside's cover letter to Josiah about the draft codicil, written for the record rather than for Josiah. *"The file was the work of a man who had never distinguished between what required a record and what deserved one."* Followed by: *"And the file was going to prove a forgery."* This is not a gloss — it's consequence, not explanation — and it properly pivots to the case rather than naming the grief. The displacement mechanism is working as intended.

**Nora Fairhurst (Ch 4).** *"She stood the way a person stands who has decided not to sit until she understands what is happening."* One sentence; she's alive from it. Her questions — *what was our mother to have had* and *how long will it take* — are exact without being aggressive, and Prescott answers without softening. The brief's requirement that the children remain people and not a cause is met here.

**Fair-play subtlety.** The "and her issue" plant in Ch 2 is buried exactly right in Walter's cover story, a single phrase in a plausible account of Josiah's intentions. A reader paying close attention will feel the charge against R2 read an act earlier; a reader not paying close attention will hear Walter's account and find it plausible. The narration never signals the variance. ✓

**The Garside attendance note in Ch 3.** The marginal note *"Or her issue: collateral; substitutional form required. — H.G."* is a genuine exhibit. It closes not only the habit door but a possible defense: that "or" was a typist's error for "and." Garside recorded the legal rationale at the time of execution. This is the compounding mechanism working exactly as the brief envisions: a prior act of professional exactitude becoming a later foreclosure.

**Document inserts.** Every set-off passage is clean: the document stands, the prose resumes from the same narrative level, nothing following explains what the document "really" meant. R1, R2, R3, R5 — all correctly deployed. R5 quoted twice (Ch 2 and Ch 6) is within the ceiling and neither instance functions as filler.

### Concerns

**Mild re-walk in Ch 7 (instrument register).** Chapters 3 and 7 both cover the instrument register and both arrive at the same conclusion: every instrument of Josiah's adult life through this firm, ending with the first codicil. Ch 3 does this through the full index and the earlier wills; Ch 7 does it again by walking the register and naming the same sequence. The diary entry for August 14 and the letter to Nora Fairhurst are genuinely new in Ch 7 — they carry the chapter and close door (f) on its "diary and correspondence silent" side. But the instrument register passage in Ch 7 (*"The original lease of Vale Mill, 1884... Nothing after it."*) re-establishes a fact already established in Ch 3 without materially narrowing or extending it. This is a mild concern — not a full re-walk, and the new material in Ch 7 justifies the chapter — but the register walk repeats.

**Four routine-procedure openings in seven chapters.** Chapters 1, 2, 3, and 6 all open in the same texture: Prescott at a desk or table, beginning to work through documents. Chapters 4 (transit), 5 (arrival/summons), and 7 (arrival by post) provide variation. For a procedural novel in this register this is expected, and the content of each procedure is distinct, but the accumulation is noticeable. Within Act 1 it is not a problem; if the pattern continues across Act 2's middle-movement chapters, it will become one.

**Bank Parade's atmospheric close (Ch 4) runs slightly long.** The park walk — the Ribble, the heron, the lit window — is earned and the period texture is right. But the lit-window closing image comes after an already complete emotional beat (Prescott's realization that *"The file had always had an address in it — Bank Parade, Preston — but it had been a legal address"*) and adds a second atmospheric gesture that slightly diminishes the first. A very minor rhythm issue; the chapter's core is excellent.

### Voice Check

The failure modes from the style guide are largely avoided. No "sentence after the sentence" is egregious — the narration generally stops rather than glosses. No passage of direct grief for Garside beyond the two controlled sentences in Ch 5. Period-legal vocabulary (substitutional form, letters of administration with the will annexed, solemn form, the recital) is used correctly and without gloss throughout; the prose trusts context to carry it.

One watch point: **Walter's plausibility in Ch 2.** His cover story is presented through Prescott's attendance note and observed behavior — the *ease of a man who had spent twenty years at home in other people's offices*, the brief nod accepting the solicitor's non-answer. This is exactly right. The risk, as the middle movement develops, will be maintaining this human complexity while the record closes around him. Thread 4 is building correctly.

### Verdict

A very strong first act. The register is clean, the fair-play plants are correctly placed and unannounced, the compounding mechanism is visible and building, Garside's death lands with full weight in exactly the right register, and the displacement mechanism is earning its first real moment by chapter 7. The mild instrument-register re-walk in Ch 7 and the slightly elongated atmospheric close in Ch 4 are worth watching in revision but do not damage the act. The book is doing what it promised.
