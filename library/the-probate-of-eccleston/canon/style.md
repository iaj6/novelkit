# Style

## Register

Third person limited, past tense, Arthur Prescott throughout. The narrative voice is Prescott's
sensibility: dry, precise, professionally restrained, and capable of landing weight in a plain
sentence. The nearest exemplars are Dorothy L. Sayers's *Unnatural Death* (1927) — procedural pace,
the investigator's eye for documents and professional detail, observational wit that lives in
noticing rather than performance — and Freeman Wills Crofts's method novels: patient accumulation
of evidence, a calendar-against-the-investigator pressure that never lets up, the slow foreclosure
of each innocent explanation. This is not pastiche of either.

The book loves documents the way its narrator does. When a document is quoted verbatim, it stands as
a set-off passage and the prose gets entirely out of the way. No frame, no gloss, no comment on the
language in the paragraph that follows. The document is its own meaning.

Interiority is noticing and deciding, not ruminating. Prescott thinks in the professional vocabulary
— instruments, dates, custody, recitals — and his emotional life surfaces as displacement: onto
objects (the strong-room, the register in Garside's hand), onto procedure (the file that is better-
built than it needed to be), onto what he sees in other people's houses. He does not name his grief
for Garside; he finds it in the work Garside left behind.

## Sentence rhythm

Mid-range and exact. Short declaratives (8–12 words) cluster at moments of weight or decision; they
are a rhythm, not a default. Complex sentences earn themselves through precision: a long sentence in
this register holds several legal specifications in syntactic suspension, not several subordinate
feelings. Period vocabulary — "the instrument," "the executed original," "the strong-room register,"
"letters of administration with the will annexed" — is the furniture of this world. Use it without
gloss; context carries it.

Average sentence length: 18–26 words. Maximum for any single sentence: 45 words (for a sentence
enumerating legal specifications); beyond that, split it.

## Chapter movement

Most chapters centre on a single professional act. The compounding mechanism (every chapter closes a
door) means each chapter earns its place through a foreclosure: something is fixed in evidence at
the end that was not fixed at its start. Documents enter as set-off passages within chapters; the
prose resumes from the same narrative level afterward. The calendar countdown (days to the hearing)
appears in every chapter but varies its position — beginning, middle, or end — and must not close
every chapter like a metronome.

## Tonal register

Sober and quiet. No chase, no violence, no confrontation until the hearing. The tension is
procedural: is the record sufficient? Is there time? Period-true affect: professionals who do not
perform emotion, who are not cold but who would rather show grief in what they do than say it. When
the weight lands, it lands in plain sentences, because the sentences around it have been controlled.

## Fair-play planting

The two forms of the clause — *or her issue* in R2, *and her issue* in R3's recital and in Walter's
dialogue — are each printed verbatim, in plain view, before the discovery chapter, without the
narration ever underlining the variance. The reader is invited to notice. The narration does not help
them notice, and does not penalize them for missing it.

Walter's spoken misquotation appears exactly three times before the discovery, pinned in the brief
(R4 printed as a financial exhibit; Walter to Prescott at the funeral caveat meeting; Walter,
kindly, to Nora and George at Bank Parade). No further occasions.

## Lean-into patterns

Patterns the drafter and editors should NOT treat as failures, because this register depends on them.

- **Verbatim document quotation:** printing a canonical text in full is fair-play planting, not
  padding. R1–R3 may each appear at most two or three times in full before the discovery; after the
  discovery, they are referenced by name or fragment. Within that ceiling, printing the full text is
  the contractual act of fair-play.
- **Displacement onto objects:** Prescott's grief for Garside surfaces through professional
  discovery (the file built too precisely, the procedure followed beyond what was required), not
  through direct sentiment. This is not evasion; it is the register's correct form of interiority.
  Do not compress it.
- **The plain sentence that lands hard:** mid-register prose earns the occasional sentence of
  disproportionate weight by controlling everything around it. Do not follow such a sentence with a
  gloss; do not precede it with a softening qualifier. The weight is produced by the contrast.
- **Period-legal vocabulary as texture:** "the executed original," "due execution," "solemn form,"
  "the recital," "letters of administration with the will annexed" — these are not jargon insertions.
  They are how this world is described by someone who lives in it. Use them; do not italicize or
  explain them.

## Failure modes

The AI-author patterns most tempting in this story's register. The drafter should actively avoid
these; the compression pass should cut them.

- **Sentence-after-the-sentence** — Prescott's narrated noticing must be allowed to land without the
  next sentence explaining what it was. When an observation has weight, the prose earns it by
  stopping. A gloss sentence immediately after ("He had not expected that to matter as much as it
  did") deflates the whole register.
- **Meta-commentary on cognition** — "He noted that," "It occurred to him that," "She registered" —
  these are labels for thinking that Prescott's perspective already performs. The professional
  sensibility is shown through what he attends to, not through naming the act of attention.
- **Post-scene subtext naming** — after a document is quoted, the narrative must not explain what it
  "really" meant. The paragraph after a document insert continues the scene; it does not annotate the
  text. This is also a fair-play rule: the reader must encounter the document uninterpreted.
- **Emotional structural analysis** — the register does not explain grief. It shows the strong-room
  register in Garside's hand. Any passage that begins to anatomize Prescott's feelings about
  Garside's death must be cut back to the displacement form.
- **Redundant transition / re-summary** — Prescott does not recap what the file contains. He works
  forward. A paragraph that begins "So far Prescott had established…" belongs in a different kind of
  detective novel.
