# Glossary

**Administrator pendente lite** — A neutral administrator appointed by the court to manage a
deceased's estate while a contentious probate action is pending. Can collect assets and pay debts;
may not distribute to any beneficiary. In this book: a Preston accountant, his monthly figures the
reader's window onto the mill's deterioration.

**Administration of Estates Act 1925 (AEA 1925)** — In force 1 January 1926. Its central reform for
this story: all of a deceased's estate (real *and* personal) now vests in the personal
representative, not directly in the heirs. A sale conditioned on "a grant of representation
establishing the vendor's title" is the correct post-1925 formulation.

**Attestation clause** — The clause in a will or codicil, signed by the attesting witnesses,
recording that the instrument was executed in the testator's presence. Creates a rebuttable
presumption of due execution.

**Caveat (probate)** — A formal notice lodged at the Probate Registry preventing any grant of
representation from issuing. The caveator states their contrary interest; if they enter an
appearance, the caveat persists until the court resolves the dispute. Edgar Fairhurst enters the
caveat on behalf of the minor children.

**Codicil** — A supplementary document to a will, executed with the same formalities (Wills Act 1837
s.9 and s.10), that adds to, alters, or revokes the will's provisions.

**Common form** — Routine, uncontested grant of probate on the executor's oath. Vulnerable to
challenge for up to thirty years; not appropriate here, where the instrument is disputed.

**Deed box** — A painted steel box with a hinged lid, used by solicitors to hold a client's
documents. The Eccleston deed box is black, "ECCLESTON" in white on the lid.

**Due execution** — Compliance with Wills Act 1837 s.9: the will or codicil is signed by the
testator, in the presence of two witnesses present at the same time, each of whom attests and signs
in the testator's presence. A facially correct attestation clause raises a presumption of due
execution (*omnia praesumuntur rite esse acta*).

**Engrossment** — The final fair copy of a legal document, on good paper, ready for execution. By
the 1920s, engrossment is typically typed rather than handwritten in a provincial solicitors' office.

**Issue** — In English legal usage: all lineal descendants — children, grandchildren, and further.
"Or her issue absolutely" in R2 means the gift passes to Margaret's children (and potentially
grandchildren) if she predeceases Josiah.

**Lapse** — Failure of a testamentary gift when the legatee predeceases the testator. The failed
gift falls back into residue (or, if the residuary gift itself fails, on intestacy). Wills Act 1837
s.33 prevents lapse for gifts to the testator's own children or remoter descendants; it does not
extend to nieces or other collateral relatives. Margaret is Josiah's niece; s.33 affords her no
protection.

**Letter-book** — The firm's bound record of outgoing correspondence, carbon-copied from the 1920s
onward. The letter-book carbon of R5 (Garside & Prescott's November 1926 notice letter) survives as
a corroborating exhibit.

**Letters of administration with the will annexed** — A grant issued when no executor is able or
willing to act. Gives the administrator the same powers as an executor. If the second codicil fails,
the grant goes to Edgar Fairhurst as guardian of the minor children; Walter's executorship was
revoked by the first codicil.

**Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division (PDA Division)** — The division of the High Court that
heard contentious probate matters in 1926. Created by the Judicature Acts 1873–75; renamed by the
Administration of Justice Act 1970.

**Probate in solemn form** — Proof of a will in open court, with all interested parties cited. Has
res judicata effect — binds everyone who appeared or was cited. The correct procedure when the
instrument's validity is in dispute.

**Recital** — The explanatory clause in an instrument, recording background facts. In the second
codicil the recital quotes the terms of the first codicil. A recital has no operative legal effect;
an error in a recital is probative evidence about the maker's knowledge of the underlying document.

**Short time** — The period term for reduced-hours working: running a mill for fewer days or hours
than normal to reduce output while keeping the workforce partially employed. The standard state of
the Lancashire weaving industry in 1926. Vale Mill is working short time throughout the book.

**Solemn form** — See *Probate in solemn form*.

**Strong-room** — A fireproof vault (typically in the basement or a reinforced ground-floor room of
a solicitors' office) holding original deeds, wills, and clients' title documents.

**Strong-room register** — The bound ledger recording every deposit and withdrawal from the strong-
room: date, document described, clerk's signature. Garside's handwriting is in this register for
twenty years. It records the September 1926 fetch and same-day return of the first codicil.

**Substitutional gift** — A gift drafted so that a secondary donee takes if the primary donee cannot.
"Unto my niece Margaret Fairhurst or her issue absolutely" (R2) is a substitutional gift: Margaret
takes if living at Josiah's death; her issue take if she has predeceased. The word "or" makes the
substitution express and unambiguous — the formulation a careful 1924 draftsman uses for a niece
because s.33 affords no statutory fallback.

**Trustee Act 1925, s.27** — Allows a personal representative to advertise in the *London Gazette*
for creditors and claimants; after two months, the representative who distributes is protected from
unknown creditors. The Gazette advertisement for any solicitor who took Josiah's testamentary
instructions falls within standard post-death estate administration.

**Vale Mill** — A stone weaving mill above Ribchester, on the hillside above the Ribble valley
floor. Owned through the Eccleston estate; managed by Walter Eccleston for fifteen years. Working
short time throughout; mortgaged; Walter's sale contract conditioned on the estate grant.

**Winckley Square** — A Georgian square in Preston, developed from 1799, by the early twentieth
century the centre of the town's legal profession. Garside & Prescott occupies a town house on the
square.

**Wills Act 1837 s.9** — The statutory formality requirements for a valid will (and, by s.10, a
codicil): in writing; signed by the testator; signature made or acknowledged in the presence of two
witnesses present at the same time; each witness attests and signs in the testator's presence.

**Arthur Prescott** — Junior partner at Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston. Age thirty-eight. Sixteen years with the firm; twelve as a qualified solicitor; articles under Garside. POV character throughout. His professional faith — absorbed from Garside — is that the file does not forget.

**Henry Garside** — Senior partner at Garside & Prescott. Approximately seventy-four. Drew Josiah's will in 1919 and the first codicil in 1924. Trained Prescott. Dies at his desk in December 1926. His handwriting is in the strong-room register for twenty years; his file discipline is what wins the case.

**Josiah Eccleston** — Cotton spinner, magistrate, owner of Eccleston Hall and Vale Mill, Ribchester. Died October 1926, aged eighty-one, in the east bedroom at the Hall. Client of Garside & Prescott since the 1890s.

**Walter Eccleston** — Josiah's nephew, approximately fifty. Manager of Vale Mill for fifteen years; expected to inherit under the will. Forger of the second codicil. Never POV. Always says 'and her issue' when quoting the first codicil in dialogue.

**Margaret Fairhurst** — Josiah's niece (collateral). Died May 1925, predeceasing Josiah. Residuary beneficiary under the first codicil ('or her issue'); her issue are Nora and George Fairhurst.

**Edgar Fairhurst** — Schoolmaster, Preston. Husband of the late Margaret. Lives at Bank Parade with Nora (19) and George (16). Enters the caveat; cannot fund the probate action; cooperates fully.

**Nora Fairhurst** — Older child of Margaret and Edgar. Nineteen at Josiah's death; minor (majority 21). Appears at Bank Parade with opinions and a voice. Thread 3 (the children) runs through her.

**George Fairhurst** — Younger child of Margaret and Edgar. Sixteen at Josiah's death; minor (majority 21).

**Garside & Prescott** — Preston solicitors, Winckley Square. Holds every instrument Josiah Eccleston executed in thirty years. Carries the probate action at its own risk; costs recoverable only on winning.

**Eccleston deed box** — Black painted steel, 'ECCLESTON' in white on the lid, label in Garside's copper-plate. Holds the will and first codicil in the strong-room. Inventoried at the Hall one week after Josiah's death: no second codicil in it at that time.

**Strong-room register** — Cloth-bound ledger, maintained in Garside's hand since 1906, recording every deposit and withdrawal from the strong-room: date column, document described, clerk's signature. The September 19, 1926 entry — first codicil fetched, read entire at the Hall, returned same day — is load-bearing.

**Despatch-box attendance note** — Contemporaneous note in Prescott's hand from approximately one week after Josiah's death; records the despatch-box contents item by item; no codicil of any kind listed. Walter Eccleston was present.

**Thurston & Marsh** — Walter Eccleston's solicitors, Preston. Produce the second codicil. Act for Walter in the probate action.

**Bank Parade** — Early-Victorian terrace, Avenham district, Preston, above the River Ribble. Edgar Fairhurst's address; named in the first codicil as Margaret Fairhurst's address.

**Vale Mill** — Stone weaving mill above Ribchester. Owned through the Eccleston estate; managed by Walter Eccleston fifteen years. Working short time; mortgaged. Walter's sale contract conditioned on the estate grant.

**Eccleston Hall** — Josiah Eccleston's residence, half a mile up the valley from Vale Mill. Moderate estate. No typewriter; Josiah used the mill office for correspondence.

**Mr. Booth** — Managing clerk of Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston. Has run the firm's office since 1911; knows where every document is. After Garside's death the firm is Prescott and Mr. Booth. Does not editorialize; acts.

**Thurston & Marsh** — Preston solicitors acting for Walter Eccleston. They produce the second codicil in early November 1926 with a covering letter claiming it was found privately among the testator's papers. They eventually take instructions to abandon the action.

**Thomas Birch** — Carrier of Ribchester; named as first attesting witness on the forged second codicil. Died of influenza 28 September 1926, six weeks before the codicil was produced — beyond examination. His genuine signatures on earlier estate papers exist for comparison.

**Frederick Hallett** — Jobbing gardener, formerly of a lane near Salesbury; named as second attesting witness on the forged second codicil. Sailed to Canada in mid-October 1926 without leaving a fixed address — beyond examination. His genuine signatures on earlier estate papers exist for comparison. The application for a commission to examine him in Canada fails.

**Caveat (Eccleston estate)** — Entered by Edgar Fairhurst at the Probate Registry on 8 November 1926, on Prescott's advice. Formally joins the dispute over the second codicil; a solemn-form action in the Probate Division is now inevitable.

**Solemn-form action** — Probate in solemn form: a contested proceeding before the Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division of the High Court in which the propounder of a will or codicil must prove due execution and other requirements, with the opposing party having full rights of challenge. The hearing is in London; evidence is managed from Preston.

**Walter attendance note** — Prescott's contemporaneous attendance note recording Walter Eccleston's unannounced visit to Garside & Prescott in early November 1926: his cover story (found codicil in despatch-box after inventory; Josiah typed it at mill office), his misquotation ('arrangement for Margaret and her issue'), and the despatch-box timeline as Walter described it.

**Eccleston Index** — Three-page bound list in Garside's hand of every Eccleston legal matter and instrument, from the first conveyance (March 1884) to the first codicil (15 March 1924). Pulled from the firm's cabinets by Prescott in Ch 3 as the first step in establishing the habit of exclusive use of Garside & Prescott.

**Garside Attendance Note, 15 March 1924** — Garside's internal attendance note recording Josiah Eccleston's instructions for the first codicil: the reconciliation with Margaret Fairhurst; the codicil revoking clause 4 and giving the residue to 'Mrs. Margaret Fairhurst or her issue absolutely'; the annotation 'Or her issue: collateral; substitutional form required.' A designated corroborating exhibit (record id: garside-1924-attendance-note).

**Vale Mill mortgage, 1921** — Mortgage on Vale Mill, dated 1921, with a Blackburn bank. Prepared by Garside & Prescott on Josiah Eccleston's instructions, 17 March 1921. Keeps the mill running during the cotton slump; its debt bears on Walter's financial position throughout the book.

**Blackburn bank** — The bank that held the Vale Mill mortgage from 1921. Named in the Garside & Prescott file; otherwise not a character in the action.

**Josiah's letter to Margaret (15 March 1924)** — Personal letter from Josiah Eccleston to his niece Margaret Fairhurst, written the day the first codicil was executed. Confirms the reconciliation as deliberately settled and final: 'I did not act in a soft mood that I expected to revise.' Original taken by Prescott as Exhibit A in the probate action. Registered record id: josiah-letter-to-margaret-mar1924.

**administrator pendente lite** — Neutral Preston accountant appointed by the court to manage the Eccleston estate during the pending probate action. Can collect rents and dividends and pay estate debts but cannot distribute anything to either side. His monthly figures on the estate's condition are produced in the proceedings — the reader's window onto the mill's deterioration.

**Bank Parade (Fairhurst house)** — The Fairhurst household at Bank Parade, Avenham, Preston. Rented. A schoolmaster's house: books throughout, exercise books on the hall table. Edgar Fairhurst, Nora (19), and George (16) live here. One upstairs window with a lamp lit in the early dark of late November 1926.

**Exhibit A** — The designation given by Prescott to Josiah's 15 March 1924 letter to Margaret Fairhurst, on taking the original from Edgar Fairhurst at Bank Parade.

**Elias Beckett (Mr. E. Beckett)** — Josiah Eccleston's steward at Eccleston Hall. Present at the September 1926 reading of the first codicil, as named in the strong-room register entry in Garside's hand. One of the two household members in attendance.

**Probate Division Hearing (sixth of October 1927)** — The fixed hearing date for the solemn-form action to prove the will and codicils in the Probate Division of the High Court. Set down in December 1926; 290 days from December 20, 1926. The monotonic countdown begins here.

**September Register Entry (19 September 1926)** — Garside's entry in the strong-room register recording the fetch and return of the first codicil: read entire (including date) at Josiah's request to the assembled family; signed H.G. Verbatim text registered as record id 'september-register-entry'. Load-bearing for door (h) and, later, for the discovery (Ch 17).

**Dr. Ainsworth** — The Prescott family or firm doctor who attended Garside after his death in December 1926 and confirmed the cause as heart failure.

**Letter-book (Garside & Prescott)** — The firm's bound outgoing-correspondence ledger; each entry carries the date, addressee, reference, and a margin column listing enclosures. Maintained by Garside at the same standard as the strong-room register. The Eccleston entries 1924–1926 are reviewed in Chapter 6; they confirm that no text of the first codicil was ever transmitted to any external party.

**Edgar Fairhurst letter, 19 March 1924** — Garside & Prescott's letter to Edgar Fairhurst (Bank Parade) notifying him that Josiah had made a testamentary provision for Mrs. Fairhurst and her family, and that the firm holds the instrument. The letter does not quote or disclose the operative clause. Carbon in the firm's letter-book; original at Bank Parade.

**Prescott working note — firm channel (14 January 1927)** — Working note written by Prescott establishing that no request for the text of the 1924 codicil was ever received from Thurston & Marsh, Walter Eccleston, or any other party; and that R5 transmitted only the date. Dated, placed at front of file, to go into the brief to counsel. Door (e) — the firm — closed.

**Garside letter to Edgar Fairhurst (19 March 1924)** — Garside's letter notifying Edgar Fairhurst that Josiah had made a testamentary provision for Mrs. Fairhurst and her family. Gives no operative clause, no wording from the first codicil. In the firm's letter-book carbon; never left the office. Established in Ch 6 as evidence that the terms of the first codicil were never communicated to any Fairhurst party.

**Josiah Eccleston's last letter to the firm (8 June 1926)** — Josiah's final letter to Garside & Prescott, about the farm tenancy; handwriting notably more effortful than prior correspondence. After this date the letter-book is blank until the death notices. First examined in Ch 6.

**Prescott working note — firm channel finding (14 January 1927)** — File item written by Prescott on 14 January 1927 establishing as a record item that no request for the text of the 1924 codicil was ever received from any party; R5 transmitted the date only. Dated and placed at front of file; will go into the brief to counsel. Door (e) closed.

**letter-book carbon of R5** — The firm's outgoing-correspondence carbon of R5 (firm notice letter of 2 November 1926), extracted by Prescott in Ch 6 and clipped separately as an exhibit for the brief to counsel. Corroborates that only the date of the first codicil was transmitted to Thurston & Marsh.

**Josiah Eccleston's 1926 pocket diary** — Small leather-covered pocket diary, Josiah's handwriting; sparse entries throughout; last mill entry 14 May 1926; last entry 14 August 1926 (mundane: weather and steward's visit); blank thereafter. Produced through the administrator, examined by Prescott 22–27 January 1927. Contains no reference to any testamentary act.

**Josiah to Nora Fairhurst, 3 April 1926** — Warm personal letter from Josiah to his grandniece Nora Fairhurst; confirms the reconciliation with the Fairhurst family as settled and permanent ('I am very glad we have the matter settled between us, and I mean it to stay settled'). Corroborating exhibit; record id: josiah-nora-letter-april-1926.

**Garside covering letter, 14 March 1924** — Three-sentence letter from Garside to Josiah transmitting the engrossed first codicil for execution; written for the record, more precise than the occasion required; witness requirements stated in full; corroborating exhibit; record id: garside-covering-letter-march-1924.

**Prescott working note, 27 January 1927** — Formal file item placed at front of working file; closes door (f) — the habit; establishes that every Josiah Eccleston instrument for forty years was made through Garside & Prescott, the last being the first codicil; record id: prescott-working-note-jan27-1927.

**Dr. Cullis** — Josiah Eccleston's physician in 1926; noted in the diary as visiting in February (two words, apparently routine).

**Door (f) — the habit** — One of the nine enumerated innocent explanations for the second codicil, closed in Ch 7: the lifelong habit of making all instruments through the firm of Garside & Prescott, and the silence of Josiah's diary and correspondence on any change of mind after March 1924. Closed by Prescott's working note of 27 January 1927.

**London Gazette advertisement (Eccleston)** — Advertisement placed by Garside & Prescott in the London Gazette (week of 3 February 1927) inviting any solicitor who had acted for Josiah Eccleston on any testamentary instrument, codicil, or revocation after March 1924 to communicate with the firm. Produced no response. A record item in the action.

**Lancashire solicitor canvass** — Circular letters sent by Garside & Prescott on 3 February 1927 to all 46 Lancashire solicitors' firms known to practise in the county, seeking evidence of testamentary instructions from Josiah Eccleston after March 1924. All 46 firms replied; every reply was negative. Completed 24 February 1927. Closes door (d) — the profession.

**profession canvass note (24 February 1927)** — Consolidated working file note by Prescott (registered record: prescott-canvass-note-feb1927), dated 24 February 1927. Establishes as a permanent file item that no solicitor in Lancashire took testamentary instructions from Josiah Eccleston after the execution of the first codicil, 15 March 1924.

**Hadfield, Mr.** — Neutral Preston accountant appointed administrator pendente lite for the Eccleston estate. Keeps the estate's monthly accounts; meets Prescott at Vale Mill in Ch 09. Reports that the mill's reserves will last approximately four months at the current rate and that the sale contract is in place but will not be extended again.

**Naylor** — Foreman at Vale Mill, Ribchester. Keeper of the mill daybook. When asked, produces the daybook showing Josiah Eccleston's last confirmed mill visit: 14 May 1926. Has habitually distinguished 'Mr. Walter Eccleston' from Josiah for fifteen years.

**Vale Mill daybook** — Working log kept in the Vale Mill counting house, bound in green boards, entries in Naylor the foreman's running hand. Load-bearing document: records Josiah Eccleston's last confirmed mill visit as 14 May 1926 (entry: 'Mr. Eccleston visited, inspected the shed, attended the office; May 14, 1926.').

**Vale Mill office typewriter** — Underwood Standard typewriter in the mill office, Ribchester. Worn: e-slug sits a fraction high, full-stop key worn across its face. The only typewriter at the estate's premises (Eccleston Hall has none). Identified by Prescott on site, early March 1927; not touched. Document-examiner comparison with the second codicil is the subject of Ch 10 (door a — the machine).

**A. J. Croft** — Document examiner, Manchester; practising since 1912; retained by Garside & Prescott in January 1927 to examine the typewriting of the propounded second codicil. His eleven-page report (March 1927) concludes the codicil was typed on the mill-office Underwood Standard, identified by typeface design and four concurrent individual machine defects.

**Croft report** — Eleven-page typewriting examination report by A. J. Croft, March 1927; identifies the second codicil as the product of the Vale Mill office Underwood Standard typewriter; eliminates all solicitors' office machines and confirms the Hall has no typewriter; explicitly limits its finding to machine identification (not operator or date).

**door (a) — the machine** — Closed Ch 10. The second codicil was typed on the Underwood Standard typewriting machine at Vale Mill office, Ribchester — identified by Croft's examination from typeface design and four individual machine characteristics. No other machine matches. The Hall had no typewriter.

**Eccleston estate sale contract** — Conditional sale contract (43 pages, standard post-1925 form) for the mill and Eccleston estate to a textile firm; completion conditioned at clause 22 on a grant of representation establishing the vendor's title. Two extension memoranda granted (January and March 1927); no third extension expected. Without the grant the sale cannot complete.

**Mr. Holt** — Walter Eccleston's bank manager. Named in R4. Recipient of nine letters from Walter (1924–1927) in which Walter assures the bank the estate will come through; his five replies grow shorter over time. His correspondence is produced through discovery as financial exhibits in the action.

**Walter Holt correspondence** — Nine letters from Walter Eccleston to his bank manager Mr. Holt (spring 1924 – January 1927) and five replies; pattern throughout: Walter assures the bank that the codicil will not stand and the estate will come through. Filed as financial exhibits in Ch 11.

**extension memoranda** — Two standard-form memoranda extending the completion date of the Eccleston estate sale contract: first (January 1927) and second (15 March 1927), each granting a further eight weeks. No third extension expected.

**Atkinson** — Ribchester solicitor, agricultural conveyancing practice; engaged by Prescott in April 1927 for local inquiry into Frederick Hallett's whereabouts and departure for Canada.

**Mitchell** — London counsel instructed by Garside & Prescott; filed the commission application to the Probate Division to examine Frederick Hallett in Canada (April 1927).

**Ogden** — Farmer on the Salesbury road near Ribchester; Frederick Hallett's last employer before his departure for Canada in early October 1926.

**commission application** — Application to the Probate Division for a commission to examine Frederick Hallett in Canada. Filed by Mitchell, end of April 1927. Discloses absence of fixed Canadian address. Refused in Ch 14 — the calendar-against-Prescott beat.

**instrument index** — Garside's index ledger, maintained since 1906, recording the name, trade, address, and instrument for every witness engaged in connection with any document the firm drew. The index is what yields the authenticated signatures of Birch and Hallett in Ch 12.

**Vale Mill Mortgage (1921)** — £8,500 at 4.5% per annum, secured on Vale Mill's plant, machinery and goodwill; held by County Bank, Blackburn; Garside's drafting; interest payable quarterly; principal untouched as of spring 1927.

**Conditional Sale Contract (Vale Mill Estate)** — Contract for sale of Vale Mill and Eccleston Hall estate to a textile firm; completion conditioned on clause 24: 'a grant of representation establishing the vendor's title to the said estate and hereditaments.' Two eight-week extension memoranda granted (January 1927 and 15 March 1927); no third extension anticipated.

**Mr. Holt** — Walter Eccleston's bank manager; recipient of Walter's nine banking letters (spring 1924–January 1927); present only through correspondence. Named in R4.

**SS Aurania** — Vessel on the Liverpool–Quebec assisted-passage route. Frederick Hallett sailed aboard her in the second week of October 1926, departing Ribchester and leaving no fixed address in Canada.

**Commission application (Hallett)** — Application by Garside & Prescott to the Probate Division for a commission to examine Frederick Hallett in Canada on the question of due execution of the second codicil. Filed by Mitchell (London counsel) in the last week of April 1927 with the address difficulty disclosed; outcome decided in Ch 14.

**Atkinson** — Ribchester solicitor, agricultural conveyancing practice; engaged by Prescott to trace Frederick Hallett. His two-page report identified Hallett's last employer (Ogden, farmer on the Salesbury road) and his departure for Canada on the SS Aurania.

**Mitchell** — London counsel instructed by Garside & Prescott. Recommended and filed the commission application for Hallett in the Probate Division.

**Walter's visit to Bank Parade (May 1927)** — Walter Eccleston called at the Fairhurst house on Bank Parade in early May 1927. He wrote first, came on a Tuesday (approx. 8 May), was kind, and told Nora and George what their mother had been to receive — using the phrase 'and her issue' (Misquotation 3). He expressed sorrow that it had come to a legal action.

**Hadfield's April 1927 account** — Monthly estate account from the administrator pendente lite covering April 1927: loss of just under £300 against projected income; third consecutive quarter-day of mortgage interest paid from estate capital; mill working approximately 3.5 days per week; no improvement expected before late summer.

**Miller Park** — Public park in Preston's Avenham district, above the River Ribble, on Prescott's walking route between Winckley Square and Bank Parade.

**Elias Beckett** — Steward at Eccleston Hall for fourteen years; principal deponent in Ch 15 establishing that Josiah Eccleston did not leave the Hall after 14 May 1926. Keeps a household journal whose September 15 entry corroborates the strong-room register.

**Mrs. E. Hargreaves** — Cook at Eccleston Hall for nine years; deponent in Ch 15 confirming Josiah did not leave the house in August 1926.

**Ellen Bradshaw (Miss E. Bradshaw)** — Housemaid at Eccleston Hall for two years; deponent in Ch 15 confirming Josiah did not leave the house in August 1926.

**Dr. T. Hartley** — Josiah Eccleston's physician in Ribchester; his certified medical notes for August 1926 (four visits, 5th through 27th) confirm progressive frailty; professional opinion that Josiah was not capable of a twelve-mile journey that month.

**Beckett's household diary (household journal)** — Clothbound household journal kept by steward Elias Beckett at Eccleston Hall; entries for 14 May 1926 (Josiah's last mill visit) and 15 September 1926 (Garside and Prescott's visit with the deed-box) are key corroborating documents in the Hall chapter.

**Hearing brief** — The complete numbered exhibit bundle assembled by Prescott at Garside & Prescott in July 1927 for dispatch to London counsel. Twenty-three exhibits as of 16 July 1927; indexed in schedule order by Mr. Booth.

**Mr. A.V. Mathers** — Document examiner retained by the plaintiff (Garside & Prescott) in the Eccleston probate action. Finds the signature on the propounded second codicil not genuine to the hand of the testator. His opinion directly contradicts Whitmore's; neither resolves.

**Mr. G.T. Whitmore** — Document examiner retained by the defendant (Walter Eccleston via Thurston & Marsh). Finds the signature on the propounded second codicil genuine. His opinion directly contradicts Mathers's; neither resolves.

**Hearing Brief** — The complete brief prepared by Garside & Prescott and dispatched to London counsel on 15 August 1927. Argument structure: recital variance as spine; nine door-closures as exclusion argument; authorship from exhibit 14; handwriting evidence stated as subsidiary and inconclusive. 31+ pages; exhibits in schedule order.

**Keane** — Plaintiff's counsel (K.C.) at the October 1927 solemn-form hearing; approximately forty-five; retained by Garside & Prescott after the brief was dispatched in August 1927.

**Ardern** — Defendant's counsel at the October 1927 solemn-form hearing; approximately twenty-five years at the bar; instructed to withdraw the claim after Walter reads the executed first codicil.

**Solemn form (probate in solemn form)** — The contentious probate process before the Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division of the High Court; as opposed to common form (uncontested). The action is to prove the second codicil in solemn form; the result is that the second codicil fails and the earlier instruments stand.

**Letters of administration with the will annexed** — The grant of authority to administer an estate when the will has no valid executor — here, because the first codicil revoked Walter's executorship entire and the second codicil failed. Granted to Edgar Fairhurst as guardian of both minor children in November 1927.

**Garside & Prescott** — Preston solicitors' firm on Winckley Square; drew the will (1919) and first codicil (1924); sole actors in the probate action; Prescott sole partner from December 1926 after Garside's death at his desk.
