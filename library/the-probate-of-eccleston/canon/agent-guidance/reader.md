# Reader guidance — The Probate of Eccleston

## Intended audience

Adult readers of literary detective fiction — specifically readers who know and value the Golden Age
fair-play tradition (Sayers, Crofts, Christie) and are comfortable with a patient, procedural pace
in a literary register. This is not a cozy mystery and not a thriller. The reader is invited to
solve the puzzle before Prescott does; the book assumes they will try. The satisfaction is
intellectual and emotional in equal measure, and the two are not in tension.

## Brief-adherence checks to perform

In addition to general craft review, the Reader must answer each of the following as a yes/no with
evidence:

**1. Fair-play contract: is every necessary fact on the page before the discovery?**
R2 ("or her issue") must appear verbatim before the discovery chapter. R3's recital ("and her
issue") must appear verbatim before the discovery chapter. R4 must appear in full as a financial
exhibit before the three-quarter mark. If any of these are absent or appear only at or after the
discovery, the fair-play contract is broken. This is a **structural failure, severity: critical**.

**2. Walter's misquotation — exactly three planted occasions before the discovery?**
The brief pins the count and locations: R4 printed as exhibit (financial thread chapter); Walter to
Prescott at the early caveat meeting (mid-sentence); Walter to Nora and George at Bank Parade.
Fewer than three: the fair-play plant is too thin. More than three: the variance is over-signalled
and the fair-play effect is compromised. Either count is a finding; extra occasions are the more
common risk.

**3. Single POV discipline: every chapter in Prescott's perspective?**
Flag any passage that accesses another character's interior, or that narrates an event Prescott
could not have witnessed and no period-true channel has relayed to him. This includes any scene
centred on Walter's private actions, any interior access to Edgar or Nora, and any scene at the
mill or Hall that Prescott did not visit.

**4. The compounding mechanism: does every chapter close a door?**
Each chapter should close or materially narrow at least one of the nine enumerated doors (machine,
Josiah's presence, witnesses, profession, firm, habit, despatch-box, September reading, date
asymmetry). Flag any chapter that consists entirely of atmosphere, recap, or procedural activity
without a foreclosure. Flag any door that is re-walked after it has been closed.

**5. Calendar monotonicity: do the days decrease without exception?**
The days remaining to the hearing must be named in every chapter and must decrease monotonically
(with one exception: the refused commission, where the calendar moves against Prescott). Flag any
chapter where the days are absent, or where they appear to have increased.

**6. The ending is complete: does it answer every opened question?**
The last movement must resolve: (a) Was the second codicil a forgery? (b) Who made it? (c) How is
the authorship proven? (d) What happens to the estate? (e) What happens to Walter? (f) What happens
to the children and the firm? Any withheld answer is a **structural failure**. The clean, fully-
resolved ending is the contract of the book; the Reader must not flag it as over-neat or suggest
re-complicating it.

**7. Document fidelity: does every verbatim quotation of R1–R5 match the registered text exactly?**
Call `read_record` for each of R1–R5 and compare. Any divergence — even a single word — is a
**critical finding**. The sole exception: Walter speaking from memory in dialogue (not quoting a
document as narrator) always says "and her issue" — this is correct and must not be flagged as a
continuity error.

**8. Quotation ceiling: is the verbatim ceiling respected?**
R1, R2, and R3 should each appear in full at most two or three times before the discovery chapter.
After the discovery, they should be referenced only by name, date, or brief fragment. Excess
full-text quotation of the same document is a continuity finding (over-quotation as filler).

## What "good" looks like for this book

A good chapter of *The Probate of Eccleston* opens in the middle of a professional act, proceeds
through procedural accumulation that closes one named door, and ends with the foreclosure fixed —
then stops. The legal vocabulary is used precisely and without gloss. The period texture is present
without becoming a standing display of research. Document inserts are clean: they stand, the prose
continues from the same narrative level, nothing follows that explains the insert.

The register is correct when it reads like a man who notices things precisely but does not explain
what noticing them means. When the displacement object (the strong-room register, the file built too
carefully, Bank Parade in a specific light) carries emotional weight without a sentence naming the
emotion — that is the register working.

The Garside thread is working when it accumulates through small, specific discoveries across the
middle movement — details in the file that were more precise than occasion required — without any
summary paragraph declaring their significance. It is *not* working if it is announced ("He thought
of Garside again") or if it is concentrated into one scene that functions as a grief chapter.

The Walter misquotations are working as fair-play when a reader paying careful attention will notice
the "and/or" variance each time Walter speaks — but only if they are paying attention, and only
because they are comparing against R2 which they have already read, not because the narration flags
the contrast. The narration must never flag it before the discovery.

## What to flag explicitly if the manuscript drifts

**1. Extended abstract interiority at chapter openings.**
If more than four sentences of Prescott's reflection appear before the first sensory anchor or
professional act, name it as register drift. The literary attractor produces this; the book does
not want it.

**2. Gloss paragraphs after document inserts.**
If the paragraph following any document insert explains what the document "really" meant or
summarises its significance, cut it or flag it as a failure mode. This is both a register failure
and a fair-play violation — the reader must encounter the document uninterpreted.

**3. Grief articulated directly.**
If Prescott's feelings about Garside are expressed in more than one direct sentence (not
displacement), flag it as over-articulation. The displacement mechanism is doing that work; direct
sentiment is the wrong register for this book.

**4. The ending re-complicated.**
If the final movement withholds any of the six answers in check 6, or introduces ambiguity about
the forgery's authorship, flag it as a structural failure. The editor and compression passes must
not re-open the ending. The clean resolution is the book's contract with its reader.

**5. Thriller-pace drift.**
If chapters have shortened to 1,000 words or fewer without document content to explain the
compression, if physical peril has entered, or if the pace has become punchy and cliffhanger-
dominant, name it as register drift in the wrong direction.

**6. Walter as villain.**
If the narration (through Prescott's observations) has tilted toward contempt for Walter — if his
exposure is satisfying rather than painful — flag the tonal balance. The brief requires that the
exposure hurt. A Walter who is simply a bad man does not hurt.

## What to applaud

- **The displacement mechanism working in accumulation.** If Prescott's grief for Garside surfaces
  consistently through specific professional discoveries across the middle movement — the file built
  too well, the procedure followed beyond what was required — without announcement or summary,
  defend this from any compression impulse that would trim the individual instances for efficiency.
  The accumulation is the effect; removing the instances removes the effect.

- **The plain sentence that lands hard.** If the manuscript has a sentence that carries
  disproportionate weight because everything before it has been exact and controlled, defend it from
  any impulse to contextualise or cushion it. A gloss sentence after it destroys it.

- **The fair-play subtlety of the Walter occasions.** If the "and/or" variance in Walter's dialogue
  is present but not flagged by the narration, and if a reader paying attention will notice it while
  a reader not paying attention will not — the fair-play contract is being honoured. Defend this
  subtlety from any impulse to make the variance more visible before the discovery.

- **The compounding mechanism producing visible accumulation.** If each chapter's foreclosure
  visibly tightens a prior chapter's established fact — if the record's growth is perceptible as
  accumulation rather than repetition — name this as structural success and defend it.

- **Procedural texture used without explanation.** If the legal vocabulary (engrossment, the
  recital, letters of administration with the will annexed) is used correctly and in context,
  without a glossing sentence, and if the reader's understanding is carried by context rather than
  by explanation, this is the register working. Do not flag unexplained legal terms as inaccessible;
  they are period texture.
