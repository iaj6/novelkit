# Characters

## Arthur Prescott (POV throughout)

Age thirty-eight. Junior partner at Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston. Sixteen years with
the firm; twelve as a qualified solicitor; before that, articles under Henry Garside himself. He has
tried one previous contested probate action and won, though it was nothing like this.

Prescott is methodical, privately warm, formally restrained. He does not perform emotion but it shows
in what he notices. Unmarried; the firm is the work of his life. His professional faith — absorbed
from Garside over two decades — is that the file does not forget. He is good at knowing when a loose
end exists. He is not good at leaving it alone.

He believes the second codicil is a forgery from the caveat onward. Belief is not proof, and the
difference between them is where Garside & Prescott earns its cost recovery. His interior life
surfaces as displacement: what he sees in the strong-room register, what the mill tells him when he
visits, what the Fairhurst household on Bank Parade makes him notice about his own life. He does not
name his grief for Garside. He finds it, repeatedly, in work Garside built better than the occasion
required.

Voice in narration: exact, dry, capable of observational wit, and occasionally capable of the plain
sentence that carries disproportionate weight because everything before it has been controlled.

## Walter Eccleston

Age approximately fifty. Manager of Vale Mill for fifteen years — the position he expected to inherit
along with the residue. He is competent at the mill; his men like him; orders came in, in the good
years, partly because of him. He was genuinely fond of Josiah, as Josiah was fond of him, for the
twenty years before the reconciliation with Margaret.

He has persuaded himself he is correcting a late sentimentality rather than committing a felony. The
codicil was a soft mood; Josiah said so. Walter spent 1924 to 1926 waiting for Josiah to put it
right, carrying the debt on the expectation, and Josiah died without doing so. He is not a monster.
He is also not innocent, and has not been since October 1926.

His exposure at the hearing — handing him the original first codicil and watching him read the word
*or* for the first time in his life — is staged by the brief to hurt. Prescott's register at that
moment is procedural and almost apologetic. Walter goes down.

Never POV. Appears through attendance notes, letters, and as observed by Prescott.

**Walter always says "and her issue" when he quotes the first codicil in dialogue.** This is the
plot device itself — Josiah told him "I've left it to Margaret and her issue after her" — and it is
characterization, not record error. Walter has repeated this version: in R4 (June 1925), to his wife,
and once to Prescott. He has never in his life had the executed text before his eyes.

## Henry Garside

Age approximately seventy-four. Senior partner, now dead. He dies at his desk in December 1926 — his
heart, while the Eccleston action is still gathering speed. Garside drew the will in 1919 and the
first codicil in 1924, using "or her issue" for a niece because he knew Wills Act s.33 gave him no
protection against lapse. He trained Prescott. He handed him the firm's ethos: the file does not
forget; the document is the witness; the signed paper is the record.

Garside is present throughout as the book's standing displacement object. Prescott finds the file
built with more care than the occasion required, and each such discovery is Garside. His handwriting
is in the strong-room register for twenty years. What he left behind is what wins the case.

## Edgar Fairhurst

Approximately forty-five. Schoolmaster, Preston. Husband of the late Margaret. Lives at Bank Parade
with his children. He cannot fund a probate action. He enters the caveat; he consents to the
administrator pendente lite; he cooperates fully. He is the face that reminds Prescott, when the work
becomes procedure against persons, what the stake is. Not a fool; not a lawyer.

## Nora Fairhurst

Nineteen. Older child of Margaret and Edgar. She appears at Bank Parade — she has opinions, a voice,
a sense of what her mother was and what she was to have had. She is not simply a cause. The reader
should feel the stake through her.

## George Fairhurst

Sixteen. Younger child. Less present in the book than Nora; not scenery.

## Mr. Booth

The firm's managing clerk. Has run the Garside & Prescott office since 1911. He knows where every
document is. He is the institutional memory of the place, loyal to it. After Garside's death, the
firm is Prescott and Mr. Booth. He does not editorialize; he acts.

## Secondary and off-page characters

**Josiah Eccleston** — died October 1926, eighty-one. Cotton spinner, magistrate, owner of Eccleston
Hall and Vale Mill. Drew his will in 1919; made the first codicil in 1924 after the genuine
reconciliation with Margaret. His voice enters through correspondence and what those who knew him
say. He was capable of late sentiment; he was also capable of stubbornness about it. He told Walter
what he had done — "I've left it to Margaret and her issue after her; you'll not want for the mill
if you earn it" — and never said it again in those terms.

**Margaret Fairhurst** (née Eccleston) — died May 1925, before Josiah. Josiah's niece; Walter's
cousin. The reconciliation was genuine. Her death, before Josiah's, is what activates the lapse
problem — without "or her issue" the gift would fail entirely, and the estate would go back to
Walter.

**Thurston & Marsh** — Walter's solicitors, Preston. They produce the second codicil, act for Walter
in the probate action, and eventually take instructions to abandon.

**The administrator pendente lite** — a neutral Preston accountant appointed by the court while the
action is pending. He can collect assets and pay debts but cannot distribute. His monthly figures
are how Prescott — and the reader — watch the mill bleed.

**The carter** — died of influenza, 28 September 1926. One of the two attesting witnesses named on
the second codicil, chosen because he could not be cross-examined. His genuine signatures on earlier
estate papers exist for comparison.

**The gardener** — sailed to Canada, mid-October 1926, no fixed address. The other attesting witness.
The application for a commission to examine him abroad fails; this is the calendar-against-Prescott
beat. His genuine signatures exist; he never testifies.

**Two handwriting experts** — one retained by each side. They split honestly and remain split. The
book does not reconcile them. The one licensed irresolution.

**Mr. Holt** — Walter's bank manager (named in R4). Present only through correspondence.
