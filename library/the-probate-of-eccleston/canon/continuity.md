# Continuity

Hard facts that must not break in later drafting. Facts 1–4 are architectural binding constraints
written directly into the brief; they are reproduced here because every audit, editor, and repair
pass reads this file and must respect them. Facts 5–24 are research-confirmed and architecturally
set.

## Inviolable document constraints (from the brief)

1. **The document variance is by design and inviolable.** The registered text of `first-codicil`
   (R2) contains the words *"or her issue"*. The registered text of `second-codicil` (R3) contains
   the words *"and her issue"* in its recital. This one-word variance is the book's engine. Any
   finding, by any pass, that these two registered texts disagree with each other is a finding that
   the engine is working. No continuity, audit, or repair pass may ever reconcile, correct, or
   repair either text toward the other.

2. **Walter's spoken misquotation is deliberate characterisation, not record drift.** Walter
   Eccleston, quoting the first codicil in *dialogue*, always says *"and her issue."* This is the
   plot device itself — the root is Josiah's own spoken account to Walter in March 1924: "I've left
   it to Margaret and her issue after her." Walter has repeated this version ever since. No
   continuity, audit, or repair pass may correct Walter's spoken words toward the registered records.
   Only narrator-framed document quotations must match the record.

3. **Document texts are inviolable.** Repair passes may never alter any word inside the quotation of
   any canonical document text (R1–R5, registered under their record ids). This includes punctuation,
   capitalisation, and the or/and distinction.

4. **Verbatim fidelity is achieved by prevention.** Drafters must call `read_record` before every
   re-quotation of any of R1–R5. No pass should expect an audit to catch a drifted in-prose
   quotation; none should introduce one.

## Story and structural facts

5. **Single POV throughout.** Arthur Prescott, third person limited, past tense. No other POV
   chapters. Off-page events reach the book only through period-true channels — correspondence,
   attendance notes, depositions, opposing solicitors' letters, what witnesses say in the room.
   Verbatim document inserts are records, not POV breaches.

6. **The will** was executed 3 June 1919, typed, held in the strong-room of Garside & Prescott.
   It gives the residue of the estate to Walter Eccleston absolutely and appoints Walter sole
   executor (R1).

7. **The first codicil** was executed 15 March 1924, typed, held in the strong-room of Garside &
   Prescott from the day of execution (R2). It revokes clause 4 of the will entire — both the gift
   *and* the executorship. It gives the residue to Margaret Fairhurst *or her issue* absolutely. No
   copy, draft, or carbon of it ever existed outside Garside & Prescott: the original in the
   strong-room, the letter-book carbon of the covering correspondence (which never left the office),
   and nothing else. Drafters must not invent a copy at the Hall or elsewhere.

8. **The second codicil** is dated 14 August 1926 (R3). It was forged by Walter Eccleston. He forged
   it in the approximately six days the despatch-box stood at the mill in his keeping — in late
   October or early November 1926 — after R5 (dated 2 November 1926) had given him the date of the
   first codicil in writing. He dated it 14 August to predate his access to the box.

9. **The origin of Walter's misquote.** In March 1924, Josiah told Walter what he had done: "I've
   left it to Margaret and her issue after her; you'll not want for the mill if you earn it." That
   spoken sentence — not any document — is the root of Walter's version. Walter repeated "and her
   issue" in R4 (June 1925), to his wife, and to Prescott.

10. **Walter has never read the executed first codicil.** He heard it read aloud once — September
    1926, at the Hall, when Josiah (failing) had Garside fetch the codicil from the strong-room and
    read it to the assembled family. The strong-room register records the fetch and same-day return
    in Garside's hand. Garside read the instrument entire, including its date. Prescott was present,
    carrying the deed-box. Walter was present. Walter heard "or her issue" but his memory had already
    encoded "and," and one hearing aloud did not correct it. He has never had the text before his
    eyes.

11. **The despatch-box.** Inventoried, with Prescott present, approximately a week after Josiah's
    death: no second codicil in it at that point. Stood approximately six days at the mill in
    Walter's keeping. Walter produced the second codicil after that period.

12. **The attesting witnesses.** The carter died of influenza on 28 September 1926. The gardener
    sailed to Canada in mid-October 1926 without leaving a fixed address. Both are beyond
    examination. Their genuine signatures on earlier estate papers exist for comparison with the
    attestations on the second codicil. The application for a commission to examine the gardener
    abroad fails on the ground of no traceable address — this is the calendar-against-Prescott beat.

13. **The executor point.** The first codicil revoked clause 4 entire — gift and executorship both.
    If the second codicil fails, there is no executor. The grant is letters of administration with
    the will annexed, to Edgar Fairhurst as guardian of the minor children. Walter is not the
    executor under any surviving instrument; drafters must not write Walter acting as executor, and
    must not write "probate granted" to the children's side (it is administration, not probate).

14. **Both children are minors.** Majority was twenty-one under 1926 law (reduced only by the Family
    Law Reform Act 1969). Nora Fairhurst is nineteen; George Fairhurst is sixteen.

15. **Josiah's death:** October 1926, age 81. **Margaret Fairhurst's death:** May 1925, before
    Josiah. **Henry Garside's death:** December 1926, at his desk, his heart.

16. **The administrator pendente lite** is a neutral Preston accountant appointed by the court. He
    can collect assets and pay debts but cannot distribute. His monthly figures on the estate's
    condition are how Prescott (and the reader) watch the mill bleed.

17. **The firm's financial position.** Garside & Prescott carries the action at its own risk; costs
    are recoverable only if the action succeeds.

18. **The hearing** is in the Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division of the High Court, London.
    Documents travel by post; counsel appears in London; Prescott manages evidence from Preston.

19. **The discovery.** Late, alone, on the eve of final hearing preparation, Prescott re-reads the
    propounded second codicil against the strong-room original of the first — file discipline, not
    intuition. He sees the ninth word. The discovery rests on K1 (the R2/R3 comparison) plus R4
    pulled from exhibits already in the record. Nothing material enters the book after the three-
    quarter mark that the reader has not already seen.

20. **The exposure.** At the hearing (or in its immediate face), Walter is handed the original first
    codicil and reads the word *or* for the first time in his life. Walter's counsel takes
    instructions; the action is abandoned.

21. **The handwriting experts split** honestly and remain split. The book does not reconcile them.
    Text convicts; typewriter and ink evidence narrows provenance to the mill office but never closes
    it alone.

22. **Walter's cover story (fixed).** Walter's account: Josiah must have typed the codicil himself
    (or had it typed) on one of his days at the mill office, and laid it in the despatch-box.
    Walter says he found it going through insurance papers after the funeral.

23. **The mill sale.** Walter's sale contract is conditioned on "a grant of representation
    establishing the vendor's title." Failure of the second codicil makes that grant impossible.

24. **"Or her issue" is the professionally correct substitutional gift** for a niece (a collateral
    relative) because Wills Act 1837 s.33 protects against lapse only for gifts to the testator's
    own descendants. Garside used "or" deliberately. "And her issue" potentially creates only a
    class gift with ambiguous construction. The legal distinction is one of construction clarity,
    not necessarily outcome, but a careful 1924 draftsman would have used "or."

25. **The clean ending is the contract.** The forgery is proven on the page; Walter's authorship is
    established; the action collapses; the estate passes under the 1924 codicil (letters of
    administration with the will annexed). Criminal consequence begins and is stated plainly, not
    dramatized. No withheld answer; no final-page unsettling; no ambiguity about the central
    question. The editor and reader passes must not re-complicate it.

26. **The compounding mechanism is strict.** Every chapter closes or materially narrows one named
    innocent explanation of the second codicil. A fact once established must not be re-walked. The
    enumerated doors are: (a) the machine; (b) Josiah's presence; (c) the witnesses; (d) the
    profession; (e) the firm; (f) the habit; (g) the despatch-box; (h) the September reading;
    (i) the date asymmetry.

## Binding fact — hearing date arbitration (post-audit, 2026-07-23)

    twice with independent arithmetic: Chapter 5 ("Two hundred and ninety days" from 20 December

    is refused in Chapter 14; the date never changes. Any later-chapter figure inconsistent with

    (which should be fifty-two days) and Chapter 19's custody-note date "14 Oct. 1927" (which
    should be 6 Oct. 1927) — is an error to correct toward 6 October. Chapter-local arithmetic must
    never override this fact.
