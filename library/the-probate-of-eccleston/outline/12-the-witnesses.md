# Chapter 12 — The Witnesses

**One-line summary:** Prescott establishes the deaths and unavailability of both attesting witnesses: the carter's death is confirmed, his genuine signatures collected for comparison; the gardener has sailed to Canada without an address; Prescott submits an application for a commission to examine him.

*[NOTE: This chapter is Chapter 12. The Sale Contract chapter precedes as Chapter 11, ensuring the three pinned misquotations land in non-consecutive chapters: Ch 2, Ch 11, Ch 13.]*

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston; with brief external inquiries in Ribchester and Preston records. April 1927. A week of active inquiry followed by the drafting of the commission application.

## Chapter Shape
**Multi-scene — correspondence and inquiry.** Two parallel threads: the carter thread (confirmable; the death register exists) and the gardener thread (open; his whereabouts unknown). The chapter moves between them and settles into the commission application at the close.

## Ending Mode
**Mid-action-cut.** The chapter ends with the commission application submitted but unanswered. The outcome is not known; the clock is running.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Establish the status of both attesting witnesses and determine whether either can be examined. If not both, whether one can be.

**Obstacle:** The short answer is: neither can. The carter is dead. The gardener is in Canada without an address. But Prescott must establish these facts formally — a deposition from a registrar, a proper search — because "we cannot find him" is not an exhibit.

**Beats:**

1. **The witnesses identified from the codicil:** The second codicil's attestation clause names two witnesses. One is Thomas Rigg, carter, of Ribchester. One is Edward Hargreaves, jobbing gardener, formerly of Ribchester. Both are real men — Prescott confirms this from Josiah's earlier estate papers, where both men's signatures appear as witnesses to minor instruments years before.

2. **The carter — Thomas Rigg:** Prescott or Mr. Booth writes to the Ribchester parish register and the local registrar. The reply comes in within the week: Thomas Rigg, carter, died of influenza, 28 September 1926. The death is registered. Prescott obtains a copy of the death certificate. The date is 28 September 1926 — before Josiah's own death in October.

3. **Genuine signatures compared (the carter):** Among the Eccleston estate papers, Prescott locates two earlier instruments witnessed by Thomas Rigg: a property schedule from 1921, a document he signed as witness in 1918. His genuine signatures exist. Prescott sends these to the document examiner along with the attestation signatures from the second codicil. The examiner's brief report returns: the attestation signatures attributed to Rigg are consistent with his genuine hand; he cannot rule out forgery (the copies are good), but he cannot establish it either. The attestation cannot be broken on the signatures alone.

4. **The gardener — Edward Hargreaves:** More difficult. Hargreaves is alive (no death registered) but has left Ribchester. Prescott traces him to a reference from a local agent: sailed from Liverpool on an assisted passage to Canada in mid-October 1926, leaving no fixed address in Canada. His last known employer in Ribchester confirms the departure.

5. **The problem of the gardener:** Hargreaves's genuine signatures are also in the estate papers (an earlier instrument). Prescott compares them briefly with the attestation on the second codicil — also consistent; cannot be broken. But Hargreaves is alive, in Canada, and theoretically available to testify. In theory, a commission could be issued to examine him in Canada.

6. **Drafting the commission application:** Prescott instructs London counsel to apply to the Probate Division for a commission to examine Hargreaves in Canada. The application: the instrument, the witness's existence, the need for his testimony on due execution. The obvious difficulty — his address in Canada is unknown — is disclosed in the application. Prescott knows the odds are not good; but he is obliged to attempt it.

7. **The submission:** The application is submitted. The outcome is not yet known. Prescott notes the date; 172 days to the hearing; the commission, if it proceeds at all, will take time.

8. **The witness thread's logic:** What Prescott notes (without stating directly) is the shape of the witness situation. Both witnesses are beyond examination. The carter is dead. The gardener is untraceable. Both men were chosen precisely because they could never be cross-examined. The design of the attestation — who was chosen and when they disappeared — is visible to anyone who looks at it, but it cannot be stated as evidence. The text argument must carry the weight.

## Outcome / Turn
The carter is dead; his signatures are consistent but unbreakable. The gardener is in Canada; his signatures are consistent but unbreakable; a commission application has been submitted. Door (c) is open; the outcome of the commission is unknown.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
If the commission fails — if Hargreaves cannot be found in Canada — the attestation is unbreakable by testimony. The case must rest entirely on the text. Is the text evidence enough? Can the recital's error close the gap the witnesses leave open?
