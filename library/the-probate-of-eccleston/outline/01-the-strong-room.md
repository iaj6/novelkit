# Chapter 1 — The Strong-Room

**One-line summary:** On the week following Josiah Eccleston's death, Prescott opens the estate file with Garside: R1 and R2 are read in full, the September 1926 register entry surfaces, and Prescott's own inventory note confirms that the despatch-box contained no second codicil.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston. Late October 1926 — approximately one week after Josiah Eccleston's death. Autumn light short; the coal fire going in Prescott's room; the strong-room register open on the table.

## Chapter Shape
**Single scene — document examination.** Prescott and Garside in the office with the Eccleston deed box. One location; near real-time. No external action: the chapter is entirely the reading of documents and the examination of a register.

## Ending Mode
**Declarative-close.** The chapter ends on the plain statement of what has been established: the instruments are in order, the strong-room is exact, the estate waits on whatever comes next.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Establish exactly what testamentary instruments exist and what they say before advising on the estate.

**Obstacle:** None yet — the chapter is professional procedure, not conflict. The obstacle is the complexity of what the instruments have produced: no executor, two minor beneficiaries, a dead primary legatee.

**Beats:**

1. **Opening:** Prescott arrives at the office to find the Eccleston deed box already on the table — Garside has pulled it from the strong-room the morning after the news of Josiah's death. A brief note on the man (cotton spinner, magistrate, dead at eighty-one) and the firm's relationship with him: every instrument for thirty years. Garside as the presiding presence — his handwriting on the deed-box label.

2. **R1 read:** Garside opens the box and reads Will clause 4 aloud (R1 quoted in full as a set-off passage). Walter Eccleston, residue and sole executor. Prescott notes the date: 3 June 1919. He makes the necessary notes.

3. **R2 read:** The first codicil follows (R2 quoted in full as a set-off passage). 15 March 1924. Revokes clause 4 entire. "Or her issue absolutely." Garside explains the executor point — clause 4 revoked means no executor; if the first codicil stands, there will be no executor at all, and administration will go to the children's father as guardian. He notes "or her issue" as the professionally correct substitutional gift for a collateral niece — s.33 affords no protection. The point is made once, professionally, and not repeated.

4. **The lapse situation established:** Margaret Fairhurst died May 1925. Under the first codicil, the residue passes to her issue — her two children, Nora (nineteen) and George (sixteen), living with their father at Bank Parade. The firm knows them only by correspondence. Prescott notes that both are minors and majority is twenty-one.

5. **The strong-room register examined:** Prescott opens the register to check the codicil's custody history. There, in Garside's hand, is the entry from September 1926: the first codicil fetched from the strong-room, taken to Eccleston Hall, read to the assembled family (names recorded in the margin: Prescott himself, Walter, one or two others from the household), returned same day. Garside's signature. The date of the entry is in the margin column. This is Garside's discipline: he noted the purpose — Josiah, failing, had wanted no surprises — and the fact that the instrument was read entire.

6. **The inventory note:** Prescott finds, in the file's working correspondence, his own attendance note from the despatch-box inventory at the Hall, made approximately a week after Josiah's death. The box was opened in Walter's presence; Prescott listed every item; the list is in his hand. No second codicil in the box.

7. **Close:** Garside restores the instruments to the strong-room. The box is returned to the shelf. Prescott notes what remains to be done: letter to Thurston & Marsh (Walter's solicitors) to discuss proving the will in proper form; letter to the Fairhurst household. For now, the file is what it is — exact, complete, and in order.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The file is in perfect order. Two instruments: the will and the first codicil. The estate is Margaret's children's if the instruments stand. There is no executor. There is no second codicil.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
Walter Eccleston has managed the mill for fifteen years and expected to inherit it. He has not been heard from yet. What will he do?

---

## What Compounds

- **Door (g) — the despatch-box:** Opens. Prescott's own inventory note (already in the file) establishes the state of the box approximately one week after Josiah's death: no second codicil. This note is a contemporaneous record in Prescott's hand; it cannot be disputed.

- **Door (h) — the September reading:** Opens. The strong-room register entry surfaces for the first time: the first codicil was fetched, read entire to the assembled family at the Hall, and returned the same day. Who was present is recorded; the date is in the column. This will matter.

- **Prior chapter advanced:** None (first chapter).

- **Days to hearing:** Not yet set. No action is pending; no hearing date exists.
