# Chapter 4 — Bank Parade

**One-line summary:** Prescott visits Edgar Fairhurst at Bank Parade for the first time, meets Nora and George, explains the action and its costs, and arranges for an administrator pendente lite.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Bank Parade, Avenham district, Preston; and briefly, the walk there from Winckley Square. Late November 1926. An overcast afternoon; the Ribble below the parks; the terrace of early Victorian town houses, stone and brick, respectable and quiet.

## Chapter Shape
**Bottle — witness interview with domestic texture.** One location (the Fairhurst parlour, except for the opening transit); near real-time. Prescott visits, sits, speaks with Edgar and Nora; George briefly present. The chapter is the establishment of the human stake.

## Ending Mode
**Coda.** The chapter closes on Prescott's walk back to Winckley Square — the parks, the early dark, the children he has just met. He does not name what the visit has done to the file's abstraction. He walks back to the office and picks up the work.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Explain the nature and cost of the action to Edgar Fairhurst; take his instructions; discuss the administrator pendente lite; understand the family's situation.

**Obstacle:** Edgar is not a fool but he is not a lawyer, and the cost and uncertainty of what Prescott is proposing are real. He cannot fund the action; Prescott is proposing the firm carry it — costs recoverable only on winning. Edgar must decide whether to trust the proposition. Also: Nora is nineteen and has opinions; she is not simply a presence in the room.

**Beats:**

1. **Transit:** Prescott walks from Winckley Square to Bank Parade — ten minutes across the Avenham district, past the parks above the Ribble. The geography of Preston: the legal quarter behind him, the residential terraces ahead. Brief external texture; the cold.

2. **The house:** The Fairhurst house: rented, clean, the furniture of a schoolmaster's household. Edgar Fairhurst opens the door — a man of about forty-five, quiet, visibly composed. He has been expecting Prescott. Nora is in the parlour; she stands when Prescott enters. George appears briefly in the hall and is directed back upstairs.

3. **The explanation:** Prescott explains the situation plainly: a second codicil has been produced by Walter Eccleston's solicitors; a caveat has been entered; an action to prove the will in solemn form is now likely. The cost and the risk. The firm's position: carrying the action at its own risk, costs recoverable only on winning. He says this without softening it.

4. **Edgar's response:** He asks the questions a careful man asks — what the firm believes, what the likelihood is, what happens to the children in the meantime. Prescott says: the firm believes the second codicil is not genuine, but belief is not evidence, and gathering evidence takes time. Edgar says he cannot fund it; Prescott says the firm has already understood that. Edgar gives his instructions.

5. **Nora:** She asks, directly, what their mother was to have had — not abstractly, but what the estate comprises. Prescott tells her: the Hall, the mill shares, the residue. She thanks him without sentiment. She asks how long it will take. Prescott says he does not know yet.

6. **The administrator pendente lite:** Prescott explains that the estate must be managed while the action is pending — an independent accountant will be appointed by the court, who will collect the estate's income and pay its debts but will not distribute anything to anyone. Edgar accepts this. The mill's state is mentioned in passing: working short time; its monthly figures will be produced by the administrator.

7. **Josiah's letter:** Somewhere in this visit — Edgar produces a letter from Josiah to Margaret from March 1924, written shortly after the codicil was made. It confirms the reconciliation warmly; it says nothing explicit about the codicil's terms but its tone is of a final, settled arrangement, not a temporary sentiment. Prescott asks if he may take a copy; Edgar says he may take the original for the file.

8. **Close:** The visit ends. Prescott declines tea. He notes, walking away, the light in the upstairs window — George presumably at a desk. The terrace, the early dark, the parks below. He is thinking about the file, but the file now has faces in it.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The action is properly instructed. The administrator pendente lite is to be appointed. The children are people: Nora has a voice; Edgar is capable and cooperative. The estate is their mother's and it is real.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
The firm carries this at its own cost. If the second codicil stands — if the proof fails — the firm has carried an action it cannot recover. How long can Garside & Prescott sustain that, and what will Prescott do if the clock runs out before the proof is built?

---

## What Compounds

- **Stake established:** The children are present on the page as people, not a legal category. The displacement function is operative: seeing Nora and George and Edgar tells Prescott — and the reader — what the two codicils actually mean as a matter of lives. This is not a door closure, but it is the emotional engine the procedural middle movement runs on.

- **Door (f) corroborated:** Josiah's letter to Edgar (March 1924) confirms the reconciliation was genuine and settled. This letter, now in Prescott's file, is a corroborating exhibit for the habit — Josiah acted through his solicitor and then settled the matter in correspondence. No later letter from Josiah to Edgar expresses any second thoughts.

- **Prior chapter advanced:** Ch 2 — the caveat (entered by Edgar at Prescott's advice) is now confirmed; Ch 3 — the file's completeness (no private drafting; genuine reconciliation) is supported by the March 1924 letter.

- **Days to hearing:** Not yet set.
