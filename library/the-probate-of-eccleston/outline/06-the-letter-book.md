# Chapter 6 — The Letter-Book

**One-line summary:** Prescott works through the firm's complete outgoing correspondence on the Eccleston estate and confirms that no request for the first codicil's text — or for so much as its date — was ever received from anyone before R5.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston. January 1927. A working morning; the letter-book open on the desk; the Eccleston correspondence file assembled beside it.

## Chapter Shape
**Single scene — document examination.** Prescott reviews the firm's outgoing letter-book and the corresponding incoming letters for the Eccleston matter, start to finish. One location; methodical.

## Ending Mode
**Resolved-beat.** The chapter closes with a fact fixed: no one ever asked for the text of the first codicil, or for its date, through any channel. Only R5 went out — giving the date, not the text. This is established as a record item; it cannot be revisited.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Establish whether anyone — Thurston & Marsh, Walter, any third party — ever obtained the text of the first codicil or its date from Garside & Prescott by any channel, before or after Josiah's death.

**Obstacle:** The answer, if it is "no," proves only a negative. A negative is not nothing — but it is the beginning of an argument about how the second codicil's recital was composed, not the end of it.

**Beats:**

1. **Opening:** Prescott begins with the letter-book for 1924, the year the first codicil was made. He knows what he is looking for: any outgoing letter that quotes, reproduces, or paraphrases the text of the first codicil; any acknowledgment of an incoming request for the same. He works forward.

2. **The 1924 correspondence:** The execution of the first codicil: Garside's covering letter to Josiah enclosing the instrument for execution; a brief acknowledgment from Josiah; the carbon of the letter to Edgar Fairhurst notifying him of the new provision. None of these quote the operative clause; none go to Walter or Thurston & Marsh. The letter to Edgar says only that Mr. Eccleston has made "a testamentary provision" for Mrs. Fairhurst and her family and that the firm holds the instrument.

3. **1925 and 1926 correspondence:** The mortgage renewal (March 1925); Josiah's routine correspondence; a brief exchange about the Hall's insurance. Nothing from or to Thurston & Marsh about the first codicil's text. Nothing from Walter. Nothing from anyone.

4. **R5 examined in full (first full appearance):** In November 1926, the firm's notice letter to Thurston & Marsh. [R5 quoted in full as a set-off passage.] It gives the date of the first codicil — "a codicil thereto dated the 15th March 1924" — but nothing of the text. It says the codicil "materially affects the disposition of the residue" and nothing further. This is the only occasion the first codicil's date was transmitted by the firm to any external party. Prescott reads it again and notes: the letter gives the date; it gives nothing else. Thurston & Marsh, and through them Walter, had the date in writing from 2 November 1926. They had nothing of the text.

5. **The letter-book carbon:** The letter-book entry for R5 — the carbon of the outgoing letter — sits in the firm's own record. It is its own corroborating exhibit: dated, addressed, the firm's reference. It confirms the transmission of the date and the absence of any text.

6. **The conclusion stated in the working note:** Prescott writes a note for the file: "No request for the text of the 1924 codicil, or its full terms, was ever received from Thurston & Marsh or from Mr. Walter Eccleston or from any other party. R5 (November 1926) transmitted the date of the instrument only." He dates it. The working note becomes a file item; it will be in the brief to counsel.

7. **Prescott's private observation:** Looking at the letter-book entries, he sees again the precision of Garside's practice — every outgoing letter complete, every enclosure noted, every reference carried. The letter-book is as exact as the register. For a forger who never asked for the text, there is nothing here that would have helped him get it right.

---

## Outcome / Turn
Door (e) is closed. No one asked for the first codicil's text. Only the date was transmitted — from R5, on 2 November 1926, to Thurston & Marsh. Any recital of the first codicil's terms in any document produced after that date must have its source somewhere other than the firm.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
If the text of the first codicil never left the firm, how did the second codicil's recital know the date so precisely — and get the text wrong? Where did the date come from (answer: R5), and where did the text come from (not the document — but from somewhere)?

---

## What Compounds

- **Door (e) — the firm:** Closes. The letter-book confirms no request for the codicil's text reached the firm from anyone. The only transmission was R5 (the date alone) on 2 November 1926. The text was never given out.

- **Prior chapters advanced:** Ch 2 — R5 was sent; this chapter examines the letter-book carbon that confirms it; the absence of any prior request is now a file item. Ch 5 — Garside's precision in the register is mirrored in the letter-book; the firmness of both records is confirmed.

- **Days to hearing:** 265 days. Stated mid-chapter, not at the close.
