# Chapter 13 — The Fairhursts

**One-line summary:** Second visit to Bank Parade: Nora reports that Walter called last week and told her and George what their mother was to have had — his version of the first codicil's terms enters the record; the human stake is renewed at the case's midpoint.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Bank Parade, Avenham district, Preston. April/May 1927. A spring afternoon; the parks visible from the terrace window; the parlour recognisable from the first visit.

## Chapter Shape
**Bottle — witness interview with human texture.** One location; near real-time. Prescott visits to take a deposition from Edgar on the administrator's figures and to discuss progress with the family. Nora and George are present; the chapter carries the human stake.

## Ending Mode
**Coda.** The chapter closes quietly: Prescott walks back from Bank Parade in the spring evening. The children are still people; the stake is not abstract. He does not name what the visit does to the dry record accumulating at his desk.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Update Edgar on the case's progress; obtain whatever the family can provide as further evidence; and ensure the children are not simply abstractions in the brief.

**Obstacle:** The children live in a state of suspended uncertainty — the estate will come to them, or it will not; they are minors and cannot act; they must wait. Prescott must manage the communication of what is uncertain without either over-promising or withdrawing the human reality of what is at stake. Also: George is sixteen and finding the situation harder than he shows.

**Beats:**

1. **Arrival:** Prescott walks from Winckley Square again; Bank Parade in spring is a different place from the November visit — the parks greener, the light longer. The house is the same. Nora opens the door.

2. **The figures:** Edgar and Prescott discuss the administrator's figures — the mill bleeding, the estate's position, the hearing timeline. Edgar asks the question all clients ask: how confident is the firm? Prescott says: the record is building; the hearing date is fixed; the firm is confident but not yet in a position to say it is complete. This is honest; it is also controlled.

3. **George:** Briefly present — a sixteen-year-old under sustained uncertainty. He asks a direct question about what happens to the mill if the action fails. Prescott answers plainly: the estate passes to Walter under the second codicil; the mill is sold under Walter's sale contract; the money goes to Walter and his creditors. George says nothing further. He has understood.

4. **Nora's account of Walter's visit [PINNED MISQUOTATION 3 ON PAGE]:** Nora mentions, in the course of conversation about the estate, that Walter Eccleston came to call last week. He was kind; he seemed genuinely fond of their mother. He told them what their mother had been to receive — explained the first codicil and what it meant. He said — and Nora quotes him with evident accuracy — that Josiah had made a codicil "giving everything to our mother and her issue, which meant us." She is repeating Walter's words: "and her issue." She adds: Walter said their uncle had always meant to put it right, and he was sorry it had come to this. Prescott takes down the substance. He does not correct or remark on Walter's phrase.

5. **Nora's character:** She is nineteen, clear-eyed, and not given to sentimentality about the money. She is given to sentimentality about her mother. She says she thinks her mother would have been uncomfortable being made the subject of a legal action; she says she hopes the record will be enough. Prescott says he believes it will be. He believes this; it is also what a solicitor says to a client.

6. **The displacement:** Something in this visit — the spring light, Nora's directness, the March 1924 letter from Josiah to Margaret (mentioned in passing, the tone of it) — settles the case for Prescott in the way that cases must be settled before the work will come right. He does not name this. He gathers his notes and prepares to go.

7. **The close:** Prescott declines tea again; he has a train to catch or a brief to finish. He walks back through the parks, the river below. The hearing is 155 days away. He has what he came for, and more than what he expected.

## Outcome / Turn
Misquotation 3 is in the record — not flagged, not underlined, but in the working note from the visit, in Nora's words as reported to Prescott. Walter told the children "and her issue." He did not know, at that moment, that he was saying the wrong word. He said it kindly, without any consciousness that it was wrong. That is the cruelest thing about it.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
Walter said "and her issue" — to the children — unprompted, kindly, in a private context. Not in a legal document, not in a formal statement: in conversation, to the people whose inheritance he has stolen. How many times has he said these words, in how many rooms, since Josiah told him in 1924?
