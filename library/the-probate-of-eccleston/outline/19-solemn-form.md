# Chapter 19 — Solemn Form

**One-line summary:** At the threshold of the hearing in the Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division — counsel to counsel, before argument begins — Walter is handed the strong-room original of the first codicil and reads the word "or" for the first time in his life; his counsel takes instructions; the action is abandoned.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
London: the Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division of the High Court; the building's corridor outside the court; and briefly the interior of the courtroom as it empties. Autumn 1927. The hearing's first morning.

## Chapter Shape
**Action-heavy — multi-scene.** The chapter moves through the morning: arrival at the court; the pre-hearing exchanges between counsel; the handing-over of the codicil; the instruction-taking; the abandonment. The pace is concentrated. The climactic act (Walter reading "or") is brief; its weight comes from what surrounds it.

## Ending Mode
**Turn.** The exposure is the second major structural pivot of the novel. The action is abandoned; the second codicil fails; everything that follows is consequence.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Appear at the hearing, support counsel, and ensure the record is before the court in proper form.

**Obstacle:** The hearing begins without certainty — Thurston & Marsh may have a counter-argument not yet disclosed; the handwriting experts will give evidence that splits; Prescott does not know, until the moment arrives, whether the judge will find the text argument sufficient. The obstacle is the uncertainty of a trial until it is over.

**Beats:**

1. **London — the night before:** Brief. Prescott in London; the evidence bundle; the originals in the deed-box; a room at a commercial hotel near the Strand. He does not sleep easily. He does not dramatise the not-sleeping. He reviews the brief one more time and puts it down.

2. **The court — the morning:** The Probate, Divorce and Admiralty Division: the building, the corridor, the benches outside the court. Prescott carrying the deed-box. Other solicitors; barristers in gowns; the institutional smell of the place. Their counsel introduces himself to Prescott outside the court.

3. **The pre-hearing exchange:** Counsel approach one another before the judge takes his seat. Walter's counsel has had the brief; Walter has finally understood what the variance argument amounts to. The exchange is formal, professional. Walter's counsel asks whether Prescott's side is prepared to allow a brief adjournment for Walter to review the executed original of the first codicil. This is the moment — a request framed professionally, but its meaning is clear: Walter has not yet read the word.

4. **The handing of the original:** Prescott opens the deed-box and takes out the first codicil — the executed original, the instrument in the strong-room since March 1924. He hands it to Walter's counsel. Walter is in the corridor behind him. Walter's counsel passes it to Walter.

5. **Walter reads:** Walter reads the first codicil — the whole instrument, quickly at first, then returning to the recital-clause. He reads the operative words. He reads the word "or." He reads it again. He is not reading to understand the legal argument; he is reading the thing itself for the first time in his life. He is fifty or so years old; he has been certain of what this document said since March 1924; he has been wrong since March 1924.

6. **Prescott's register:** Prescott watches Walter. He keeps his face and voice professional and almost apologetic in its restraint. There is nothing to say. Walter knows, reading the word, what the record shows — that he never had the text, only Josiah's spoken account, and that his own letter to the bank and his own words to the children confirm that his version was "and." He forged around a document he had never read. The record is exact and memory is not.

7. **Instructions taken:** Walter's counsel withdraws with Walter. A few minutes. Prescott waits with their own counsel in the corridor. When Walter's counsel returns, he says: his client is not in a position to proceed; he is instructed to request a withdrawal of the claim. The action is to be abandoned.

8. **The court — brief:** The judge is advised; the matter is disposed of in short compass. The second codicil fails; the earlier instruments stand; letters of administration with will annexed will be sought. The handwriting experts' split is confirmed on the record as part of the proceeding — and confirmed as irrelevant, since the case was not decided on the signature. The judge notes it drily.

9. **The costs:** Walter's side is liable for the costs of the action. Garside & Prescott's position — carrying the action at its own risk — is resolved: the costs are recoverable. The firm will survive.

10. **Close:** Outside the court. Prescott stands in the corridor for a moment. He does not celebrate; that is not the register. He will telephone Mr. Booth from the hotel. He will write to Edgar Fairhurst today. He picks up the deed-box and walks toward the exit.

---

## Outcome / Turn
Walter reads "or" for the first time. The action is abandoned. The second codicil fails. The estate passes under the first codicil. Costs are recoverable. The firm survives.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
The legal action is resolved. What happens now to Walter, to the mill, to the children, to the firm? What does the outcome cost — and what does it buy?
