# Chapter 15 — The Hall

**One-line summary:** Depositions from Eccleston Hall's household staff establish that after May 1926 Josiah Eccleston never left the Hall unaccompanied; his last confirmed outing was to the mill on 14 May 1926; he could not have visited the mill office in August 1926 to type the codicil.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Eccleston Hall, Ribchester; and the administrator's office in Preston. May/June 1927. A day at the Hall taking depositions; a follow-up note from the administrator.

## Chapter Shape
**Witness interview — site visit.** Prescott travels to Eccleston Hall to take depositions from the steward (Mr. Elias Beckett) and the household staff. The Hall is briefly described as a place; the depositions are the substance of the chapter.

## Ending Mode
**Resolved-beat.** The door closes cleanly: the depositions establish the fact; Josiah could not have visited the mill office in August 1926. The chapter ends with the finding stated in a working note, the last movement-II door locked.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Establish by sworn deposition that Josiah Eccleston did not visit the mill office (or any comparable location) between May and his death in October 1926, and therefore could not have typed the second codicil himself on 14 August.

**Obstacle:** The household staff may have imperfect memories; they are not legal professionals; their depositions must be carefully taken. Also: the Hall is Walter's family property by long familiarity, and some of the staff may be uncomfortable testifying against Walter. Prescott must be scrupulous and patient.

**Beats:**

1. **Transit to the Hall:** The road to Ribchester again; a different route from the mill visit (the Hall is half a mile up the valley). The Hall: moderate size, not a great estate; good grounds; an older house. Currently maintained but with fewer staff than it once had — the estate in limbo.

2. **The steward — Mr. Elias Beckett:** Prescott's principal deponent. Beckett has been at the Hall for fourteen years and is a thorough man. He confirms: from May 1926 onward, Mr. Eccleston did not leave the Hall except in his motor or with at least one attendant. His health was failing by June — the doctor's visits increasing; his movement restricted by June, limited to the house and garden by July. Beckett's last definite note of Mr. Eccleston going anywhere further than the garden: May 14, the mill visit (consistent with the mill daybook from Ch 9).

3. **Beckett confirms the September reading:** Garside and Prescott came to the Hall in September (the September reading confirmed in Ch 5's register). Beckett remembers this — the deed-box; Garside reading the instruments; Walter and the others in the drawing room. He confirms the date (15 September 1926) from his own household diary. Josiah was in the drawing room; he could not have left the house by that point without Beckett knowing.

4. **The cook and housemaid:** Two brief additional depositions. The cook confirms Josiah did not leave the house in August 1926; she was on the premises throughout. The housemaid confirms the same. Neither deposition is dramatic; their consistency is the point.

5. **The doctor's evidence (through the administrator):** Through the administrator, Prescott obtains confirmation from Josiah's doctor: Josiah's health in August 1926 was such that he would not have been able to travel to the mill (twelve miles from the Hall, on a hillside). The doctor's notes record the August visits; none mention any outing; they record increasing frailty.

6. **The combined finding:** Josiah Eccleston did not visit the mill office after May 14, 1926. On August 14, 1926 — the date on the second codicil — he was at the Hall, in declining health, with no capacity for a twelve-mile journey. The machine is at the mill. Josiah was not at the mill. The cover story is physically impossible.

7. **The formal working note:** Prescott drafts the finding: "Depositions from Mr. E. Beckett (steward), [cook], and [housemaid] of Eccleston Hall, corroborated by the medical notes of Dr. [name], confirm that the late Mr. Josiah Eccleston did not leave Eccleston Hall for any external destination after 14 May 1926. He was physically incapable of travel to Vale Mill from June 1926 onward. The second codicil's date (14 August 1926) cannot represent a visit by the testator to the mill office." Signed, dated. Filed.

8. **Close:** 125 days remaining — noted on the file page. The record is nearly complete for Movement II. The nine doors are now all either closed (g, h, e, f, d, a, c) or definitively closing (b, and (i) pending the date chapter). This last resolved-beat of Movement II is deliberate: the building of the record ends firmly, not ambiguously.

## Outcome / Turn
Door (b) is closed. Josiah Eccleston was not at the mill in August 1926; he was not capable of going there; the cover story is physically impossible. The second codicil was typed on a machine in a room Josiah had not entered since May, dated to a day he could not have been there.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
Eight of nine doors are now closed; (i), the date asymmetry, is being assembled. The record is nearly complete. What does it look like, assembled? Can Prescott see the argument whole — or does the discovery remain to be made?

## What Compounds

- **Door (b) — Josiah's presence:** Closes. Depositions and medical evidence establish: Josiah did not leave Eccleston Hall after 14 May 1926. On 14 August 1926, he was at the Hall in declining health, incapable of traveling to the mill. The second codicil cannot have been typed by or in the presence of Josiah at the mill on its stated date.

- **Prior chapters advanced:** Ch 9 — the mill daybook's May 14 entry is corroborated from the Hall side; the two records (mill and household) are now consistent and mutually reinforcing; Ch 10 — the machine is at the mill; the machine proves the location; Josiah wasn't at the location.
