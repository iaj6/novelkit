# Chapter 11 — The Sale Contract

**One-line summary:** Walter's mortgage, his conditional sale contract, and his banking correspondence are assembled as exhibits; R4 enters the file as a financial exhibit — its "and her issue" wording unremarked by the narration.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston. March–April 1927. Documents obtained through the administrator and through discovery in the action.

## Chapter Shape
**Document or epistolary — single scene.** Prescott reviews the financial thread: the mortgage documents, the sale contract, the banking correspondence. The chapter is a document examination chapter with an external element (the administrator's latest figures).

## Ending Mode
**Mid-action-cut.** The chapter ends with the financial exhibits assembled but the pressure unresolved. The mill is bleeding; Walter's position is untenable; R4 is in the file. The reader has seen R4 on the page; nothing in the narration remarks on its language.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Document Walter's financial position as a strand of the case — establishing motive and the circumstances that make the second codicil's appearance explicable — and assemble the relevant documents as exhibits.

**Obstacle:** Financial motive is background to the forgery case; it is not the proof. Prescott must handle it as circumstance, not as the spine. The risk is over-relying on motive (which proves nothing about authorship) at the expense of the text argument (which proves everything). He keeps both in view.

**Beats:**

1. **The mortgage:** Through the administrator's papers, Prescott obtains the terms of the Eccleston estate mortgage — a 1921 loan against the mill's assets, with the mortgage held by a Blackburn bank. The interest payments are running; the principal is overdue. The administrator is making the interest payments from the estate's income (barely); the principal cannot be repaid without a sale.

2. **The sale contract:** Walter's conditional sale contract: the mill and estate, to be sold to a textile firm on completion conditioned on "a grant of representation establishing the vendor's title." This is the standard post-1925 form. The completion date has already been extended twice; a third extension is not expected. Without a grant — which requires the action to be resolved — the sale falls through.

3. **Walter's banking correspondence:** Prescott obtains, through the administrator, copies of Walter's banking correspondence with his bank manager, Mr. Holt. Several letters; the picture is consistent: a mill manager carrying debt on the expectation of an inheritance, repeatedly telling his bank that the arrangement will be resolved in his favour. The most recent letters are from late 1926 and early 1927: Walter still assuring the bank the estate will come through.

4. **R4 enters the file [PINNED MISQUOTATION 2 ON PAGE]:** Among Walter's banking correspondence — in a letter dated 11 June 1925 from Walter to Mr. Holt — is the following passage [R4 quoted in full as a set-off passage]: *"Dear Mr. Holt, — You ask what further security I can offer beyond the mortgage. My uncle has made a codicil, as I told you, in favour of my late cousin Mrs. Fairhurst and her issue, but I am assured the arrangement will not stand; he made it in a soft mood and has spoken since of putting it right. My expectations are otherwise what they have always been. — Yours faithfully, WALTER ECCLESTON"*. The letter is filed among the exhibits. Its wording is not remarked on by the narration; the chapter moves on.

5. **The financial thread's meaning in the case:** Prescott notes the outline of motive: the mill mortgaged; the sale conditioned on the grant; Walter carrying the debt since 1924 on the expectation that the codicil "would not stand." By October 1926 — after Josiah's death without correcting the codicil — Walter's position was untenable. The debt is compounding; the sale cannot complete without the grant.

6. **The administrator's current figures:** The administrator provides the latest figures: the mill has lost another three hundred pounds against income projections; the wage bill is met but mortgage interest is not; the creditors are accumulating. At this rate, a further six months will exhaust the estate's liquidity.

7. **Close:** Prescott files the exhibits — the mortgage, the sale contract, R4 and the other bank letters — in the brief. 185 days to the hearing. The financial thread is assembled; it explains the forgery but does not prove it. The text must prove it.

## Outcome / Turn
Walter's financial situation is fully documented as a case exhibit. R4 is in the file and on the page — its language is exactly what it is; the narration has not glossed it. The motive is clear. The proof is elsewhere.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
R4 says "and her issue." The executed codicil says "or her issue." The two texts are now both somewhere in the record. Nobody in the book has yet placed them side by side.
