# Chapter 9 — Vale Mill

**One-line summary:** Prescott makes his single site visit to Vale Mill and Ribchester: the mill in short time; the mill office's typewriter; the administrator's monthly figures; and Josiah's last confirmed presence at the mill placed at May 1926.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Vale Mill and the mill office, Ribchester; with brief transit from and to Preston. Late February or early March 1927. A working day; cold; the Ribble visible from the road.

## Chapter Shape
**Site visit — observation-heavy.** One long scene on location (the mill, the office, the administrator's meeting) with the transit as an opening. Prescott observes; the chapter is heavy on physical description of the working mill and its economic texture.

## Ending Mode
**Literary-fade.** The chapter closes on Prescott's journey back to Preston — the winter road, the valley below, the mill already silent behind him (between shifts). The stakes and the physical facts have settled, but no conclusion is stated aloud. The chapter ends in atmosphere rather than in a record note.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** See the mill and the mill office; speak with the administrator pendente lite about the monthly figures; establish the location of the typewriter; and confirm, if possible, when Josiah last visited the mill.

**Obstacle:** Walter Eccleston manages the mill. The administrator is the neutral figure; Walter is still on the premises, as manager. Prescott must gather what he needs without making his purpose explicit to Walter. Also: the mill is a living place with men in it, and it is bleeding.

**Beats:**

1. **Transit — the road to Ribchester:** Prescott travels by motor or train; the twelve miles from Preston; the Ribble valley flattening east. Brief sensory notes: the moorland, the stone walls, the early-year cold. He has not been to the mill since September 1926 — when he brought the deed-box.

2. **The mill — exterior:** Vale Mill on the hillside above the village: stone-built, Victorian, of moderate scale. Forty men where once it ran eighty. Short time is visible in the silence between the morning and afternoon shifts — the looms not running when Prescott arrives. Smoke from the engine room but the weaving shed quiet.

3. **Meeting the administrator:** The neutral Preston accountant meets Prescott in the mill's counting house (a modest room adjacent to the mill office). He produces the monthly figures: output figures, wage bill, costs, the mortgage interest. The figures are bad. The mill is running at perhaps forty per cent capacity; income does not cover the fixed charges; each month's figures are worse than the last. The administrator is precise and unemotive: his job is to report, not to conclude.

4. **The mill office — the typewriter:** Prescott is given a few minutes in the mill office while the administrator retrieves a file. He sees the room: stone floor, two filing cabinets, a battered desk, a typewriter. He observes the typewriter without touching it: a machine of a particular make and model (he notes both, writing it in his pocket-book). The machine is worn; several keys are visibly uneven in their impression. This is the only typewriter in the building; the Hall has none.

5. **Josiah's last visit:** He asks the administrator, who asks the mill's foreman: when did Mr. Eccleston last visit the mill? The foreman produces the mill daybook — a working log kept by the foreman. Josiah Eccleston: last entry, "Mr. Eccleston visited, inspected the shed, attended the office; May 14, 1926." After that, nothing. The old man's health declined; by summer he was not travelling. The daybook entry is contemporaneous, in the foreman's hand. Prescott notes the date.

6. **Walter glimpsed:** Prescott sees Walter Eccleston crossing the yard — not a confrontation, not a conversation. A solid man in a mill manager's coat, speaking briefly to a mechanic. He moves with the authority of a man who knows the building. He does not look toward the counting house. He is, by every visible measure, a good mill manager. He will go down anyway.

7. **The administrator's final note:** The administrator advises Prescott that the sale contract is still in place — but the purchaser has extended the completion date twice; they will not extend again. The mill will not be sold while the action is pending. Without the estate's resolution, Walter cannot complete, and the creditors are accumulating.

8. **Close — transit back:** Prescott on the road back to Preston. The valley below; the mill behind him, the looms beginning their afternoon shift — he can hear them faintly from the road. He thinks about what he has seen and writes nothing yet; the pocket-book can wait until the office. The valley is quiet and the road is cold.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The typewriter is identified by make and model at the mill office. Josiah's last confirmed visit to the mill was 14 May 1926 — documented in the foreman's daybook. The second codicil is dated 14 August 1926. The mill is bleeding. Walter's situation is worse than the documents described.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
If the typewriter at the mill is the one that typed the second codicil, it was typed in a room Josiah had not visited since May — three months before the codicil's date. The cover story requires Josiah to have typed it there. But the foreman's daybook says Josiah was not there. How does Prescott prove the typewriter is the source?
