# Chapter 14 — The Commission

**One-line summary:** The application to examine Edward Hargreaves in Canada is refused on the ground that his address is unknown and no mechanism of service exists; the calendar moves against Prescott; the witness door is permanently closed.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston. May 1927. A Tuesday morning; the letter from London counsel arrives with the court's response.

## Chapter Shape
**Correspondence chapter.** A letter received; its consequences worked through; a telephone call or further letter to London counsel; an immediate recalibration of the case plan. One location; the action concentrated in the morning.

## Ending Mode
**Turn.** The refused commission is the one moment in the book where the calendar moves against Prescott rather than with him. Days are lost to the application; the expected witness avenue is definitively closed. This is the structural setback of Movement II.

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Receive the court's response on the commission application and determine the consequences for the case.

**Obstacle:** The commission is refused. The witness is untraceable; without a fixed address in Canada, there is no mechanism of service and no commission can be executed. The English court will not issue a commission when it cannot be delivered. The application consumed weeks; the hearing date is now 140 days away; the time spent on the application is gone.

**Beats:**

1. **The letter arrives:** Counsel in London writes: the court has declined to issue the commission to examine Hargreaves. The ground: the applicant has been unable to supply a fixed address in Canada at which the commission could be served; without a traceable address, a commission cannot be executed and the application cannot succeed. The court's decision is not appealable on these facts; a fresh application would require a verified address.

2. **Prescott's reaction (controlled):** He reads the letter twice. He sets it on the desk. He walks to the window. He does not say what he feels. What he thinks: the witness avenue is gone. He knew the odds; he submitted anyway because he was obliged to try. The outcome is what it is.

3. **The calendar cost:** The application was submitted in April; it is now May. Eight weeks lost in the commission thread — weeks that cannot be recovered. Hearing date: 140 days. He has 140 days, not the 172 he had when the application was filed. The lost time is real.

4. **What the closure means for the case:** Without the gardener's evidence, the attestation cannot be examined by any testimony. The carter is dead; the gardener is gone. The codicil's due execution story is unbreakable by witness evidence. Which means: the attestation, considered alone, is not a route. The text must do everything.

5. **The compensating clarity:** There is, Prescott notes, a kind of negative argument available. The witnesses were chosen — the carter who died in September, the gardener who sailed in October — chosen at a moment when no living person could have known they would both be unavailable. Unless the second codicil was prepared AFTER both men were unavailable — after October 1926 — but was deliberately dated to August. The despatch-box stood at the mill for six days in late October. Walter was the only one with access in those six days. The timing of the forgery, including the witness selection, fits exactly the period when both men were newly unavailable. But this is argument, not evidence, and Prescott notes it as such.

6. **A letter to London counsel:** Prescott writes to counsel: the commission has failed; the case must be made on typewriter evidence and text; please confirm we are proceeding on that footing. He also notes the calendar: 140 days; the brief must be complete in eight weeks at the outside.

7. **Close:** The refused commission letter goes into the file. The file grows. 140 days to the hearing. Prescott takes out the next matter to be addressed; the work continues, as it must. The setback is in the file; it is not named as a setback.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The commission is refused; the witness door is closed permanently. Calendar cost: eight weeks. Days remaining: 140. The attestation cannot be attacked by testimony; the text must carry the entire weight of the case. This is the case's crisis point — not a loss, but a constraint that defines everything that follows.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
The text must do it. Can a recital's error — one word wrong in a formal instrument — convict a man? What else does the text say, and what does the rest of the record say about who could have composed it that way?

---

## What Compounds

- **Door (c) — the witnesses:** Closes. The witnesses are permanently beyond examination. Carter dead; gardener untraceable in Canada; commission refused for want of an address. The attestation cannot be challenged or confirmed by any testimony now or at the hearing. This is, from the defence's perspective, an unassailable point — but it is equally unassailable from Prescott's: the silence of the witnesses, once authorship is shown, becomes part of the design.

- **Prior chapters advanced:** Ch 12 — the commission application was submitted in the witnesses chapter; this chapter ends it; the witness thread is exhausted.

- **Days to hearing:** **140 days** — and the calendar has moved against Prescott (from 172 at Ch 12 to 140 now, a loss of 32 days to the commission application rather than the proportionate reduction). The eight weeks lost to the commission are a real cost. This is the one occasion in the book where the calendar moves the wrong way.
