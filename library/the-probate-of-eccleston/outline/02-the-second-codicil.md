# Chapter 2 — The Second Codicil

**One-line summary:** Three weeks after the funeral, Thurston & Marsh produce a second codicil revoking the first; Walter attends the firm with his cover story and his first misquotation; Prescott reads R3 for the first time; the caveat is entered.

---

## POV and Tense
Arthur Prescott — third person limited, past tense.

## Setting / Time
Garside & Prescott, Winckley Square, Preston. Early November 1926. Two events in the same day or adjacent days: the letter from Thurston & Marsh with the enclosure; then Walter's attendance at the office.

## Chapter Shape
**Multi-scene** (correspondence received and read; then in-person attendance). Two distinct beats in the same location and approximately the same period.

## Ending Mode
**Elliptical.** The chapter ends with the caveat entered and the dispute formally joined — but nothing is resolved about the second codicil's authenticity. The question hangs: is it genuine?

---

## Goal, Obstacle, Action Beats

**Prescott's goal:** Understand what Thurston & Marsh are claiming and advise Garside and, through him, the children's father.

**Obstacle:** The second codicil looks plausible. Its paper is right; the signature is of the right general form; it has an attestation clause. Prescott cannot immediately say it is wrong.

**Beats:**

1. **Scene 1 — the letter arrives:** Garside opens the letter from Thurston & Marsh. Their client, they write, has lately found among his late uncle's papers a codicil dated 14 August 1926 which he believes was made privately by the testator. They enclose a copy. They trust that, in the circumstances, the will may now be proved in common form in the ordinary way.

2. **R3 read in full:** Garside reads the propounded second codicil aloud (R3 quoted in full as set-off passage). The recital: Josiah giving the residue "unto my niece Margaret Fairhurst and her issue absolutely." The operative provision: revoking the first codicil in its entirety and confirming the 1919 will. The date: 14 August 1926. Two attesting witnesses named. Garside and Prescott observe: the firm has never seen this document; no instruction was ever given to anyone here to draw it; no copy was ever requested.

3. **Garside's immediate assessment:** The firm held the first codicil and knows nothing of this. The 14 August date is before Josiah's death but after the inventory of the despatch-box at which no codicil appeared — Prescott points this out. Garside says quietly that the box stood at the mill afterward. He instructs Prescott to write to Edgar Fairhurst before any further step.

4. **R5 context:** R5 (the firm's November 2 notice letter) was sent before or at the same time as this exchange — it gave Thurston & Marsh (and hence Walter) the date of the first codicil in writing. The letter-book carbon is briefly noted as being in order. [The letter-book carbon's significance is not remarked on; it is a detail that will matter later.]

5. **Scene 2 — Walter's attendance:** Walter Eccleston calls at the firm, unannounced but not unexpected. He is in his fifties, solid, a mill manager's bearing. He is polite and even warm — he worked with Garside for years. His account: going through his uncle's insurance papers after the funeral, he found the codicil in the despatch-box. He had thought, given his uncle's evident change of mind, this was the right outcome. He is concerned that there may be an unnecessary dispute. **Mid-sentence, speaking of the first codicil's effect:** *"... the arrangement Uncle made for Margaret and her issue — which of course he always said he meant to put right, and I've no doubt it was in that spirit that he —"* The misquotation ("and her issue") lands in the middle of a subordinate clause about intentions; it is not the topic of the sentence. Prescott takes the attendance note; he does not correct or remark on the phrase.

6. **Garside's response:** Measured. The firm must advise its clients and take instructions in the ordinary way. Nothing further can be said at this stage.

7. **The caveat:** Edgar Fairhurst, on Prescott's advice by letter, enters a caveat at the Probate Registry. The dispute is now formally joined. A solemn-form action is inevitable.

---

## Outcome / Turn
The second codicil is on the table. The caveat is entered. The action is open. Walter's cover story is in the attendance note. And the misquotation "and her issue" is in the record — though nothing indicates it will matter.

## New Question or Pressure Raised
The second codicil is dated before Josiah's death and has two witnesses. The firm can say it never drew it and knows nothing of it. But is that enough? What will prove it false?

---

## What Compounds

- **Door (g) — the despatch-box:** Closes. The gap is confirmed in Walter's own account: the box was inventoried at the Hall with no codicil, stood at the mill in Walter's keeping for approximately six days, and Walter then found the codicil in it. The timeline is in the attendance note; it cannot be disputed. The question is what happened in those six days.

- **Door (e) — the firm:** Opens. Prescott notes, reviewing the file after Walter leaves, that no request for the codicil's text (or for so much as its date) was ever received by the firm from anyone. Only R5 went out — and R5 gave the date alone. The absence of any request is now a fact of the record.

- **Prior chapter advanced:** Ch 1 — the inventory note first established there was no codicil in the box; this chapter's attendance note confirms when the box next appeared and in whose hands it stood.

- **Days to hearing:** Not yet set. No action formally pending; the caveat has been entered but proceedings not yet commenced.

## Continuity Notes

- **R3 quoted in full** (first and one of at most two or three full appearances before the discovery; the recital says "and her issue" — the reader who saw "or her issue" in R2 last chapter has now seen both, unremarked).
- **Walter's first misquotation on the page:** mid-sentence, in an attendance note context, doing other work in the sentence. "And her issue." Not the topic. Never commented on by the narration.
- The second codicil is dated 14 August 1926. It names two attesting witnesses.
- Walter's cover story (fixed): found the codicil going through insurance papers in the despatch-box after the funeral. Josiah must have made it privately.
- The despatch-box stood approximately six days at the mill in Walter's keeping before the codicil appeared.
- The firm sent R5 to Thurston & Marsh on 2 November 1926, giving the date of the first codicil in writing.
- **R5 quoted in full** in the brief; the drafter should quote it in full in this chapter (or in the following chapter when Prescott reviews the letter-book). First appearance.
- Walter is NOT POV; everything about him comes through the attendance note and Prescott's observation.
- Do NOT have Prescott comment on or notice the "and/or" variance. He does not notice it yet.
