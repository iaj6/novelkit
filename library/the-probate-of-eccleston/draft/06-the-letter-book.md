# Chapter Six — The Letter-Book

By nine on the fourteenth of January Prescott had the letter-book for 1924 open on his desk and was working through the Eccleston entries for that year.

He was looking for one thing: any outgoing letter in which the firm had quoted, reproduced, or paraphrased the terms of the first codicil; any acknowledgment of a request for the same from any quarter.

The 1924 entries for the Eccleston matter came to four. The first was the covering letter to Josiah, dated the twelfth of March, enclosing the draft codicil for his approval before execution — the draft noted in the margin column, the reference given, the instruction to return if any amendment was required. The instrument was the enclosure, not the letter. The second entry, the attendance note of the fifteenth, was in the bundle already; it was not outgoing correspondence. The third was Josiah's acknowledgment of the seventeenth: one sentence confirming receipt, in the hand Prescott had come to know across fourteen years of Josiah's letters to the firm.

The fourth had been written by Garside himself to Mr. Edgar Fairhurst at Bank Parade, dated the nineteenth of March 1924. It said that Mr. Eccleston had made a testamentary provision for the benefit of Mrs. Fairhurst and her family, and that the firm held the instrument. Nothing else. No operative clause, no wording from the instrument, no indication of what the provision said beyond its existence. Prescott marked the margin of the carbon in pencil: *terms not communicated*. He turned the page.

The 1925 entries for the Eccleston matter were six. A mortgage renewal query in March; Garside's reply by the eighth, the revised schedule noted. An inquiry about the Hall's insurance in May. A brief exchange in June about a farm tenancy — one letter from Josiah, one from Garside, concluded in two. Nothing in any of the six from or to Thurston & Marsh about the first codicil. Nothing from Walter. No inquiry of any kind about its terms, its date, or what it provided.

He was two hundred and sixty-five days from the hearing.

He closed the 1925 section and turned to 1926.

The entries before December were fewer. The tenancy question from June, carried forward. Josiah's last letter to the firm — the eighth of June, the handwriting more effortful than it had been in the correspondence from the year before — thanking Garside for the advice and noting he would speak to the tenant himself. After the eighth of June there was a blank quarter: nothing from the Hall, nothing from the mill, nothing from Thurston & Marsh, nothing from Walter Eccleston or from anyone acting on his behalf.

In November, one letter, addressed to Thurston & Marsh.

He turned to its carbon.

---

> Dear Sirs, — We act in the estate of the late Mr. Josiah Eccleston of Eccleston Hall. We hold his will dated the 3rd June 1919 and a codicil thereto dated the 15th March 1924 which materially affects the disposition of the residue. We shall be glad to receive your client's proposals for proving the will in proper form. — Yours faithfully, GARSIDE & PRESCOTT

---

The reference: *Eccleston dec.* The date at the head: the second of November 1926.

He read it a second time.

---

He put the letter-book back on its shelf.

The volume sat between its neighbours as it had always sat, the label square. Nothing in it abbreviated; nothing missing its enclosure note or its reference. Every outgoing letter: complete, dated, the enclosures listed in the margin column whether the enclosure was a draft codicil or a farm-tenancy schedule. Most provincial offices did not maintain a letter-book at this standard. The strong-room register had not required it either — and both were at the same level; they had always been at the same level; they had been built by the same hand, and to the same purpose.

He drew a sheet toward him and wrote a working note for the file.

*No request for the text of the 1924 codicil, or its full terms, was ever received from Thurston & Marsh or from Mr. Walter Eccleston or from any other party between the date of the instrument and the date of this review. The firm's notice letter of the 2nd November 1926 (R5 in these proceedings) transmitted the date of the instrument only: "a codicil thereto dated the 15th March 1924." The terms of the instrument were not transmitted by any letter, communication, or other channel from Garside & Prescott to any external party at any time.*

He dated it and set it at the front of the file. The carbon of R5 he extracted from the letter-book and clipped separately behind the note: when the brief to counsel was assembled, it would go in as an exhibit — the distinction now a matter of record.

He picked up the file and took it through to his room.
