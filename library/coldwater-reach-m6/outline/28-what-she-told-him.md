# Chapter 28 — What She Told Him

**One-line summary**: Hannah tells Marcus what she knows — the photograph, Karl's note, Albrecht's acknowledgment — not knowing she is giving him the last piece.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Coldwater Reach High School — Marcus's classroom  
**Time**: January 1945  
**Target words**: ~2,500

**Chapter shape**: Single scene  
**Ending mode**: Literary fade

---

## Goal
Hannah completes the picture for Marcus — not knowing she is doing so — and in the act of telling him, completes something for herself as well: she is no longer carrying it alone. The chapter is the payoff of Hannah's long arc of noticing and not-yet-acting; this is what she tells. The shape of who she is becoming is defined partly by what she chooses to bring to light.

## Obstacle
Hannah does not know how much Marcus already knows. She does not know about his letter, his research, his desk drawer. She is bringing him her pieces of a puzzle she does not know he has been assembling from his side. Her obstacle is simple: she has been carrying this and she needs to put it down somewhere. Marcus's classroom is where she can.

---

## Opening texture
Dialogue: Hannah stays after Advanced German class. She has, ostensibly, a question about the translation assignment. The chapter opens on the exchange — Marcus at his desk, Hannah with her coat already on, not quite ready to say what she came to say.

---

## Action beats

1. **After class**: The other students have gone. Marcus is gathering papers. Hannah says she has a question about the translation assignment — a real question, which she asks first; he answers. Then she doesn't go.

2. **She says it**: She begins with: "Dr. Klein — there's something I thought you should know. Because of the German." She means: because he is the person in Coldwater to whom German-language things can be brought. She does not say: because you asked about the Pfeiffer family at the harbor last summer. She doesn't know about that conversation.

3. **The photograph**: She tells him about the tin box in her parents' room; the photograph; the two men in front of a building in Bamberg, 1921; her father's name and W. Grau's. She says she doesn't have it — she left it in the box — but she saw it clearly.

4. **Karl's note**: She takes the folded piece of paper from her coat pocket. (She has been carrying it since November.) She reads it to him — or shows it to him; he can read it himself. "Werner Grau. From Ansbach. He is dead. I am sorry." She says that Karl Reinmar — the young German from the farm — gave it to her when he was transferred.

5. **Albrecht's response**: She tells him what happened in the kitchen: she said "Werner Grau ist tot," and her father said "Ich weiß." She says it simply, not as a revelation but as a fact she has been sitting with for two months.

6. **Marcus's listening**: Marcus listens with the total attentiveness he brings to things that matter. He asks two questions, precisely: How old did the man in the photograph appear to be — middle-aged? (Yes.) And: Did Karl indicate when Werner came to Maine? (Karl thought before the big storm in October 1943.) Marcus does not reveal his own knowledge. He does not tell her about the letter, or the research, or what he knows. He says: "Thank you. You were right to tell me."

7. **Why she did it**: She picks up her coat and her bag. She says: "I thought you should know, because of the German." She means: this is German-world knowledge, and Marcus is the German-world in Coldwater. He is the person to whom it belongs. She has given it to the right person.

---

## Ending (literary fade)
She goes out into the January afternoon. The school hallway, the cold, the bus stop. She feels, without naming it, lighter — the weight of the carried thing diminished by the having said it. Behind her, in the classroom, Marcus sits with what she has given him, which is the last piece of the account assembled in his desk drawer. She does not know this. She knows only that she told the truth, and that it went somewhere it could be held.

---

## New question raised
What will Marcus do with the complete account? And for Hannah: she has acted — she has told, rather than withheld — and this is the shape of who she is becoming. What remains?

## Continuity notes
- This chapter is paired with ch. 10 (What She Didn't Ask): in ch. 10 she did not ask; in ch. 28 she tells; the parallel is deliberate and should be felt without being announced
- Hannah does NOT know about Marcus's previous knowledge; she is bringing him her pieces without knowing he has pieces of his own; this is a genuine gift, not a collaboration
- The exact words of Karl's note (established in ch. 24): "Werner Grau. From Ansbach. He is dead. I am sorry." plus the bird names; the drafter should use these exact words
- Marcus asks two questions only; he does not interrogate her; his restraint is professional and also kind — he knows more than she thinks, and he will not complicate what she has done by showing his hand
- Hannah's statement "because of the German" is the chapter's thematic close: she has identified Marcus correctly as the repository of the German-world knowledge in Coldwater; this is what he is; this is what belonging looks like from the outside
- The "lighter" feeling at the end: render through physical image and sensation, not through the word "lighter" or any labeled emotion
