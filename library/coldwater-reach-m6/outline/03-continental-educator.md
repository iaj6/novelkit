# Chapter 03 — Continental Educator

**One-line summary**: Marcus in his Coldwater routine hears of the body; the texture of Henry and Lillian's household; the quality of being not-yet-home.

**POV**: Dr. Marcus Klein  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Coldwater Reach High School → street → Henry and Lillian Crow's house  
**Time**: Late October 1943 (a week or two after the body)  
**Target words**: ~2,500

**Chapter shape**: Two-scene with transition  
**Ending mode**: Literary fade

---

## Goal
Establish Marcus's register and his position in Coldwater — the careful, slightly formal quality of the translated man; his household; his relationship with Henry; the intermediate state between temporary and rooted.

## Obstacle
Marcus has no dramatic obstacle in this chapter. The difficulty is his ongoing condition: he is good at being provisional, and Coldwater is where that skill is currently exercised. The body is a thing he has heard about but has no access to yet.

---

## Opening texture
Transit: Marcus walking to school in the late-October morning. His register comes in at once — precise, slightly formal, the subordinate clause structure of a man who thinks in the architecture of complete propositions. He notices the harbor briefly at the end of the road as he turns. He does not dwell on it.

---

## Action beats (Scene 1 — school)

1. **The Latin class**: Marcus at the board with a Latin sentence — a subordinate clause from Cicero that is not translated, but sat with. Three students. He is teaching them to read rather than decode, and this distinction matters to him. His English in the classroom is careful, slightly formal; the students do not find it odd because they have learned to hear it as his.

2. **The staffroom gossip**: In the break between periods, the other teacher on the floor — a geography teacher named Stillman — mentions the body on the breakwater. "Man with no papers, they're saying. Drowned in the storm." Marcus asks one or two precise questions. Stillman doesn't know more than the surface. Marcus files it — not dismissively, but in the part of his mind where uncertain data waits.

3. **"Continental educator"**: Walking between classrooms, Marcus passes the principal's open door; on the desk, in plain view, is a folder with his name and the note the town meeting appended to his hire — "Continental educator." He has seen this before. He keeps walking.

---

## Transition

*Late afternoon, the walk home.* Marcus passes the post office, where the Western Union boy's bicycle is leaned against the wall. The bicycle is a shape of dread in this town: telegrams. He has watched a woman receive one from the door of the post office, from a distance, and has known what the boy was carrying without being told. He walks past and thinks, briefly, of Sherbrooke — not of the internment itself, but of the moment of arrival, the moment of papers being taken from his coat. He does not linger in this thought.

---

## Action beats (Scene 2 — Henry and Lillian's kitchen)

4. **Coming home**: Marcus enters through the back door into the kitchen, where Lillian is at the stove. She sets a plate without asking. This is, for Marcus, close to what home feels like: the lack of questions about his preferences, because after two years his preferences are known.

5. **Henry at the boiler**: Henry is downstairs fixing something — the familiar sounds of competent work coming through the floor. Marcus and Lillian exchange a few words; she asks about his day; he answers briefly and precisely. She asks no follow-up questions. This is also something like home.

6. **James's photograph**: On the kitchen wall, James Crow's Army photograph — formal, taken before deployment, Pacific theater written in the set of his shoulders. Marcus looks at it as he always does when he sits down to eat. He knows Henry and Lillian's son only from this photograph and from the letters that arrive and are not shared in full.

---

## Ending (literary fade)
Marcus in his room upstairs after supper with a book he is not reading. He can hear Henry closing the basement door, the house settling. The title of the chapter — "Continental educator" — has been in his head all day. In Vienna, he was Dr. Klein. In Paris he was the Austrian. In London he was the refugee. In Sherbrooke he was the enemy. In Cambridge he was the displaced scholar. In Coldwater he is the Continental educator. He is practicing something. He does not yet know if what he is practicing is Coldwater or himself.

---

## New question raised
What will it take for this place to become something other than provisional?

## Continuity notes
- Establishes: Marcus's school routine, classroom style, walking habit; Henry and Lillian's household as texture of warmth and practical competence; James Crow as absence on the wall
- Marcus has heard about the body but has no specific information and no connection to it yet — do NOT plant any premonition here; he is filing uncertain data, nothing more
- The Western Union bicycle: established now so it can be returned to later without reintroduction
- The "Continental educator" folder: Marcus has seen it before; this is a continuing small indignity, not a fresh wound; render it drily, not painfully
- Sherbrooke is named (the internment camp) but not explored — Marcus does not dwell in it; the prose should honor this
- Period texture: the school (1912 brick building), the staffroom, the walk, the kitchen; no dramatics; the ordinary life of a man living between two states of belonging
