**Rotation note**: POV alternates every chapter throughout — no sustained single-POV runs. This is deliberate: the braided structure functions like *The Hours* — three complete movements interleaved — and sustained interruption keeps each strand distinct without requiring omniscience to bridge them. The three exceptional chapters serve as structural correctives, each placed at a moment where the mystery requires a vantage no primary POV can occupy.

## The mystery assembly (reader's picture, assembled across 30 chapters)

**Werner Grau** — German national, emigrated to New England 1936 on an emergency visa that expired 1940 (illegal resident thereafter). Knew Albrecht Pfeiffer from Bavaria before either emigrated. Heard through the German expatriate network that a Karl Reinmar from near Ansbach was billeted at the Pfeiffer farm under the POW labor program. In late September 1943 he mailed a letter to Albrecht announcing his intention to travel to Coldwater; the letter was misdelivered to the school (Marcus receives it in ch. 06). Werner came up the coast by rented dinghy from Portland in early October. He arrived the night before the peak of the three-day blow. He was on the Coldwater waterfront — Eira saw him on the south dock around midnight with a second figure (whose gait she reads as "farm-type": Albrecht came down from the farm to meet him). Werner may have been already ill. The storm rose. He did not return to the dinghy. Three days later his body came in on the breakwater. His papers were in the dinghy, which the storm drove into the south cove where Eira recovers it in ch. 22.

No foul play is established. What happened between midnight and the storm's peak, and what passed between Werner and Albrecht, remains in shadow. The novel does not refuse to answer; it refuses to announce.

**Pieces and their sources**:
- Eira (chs. 01, 04, 07, 15, 22, 29): saw two figures on the dock; dinghy trace; Portland registration of the dinghy → name W. Grau; harbor log entry
- Marcus (chs. 06, 09, 13, 16, 23, 28): the misdelivered letter; Albrecht's recognition; Sondheimer's reply on Grau's illegal status; the complete picture assembled from Hannah's testimony
- Hannah (chs. 05, 10, 14, 20, 24, 28): photograph labeled "W. Grau, A. Pfeiffer, Bamberg 1921"; Karl's identification and his note "Werner Grau. From Ansbach. He is dead"; Albrecht's "Ich weiß"
- Karl (ch. 12): suspects Werner came to Maine; received word Werner was in America; uses past tense about him before he confirms
- Lonzo (ch. 21): saw an unknown man on the farm road the night of the storm
- Henry (ch. 26): saw an unknown man walking toward the harbor in the pre-dawn

## Arc shape

```
ACT I   (chs. 01–05)   SETUP        Body arrives; three principals established; mystery alive
ACT II  (chs. 06–11)   DEEPENING    Letter surfaces; POW-farm thread builds; harbor board threat;
                                     each POV character carrying something privately
ACT III (chs. 12–18)   MIDPOINT     Farm thread at pressure; mystery shifts (ch. 15-16 turn);
                                     D-Day; each arc takes on independent pressure
ACT IV  (chs. 19–24)   CONVERGENCE  Threads approach each other; Karl's name; transfer;
                                     the picture almost complete
ACT V   (chs. 25–30)   RESOLUTION   Harbor proceeding; exceptional chapters; arcs settle;
                                     war ends in background; last chapter quiet and irreversible
```
