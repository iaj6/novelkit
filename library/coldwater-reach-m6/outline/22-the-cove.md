# Chapter 22 — The Cove

**One-line summary**: Eira finds the abandoned dinghy in a south cove; the Portland registration returns a name: W. Grau, Franklin Street.

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Coldwater Reach harbor and outer coves → harbormaster's shed  
**Time**: September 1944  
**Target words**: ~2,500

**Chapter shape**: Single scene  
**Ending mode**: Elliptical

---

## Goal
Give Eira the documentary piece that connects the dinghy to Werner Grau: the Portland registration. She now has a name: W. Grau. She does not yet know what Marcus knows; she does not yet know what this name means to the Pfeiffer family. She has a name in a desk drawer and the column without a label now has a name in it.

## Obstacle
The dinghy is barely legible — storm-damaged, submerged for nearly a year. Reading the registration requires patience and the kind of attention Eira brings to physical evidence. The obstacle is not finding the dinghy; it is reading what's there.

---

## Opening texture
Routine-procedure: the autumn harbor survey. Eira in the harbor launch, conducting the annual outer-cove check. This is established work — every autumn, she checks for abandoned gear and unreported moorings.

---

## Action beats

1. **The autumn survey**: Eira runs the harbor launch through the outer coves in September — standard procedure, checking for abandoned equipment, drifted gear, unreported moorings. The weather is clear; the light in September has a particular quality (flat and honest, as she thinks of it).

2. **The south cove**: In the small cove behind the south headland — a sheltered spot where drifted things accumulate — she finds the dinghy. It is stove in amidships, partially sunk, its bow caught on a granite ledge. It has been here most of the year; the waterline growth says late autumn or winter.

3. **Reading the transom**: She beaches the launch and pulls the dinghy up by its bow ring. The transom is half-readable: the name is worn almost gone, but two letters survive: "—STEN" and below it a Portland harbor registration number. She writes the number in her log.

4. **Back at the shed**: She writes to the Portland Harbor Authority for the registration record. Standard form, routine inquiry — she reports abandoned vessels from time to time; this is the procedure.

5. **Three days later, the reply**: The Portland Harbor Authority's letter is brief: the registration number belongs to a rental dinghy operated out of a chandler's on Commercial Street, Portland. The dinghy was last rented in late September 1943. The renter gave a name and a street address.

6. **The name**: W. Grau, 114 Franklin Street, Portland, Maine.

---

## Outcome
She holds the paper. W. Grau. She does not know who that is. She has never heard the name — Marcus mentioned nothing to her in their brief harbor exchange (he asked about the Pfeiffer family; he didn't mention a name). She has a name. She does not yet have a person.

---

## Ending (elliptical)
She puts the Portland letter in the middle desk drawer with the CG form she has still not completed. The column without a label has a name in it now: W. Grau, 114 Franklin Street, Portland. A name is the beginning of something, not the end. The harbor is quiet in September; the fleet is in; the boatyard is operating on maintenance schedule. She looks at the drawer. She closes it.

---

## New question raised
W. Grau. What is the next step — does she try to confirm who W. Grau was, and if so, how? Does she send the CG form, finally, with the name included?

---

## Continuity notes
- The dinghy name: "—STEN" is legible on the stove-in transom; the full name was "ROTERSTEIN" (Red Stone) but this full name need not appear; the Portland registration is the evidence, not the dinghy's name
- The Portland chandler: Commercial Street address; a rental operation; the drafter does not need to make this specific beyond "chandler on Commercial Street"
- The street address given by Grau: 114 Franklin Street, Portland — the address he gave was his last known address; it will be referenced when Marcus is given this information (indirectly, through Hannah in ch. 28)
- Eira does NOT make the connection to Marcus (who asked about the Pfeiffer family) in this chapter; that connection is not available to her yet; it comes in ch. 29 when she finally makes the harbor log entry
- The CG form is still in the drawer, still unanswered; it is now accompanied by the Portland letter; the accumulation is building toward ch. 29
