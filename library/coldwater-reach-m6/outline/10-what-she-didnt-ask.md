# Chapter 10 — What She Didn't Ask

**One-line summary**: Hannah watches Marcus's visit from the farmhouse window; her father brings a folded paper inside; she does not ask.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Pfeiffer farmhouse — kitchen, window  
**Time**: January 1944 (the same day as ch. 09, from Hannah's angle)  
**Target words**: ~2,400

**Chapter shape**: Single scene  
**Ending mode**: Elliptical

---

## Goal
Show the same event from Hannah's angle — she witnesses the visit without knowing what it means; her father's response changes him fractionally; she notices and holds the question she doesn't ask. This chapter establishes Hannah's habit of noticing-without-acting, which is both her strength and a cost she is beginning to feel.

## Obstacle
Hannah is inside the house. She cannot hear what passes between Marcus and her father. She sees pantomime and must read it. The obstacle is: she is accurate in her reading and still doesn't know what to do with what she reads.

---

## Opening texture
Observation: Hannah at the farmhouse window, inside. She is at the kitchen table with a book; she looks up at a sound on the road and sees Marcus at the far end of the farm track. This is how the chapter opens — her attention turning outward before she knows what she's looking at.

---

## Action beats

1. **Recognition**: Hannah, from the kitchen window, watches the figure on the farm road come into focus: Dr. Klein, her German teacher, walking toward the farm. In January. On foot. She is surprised — not alarmed, just surprised. He is not a farm visitor. He is not from the farm world.

2. **Her father's face**: She watches through the window as Marcus reaches her father at the barn door. She cannot hear. Her father is reading something — an envelope, then a letter. She watches her father's face and reads it the way she reads faces: her father's is harder to read than most, because he has been practicing illegibility for months. But this: a change. Not surprise. Something that had been held very still is, briefly, not still.

3. **The folded paper**: The exchange ends; Marcus turns and walks back down the road. Her father stands at the barn door for a moment, then goes inside — not the barn, the house. He comes into the kitchen without noticing Hannah, which means he is thinking about something else. He has a folded paper in his hand, which he puts in his coat pocket.

4. **Coffee and bread**: Hannah sets bread and coffee before him without being asked — it is January, he has been outside, this is what you do. He sits. He puts his hands around the cup. He does not speak.

5. **What she could ask**: She could ask: *Who was that man?* She knows who it was — her teacher. She could ask: *What did he bring you?* She could ask: *Do you know something about the body on the breakwater?* These questions are available to her. She holds them all.

6. **What she asks instead**: She asks about the afternoon's milking — whether the herd count is off this week or if she miscounted on Tuesday. Her father answers, briefly, in English. This surprises her into not asking again: he has spoken in English.

---

## Ending (elliptical)
She washes up after dinner. Her father goes to his chair with his accounts. The folded paper is in his coat pocket, not her hands. She lies in bed and turns the question over: who writes to her father in German, and why does Dr. Klein deliver the letter by hand, and why did her father answer her in English for the first time in months? These questions have no place to go. She holds them in the dark.

---

## New question raised
What is Hannah's self-restraint costing her — and when will the accumulation become unsustainable?
