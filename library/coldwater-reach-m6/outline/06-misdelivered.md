# Chapter 06 — Misdelivered

**One-line summary**: A letter in German addressed to Albrecht Pfeiffer is misdelivered to Marcus at the school; the date and the signature connect it to the breakwater.

**POV**: Dr. Marcus Klein  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Coldwater Reach High School — Marcus's classroom, school office  
**Time**: November 1943 (mid-to-late November)  
**Target words**: ~2,500

**Chapter shape**: Single scene  
**Ending mode**: Turn

---

## Goal
Introduce the Werner Grau letter as the first piece of documentary evidence; establish Marcus as the person equipped (by language) to read what no one else in Coldwater can; begin his thread of the mystery.

## Obstacle
Marcus has no obligation to open the letter, no authority over it, and no standing to act on what it says. He reads it because he can and because it was left with him. What he does with it is the question the chapter raises but does not answer.

---

## Opening texture
Summons: a letter in his school mailbox, not addressed to him. The secretary's handwriting on the re-routing note. Marcus's register arrives immediately — precise, careful, the academic habit of attending to things in order.

---

## Action beats

1. **The school mailbox**: After morning classes, Marcus collects his mail from the school office box. Along with the usual (a circular from the state education department, a late student paper left in the slot) is an envelope addressed to "A. Pfeiffer, Coldwater Reach, Maine." The school secretary, Mrs. Ledbetter, has clipped a note: *Dr. Klein — this came to the school by mistake. As it's in German, I thought you'd be best placed to handle it. —M.L.*

2. **The envelope**: Marcus examines it in his classroom with the door closed. The handwriting is careful — not the educated hand of an academic, but not uneducated; the hand of someone who learned on the page in a particular tradition (German Kurrent-influenced but readable). Return address: a street in Portland, Maine. The postmark: September 28, 1943.

3. **The question of opening it**: He is aware that the letter is not his to open. He is also aware that no one else in Coldwater will be able to read it if he doesn't. He sets it on the desk. He picks it up. He opens it.

4. **The letter's content** (in Marcus's careful reading): The letter is in German, two pages in the same cramped hand. It is addressed "Lieber Albrecht" — Dear Albrecht — and is from someone who knew Albrecht before the war, before America, before everything. He is planning to travel up the coast in October; he hopes to see Albrecht "after so many years." He mentions that he has heard about the work camp arrangement — "that they have sent Reinmar's boy to your farm" — and he wants to leave a message for the Reinmar family through Karl, if possible; he has news about the Reinmar family in Ansbach that he has been carrying for months. He will come when he can arrange the transport. He signs it: *Ihr alter Freund, Werner Grau* — Your old friend, Werner Grau.

5. **The calculation**: Marcus reads it twice. He does not immediately announce the connection; he arrives at it in stages, the way a careful man arrives at conclusions. The postmark: September 28. The body on the breakwater: October 12. Werner Grau planned to travel to Coldwater in October. A man came and died in Coldwater in October.

6. **What he holds**: He folds the letter into its envelope. He puts it in his desk drawer, under a Latin dictionary. He sits for a long moment. The connection is plausible. It is not proof. He does not know what to do with plausible.

---

## Outcome / turn
Marcus has the letter. He has made the connection. He has no next move available to him that he finds clearly correct. The letter is in his desk drawer.

---

## Ending (turn)
He locks his classroom at the end of the day and walks home. On the walk, he passes the south dock. He does not know that the south dock is where Eira saw the figures. He glances at it as he always does and keeps walking. The turn is in his coat pocket where the connection between Werner Grau and the body on the breakwater sits, sealed.

---

## New question raised
What should Marcus do with this letter? Who has standing to act on it — and does he?

---

## Continuity notes
- Werner Grau's letter is the documentary spine of Marcus's thread; its contents as summarized above are precise and must be consistent across later chapters when they are referenced
- The letter is in German; its contents are rendered to the reader through Marcus's reading, not as a document insert
- Key contents: "Lieber Albrecht"; plans to come in October; "Reinmar's boy at your farm"; news of the Reinmar family in Ansbach; signed "Werner Grau, Portland"
- Postmark: September 28, 1943 — this date is important for later chapter (ch. 13) when Marcus confirms the illegal status, and ch. 09 when he brings it to Albrecht
- Marcus does NOT mention the letter to anyone in this chapter; it goes into the desk drawer, not into conversation
- Mrs. Ledbetter (secretary) is established as a named minor character; she is not suspicious, just pragmatic
- The south dock: Marcus walks past it without significance; he doesn't know about Eira's dock observation yet; this is a structural irony for the reader
