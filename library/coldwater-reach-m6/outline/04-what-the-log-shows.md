# Chapter 04 — What the Log Shows

**One-line summary**: The CG lieutenant closes the case; Eira gives him the minimum; the harbor log entry she writes leaves the dock figures off the page.

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Harbormaster's shed; the south dock  
**Time**: Late October / early November 1943 (ten days to two weeks after the body)  
**Target words**: ~2,400

**Chapter shape**: Single scene  
**Ending mode**: Resolved beat

---

## Goal
Complete the official closure of the breakwater case, establish that Eira is withholding information (not out of guilt but out of insufficient certainty), and show the cost of keeping a misfiled entry.

## Obstacle
Lt. Mercer's professional courtesy is itself the obstacle: he gives Eira every reasonable opportunity to add information. She doesn't take them, because what she has doesn't yet constitute an entry that belongs anywhere official.

---

## Opening texture
Summons: Lt. Mercer's second visit. Eira is doing winter mooring paperwork when he arrives. The chapter opens on the task — what needs doing — and the summons interrupts it.

---

## Action beats

1. **Mercer's second visit**: Lt. Mercer arrives at the shed, polite and efficient. He has the manner of a man who has done this before: the follow-up visit that closes a case. He carries a manila folder, which will leave with him closed.

2. **The questions**: He asks whether she has noticed anything unusual in the days before the storm — unexpected vessels, anything in the harbor not registered. She answers truthfully: nothing in the harbor log she didn't account for. This is technically true. The dock is not the harbor; she logged the dock separately in her memory and nowhere official.

3. **What he tells her**: The case will be closed as unidentified drowning. No evidence of crime. The man may have been off a boat that didn't report him overboard, or a transient who got too close to the water in the dark. No identification has been made; inquiries to the Portland and Rockland harbormasters have turned up nothing. The Coast Guard thanks her for her prompt response.

4. **What she does not tell him**: She does not describe the two figures on the south dock. She has thought about this more than once in the past ten days. What she has is: an image, a time (approximately midnight), a description (two men, one not a sailor). She has no name, no vessel, no corroboration. She is not a detective and it is not her case. This reasoning is correct and she knows the reasoning is doing something it should not.

5. **Mercer leaves**: The exchange ends, the folder goes with him. She walks Mercer to the dock and watches him drive away.

6. **The harbor log**: Back in the shed, she opens the log to the October 12 entry. She reads what she wrote: weather conditions, body discovered, CG notified, time, CG responded, body removed. She considers, for a measured moment, adding the dock observation. She turns the page and does not.

7. **The cost**: She stands at the window and looks at the south dock. The column is wrong — she can feel the wrongness of it the way a navigator feels a miscalculated fix. She accepts it, for now, as the cost of not knowing enough. An incomplete entry is not a false entry. This distinction is the line she is standing on.

---

## Outcome / turn
The case is officially closed. Eira has kept her private knowledge private, and she has made a small internal accounting of why — not out of dishonesty but out of epistemological fastidiousness. She doesn't know enough to make the entry correct. So she does not make it.

---

## Ending (resolved beat)
She closes the log. The harbor is hers to run; the weather report for the weekend is unfavorable; she has mooring paperwork to finish before the light goes. She sits back down at the desk. The entry is wrong. The work continues.

---

## New question raised
Will she ever put the dock figures in the right column? And when she does — will it be too late for it to matter?

---

## Continuity notes
- Lt. Mercer established as a recurring name/face (he'll be referenced later when Eira considers reporting what she knows)
- The harbor log is established as the official record of the harbor; chapter 29's addition to it will require this precedent
- Eira's withholding is NOT motivated by protection of anyone — she does not know who the figures were; she is withholding because she doesn't have a complete entry, not to shield Albrecht or anyone else
- Keep Eira's interior passages short; three sentences maximum before she returns her attention to a physical task; her reasoning about the log must feel like inventory, not self-justification
- No dramatic tension; this is bureaucratic procedure and its private cost; the emotional register is the gap between what she filed and what she knows
