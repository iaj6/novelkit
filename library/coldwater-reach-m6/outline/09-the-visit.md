# Chapter 09 — The Visit

**One-line summary**: Marcus brings the misdelivered letter to Albrecht in person; Albrecht's face on reading the name tells Marcus that Albrecht already knew Werner had come.

**POV**: Dr. Marcus Klein  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Route from Coldwater to Pfeiffer farm → the farm itself  
**Time**: January 1944  
**Target words**: ~3,000 (intensive chapter — the recognition scene is a structural pivot)

**Chapter shape**: Single scene  
**Ending mode**: Turn

---

## Goal
Deliver the letter to Albrecht and read his response; establish that Albrecht's knowledge predates the letter's delivery (he knew Werner had come, which means he met him, which means the second figure on the dock was Albrecht); advance Marcus's understanding without arriving at certainty.

## Obstacle
Marcus is making a visit to a man he barely knows, carrying a letter that was never meant for him, about a dead man whose identity he can only infer. He has no standing for any of this. He does it anyway because his particular kind of attention will not let him file a misfiled thing.

---

## Opening texture
Transit: Marcus walking the three miles from town to the farm. The January road, the landscape flattening into drumlins. His thoughts on the walk establish why he is going (not to investigate; to restore — the letter belongs to Albrecht) and what he does not intend to say (he will not mention his connection of Grau to the body unless Albrecht opens that door).

---

## Action beats

1. **The walk out**: Three miles, January cold. Henry offered to drive him; Marcus declined — not out of pride but because the walk is how he prepares for things; movement before a difficult conversation. He thinks, on the walk, about what he knows and what he doesn't: Werner Grau wrote to Albrecht; Werner Grau came to Coldwater; a man without papers died on the breakwater in October. The last step in this sequence is inference, not fact. He carries this carefully.

2. **First view of the farm**: The farm road; the barn, the icehouse bunkhouse; a POW crossing the yard with a feed bucket (not Karl — one of the older men). The POW looks at Marcus with the flat recognition of a man who has learned to assess strangers quickly. Marcus nods. The POW goes about his work.

3. **Meeting Albrecht**: Albrecht is at the barn door, examining a wheel on the manure spreader. He sees Marcus coming and straightens. Marcus introduces himself carefully: he teaches at the school; he knows Hannah; a letter was misdelivered to him that he believed belonged to Albrecht. He takes the envelope from his coat.

4. **Albrecht reads the letter**: Albrecht opens it. He reads it. Marcus watches him with the careful attention he brings to language: the way a face is a text, if you know how to read it. What passes over Albrecht's face is not surprise. Marcus identifies it precisely: recognition. Not the recognition of learning something new — the recognition of receiving confirmation of something already known. Albrecht already knew Werner had come to Coldwater. He knew it in October, when the body came in.

5. **The exchange**: Albrecht folds the letter and asks, in careful English: "When did this come to you?" Marcus says: November. Albrecht nods, once. He says, "Thank you," and the words close the conversation — not rudely, but with the finality of a door latched from inside.

6. **Marcus doesn't push**: He does not ask Albrecht the questions he is holding. He has no right to them and would not get answers if he asked. He says it is no trouble, turns, and walks back down the farm road.

7. **The walk back and the inference**: On the walk back, Marcus runs the sequence again. Albrecht read the letter and was not surprised. Which means Albrecht knew Werner Grau had come in October. Which means Albrecht saw him — or heard that he came — before the storm. Albrecht has been sitting with this since October. The letter in his hand is not new information; it is Werner's voice arriving three months after Werner.

---

## Outcome / turn
Marcus reaches town. He knows, with near-certainty, that Albrecht Pfeiffer met or saw Werner Grau before the storm, and that the body on the breakwater was Werner Grau. He has no proof. He has the letter and Albrecht's face.

---

## Ending (turn)
He enters Henry's house through the back. Lillian is at the stove. He hangs up his coat. In the pocket where the letter was there is now nothing. Albrecht has it; it was always Albrecht's. The space where the letter was feels like the shape of a question.

---

## New question raised
Did Albrecht meet Werner on the south dock the night before the storm? Is the farm-type figure Eira saw on the dock Albrecht? And what does Albrecht know about what happened that night?

---

## Continuity notes
- This chapter is a structural linchpin: it establishes (for Marcus, and through his inference for the reader) that the body is Werner Grau, and that Albrecht Pfeiffer's knowledge predates the letter
- Marcus does NOT verbalize his conclusion to anyone in this chapter; it stays internal
- Albrecht's English is established as "careful, slightly stilted" — not phonetic German, just the English of a man who has decided to stop speaking it easily; render this through his few spoken lines
- Karl is NOT in this scene — he may be working in the fields or elsewhere; Hannah may also be absent (at school on a weekday); the scene is between Marcus and Albrecht only
- The farm is rendered through Marcus's detailed observational attention — he notices things a scholar notices: the dates on the barn timbers, the Latin name of a weed in the dooryard (he can't help it)
- Marcus carrying the letter in his coat and bringing back nothing: a physical gesture that carries the emotional weight of transfer — he has given over what he was holding, and now holds something else (inference)
