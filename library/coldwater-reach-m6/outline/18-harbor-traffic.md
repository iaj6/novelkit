# Chapter 18 — Harbor Traffic

**One-line summary**: The harbor in summer; Marcus passes and asks, in three sentences, about the Pfeiffer family; Eira files this in a column with no label yet.

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Coldwater Reach harbor — the fish pier, the fuel dock  
**Time**: June 1944 (post-D-Day; high summer)  
**Target words**: ~2,400

**Chapter shape**: Single scene  
**Ending mode**: Resolved beat

---

## Goal
Eira's arc in the post-D-Day world: the harbor is busier; she is managing it well; the authority challenge is dormant; she is in her competence. The encounter with Marcus — brief, apparently casual — plants a new data point that she cannot yet label: he asked about the Pfeiffer family. Why? The chapter is about noticing the small data point and filing it correctly as "unlabeled" rather than ignoring it.

## Obstacle
There is no dramatic obstacle in this chapter. It is a chapter about competence and an apparently routine day through which a small thread passes. The difficulty is in the register: keeping the chapter from feeling static while the mystery simply adds one beat.

---

## Opening texture
Routine-procedure: the harbor in summer. Marine traffic (not war traffic — the Navy's coastal operations use the outer harbor; this is the working harbor of lobstermen and draggers). Eira at the pier head, managing the afternoon's comings and goings.

---

## Action beats

1. **Summer harbor work**: More vessels, more traffic, more documentation. The mood in the harbor has changed since June 6 — not euphoric, but a different quality of patience. Men who have been waiting, with their eyes turned toward news from overseas, are still waiting but at a different angle. Eira works through the afternoon's log: fuel allotments, vessel movements, two disputed berth assignments she resolves without arbitration.

2. **The harbor as hers**: A moment here — something she does with ease that in 1942 required effort; the fishermen who come to her dock have stopped showing her their surprise. She is not registering this as triumph; she is registering it as the tide table: what's in the column, accurate.

3. **Marcus on the waterfront**: Marcus Klein walks past the harbor on his late-afternoon routine — she has seen him walk this road, the same road, at the same hour, for two years. He is the kind of man who keeps his routines. As he passes the pier entrance he pauses, which is unusual; he usually nods and continues. He says: "Good afternoon" — then: "Are the Pfeiffer family managing all right, do you think?" He asks this with no particular inflection; it is a careful question.

4. **Eira's answer**: She gives him a brief answer — something like "As far as I know" — which is accurate. She doesn't know the Pfeiffer family well; she knows the farm is viable; she knows the POW arrangement hasn't become a problem for the harbor. She has nothing more specific.

5. **He goes on**: He nods, says "Thank you," and continues his walk. The exchange has taken perhaps forty seconds.

6. **The filing**: She watches him go. She thinks about the question. He asked her — specifically — about the Pfeiffer family. Not in a social way; she can distinguish social from specific. Why would the German teacher ask the harbormaster about an inland farm family? There is no obvious reason. She puts the question in a column she has no label for yet; the column already has at least one other unlabeled entry (the dock figures in October).

---

## Outcome / resolved beat
She turns back to the pier. The afternoon is full. She has four vessels left to log before dark. She does the work. The column without a label now has two entries — the dock figures and Marcus's question about the Pfeiffer family — and she is not yet ready to label it, but she knows where it is.

---

## Ending (resolved beat)
The last boat logged at 6:15. The harbor quiet as summer evenings go. She walks back to the shed and makes her entries. Everything in the right column that she has enough to classify. Everything else — she does not pretend the unlabeled column isn't there.

---

## New question raised
What is Marcus Klein carrying about the Pfeiffer family, and what is the connection between his knowledge and what she saw on the dock?

---

## Continuity notes
- This is the only chapter where Eira and Marcus share a space and exchange words; the exchange is brief (four lines), not a scene of revelation; they do not become confidants
- Marcus's question is not an accident — he has been thinking about the Pfeiffer family since ch. 09 (the visit) and ch. 16 (the assembled account); he is checking, obliquely, without knowing what Eira has
- Eira files Marcus's question as "unlabeled" — she does not act on it here; this becomes relevant when she later considers who else might have pieces of the puzzle
- The harbor is busier post-D-Day in ways that are realistic for 1944 coastal Maine (supply operations, Navy contract work)
- Period texture: summer harbor work; the mood of the town post-D-Day (careful hope rather than celebration)
