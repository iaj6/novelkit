# Chapter 05 — Advanced German

**One-line summary**: School, Marcus's class, town talk about the body — and Karl, in the dark outside the icehouse, says a German word Hannah writes in the margin.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Coldwater Reach High School → post office → Pfeiffer farm (evening)  
**Time**: November 1943  
**Target words**: ~2,500

**Chapter shape**: Two-scene with transition  
**Ending mode**: Turn

---

## Goal
Establish Marcus through Hannah's eyes (distinct from his own chapters); develop Hannah's intellectual life and her relationship with Marcus as the one adult outside her household who meets her curiosity squarely; end with Karl as a figure she is paying attention to.

## Obstacle
Hannah is operating between two worlds — the farm, which has its own language, and the school, which has the world's. The challenge is not dramatic; it is the quiet friction of an intelligent seventeen-year-old who lives in a place that is not organized around her intelligence.

---

## Opening texture
Routine-procedure: school. Hannah's register opens differently from Eira's or Marcus's — more sensory, wider tonal range. The school bus, the cold, the particular smell of the school building (chalk, floor wax, cafeteria steam).

---

## Action beats (Scene 1 — school)

1. **Advanced German, third period**: Three students in the class: Hannah, a boy named Roland who is there because his mother insisted, and a girl named Patrice who is genuinely good. The text they're translating is from a Rilke poem — Marcus chose it because the grammar was right, not for the subject. He is teaching them syntax, not feeling.

2. **Hannah reads Marcus**: She watches him teach with the particular attention she brings to people worth watching. He chooses words the way she has noticed he always chooses words: as if the right one is available and the approximate one is not an option. She is the only one in the class who understands this about how he works.

3. **Town talk at lunch**: At lunch, two girls from her class are on the body again — a month later and it's still the only news the town has. The story has acquired details in the telling: he was a sailor, he was drunk, he was running away from something. Hannah listens and says nothing. She is aware that she noticed something (her father's hands stopping) that these girls would not have noticed.

4. **Marcus after class**: Hannah stays a moment — she has a question about the translation that turns out to be a real question, not a pretext for conversation. Marcus answers it precisely. He uses the German word first, then the English, as he always does. She notices this: he trusts the German. She has been taught that trusting the German is complicated.

---

## Transition

*After school, post office.* Hannah picks up the farm mail — seed catalog, OPA ration-book renewal notice, a letter from a cousin in Portland. At the window she sees Marcus at the postal counter, and they exchange four words — "Hello, Miss Pfeiffer" / "Hello, Dr. Klein" — and then they both go back to their business. She notices his mail: one envelope, academic address in the return corner. She doesn't read the name. She thinks: a person with letters coming to them is a person with a life somewhere else.

---

## Action beats (Scene 2 — farm evening)

5. **Albrecht at dinner**: Hannah puts the seed catalog at her father's place; he looks through it during the meal, which is his form of reading at the table. Her mother is present and not present. The radio is off. They eat.

6. **Homework at the kitchen table**: Hannah works on the German translation at the kitchen table. Her father, across the room, is doing the farm accounts. She is aware that she is the only person in the house doing German homework — her father's German is not homework; it is his retreat — and that she is translating toward a language that he has retreated into. She doesn't have a thought about this. She just has the awareness.

7. **Karl outside the icehouse**: She goes to the window to close the curtain before bed. Karl is standing outside the icehouse door — not smoking, just standing. He looks up at the sky, which is clear and cold. She can barely hear him from her window, but she hears a word: something that sounds like *Seidenschwanz*. She doesn't know it. She says it under her breath to keep the sound.

---

## Outcome / turn
She goes to bed. She opens her German translation and writes *Seidenschwanz* in the margin — not because it was on the page, but because it wants to be somewhere. She doesn't know what it means. She will find out.

---

## Ending (turn)
She has written the word. This is the turn: she is paying attention, in ink, to Karl Reinmar.

---

## New question raised
What is Karl carrying that comes out only in the dark, in German, to himself?

---

## Continuity notes
- Marcus in this chapter is seen through Hannah's eyes — his voice should be consistent with his own chapters but refracted through her perception; she sees the precision before she understands its source
- The advanced German class: Hannah, Roland (not important), Patrice — established
- The mail detail: Hannah sees Marcus receiving mail with an academic return address; this will not become significant until ch. 06, when we see from Marcus's POV what the letter is (but in ch. 06 the misdelivered letter is addressed TO Albrecht, found in Marcus's school mailbox — Hannah's observation here of Marcus's own incoming mail is a separate thing; do not conflate)
- *Seidenschwanz*: German for cedar waxwing (Bombycilla cedrorum). Karl says it as a naming, to himself, as if maintaining a private lexicon. Hannah writes it without knowing the meaning. This word recurs in ch. 08 (Cedar Waxwing) and ch. 20 (Field Birds).
- Albrecht's retreat into German is rendered here through Hannah's observational frame — no psychology, just the fact that the German translation assignment and her father's German occupy the same kitchen without acknowledging each other
