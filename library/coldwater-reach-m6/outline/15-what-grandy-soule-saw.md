# Chapter 15 — What Grandy Soule Saw

**One-line summary**: A lobsterman mentions a strange dinghy at the south pier the night of the storm; the pieces arrange themselves; Eira takes out the CG form.

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Harbormaster's shed; the fish pier; the harbor waterfront  
**Time**: April–May 1944 (spring reopening of the harbor)  
**Target words**: ~2,600

**Chapter shape**: Two-scene with transition  
**Ending mode**: Turn

---

## Goal
Give Eira the new piece that shifts the midpoint: an eyewitness to the dinghy at the south pier, corroborating the timeline and confirming that Werner came by boat. The pieces now arrange themselves into a near-complete picture for Eira: a man came from outside by dinghy, was on the dock, did not return to the dinghy. She takes out the CG form she has not answered.

## Obstacle
The information comes casually, from a fisherman who didn't think it significant — who still doesn't think it significant. Eira has to receive it without revealing what she already knows and why it matters.

---

## Opening texture
Routine-procedure: the harbor coming back to life in spring. Lobstermen bringing their boats back to the water after winter haul-out; Eira doing spring registration and the annual vessel documentation. The harbor is alive in a way it wasn't in February.

---

## Action beats (Scene 1 — the pier)

1. **Spring harbor work**: Eira registering the returning fleet at the pier head — a social and procedural event at once; fishermen she has known for years, the rhythm of the annual exchange. She is in her element. The war's presence: two fewer boats than last spring (one conscripted for patrol; one lost to age and no son to inherit it).

2. **Grandy Soule**: Grandy Soule ties up at the fuel dock — lobsterman, seventy or thereabouts, a man who has been reading this harbor since before Eira was born. He doesn't waste words. He fills his tank, pays, and as he is going says — in the context of nothing, the way he says everything — "Saw a strange dinghy at the south pier that October night. Before your blow."

3. **The telling**: Eira does not react visibly. She asks him what he means — which pier, what night. He clarifies: the night before the big storm came on, late, after midnight, he drove past on his way back from Owls Head. Saw a dinghy tied at the south pier that didn't have a Coldwater registration on the transom. Numbers he didn't recognize. He thought: someone from Rockland. He didn't stop. "Only the one man. Come off her and went toward the waterfront. Didn't see him come back."

4. **The receipt of this**: Eira thanks him. Soule goes. She does not show what she is assembling.

---

## Transition

*After Soule leaves.* Eira stands at the end of the fuel pier with the spring harbor in front of her. She lays the pieces against each other as she would lay a vessel's logs against the tide tables. A dinghy at the south pier, unregistered, before the peak of the storm. A man who came off it and went toward the waterfront. He did not return to the dinghy. Three days later, his body on the breakwater. She saw two men on the south dock that night — she saw the man and someone else. Soule saw one man going toward the waterfront; whoever the second figure was came separately, or Soule left before the second figure arrived.

The dinghy is the transport. The man who came off it is the body. This is now, if not proof, an account. It has a shape.

---

## Action beats (Scene 2 — the shed)

5. **The CG form**: Back in the shed. She opens the middle drawer and takes out the Coast Guard form letter that asked for any new information about the unidentified drowning from October 1943. She has not answered it. She sets it on the desk. She looks at it.

6. **What she has and what she doesn't**: She has: her own observation (two figures on the south dock, approximately midnight, October 11, 1943); Soule's observation (a dinghy at the south pier, one man who came off it and went toward the waterfront). She does not have: a name for the man; a vessel registration that will check; proof that the second figure she saw was anything other than a dock laborer. What she could report is accurate and incomplete in ways she cannot fill.

7. **The form**: She reads the form again. She can fill in the blank for "any new information" with Soule's dinghy observation — that, at least, is not her private knowledge. It is Soule's, and Soule would confirm it if asked. She could file this partial piece.

---

## Ending (turn)
She picks up her pen. This is the turn: she is going to do something with what she knows, rather than continue to hold it alone. She has not yet written anything, but the pen is in her hand. This is the furthest she has come.

---

## New question raised
What will she write on the form — her own dock observation, or only Soule's dinghy sighting? And will the Coast Guard do anything with it now?

---

## Continuity notes
- Grandy Soule: established in the chapter map as a named fisherman; this is his primary appearance; he does not know the significance of what he saw and never learns it in the novel
- Soule saw "one man" — he did not see the second figure (Albrecht), who came separately or later; this means Soule's testimony is incomplete but corroborating
- Eira's inference is correct but she does not have the dinghy's registration yet — that comes in ch. 22 when she finds the dinghy abandoned in the cove; in this chapter she knows a dinghy was there but not whose
- She is holding her pen at the end of this chapter; in ch. 22 and ch. 29, the question of what she files will advance further
- Period texture: spring harbor reopening; lobster boat registration renewal; the fuel dock; spring vessel inspections — these are the texture of the scene, not background
