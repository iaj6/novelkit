# Chapter 07 — Winter Registry

**One-line summary**: Harbor work in December; word that the county authority is looking at her tenure; she walks the breakwater and does not write down what she saw.

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Harbormaster's shed; the harbor waterfront; the post office; the breakwater  
**Time**: December 1943  
**Target words**: ~2,500

**Chapter shape**: Two-scene with transition  
**Ending mode**: Elliptical

---

## Goal
Establish the harbor board challenge as a slow-building pressure on Eira's arc; show winter harbor work as the texture of her daily competence; plant the first suggestion that the dock observation and the harbor authority threat are not unconnected in the way they weigh on her.

## Obstacle
The authority challenge is not yet formalized — it's a word from a fisherman, not an official notice. She cannot act on it directly. She does her work. The obstacle is the impossibility of defending herself against something that has not yet declared itself.

---

## Opening texture
Routine-procedure: the harbor in December. The lobster boats hauling the last gear before winter. Eira doing the winter mooring registry — assigning slots, verifying documentation, logging the vessels that will winter over. This is procedural and competent and quiet.

---

## Action beats (Scene 1 — harbor work)

1. **The winter registry**: Eira at the shed desk with the mooring roster; she cross-checks registrations against the harbor log; two boats are not in compliance; she writes notices. The harbor in December: half the working fleet is hauled; the boatyard railway has been running; the cold-storage house is operating on reduced hours.

2. **Wendell Foss**: The lobsterman Wendell Foss brings his boat to the fuel dock, ties up, and says what he knows without ceremony: "Heard they're putting someone from the county board to look at the harbormaster situation." He says it the way you say weather — it's information, not a judgment. He doesn't think it's any good, but he doesn't say that.

3. **Eira's calculation**: She takes the information. She doesn't ask who or how. She thanks him and goes back to the fuel record. After he leaves, she runs the numbers: eight years of service, every record correct, no casualty attributed to negligence, harbor income up eleven percent over Owen's last two years. The numbers are what they are. The numbers are not what the challenge is about.

4. **The annual report**: She writes and sends the standard annual report to the county authority — required, routine, never previously been the cause of anything. This year she is aware of writing it as if it will be read by someone looking for something to find.

---

## Transition

*Late afternoon, post office.* She picks up a registered letter — the county authority's acknowledgment of receipt of her annual report, boilerplate. Also a notice from the state harbor authority about new winter storm protocol requirements, which she will implement. The Western Union boy's bicycle is not there today; she notices its absence as a small relief, which is also a kind of accounting.

---

## Action beats (Scene 2 — breakwater walk)

5. **The evening walk**: Every November and December she walks the breakwater at the end of the day. It is not sentimental; it is maintenance — checking the face of the structure, noting any movement in the granite, watching the tide height on the south ladder. She has done this for eight years. She does it now.

6. **The place where the body was**: She passes the elbow where the body lay. The stone is clean now — storm-washed several times over. She notes the granite course at the elbow, which has shifted fractionally; she makes a note for the spring assessment.

7. **The dock figures, held**: Standing at the elbow, she catalogs them again as she has done several times since October. One unfamiliar man. One second person — farmer's build — barely seen. The time: approximately midnight. The dock: the south dock, where boats not registered at this harbor sometimes tie briefly without logging in. What does she have? She has an image. She has a time. She has the body on the other end of a three-day storm.

---

## Ending (elliptical)
She walks back along the breakwater. The tide is coming in; she reads the height on the granite marks on the south ladder — accurate as always. She does not write down the dock figures. The wind comes off the water and she turns her collar up. The entry stays wrong. She has accepted this, for now. For now is the part she does not examine.

---

## New question raised
How long can she keep something in the wrong column before the columns themselves begin to fail?
