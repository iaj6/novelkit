# Chapter 11 — Load Calculations

**One-line summary**: The county inspector arrives; Eira shows him the numbers; the challenge is delayed, not resolved.

**POV**: Eira Bowman  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Harbormaster's shed; the harbor waterfront  
**Time**: February 1944  
**Target words**: ~2,500

**Chapter shape**: Single scene  
**Ending mode**: Resolved beat

---

## Goal
Show Eira's competence in direct action: she meets the official challenge with documentation and wins the argument on the merits. But winning the argument today does not resolve the question of the post. Establish that the challenge is structural and will return.

## Obstacle
Amos Tarbell has been sent by the county to find something wrong. He is polite and not hostile, but his presence is the challenge. Eira's obstacle is that the problem is not the numbers — she knows the numbers are correct — and the numbers are all she's permitted to argue.

---

## Opening texture
Summons: Tarbell's arrival at the shed. Eira is at her desk when he comes in, which is where she is most of the time. The chapter opens on the inspection as it is happening, not on Eira waiting for it.

---

## Action beats

1. **Tarbell arrives**: Amos Tarbell, county harbor authority inspector, mid-thirties, clipboard, polite in the way of a man who has been told to be. He introduces himself. He has the manner of someone who expected to be dealing with a man and has decided not to be surprised that he is not.

2. **The credentials question**: He asks her qualifications. She answers: eight years as harbormaster, previously assistant to the harbormaster (Owen Bowman, her husband). She does not offer more than he asks for. He writes something on his clipboard.

3. **The tour**: She takes him through the harbor: the winter mooring registry (accurate to the vessel), the fuel allocation ledger (in balance), the vessel registration file (current), the safety inspection record (no outstanding citations). She answers his questions with the precision she brings to everything. He cannot find what he was sent to find.

4. **The "transitional" question**: Tarbell asks, carefully, about the "transitional nature" of her appointment — she has been acting harbormaster, not formally appointed harbormaster. She points to the county authority letter from 1935, signed by the previous director, appointing her to the post pending review. The review was never conducted. She has never pressed for it. She has simply done the work.

5. **Owen's last entry**: She pulls the harbor log from 1935 — Owen's last entry, November 7, and her first, November 9. "A continuity of record," she says, without affect. "Unbroken." Tarbell looks at the logs. He writes something longer on his clipboard.

6. **The "some parties" language**: He mentions that "some parties on the county board" have concerns about the "long-term stability" of the arrangement. She asks him, precisely, what would constitute stability to the county board's satisfaction. He doesn't have a clean answer. This tells her everything: the challenge is political, not administrative.

---

## Outcome / turn
Tarbell leaves having found no administrative deficiency. The numbers won the argument today. But the political challenge remains; it was not a numbers challenge and will not die of a numbers answer. She won the inspection. She did not win the post.

---

## Ending (resolved beat)
She closes the 1935 log back onto the shelf. The shed is hers in the way it has always been hers — every paper in order, every entry correct. She puts the tea kettle on. The harbor is quiet in February; the boats that are left are the ones that know their business. She does her work. The challenge will come again in a different form.

---

## New question raised
What form will the challenge take when it comes back, and what will Eira have to do to settle it for good?

---

## Continuity notes
- Amos Tarbell: named and characterized; he will be referenced when the formal proceeding arrives (ch. 25) — he may or may not be the same official; the county board is the relevant body
- The "acting harbormaster" detail: established here as a technical vulnerability; the formal appointment was never confirmed; this is the lever the county authority has
- Owen's harbor log: established in ch. 01 as on the shelf; used here as evidence; continues the thread of his absence as structural (his last entry before her first)
- The political nature of the challenge: explicitly named here; this sets up the harbor proceeding in ch. 25 as a political event, not an administrative one
- Period texture: wartime harbor operations under USCG and state authority; the fuel allocation ledger is real (supplemental fuel allocations for harbor operations in wartime); the vessel registration is routine
