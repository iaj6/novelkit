# Chapter 02 — Icehouse

**One-line summary**: Autumn on the Pfeiffer farm: the POWs are three weeks in, Albrecht is retreating, and Hannah begins to notice Karl Reinmar.

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Pfeiffer dairy farm — barn, farmyard, icehouse, kitchen, Hannah's bedroom  
**Time**: Mid-October 1943 (the same week as ch. 01, or a few days after)  
**Target words**: ~2,600

**Chapter shape**: Two-scene with transition  
**Ending mode**: Literary fade

---

## Goal
Introduce Hannah's register and the farm world; establish the POW arrangement and Karl; show Albrecht's retreat and Magda's silence; plant the body's arrival as background noise.

## Obstacle
Hannah is trying to see clearly — her parents, the farm, the strangers in the icehouse — but the tools she has are seventeen years old and still learning what they're for.

---

## Opening texture
Routine-procedure: morning chores in autumn. Milk, feed, the cold barn. Hannah's register comes in immediately — wider sensory range, more associative, German phrases available when her father's voice is in her head. The farm is established as a world that has its own complete logic.

---

## Action beats (Scene 1 — morning)

1. **Morning chores**: Hannah feeds the dairy cows, notes the October cold settling in, carries the milk pails in the early light. Her competence is assumed, not performed.

2. **The POWs across the yard**: The four German prisoners emerge from the icehouse bunkhouse at 6:45 under Lonzo Reed's supervision. They cross the yard to the barn. They have been here three weeks; the routines are established. Hannah observes them from the barn door.

3. **Karl Reinmar**: The youngest of the four — nineteen, slight, moving with the not-quite-competence of someone doing farm labor he didn't grow up with. He carries a bucket wrong; one of the older Germans corrects him silently. He sees Hannah watching him and looks away first.

4. **Albrecht in the barn**: Her father directs the morning work. He speaks to the POWs in German — short directives, nothing intimate. When Hannah hands him the feed tally, he says *danke* to her without looking up. She answers in English; he does not adjust.

5. **Magda**: Hannah brings breakfast to her mother in the kitchen. Magda is at the table, not eating, her hands around a cup that has gone cold. She receives the plate without speaking. Hannah sits with her for a moment. Then leaves her there.

---

## Transition

*Afternoon school bus to town.* On the bus, two girls from her class are talking about the body on the breakwater — heard from a father who heard from the fishermen. A man with no name, no papers. The Coast Guard came. It's not anyone from here. The conversation is lurid and not unkind; Hannah listens. She thinks about papers. About what it means to come somewhere without them.

---

## Action beats (Scene 2 — evening)

6. **Dinner**: Albrecht, Magda, Hannah. The radio is off. Hannah mentions, carefully, that she heard about the body on the breakwater. Her father's hands stop on his utensils for a moment. Then he resumes eating. He does not comment. Magda has not looked up.

7. **Hannah at her window**: After dishes, Hannah goes to her room. Through her window she can see the icehouse. A lamp is lit inside. One of the POWs is outside the door, smoking. She watches him: the young one, Karl. He is looking up at the sky and saying something — not to anyone, just saying it, the way you say something that wants to be said.

---

## Outcome / turn
Hannah is alone with the accumulation of the day: her father's silence about the body (which she noticed, which she doesn't know what to do with), Karl's existence across the farmyard, and Magda's hands around the cold cup.

---

## Ending (literary fade)
Karl goes inside the icehouse and the lamp dims. Hannah watches the window a moment longer. The smell of turned earth and October cold comes through her window. She closes it. The icehouse is dark.

---

## New question raised
What does her father's silence about the body mean? And what is Karl, in the icehouse — someone to be seen, or someone to look away from?

---

## Continuity notes
- Establishes: Pfeiffer farm geography; Albrecht's retreat into German; Magda's silence as established condition (not new); Karl Reinmar as specific individual (young, from outside farm labor)
- The four POWs are established as a group; the other three are background figures, not characterized in this chapter
- Lonzo Reed appears but is not yet characterized beyond his post at the icehouse door
- Hannah's knowledge of German is functional (she understands her father's directives); it is not the subject of comment here — it is simply texture
- Do NOT yet explain Albrecht's silence or connect it to Werner Grau — Hannah does not have this; it must remain at the level of her observation (she noticed his hands stopped)
- Period texture: the POW bunkhouse in the icehouse; the morning routine under escort; the material reality of the farm in wartime rationing
