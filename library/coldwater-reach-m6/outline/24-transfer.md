# Chapter 24 — Transfer

**One-line summary**: Karl's reassignment; the leave-taking; his note; Hannah tells Albrecht "Werner Grau ist tot" and Albrecht says "Ich weiß."

**POV**: Hannah Pfeiffer  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Pfeiffer farm — farmyard, farm road; farmhouse kitchen  
**Time**: October–November 1944  
**Target words**: ~3,000 (intensive chapter — the leave-taking is a structural pivot for Hannah's arc)

**Chapter shape**: Two-scene with transition  
**Ending mode**: Turn

---

## Goal
Karl's departure closes one of the novel's central relationships and opens the last phase of Hannah's arc: she now has the complete picture from her side (the photograph, Karl's naming of Werner, the past tense), and she uses it to confront her father. Albrecht's "Ich weiß" — I know — is the moment when Hannah's carrying of the secret becomes a knowing shared, even if what is known is not spoken.

## Obstacle
Hannah has four minutes with Karl before the truck comes. She has to decide, in those four minutes, what to give him and what to keep. She decides, as she usually decides, by acting before she has thought it through. After: she has Karl's written note, which gives her the courage (or the occasion) to go to Albrecht.

---

## Opening texture
Set-piece: the Army truck arriving at the farm. The POWs' transfer order; the managed departure. Hannah knows it's coming — there was a week's notice — and has been preparing not at all.

---

## Action beats (Scene 1 — the leave-taking)

1. **The truck**: An Army truck comes down the farm road at 9 AM. Two MPs in the cab. The four Germans are already in the farmyard with their belongings — not much: a kit bag each, the clothes issued at the work camp. Karl has his bird taxonomy (a folded list he made for himself, parallel to Hannah's notebook). Lonzo is at his usual post, watching.

2. **The process**: The transfer is administrative and quick — the MP checks the four names against a list; the four Germans climb into the truck bed. It takes less time than Hannah expected.

3. **The four minutes**: Before Karl gets in the truck, Lonzo looks elsewhere — a deliberate elsewhere, as if he has decided that whatever passes between Hannah and Karl in the next few minutes is not his business and will remain not his business. Hannah has perhaps four minutes. She uses them as people use moments they know are ending: imperfectly.

4. **The exchange**: She gives him the bird notebook. He receives it with both hands. He gives her a folded piece of paper — he prepared it, she can see; it was in his breast pocket, where things you intend to give are kept. She takes it and puts it in her apron pocket without reading it.

5. **The leave-taking itself**: There is no adequate English for it and no adequate German. What passes between them is mostly the act of looking: his face, which she has watched for thirteen months, and her face, which he has watched for thirteen months. Then the MP says something; Karl climbs into the truck; the tailgate goes up.

6. **Albrecht on the porch**: As the truck turns on the farm road and goes, Hannah notices her father on the porch. She has not seen him there; he came out sometime in the last four minutes. He watches the truck go with his hands at his sides. His face is illegible in the way she has learned to read: not empty, but closed.

---

## Transition

*That evening.* Hannah reads Karl's note at the kitchen table. It is on the back of an envelope, in his handwriting — the English that has cost him each word. The text as he wrote it:

*"Werner Grau. From Ansbach. He is dead. I am sorry."*

And below that: a list of five bird names, German and Latin both. Cedar waxwing at the top.

---

## Action beats (Scene 2 — the kitchen)

7. **The note's effect**: Hannah holds the note for a long time. Karl knew. He knew when he wrote it. He is sorry — not for himself, but for her: for what the knowledge costs her. The apology is for landing this in her hands. She understands this. She folds the note and puts it in her pocket.

8. **Going to Albrecht**: She finds her father in the sitting room with his accounts. She stands in the doorway. She says, in German — not because she chose German, but because German is what this sentence wants to be — "Werner Grau ist tot." Werner Grau is dead.

9. **The silence**: Her father does not move. He does not look up immediately. He is still for a long moment — perhaps ten seconds, which is a very long time for stillness. Then he looks up at her.

10. **"Ich weiß."**: He says: "Ich weiß." I know. He says it simply, in the tone of a man who has been waiting for this sentence to arrive and is relieved, in some part of himself, that it has. He has known since October 1943. He has been sitting with it for over a year.

11. **What does not follow**: She does not ask what happened. He does not offer. They are in a language together — German — but not in a conversation. The knowing is shared; the account is not. She nods once. She goes back to the kitchen. She does the dishes.

---

## Outcome / turn
Albrecht knows. He has always known. And now Hannah knows that he knows, and he knows that she knows. This circuit is closed. What happened between Albrecht and Werner on October 11–12, 1943 — this remains in the shadow where it has always been. "Ich weiß" is the limit of what will be said.

---

## Ending (turn)
She washes the dishes. The water is hot. Through the kitchen window she can see the icehouse. The lamp is not lit tonight; there is no one in the icehouse bunkhouse for the first time in thirteen months. The farm is quieter. The turn is the closed circuit — two people now sharing a name, a death, and a silence — but not sharing the account underneath it.

---

## New question raised
What does Hannah do with the shared knowledge she and Albrecht now hold? And what does the account underneath the "Ich weiß" contain — will it ever be spoken?

## Continuity notes
- Karl's note: these are the exact words — "Werner Grau. From Ansbach. He is dead. I am sorry." plus the bird names; they must be consistent when referenced in ch. 28 (Hannah tells Marcus)
- Albrecht's "Ich weiß": this is the closest the novel comes to confirming what happened; Albrecht knew Werner was dead because he was there (or was the last person to see him); what exactly he knows is not said; the drafter must not resolve this ambiguity
- Lonzo's deliberate elsewhere during the four minutes: this is the last thing his chapter (ch. 21) prepared; he has decided that this moment is not his to interrupt
- The empty icehouse at the end: a physical image that carries the chapter's emotional weight without naming it; the farm is changed; the lamp is not lit; this is the fade within the turn
- Hannah saying "Werner Grau ist tot" in German: she chose German; this is significant; she is speaking in the language of the household's private knowledge; it is not accidental
