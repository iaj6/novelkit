# Chapter 23 — One Year

**One-line summary**: October anniversary; Marcus walks the breakwater; picks up a stone; carries it home; is not going anywhere.

**POV**: Dr. Marcus Klein  
**Tense**: Third-person limited past  
**Setting**: Coldwater Reach harbor → the breakwater → the walk home  
**Time**: October 1944 (one year after the body)  
**Target words**: ~2,500

**Chapter shape**: Interiority-heavy with movement  
**Ending mode**: Literary fade

---

## Goal
The one-year mark on Werner Grau's death; Marcus's private reckoning with what he holds; the granite stone from the breakwater as the physical gesture of belonging; the chapter is a pause before the arc moves toward resolution. Marcus is not going anywhere: this is the chapter's conclusion, arrived at with precision rather than feeling.

## Obstacle
There is no external obstacle. The obstacle is what Marcus does with a year of carrying knowledge he cannot act on, of committing to a place while still holding the posture of the provisional. The chapter works entirely through his intelligence and the physical world around him.

---

## Opening texture
Transit: Marcus walking toward the harbor in October, aware of the date. His register is present from the first sentence — the careful inventory of what he knows, laid against what he doesn't know.

---

## Action beats

1. **October again**: Marcus is aware that it is October — one year. He registers this not with grief but with the precision of a man who keeps calendars: one year since the body; two and a half years since Coldwater; five since leaving Vienna. He calculates these things the way other people calculate weather. It doesn't stop him from walking.

2. **The harbor road**: He walks the harbor road as he often does in the afternoon. The harbor in October: most of the summer fleet hauled or preparing to haul; the cold coming on; the smell of salt and cold granite. He has found this smell, over two and a half years, settling into his inventory of the town.

3. **The breakwater**: He has not walked out on the breakwater before. Today, for no reason he can precisely name, he does. He walks to the elbow of the L. The granite is cold; the sea is October-green. The wind has the particular quality of harbor October — salt and diesel both.

4. **What he holds there**: He stands at the elbow. He thinks about Werner Grau, not as a puzzle to be solved but as a person: a man who came to Maine looking for connection, who wanted to leave a message for Karl Reinmar's family, who wanted to see Albrecht Pfeiffer after twenty years. He came a very long way. He was carrying something that mattered. He did not come back from the breakwater.

5. **The stone**: Without planning it, Marcus picks up a piece of granite from the breakwater — a chunk the size of his fist, rough-faced, gray-green. He weighs it in his hand. He puts it in his coat pocket.

6. **The walk back**: He walks back along the breakwater. The bell buoy at the channel entrance. The harbor lights beginning to come on in the October dusk.

---

## Ending (literary fade)
He enters Henry's house through the back. He takes the granite stone from his coat pocket and sets it on the windowsill of his room. He does not examine why. He goes down to dinner. Through the window of the dining room, looking toward the street, he can see the October dark settling over the town. He is not going anywhere. This is not a decision he has made; it is a fact he has arrived at, the way you arrive at a fact when you stop resisting it.

---

## New question raised
The stone on the windowsill: what is the difference between a gesture that carries private meaning and a fact? When does staying become staying?
