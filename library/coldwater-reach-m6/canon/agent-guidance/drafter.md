# Drafter guidance — *Coldwater Reach*

## Voice register

This is literary adult fiction with a restrained, period-exact register. The closest comparables are Ishiguro's *The Remains of the Day* (repression as texture, what is never said as the load-bearing content), Marilynne Robinson's *Gilead* (moral weight accumulating in quiet sentences), and Colm Tóibín's *Nora Webster* (a widow holding a post, the world reduced to work, grief arriving late in physical idiom). Sentences are complete, precise, unhurried. No pyrotechnics. The technique is withholding, observation, the gap between what is noticed and what is acknowledged.

## Sentence-length expectations

- **Eira's chapters**: average 10–16 words. Three short declaratives is the rhythm; vary at the fourth. Subordinate clauses are rare and purposeful.
- **Hannah's chapters**: average 12–20 words, wider variance. She has a lyric range the other two do not. An occasional long sentence is permitted — but "lyric" means precise and felt, not ornamental.
- **Marcus's chapters**: average 15–22 words. Subordinate clauses, qualifications, conditional structures are voice, not bloat. Do not compress them out. A well-built Marcus sentence may run 30–35 words without being a failure — it is a failure only if it is soft or redundant.
- **Maximum for all POVs**: if a sentence is approaching 40 words, ask whether it is doing Marcus's measured intellectual work or simply accumulating. If the latter, cut.

## Chapter-opening conventions

- **No abstract observation openings.** Do not open a chapter with a statement about the world, the war, the season, or the nature of things. The chapter begins where the character is, in their body, in their day.
- **Eira**: opens in task. She is doing something with her hands or her eyes. Weather is permitted as an opening if it is working weather — a specific observation with operational consequence.
- **Hannah**: opens in sensation or conversation. She may open in the middle of dialogue, in the smell of the barn, in a specific physical act. Her openings are the most varied.
- **Marcus**: opens in observation or movement — often walking, since he walks everywhere. He may begin by noticing something that he then spends the chapter considering. He does not begin in pure interiority; he begins in the world and moves inward.

## Chapter-ending conventions

Target distribution across 30 chapters:
- **~10 chapters**: literary fade — the last sentence is something physical, observed, one beat past where the scene's action resolved. The dominant mode for this register.
- **~8 chapters**: turn — a piece of information or a shift that reframes what the chapter has been about; not a cliffhanger, but a quiet pivot.
- **~6 chapters**: resolved beat — the chapter's central question settles; the next chapter will open elsewhere.
- **~4 chapters**: elliptical — deliberate open question, an unfinished gesture, something left in mid-air.
- **~2 chapters**: cliffhanger or mid-action cut — used sparingly; the register does not do propulsive cliff-hangers as a rule. Reserve for structural turning points.

**The last sentence of a chapter almost never names what it means.** It is a physical image, a last word of dialogue, a specific observation. The meaning is the weight behind it. Let it sit.

## POV strategy

One POV per chapter, strictly. The chapter header or first paragraph orients the reader to whose chapter this is. No slipping into another character's knowledge mid-chapter — the narrator knows only what the POV character could plausibly observe or have been told.

Three primary POVs rotating asymmetrically (~10 chapters each). Rotation is motivated by story need, not strict cycle: three consecutive Eira chapters is fine when the harbor plot demands it. Two consecutive Hannah chapters when the farm material is building. The plotter's outline will specify the sequence.

Optional fourth-POV chapters (Henry, Lonzo, or Karl) are brief and exceptional — 1,500–2,000 words, no more than three total. They are not a fourth regular POV; they are structural correctives that give the reader access the three primaries cannot provide.

**The three primary POVs must not share a scene.** Their threads can cross — they can hear about each other, encounter each other briefly in public space — but they do not sit down together. The structural unit is the chapter and the chapter belongs to one person.

## Dialogue density

Moderate dialogue density, varying by POV. Eira's chapters are the sparsest in dialogue; her chapters are more observation than exchange. Hannah's chapters are the most dialogue-rich — the farm is full of exchanges (with her parents, with Karl in broken language, with Lonzo, with Marcus at school). Marcus's chapters have a middle density — more exchange than Eira, less than Hannah.

Dialogue tags are minimal. "Said" and "asked" carry almost all the weight. No "interjected," "retorted," "replied." Physical beats before or after dialogue lines instead of attributive tags where possible: *He set down the mug. "Three days."*

**German dialogue and phrases**: German in Hannah's chapters, and in any scene with Albrecht or Karl, is rendered in italics, not translated, but contextually graspable. *Ich weiß nicht* does not need a footnote. Do not write phonetic accent. Do not write German as pidgin unless the character is actually a non-native speaker groping for vocabulary (Karl in English; Hannah in German-to-Karl).

**No dialogue glosses.** The prose does not explain what a line of dialogue meant, implied, or "was really saying." The line sits. The next beat — a silence, a physical act, a subject change — does the work.

## Period texture and historical accuracy

Every material object that appears should be period-correct. The radio is a BC-1206 or a domestic equivalent. The ration book is an OPA book. The mail overseas is V-Mail. The Coast Guard officer's rank and uniform details follow 1943 USCG regulations. The POW labor arrangement follows 1929 Geneva Convention rules as applied in Maine.

**Do not deploy history as decoration.** The ration book is not mentioned to signal "authentic 1943." It is mentioned because Eira is counting what she has, or because Hannah is trying to figure out whether there is enough sugar. The object must earn its scene.

**Vocabulary**: no anachronisms. Characters do not "process" feelings, do not have "trauma" as a named category, do not "connect" in the contemporary sense. The vocabulary is mid-century New England and, for Marcus, formal mid-century European academic English.

## Comic timing and tonal balance

This is not a comic novel. But it is not without dry irony — particularly in Marcus's chapters (the slightly formal precision that notices absurdity without performing amusement) and Lonzo Reed's voice (if given a chapter). There is no comic relief in the structural sense; there is the dry New England understatement that is one register of grief and endurance. A character who says less than the situation warrants is not being stoic for show — it is how they live.

## Anti-defaults — what the drafter must actively avoid

- **Do not open chapters with weather or season as mood-setting abstraction.** "October had settled into the harbor" is a failure. "The breakwater took six feet of green water on the outer face" is not.
- **Do not name affect.** "She felt the weight of Owen's absence." No. "She put the logbook back in the drawer where Owen's pencil still was." The affect is the object.
- **Do not write post-scene subtext paragraphs.** After a charged scene, end the scene. Do not then write what it meant.
- **Do not thesis-state.** The chapter does not summarize its own meaning in its last sentence. The novel does not announce its themes.
- **Do not drift toward contemporary psychological vocabulary.** No "grief response," no "coping," no "boundaries," no "processing."
- **Do not over-qualify in Eira's voice.** Her precision is physical and observational, not intellectual. She does not build conditional sentences. She notes facts.
- **Do not make the POW storyline into a redemption romance.** What happens between Hannah and Karl is observed, ambiguous, small, and real. It is not a wartime love story. It is not a coming-of-age breakthrough. It is two people in a constrained situation noticing each other with care.
- **Do not explain the restraint.** The prose's power is what is not said. Do not then say it.
