# Plotter guidance — *Coldwater Reach*

## POV count and rotation

**Three primary POVs**: Eira Bowman, Hannah Pfeiffer, Dr. Marcus Klein. Roughly ten chapters each across thirty chapters. Rotation is asymmetric and motivated: follow story need, not a strict cycle. Sustained runs of two or three chapters in a single POV are acceptable — even desirable — when a thread is building momentum or approaching a pressure point. But no single POV should go more than four consecutive chapters without the others being re-engaged.

**Optional fourth-POV chapters**: one to three chapters from Henry Crow, Lonzo Reed, or Karl Reinmar. These are structural correctives — they give the reader access no primary POV can provide. They should be short (1,500–2,000 words) and placed at moments where the mystery requires a vantage that none of the three principals can occupy. Do not add a fourth POV for variety or warmth alone; it must earn its structural place.

**The three principals never share a scene.** They can occupy the same public space briefly (the post office, the waterfront), but no chapter places them in conversation together. Their threads cross obliquely.

## Chapter-shape preferences

This is literary fiction with a restrained register. Each chapter is a unit of one person's attention across a day, an afternoon, or a single event. The dominant chapter shapes:

- **Single-scene focus** (one event, one time block, one main action) — most common; approximately 60% of chapters.
- **Two-scene with transition** (a chapter crosses a day; two related events with a brief connective tissue) — approximately 25%.
- **Pure interiority with movement** (a character in transit — Marcus walking, Eira doing a solitary task — whose movement allows extended reflection) — approximately 15%. Not more; this is not a meditation novel.

Avoid: chapters that exist purely to carry plot information. Every chapter must also do character work. If a chapter's only job is to reveal a piece of the breakwater mystery, it needs a second register — what does this revelation cost the POV character in their own arc?

## Episode-vs-arc structure

The structure is a continuous arc, not episodic. Chapters flow into each other — the breakwater question deepens across the thirty, the seasonal arc (October 1943 → spring 1945) structures time, each POV character's arc develops through accumulation. However: within the continuous arc, individual chapters often feel somewhat self-contained (a day, a task, an exchange). This is intentional — the chapters accumulate meaning the way seasons accumulate into time.

The mystery of the breakwater is the external spine that moves the plot. It must have new information (or new withholding) approximately every three chapters — not a revelation, but a piece, a question, a discrepancy noticed. Chapters 1–5 establish the body and the three principals; chapters 6–15 develop the threads and deepen the mystery; chapters 16–25 draw the threads toward collision; chapters 26–30 resolve the characters' arcs while leaving the mystery partially in shadow.

## Ending-mode distribution (across 30 chapters)

- **Literary fade** (sensory image, physical beat, one step past resolution): ~10 chapters — the dominant mode
- **Turn** (quiet pivot, piece of information that reframes): ~8 chapters
- **Resolved beat** (chapter question settles): ~6 chapters
- **Elliptical** (open question, unfinished gesture): ~4 chapters
- **Cliffhanger / mid-action**: ~2 chapters — reserved for structural turning points only

Avoid runs of the same ending mode. If three consecutive chapters end in literary fade, the fourth should turn or resolve. Track this.

## Chapter target words

- **Standard chapter**: 2,400–2,800 words
- **Intensive chapters** (major structural turns, climactic confrontations): up to 3,200 words
- **Short exceptional chapters** (fourth-POV corrective chapters): 1,500–2,000 words
- **Target manuscript total**: ~80,000 words across 30 chapters

No chapter should feel rushed (under 2,000 words) or bloated (over 3,500 words) without structural justification.

## Genre-specific structural conventions

This is literary historical fiction with a mystery spine. The genre conventions to honor:

**Historical fiction**:
- The period must be maintained through material objects and period vocabulary, not expository history lessons.
- The war must be ever-present as texture and as absence, not as dramatic plot machinery.
- The ending must not be teleologically determined by the historical event (VE Day). The personal resolutions should arrive in their own time; the war ends in the background.

**Mystery spine**:
- The body is introduced in chapter 1 or early chapter 2. The reader must have the three POV characters' initial relationships to the mystery established by chapter 5.
- Each POV sees a different piece of the puzzle: Eira holds what she saw on the dock; Hannah's thread yields something through Karl Reinmar's presence (the dead man may have a connection to the German community, the POW network, or simply to the world the Pfeiffers came from); Marcus's thread yields something through language (a document, a name, something that requires German to read).
- The mystery deepens at the midpoint. Around chapters 14–16, new information should shift the reader's understanding of what happened — not a solution, a complication.
- The final disposition of the mystery: the reader should be able to assemble a plausible account from the accumulated evidence across all thirty chapters. The novel does not refuse to answer; it refuses to announce the answer. The last chapter is not a detective's revelation scene.

**Three-strand structure**:
- Acts 1–2 (chapters 1–8): all three POVs established, the breakwater question live, the seasonal texture of autumn working
- Act 3–4 (chapters 9–18): the threads deepen separately; each POV character's personal arc takes on pressure independent of the mystery; the farm plot (Hannah/Karl/Lonzo) reaches a crisis; Marcus commits an act (helps someone, protects someone, reveals something) that commits him to Coldwater
- Act 5–6 (chapters 19–26): the threads approach intersection; Eira acts on what she knows; the POV characters encounter the consequences of each other's choices at one remove
- Act 7 (chapters 27–30): individual arcs resolve or settle; the mystery achieves its partial resolution; the war ends off-page; the last chapter is quiet, specific, and irreversible

## What the plotter must not do

- **Do not introduce a spy plot.** No Nazi agents, no coded messages, no submarine contact. The dead man is explicable by other means.
- **Do not engineer a three-way convergence scene.** The structural constraint (the three never meet as a group) must be respected to the end.
- **Do not resolve all three arcs in the same mode.** Eira's resolution is an action; Hannah's is a decision; Marcus's is an act of commitment. These should feel different in shape.
- **Do not let the mystery resolution overwhelm the personal arcs.** In the last three chapters, the personal arcs come first. The mystery is context, not climax.
- **Do not underuse Henry Crow.** He is not a supporting convenience. If he gets a chapter, it must illuminate something neither the three principals nor the reader could see from their positions. Time it for maximum ironic or emotional effect.
