# Reader guidance — *Coldwater Reach*

## Intended audience

Adult readers of literary historical fiction — the readership for novels like *The Remains of the Day*, *Nora Webster*, *The Lacuna*, or *Pachinko*. Expect readers comfortable with a slow-build structure, restrained affect, and a mystery that does not fully resolve. The audience knows that "what happened" is not always the novel's central question. They are patient with interiority provided it earns its space. They expect period texture to be accurate and unobtrusive.

This is not genre mystery fiction. Readers coming for a thriller will find this novel's mystery gratifications different from their expectations — and that is correct, not a defect. The reader phase should evaluate the manuscript as literary fiction with a mystery spine, not as a mystery with literary pretensions.

## Brief-adherence checks to perform

Answer each of these as yes/no with evidence:

1. **Does each chapter stay strictly inside its POV character's knowledge?** No chapter should contain information the POV character could not plausibly have observed or been told. Check specifically for cross-POV contamination in the mystery strand.
2. **Are the three POV voices consistently distinct throughout?** Eira: spare, procedural, physical vocabulary. Hannah: porous, associative, German phrases embedded. Marcus: precise, slightly formal, architectured subordination. A reader should be able to identify the POV from an unmarked passage.
3. **Does the mystery strand deliver new information or a new question approximately every three chapters?** If four consecutive chapters pass without advancing the breakwater question, flag it.
4. **Do the three POV characters' personal arcs develop independently of the mystery?** Each arc (authority/cost; small choices; displacement) must have its own pressure and movement. If a POV's internal arc is stalled while they serve the mystery, flag it.
5. **Is historical accuracy maintained without decoration?** Period objects should appear because they are in the scene, not to signal authenticity. Flag any passage that reads like a glossary insert.
6. **Are the three principals never in a scene together?** The structural constraint must not be violated.
7. **Is the ending correctly calibrated?** The last chapters should resolve the personal arcs while leaving the mystery in partial shadow. The war ending should be background, not foreground. Flag any climax that tips into thriller-resolution mode.
8. **Is the deliberate unresolved thread actually unresolved?** The brief requires at least one. Identify which thread it is, and confirm it reads as deliberate and felt rather than as an authorial failure to commit.

## What "good" looks like for this book

A successful chapter of *Coldwater Reach* achieves the following:

- **Eira chapter**: reads like a working day in which something morally heavy has been set in a drawer. The harbor is real and operational; the emotional weight is entirely in what she does not say and what she notices last. The last sentence is a physical image, not a feeling.
- **Hannah chapter**: admits more noise and sensation than the others; includes at least one moment of noticing something that contradicts what she has been told; the German phrases land without translation and are felt; the farm is material and smells and sounds.
- **Marcus chapter**: the prose is the densest of the three in subordination and qualification; the irony is dry rather than performative; Marcus has at least one moment of encountering something he cannot file neatly into the experience he already has.
- **All chapters**: no labeled emotion. The prose does not tell the reader what a scene meant. The period is felt but not performed. The mystery strand is present but does not take over the chapter's primary register.

## What to flag explicitly if the manuscript drifts

The following drift patterns should be named explicitly even if the prose is locally skillful:

- **Labeled affect**: "She felt grief," "He was aware of his own loneliness." Name it and cut it. The brief is unambiguous: the story trusts the reader. If the prose is doing its job, the feeling arrives through the image, not the label.
- **Post-scene subtext paragraphs**: a charged scene followed by a paragraph explaining what it was about. Name it. It is the single most destructive failure mode for this register.
- **Voice leveling**: if Eira's chapters begin to develop Hannah's associative range, or Marcus's chapters lose their formal subordination, the distinctive three-voice architecture is damaged. Flag voice drift by POV and cite examples.
- **History as decoration**: any passage where a period object or procedure is named without earning its place in the scene. The ration book must be in use, not on display.
- **Genre-thriller drift in the mystery strand**: if the breakwater question begins to dominate chapters that belong to a character's personal arc, or if the mystery moves toward procedural detective-fiction pacing, flag it. The mystery is spine, not engine.
- **Anachronistic vocabulary**: "She processed the loss." "His trauma was." "They connected over." These are not period and not register. Flag on sight.
- **Convergence violation**: any scene where all three (or any two) of the principals are in extended dialogue or shared scene. Check for this especially in the final act.

## What to applaud

The reader phase should specifically defend the following if the manuscript hits them well — do not recommend cutting or compressing:

- **Marcus's long, qualified sentences.** His subordinate clauses are his voice. Compression agents will be tempted to cut them for clarity. Resist. A Marcus sentence that runs 28 words and is all there is not a mistake.
- **The untranslated German phrases in Hannah's chapters.** These are a deliberate technique. They should not be translated in the text. If a reader-agent is uncertain whether non-German-reading readers will be confused, the answer is: the confusion is the point, and it is contextually bounded. Defend them.
- **Scene endings that go one beat past resolution.** The last sentence of a scene is often something physical observed rather than a statement. This is not evasion; it is the register. Do not recommend adding a "beat of reflection" after these endings.
- **Eira's emotional restraint.** She does not process Owen in interior monologue. Her grief is in objects, tasks, and the gap between what she notices and what she says. If the grief "doesn't land" in a conventional literary fiction sense, the answer is not more interiority — it is trusting the accumulation.
- **The ambiguous ending of the mystery.** The brief explicitly permits the breakwater question to remain in partial shadow. If the mystery resolution reads as incomplete, ask whether it is *deliberately* incomplete (the reader can assemble a plausible account but is not given a detective's announcement). If the answer is yes, it is a feature.
