# Continuity

Hard facts that must not break across chapters. This file is the source of truth for dates, ages, histories, and structural constraints.

## Chronology

1. The story opens in **October 1943** and closes in **spring 1945**, approximately May, before VE Day (May 8, 1945).
2. The body on the breakwater arrives after a **three-day blow**, in **October 1943** — this is the inciting event.
3. The Coast Guard takes the body. The body has **no papers**.
4. Eira saw **something on the dock the night before the body came in**. This is established in chapter 1 or very early; what exactly she saw is parceled out across later chapters.

## Ages and Life Dates

5. **Eira Bowman** is **52** in October 1943. Born approximately 1891.
6. **Owen Bowman** (Eira's husband) drowned in the **November storm of 1935**. Eira has held the harbormaster post for **eight years** as of October 1943.
7. **Hannah Pfeiffer** is **17** in October 1943, turning 18 in spring/summer 1944.
8. **Albrecht and Magda Pfeiffer** emigrated from Bavaria to Maine in **1922**.
9. **Dr. Marcus Klein** is **47** in October 1943. Born approximately 1896, Vienna.
10. Marcus was interned at **Sherbrooke, Quebec**, as an enemy alien — released to the Emergency Committee rolls in 1941. He arrived in Coldwater in **March 1942**.
11. **Henry Crow** is approximately **55** in 1943. His family came north from Norfolk, Virginia, in **1924**.
12. Henry and Lillian's son **James Crow** is in the **Army, Pacific theater**, as of October 1943.

## POW Labor Program

13. **Four German prisoners** are housed in the Pfeiffer farm's renovated icehouse and work the farm under the federal POW labor program.
14. The four POWs are escorted by **Lonzo Reed**, age **62**, a Coastal Patrol private.
15. **Karl Reinmar** is **19** years old, from **Ansbach**, Bavaria — approximately 50 km from Albrecht Pfeiffer's hometown.
16. The POW program is governed by the **1929 Geneva Convention**. Prisoners are paid in canteen credits. The no-fraternization rule is loosely kept.
17. The umbrella camp is **Camp Houlton**, Maine. The Pfeiffer farm is a satellite work camp.

## Structure

18. The three **main POVs are Eira, Hannah, and Marcus** — roughly ten chapters each, rotating asymmetrically.
19. There may be **two or three short chapters from a fourth POV** (Henry, Lonzo, or Karl) — these are exceptional, not regular.
20. **The three POV characters never meet as a group of three.** Their threads cross bilaterally and accidentally.
21. The **breakwater question** (who was the man, what happened) is assembled by the reader across all thirty chapters. It may or may not be fully answered; the characters' relationships to it resolve even if the underlying fact stays in shadow.
22. At least **one thread is deliberately unresolved** at the close.
23. The story ends just **before VE Day, spring 1945**. The war ends in the background; resolutions are personal.

## Setting

24. **Coldwater Reach** is a fictional town on the mid-coast of Maine. Real place names (Portland, Bangor, Houlton, Cambridge MA, Sherbrooke QC) may appear; **no real people** may appear by name.
25. The harbor has a **tidal range** characteristic of Maine mid-coast (approx. 10–12 feet at neap, more at spring tides). The breakwater is **granite rubble construction** on an L from the south headland.
26. The harbormaster's operations are regulated by period-accurate USCG and port authority procedures.

## Historical Constraints

27. **No spy plot.** No Nazi agents, no coded messages, no submarine landings. The U-boat scares belong to 1942 and are in memory, not present action, by October 1943.
28. The war news reaches Coldwater via **radio, V-Mail, and casualty telegrams**. The telegram is the shape of dread.
29. **OPA rationing** applies: gasoline, tires, sugar, coffee, meat, canned goods, shoes.
30. Period diction is mid-century New England maritime. No anachronistic vocabulary.
