# Characters

## POV Characters

---

### Eira Bowman — POV 1
**Age**: 52 in October 1943  
**Role**: Harbormaster, Coldwater Reach  
**Background**: Born in Coldwater. Married Owen Bowman, who held the harbormaster's post. Owen drowned in the November storm of 1935 — a sudden blow, a capsize, a body that took three days to come back. Eira has held the post for eight years, first by default and then by demonstrated competence. She is the only woman serving as harbormaster on the Maine coast.

**Drives**: To do the job correctly, because the job is real work and incorrectly done people die. To hold the post that was his without letting that fact be the most important fact about her. The two drives are slightly in tension: the post is still called "his" by the older fishermen; she notices this and says nothing. Her unspoken third drive is to understand what she saw on the dock the night before the body came in — not because she expects resolution, but because she is constitutionally unable to file something in the wrong column.

**Voice**: Spare, procedural. She thinks in tidal increments and load calculations. Her dialogue is clipped; she says less than she knows. When grief reaches her it comes in harbor idiom — she thinks of Owen as "offset from the schedule" or "overdue." The reader understands before she does. Interior passages are rare and short. The sentence-level texture is working: what needs doing, what has been done, what was left undone.

**Relationships**: 
- Owen Bowman (husband, deceased) — his absence is structural, not decorative  
- Hannah Pfeiffer (knows her as the Pfeiffer girl; the family connection is the shared commerce of a small town)  
- Marcus Klein (a nodding acquaintance; he walks the waterfront; she knows who lives here)  
- Henry Crow (knows him as the school custodian, as someone reliable in bad weather, as a man who doesn't waste words — which she respects)  
- The Coast Guard lieutenant who takes the body (a younger man; she reads him as competent but not curious)  

**Arc**: Authority and what it costs. By the end, she has made a decision she can't undo and did not ask permission for — and that decision is precisely what the post required of its holder. She becomes, in a way she could not have without Owen's death, the harbormaster.

### Hannah Pfeiffer — POV 2
**Age**: 17 in October 1943 (18 by spring 1944)  
**Role**: Farmer's daughter, high school student, observer  
**Background**: Born in Maine to Bavarian immigrant parents. Her father Albrecht and mother Magda came over in 1922, before the National Socialists were anything anyone in Maine had heard of. The family farm has been viable, barely, through the Depression. The war has fractured the household: Albrecht has retreated into German at home, Magda into silence. Hannah is caught between two languages and two loyalties she didn't choose.

**Drives**: To understand her parents without becoming them. To have a life that is not entirely circumscribed by what her parents' homeland did and what her neighbors think about it. And at the immediate, daily level: curiosity — an almost ungovernable interest in other people's interiority, which makes her dangerous in a small town and necessary to the novel's structure.

**Voice**: The most porous of the three. The narrative admits more sensation, more tonal fluctuation. German phrases surface when her father's voice is in her head — not often, never explained, always felt. Sentence lengths vary more widely than in Eira's or Marcus's chapters. Her thinking is associative rather than procedural. She is the most capable of noticing things that contradict what she has been told, and the least experienced at knowing what to do with the noticing.

**Relationships**:
- Albrecht Pfeiffer (father) — a man deformed by grief and shame in ways he will not name; Hannah loves him and cannot reach him  
- Magda Pfeiffer (mother) — the silence is medical; it began as depression after the war started and has not lifted  
- Karl Reinmar (the youngest German POW) — nineteen, from Ansbach, near where Albrecht was born; they share approximately thirty words of overlapping German-English; what happens between them is watched by Lonzo Reed and by Hannah herself with different degrees of understanding  
- Lonzo Reed (the guard) — sixty-two, Coastal Patrol private; he is not cruel, not stupid, and more aware of what is happening in the icehouse than he lets on  
- Marcus Klein (her German teacher at school) — the most complex relationship in her social world; she is one of three students in his advanced German class; he is the first adult outside her household who treats her curiosity as a credential rather than a problem  

**Arc**: The slow accumulation of small choices. By the end, she has done something — told someone something, withheld something, or made a choice with her hands and her feet — that she cannot undo, that constitutes, looking back, the shape of who she is becoming.

### Dr. Marcus Klein — POV 3
**Age**: 47 in October 1943  
**Role**: German and Latin teacher, Coldwater Reach High School  
**Background**: Born in Vienna, circa 1896. University at Vienna (classical languages, comparative literature). Left Austria in 1938, when the nature of the Anschluss became clear. Paris (1938–1939), London (1939–1940). Arrived in Canada 1940 under circumstances he does not fully explain; interned at Sherbrooke, Quebec, as an enemy alien (Austria was technically German territory) for approximately ten months. Released to the Emergency Committee rolls in 1941. Arrived in Cambridge 1941, then Coldwater in March 1942. He is Jewish, and while this is not secret, it is not a thing he discusses in Coldwater — the town is not hostile, but it is also not his milieu, and he has learned to calibrate what he says about himself.

**Drives**: To stop translating. To be in a language rather than between two of them. His displacement has been so prolonged — Vienna, Paris, London, Sherbrooke, Cambridge, Coldwater — that he has become fluent in the stance of the temporary resident, and now does not know how to be permanent. The body on the breakwater catches him because it is a problem he can think about in German — the dead man's identity, if it can be established, is a puzzle in exactly the language Marcus has been carrying alone.

**Voice**: Precise, slightly formal, the slightly overcorrect English of a man who learned the language from pages. His narration has the quality of translation — not stilted, but measured. He chooses words with the awareness of a man who knows words can be evidence. His chapters are the densest with qualification and subordination, without being academic in a distracting way. The formality is warmth, in his register.

**Relationships**:
- Henry Crow (landlord and, quietly, friend) — the relationship is conducted mostly through practical acts: Henry fixes the boiler, Marcus sits in the kitchen drinking coffee while he does; they have never had a long conversation but understand each other with the precision of two men who are used to being misunderstood by others  
- Lillian Crow (Henry's wife) — treats Marcus as a guest; sets a plate; asks no questions; this is, for Marcus, close to home  
- Hannah Pfeiffer (student) — he recognizes her particular kind of intelligence and is careful about it; his job is to teach her German, not to become her confessor  
- Eira Bowman (acquaintance) — he sees her as formidably competent; she is one of the few people in Coldwater he has not had to explain himself to  
- The dead man (unknown) — the puzzle Marcus cannot file away  

**Arc**: Whether displacement ends. By the close of the novel, Marcus has done something that commits him to Coldwater Reach specifically — not to America in the abstract, but to this place — and in doing so has stopped being temporary. The exact act is a function of the plot; the arc is about how a person stops performing rootlessness.

## Secondary Characters

### Henry Crow
**Age**: approximately 55 in 1943  
**Role**: Chief custodian, Coldwater Reach High School; Marcus's landlord  
**Background**: Family came north from Norfolk, Virginia, in 1924, during the Great Migration's secondary wave to northern New England. Henry has been at the school since 1928. He and Lillian have one son, James, who is in the Army — Pacific theater.  
**Voice** (if given a chapter): Unhurried, specific, with a dry observational humor that never tips into performance. He has watched the town for twenty years; he knows where the weight rests.  
**Note**: May anchor one or two exceptional chapters. Not a regular POV.

### Lillian Crow
**Age**: early 50s  
**Role**: Henry's wife; community church member; informal network node for the town's working poor  
**Voice**: Warm, laconic, with a practical view of what can be changed and what cannot.

### Albrecht Pfeiffer
**Age**: mid-50s  
**Role**: Hannah's father; dairy farmer  
**Note**: Has retreated into German at home since the war started. This is not loyalty to the Reich — it is grief, shame, and a kind of withdrawal. He is not a villain; he is a broken man who cannot explain himself in any language.

### Magda Pfeiffer
**Age**: early 50s  
**Role**: Hannah's mother  
**Note**: Her silence began as depression and has become something else. She is present in the novel as a weight and an absence.

### Karl Reinmar
**Age**: 19 in October 1943  
**Role**: German POW, one of four assigned to the Pfeiffer farm  
**Background**: From Ansbach, Bavaria — approximately fifty kilometers from where Albrecht Pfeiffer was born. He is not a Nazi in any meaningful ideological sense; he is a nineteen-year-old who was conscripted and captured and is now milking cows in Maine. He is lonely. He can identify every field bird in German and cannot remember the English.  
**Note**: May anchor an exceptional chapter.

### Lonzo Reed
**Age**: 62  
**Role**: Coastal Patrol private, assigned to escort duty at the Pfeiffer farm  
**Background**: Too old for the real Army, too experienced for nothing. He has been watching people for sixty-two years and misses very little. His relationship with the four Germans is correct and watchful; his relationship with Hannah is protective in a way neither of them acknowledges.  
**Note**: May anchor an exceptional chapter.

### The Man on the Breakwater
**Age**: unknown  
**Role**: The novel's structural spine — the unidentified body  
**Note**: Appears only in retrospect and description. His identity is assembled by the reader across thirty chapters; the novel does not guarantee completion of the assembly.
