# Glossary

Terms, names, places, and objects. Alphabetical. Added to by later phases.

---

**Albrecht Pfeiffer** — Hannah's father. Bavarian immigrant (arrived 1922). Dairy farmer. Has retreated into German at home since the war started. See *Characters*.

**BC-1206** — military-surplus-adjacent table radio common in home-front households; delivers war news and music.

**Breakwater** — the granite rubble L-shaped structure protecting the harbor of Coldwater Reach from the southeast. Where the body comes in.

**Camp Houlton** — real German POW camp in northern Maine; hub for satellite farm-labor work camps including the Pfeiffer farm detail.

**Coldwater Reach** — fictional working port on the mid-coast of Maine. Setting for the entire novel.

**Dr. Marcus Klein** — German and Latin teacher at Coldwater Reach High School; Viennese émigré, formerly interned at Sherbrooke. POV character 3. See *Characters*.

**Eira Bowman** — harbormaster of Coldwater Reach. Widow. POV character 1. See *Characters*.

**Emergency Committee in Aid of Displaced Foreign Scholars** — real American organization that helped European refugee academics find positions in US universities and schools, 1933–1945. Marcus was on their rolls.

**Geneva Convention (1929)** — governs treatment of POWs. German prisoners in Maine work camps are entitled to wages (paid in canteen credits), adequate food and shelter, and protection from hazardous work and abuse.

**Hannah Pfeiffer** — seventeen-year-old daughter of Albrecht and Magda Pfeiffer; farm girl and high school student. POV character 2. See *Characters*.

**Henry Crow** — chief custodian, Coldwater Reach High School; Marcus's landlord. African American, family from Norfolk VA (arrived 1924). Approximate age 55 in 1943. See *Characters*.

**Icehouse (Pfeiffer farm)** — renovated structure serving as bunkhouse for four German POWs under the labor program.

**James Crow** — Henry and Lillian's son. U.S. Army, Pacific theater, as of October 1943.

**Karl Reinmar** — youngest of the four German POWs, age 19, from Ansbach, Bavaria. See *Characters*.

**Lillian Crow** — Henry's wife. See *Characters*.

**Lonzo Reed** — Coastal Patrol private, age 62, escort guard for the Pfeiffer farm POW detail. See *Characters*.

**Magda Pfeiffer** — Hannah's mother. Silence began as depression when the war started and has not lifted.

**Man on the Breakwater** — unidentified male body found after the three-day October 1943 storm. No papers. Taken by the Coast Guard. The structural spine of the novel.

**OPA** — Office of Price Administration. Issues ration books governing wartime consumer goods.

**Owen Bowman** — Eira's husband, the previous harbormaster. Drowned in the November storm of 1935. His absence is structural throughout.

**Pfeiffer farm** — dairy farm three miles inland from Coldwater Reach; site of the POW labor detail. Includes a pre-Revolutionary barn, forty dairy cows, and the renovated icehouse.

**Sherbrooke (Internment camp)** — Camp #N, Sherbrooke, Quebec, Canada. Marcus was interned there 1940–1941 as an enemy alien (Austria, classified as German territory). He does not discuss it.

**V-Mail** — Victory Mail; overseas correspondence photographed and reduced for transport, then reprinted at the delivery end. Standard wartime letter system.

**Western Union telegram** — the delivery method for casualty notifications. Delivered by the local Western Union office; in Coldwater, by bicycle.

**Coldwater Reach** — Fictional working port on the mid-coast of Maine. Primary setting of the novel. Population ~800 in town, ~400 on outlying farms and woodlots. Has a harbor, high school, post office, diner, feed store, and Catholic church.

**The Breakwater** — Granite rubble construction in an L-shape from the south headland of Coldwater Reach harbor. The elbow is the point where the storm deposits incoming wrack. Site of the body discovered October 12, 1943.

**Harbormaster's Shed** — Eira's operational base at the pier head. Contains telephone, barometer, tide log, harbor logbooks, and a single-burner plate. South window overlooks the south dock.

**South Dock** — Dock in Coldwater Reach harbor, visible from the harbormaster's shed south window. Site of the two-figure sighting on the night of October 10–11, 1943.

**Eira Bowman** — Harbormaster of Coldwater Reach; widow of Owen Bowman; 52 years old in October 1943; has held the post for eight years. POV character 1.

**Owen Bowman** — Eira's husband; previous harbormaster of Coldwater Reach; drowned in the November storm of 1935. His 1935 logbook remains on the shed shelf.

**The Man on the Breakwater** — Unidentified male, 50 or older, found dead at the breakwater elbow on October 12, 1943. Town clothes, good wool coat, no boots, no papers. The novel's spine mystery.

**Lt. Amos Mercer** — Coast Guard lieutenant, Sprucehead station. Responds to Eira's call on October 12, 1943; removes the body to CG custody. Efficient, thorough, not especially curious.

**Coast Guard Station Sprucehead** — USCG station approximately 14 miles by road from Coldwater Reach. Lt. Mercer's posting.

**Ellsworth** — Sardine carrier based at Coldwater Reach, running the Eastport route. Out during the October 1943 storm; expected back October 18, 1943.

**Penelope D.** — Merton Galey's dragger, hauled out at the Coldwater Reach boatyard railway since August 1943.

**Harbor Log** — Eira's official record of all harbor activity: weather, vessels, incidents. Entries are precise to the quarter-hour. The October 12, 1943 entry records the body and omits the south dock sighting.

**V-Mail** — Photographed letter forms for overseas mail during WWII. Used for correspondence with servicemen.

**OPA ration books** — Office of Price Administration ration books governing wartime consumption of gasoline, tires, sugar, coffee, meat, canned goods, shoes.

**Hannah Pfeiffer** — 17-year-old daughter of Albrecht and Magda Pfeiffer; born in Maine to Bavarian immigrant parents; lives on the dairy farm three miles inland from Coldwater Reach; POV character 2. Observant, curious, caught between two languages and two loyalties she didn't choose.

**Karl Reinmar** — 19-year-old German POW from Ansbach, Bavaria; youngest of four assigned to the Pfeiffer farm satellite work camp. From approximately 50 km from Albrecht Pfeiffer's hometown. Knows the German names for birds; still learning farm labor. Wears a POW jacket with stenciling on the back.

**Albrecht Pfeiffer** — Hannah's father; Bavarian immigrant (arrived 1922) dairy farmer. Has retreated into German at home since the war started. Speaks short directives to the POWs in German. His hands stopped on his knife and fork when Hannah mentioned the breakwater body (October 14, 1943).

**Magda Pfeiffer** — Hannah's mother; early 50s; her silence began as depression when the war started and has not lifted. Sits with cold cups, does not eat consistently, speaks rarely or not at all.

**Lonzo Reed** — Coastal Patrol private, age 62; assigned to escort/guard duty for the German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm satellite work camp. Carries a carbine in the crook of his arm; stationed outside the icehouse door. Watchful in the way of a man who has been watching people for sixty-two years.

**Pfeiffer Farm** — Dairy farm three miles inland from Coldwater Reach, Maine, on a dirt road off Route 1. Pre-Revolutionary barn, forty dairy cows, farmhouse, and a renovated icehouse now serving as POW bunkhouse. The farm sits on drumlins; hay fields surround it. In autumn it smells of turned earth.

**The Icehouse** — Renovated outbuilding on the Pfeiffer farm, serving as bunkhouse for the four German POWs. Retrofitted with a bar-latch door, added wooden step (August-September 1943). Interior: two-tiered bunk frames along walls, small table with lamp. Visible from Hannah's east-side bedroom window.

**Seidenschwanz** — German word for cedar waxwing. The first word Karl Reinmar says to himself in the dark outside the icehouse (ch. 02); Hannah catches the sound but not the meaning. The seed of the Hannah-Karl naming exchange (Thread 5). English meaning revealed in ch. 08.

**Ruthanne Gorey** — Hannah's schoolmate; mentioned in ch. 02 on the school bus gossiping about the breakwater body.

**June Pullen** — Hannah's schoolmate; mentioned alongside Ruthanne Gorey in ch. 02.

**Clement Ames** — Coldwater Reach resident; was at the fish pier on October 12, 1943 when the Coast Guard truck arrived, and spread news of the breakwater body to others including Mr. Gorey.

**Dr. Marcus Klein** — German and Latin teacher at Coldwater Reach High School; Viennese Jewish émigré, age 47 in October 1943. Arrived in Coldwater March 1942 after fleeing Austria (1938), Paris, London, and internment at Sherbrooke, Quebec. Boards with Henry and Lillian Crow on Elm Street. POV character.

**Henry Crow** — Chief custodian at Coldwater Reach High School; Marcus's landlord. Family came north from Norfolk, Virginia in 1924. Has been at the school since 1928. Wife Lillian; son James is in the U.S. Army, Pacific theater. Approximately 55 in 1943.

**Lillian Crow** — Henry Crow's wife. Keeps the household on Elm Street. Treats Marcus as a guest without requiring explanation; sets his plate without asking his preferences after approximately 18 months. Community church member.

**James Crow** — Henry and Lillian Crow's son. U.S. Army, Pacific theater; deployed February 1943. Known to Marcus only through his formal pre-deployment photograph on the Crow kitchen wall and the pattern of his parents' behavior when Pacific-postmarked letters arrive.

**Stillman** — Geography teacher at Coldwater Reach High School, rooms 201 and 202. Marcus's staffroom contact; tells Marcus about the body on the breakwater as casual gossip.

**Cornelia Foss** — Student in Marcus's Latin class at Coldwater Reach High School. One of three students in the class; notable for actually reading rather than waiting to be rescued.

**Coldwater Reach High School** — Three-story brick building on the main road in Coldwater Reach, built 1912 (cornerstone dated). Gymnasium doubles as polling place. Henry Crow runs the basement boiler room October through April. Marcus teaches German and Latin here.

**Crow house** — Henry and Lillian Crow's house on Elm Street, Coldwater Reach. Marcus boards on the second floor. Kitchen has an iron stove, bread board, and James Crow's Army photograph on the wall above the backyard window. Basement boiler room is Henry's domain.

**Elm Street** — Street in Coldwater Reach running downhill toward the harbor. The Crow house is on this street. At the bottom, the road bends north onto Main Street; the harbor appears briefly between two buildings at the turn. Marcus walks this route to school daily.

**Continental educator** — The notation appended by the Coldwater town meeting to Marcus Klein's original school hire, visible on the folder on the principal's desk. Accounts for his presence without requiring analysis. Marcus has seen it before and metabolizes it drily.

**Sherbrooke internment camp** — Internment facility near Sherbrooke, Quebec where Marcus was held as an enemy alien (Austrian-classified-as-German) for approximately ten months in 1940–1941. Released to the Emergency Committee rolls in 1941. Marcus does not discuss this. His specific memory of it: the gate intake desk, a clerk's hand removing his document from his coat pocket.

**Western Union bicycle** — The recurring dread-shape of telegram delivery in Coldwater Reach. The Western Union boy's bicycle leaned against the post office wall signals that a casualty telegram may be in transit. First established as a symbol in ch. 03 (Marcus's walk home).

**Clara M.** — Trap hauler belonging to the Gale brothers; berthed at the inner float in Coldwater Reach harbor as of October 1943. The Gale brothers moved from the south dock to the inner float in September 1943.

**lay-up slip** — Harbormaster paperwork: a vessel owner's formal notification that their boat is being taken out of service for the winter season.

**ground tackle** — The anchor, chain, pennant, and mooring hardware that holds a vessel at a fixed mooring. Subject to annual inspection in the winter mooring schedule.

**Portland ME** — The Portland, Maine medical examiner's office; the facility that examined the man on the breakwater and determined probable cardiac event as cause of death, with no water found in the lungs.

**Roland Bates** — Student in Hannah's advanced German class at Coldwater Reach High School. Placed there by his mother's arrangement; no aptitude or interest. A minor character — present for contrast with Hannah and Patrice. Not important to the plot.

**Patrice Audette** — Student in Hannah's advanced German class at Coldwater Reach High School. Genuinely skilled in German; knows it without making a performance of the knowing. Appears in class scenes but is not a significant character.

**Room 114** — The smallest classroom in Coldwater Reach High School — southwest corner, ground floor. Used for advanced German class (three students) after extra desks were removed at Marcus Klein's request by Henry Crow, September 1943.

**Herbsttag** — Autumn Day — a poem by Rainer Maria Rilke (from Das Buch der Bilder, 1902). The translation text for Marcus Klein's advanced German class, November 1943. Assigned for its syntax (indefinite relative pronoun wer, dative reflexive, retroactive finality in mehr), not for its subject matter. Its subject matter is present in the chapter anyway: autumn, solitude, a man with no house.

**Korrelat** — German grammatical term for a syntactic placeholder — a pronoun or es that holds a place in a clause structure for a predicate or adjective already established or anticipated. Marcus Klein explains it to Hannah after class in November 1943 in the context of Rilke's line 'Wer jetzt allein ist, wird es lange bleiben.' The es is the Korrelat. Hannah writes the word in her translation margin.

**Marta** — Hannah Pfeiffer's cousin, living in Portland, Maine. Sends letters to the Pfeiffer farm. Mentioned by letter in November 1943; no other appearances to date.

**Werner Grau** — Old friend of Albrecht Pfeiffer from before either man emigrated from Bavaria. Living in Portland, Maine, as of September 1943. Wrote to Albrecht on September 28, 1943 planning to visit Coldwater in October; carried news of the Reinmar family in Ansbach that he would not commit to paper. Signed his letter 'Ihr alter Freund.' Plausibly the unidentified man found on the breakwater in October 1943 — but not confirmed. The letter he wrote is the documentary spine of Marcus Klein's thread.

**Werner Grau's letter** — Two-page German letter (single sheet, both sides) from Werner Grau in Portland, Maine, to Albrecht Pfeiffer, postmarked September 28, 1943. Misdelivered to Coldwater Reach High School; rerouted to Marcus Klein by school secretary Mrs. Ledbetter. Opened by Marcus, read twice, placed in his classroom desk drawer under the Latin dictionary. Contents: old friendship renewed, planned October visit, knowledge of Karl Reinmar at the farm, news of the Reinmar family in Ansbach ('ich trage es seit Monaten'), signed 'Ihr alter Freund, Werner Grau.'

**Mrs. Ledbetter** — School secretary at Coldwater Reach High School. Identified by initials M.L. Rerouted Werner Grau's misdelivered German letter to Marcus Klein on pragmatic grounds — as it's in German, she thought he'd be best placed to handle it. Not suspicious; practical.

**German Kurrent** — Historical German cursive script taught in German schools through the early twentieth century; a handwriting style Marcus identifies in Werner Grau's letter, indicating the writer's schooling and likely national origin.

**Wendell Foss** — Lobsterman, Coldwater Reach. Reliable conduit for town information; ties his boat stern-first at the fuel dock. First appears in ch07 telling Eira about the county board's interest in her tenure.

**Ida B.** — The Gale brothers' second lobster boat. On the boatyard railway since November 3, 1943 for winter lay-up. Their first boat is Clara M.

**Harbor board challenge** — The county authority's review of Eira's harbormaster tenure. First surfaces as a rumor in December 1943 (ch07) via Wendell Foss citing Clement Ames; formalizes as an inspection in ch11 and a formal proceeding in ch25.

**winter registry** — The December harbor procedure by which Eira assigns winter mooring slots, verifies vessel documentation, and logs the hulls that will winter over in the inner harbor. Establishes which vessels are accounted for and which are not in compliance.

**Seidenschwanz** — German for cedar waxwing (literally: silk-tail). The pivot word of the Hannah/Karl shared vocabulary. First said by Karl to himself in ch02 (October 1943); written by Hannah in her German notebook margin in ch05 (November 1943); exchanged bilaterally at the Pfeiffer barn water trough in ch08 (January 1944) when a cedar waxwing lands on the trough rim. The word recurs across the novel as a marker of the naming relationship between Hannah and Karl.

**the Stuttgarter** — Hannah's private name for one of the four German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm — the one who once identified himself as from Stuttgart. Fast, efficient, impersonal in the way of long practice. One of two older German POWs who are background figures.

**the Bavarian** — Hannah's private name for the third German POW at the Pfeiffer farm — clearly from farm-labor background, slower than the Stuttgarter but the cows don't startle with him. Background figure.

**cedar waxwing** — Bombycilla cedrorum. A crested, masked bird with a yellow tail-tip and red waxy spots on the secondary feathers. Appears at the Pfeiffer farm in winter, working frozen berries from the old crabapple at the stone wall. The English equivalent of Seidenschwanz; exchanged with Karl in ch08.

**Pfeiffer Farm Barn** — Pre-Revolutionary barn on the Pfeiffer farm; cornerstone dated 1763. Gray silver-pine board siding, gambrel roof. Albrecht Pfeiffer maintains it and the farm equipment (manure spreader, etc.) near its west door. Observed by Marcus Klein during his January 1944 visit.

**Arctium (burdock)** — Weed growing in the Pfeiffer farm dooryard; visible in winter as dry waist-high stalks stripped to skeleton with burred seed heads through the snow. Marcus identifies it by Latin name (Arctium) before the English equivalent — an established character habit.

**The Bavarian (POW)** — One of the four German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm — an older man (mid-thirties or beyond), heavy build from agricultural labor, identified by Hannah as 'the Bavarian.' Has been at the farm since approximately late September 1943. Animals are calm with him. Seen by Marcus carrying a feed bucket during the January 1944 visit; assessed Marcus efficiently and went on without comment.

**Manure spreader (Pfeiffer farm)** — Farm equipment at the Pfeiffer barn, west door. Albrecht Pfeiffer was examining its axle-bearing housing when Marcus arrived in January 1944. Established as background physical detail of the farm's working life.

**Amos Tarbell** — County harbor authority inspector, mid-thirties, who conducts the February 1944 routine inspection of Coldwater Reach harbor. Finds no administrative deficiency. Represents the county board's political challenge to Eira's tenure, though he cannot answer what process would satisfy the board.

**Director Pearce** — County harbor authority director in 1935; signed the November 21, 1935 letter appointing Eira Bowman as harbormaster of Coldwater Reach 'pending formal review by the county harbor authority board.' Historical figure; the review was never conducted.

**1935 County Authority Letter** — Typed letter on county authority letterhead, November 21, 1935, signed by Director Pearce. Appoints Eira Bowman harbormaster of Coldwater Reach pending formal review. Review scheduled December 1935, rescheduled once, then never rescheduled again. Document is Eira's formal but technically provisional claim to the post. Taken by Tarbell in February 1944 to copy for his file.

**Harbor Log 1935** — The harbor log volume for 1935, green board cover, year inked on spine in Owen Bowman's hand. Contains Owen's final entries through November 7 (storm preparations, harbor secured) and Eira's first entries from November 9 (husband missing, harbor open). November 8 is blank. Kept on the north shelf of the harbormaster's shed between the 1934 and 1936 volumes.

**Ida May** — Lobster boat based at Coldwater Reach; on winter mooring in the inner harbor as of February 1944.

**Sadie H.** — Lobster boat based at Coldwater Reach; on winter mooring in the inner harbor as of February 1944.

**Clara B.** — Lobster boat based at Coldwater Reach; on winter mooring in the inner harbor as of February 1944.

**East Wind** — Dragger based at Coldwater Reach; works the offshore grounds; out at first light and returning by afternoon in winter operations as of February 1944.

**winter mooring registry** — Harbor document listing all vessels on winter moorings in Coldwater Reach inner harbor. Maintained by the harbormaster. As of February 1944: 17 vessels.

**fuel allocation ledger** — Harbor document recording each disbursement of the wartime supplemental fuel allocation (from the Department of the Interior) by vessel name, date, and gallons. Each entry initialed by the harbormaster. Column must balance to the gallon against the quarterly DOI authorization.

**Fischer** — One of the three older German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm satellite work camp. Former Feldwebel (sergeant). Makes pronouncements to the air rather than addressing people directly. First named in ch. 12 (Ansbach).

**Bauer** — One of the three older German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm satellite work camp. First named in ch. 12 (Ansbach).

**Hentschel** — One of the three older German POWs at the Pfeiffer farm satellite work camp. First named in ch. 12 (Ansbach).

**Feldwebel** — German military rank equivalent to sergeant. Fischer's prior rank before capture. First mentioned in ch. 12 (Ansbach).

**Karolinenstraße** — Street in Ansbach, Bavaria, where Karl Reinmar grew up and where Werner Grau knew Karl's father. A bakery on the street opened at five in the morning. First named in ch. 12 (Ansbach).

**Hefegebäck** — German sweet yeast pastry. The smell of it from the Karolinenstraße bakery is Karl's specific Ansbach memory. First named in ch. 12 (Ansbach).

**Seidenschwanz** — German word for cedar waxwing (Bombycilla cedrorum). First said aloud by Karl in ch. 2; exchanged between Karl and Hannah in ch. 8; the first and last word of Karl's chapter (ch. 12). Part of Karl's private taxonomy of 41 Maine birds named in German.

**Dr. Heinrich Sondheimer** — Columbia University professor (German department); was on the intake end of the Emergency Committee for Displaced Foreign Scholars rescue network, 1940–1943 period; holds informal files on displaced Europeans. Met Marcus once in Cambridge, 1941. Appears only in letters; does not visit Coldwater.

**Boston Alliance for the Arts** — Boston-based arts organization that sponsored Werner Grau's US entry visa in March 1936, as part of a cultural exchange program for European painters and printmakers. Program ended 1937 when funding was redirected.

**Graves** — Junior student in Marcus Klein's six-person advanced German section at Coldwater Reach High School. Natural ear for German syntax; impatient with long subordinate clauses.

**Camp Upton** — U.S. Army camp (Long Island, NY) where James Crow was photographed in uniform before shipping to the Pacific theater. The photograph hangs above the kitchen radiator shelf in Henry and Lillian's house.

**Emergency Committee for Displaced Foreign Scholars** — The network through which Marcus Klein was placed in the United States after his release from Sherbrooke internment in 1941. Sondheimer was on the intake end. Referenced in ch13 as context for Marcus's inquiry about Werner Grau.

**Pfeiffer Tin Box** — Small dark-painted metal box with corners worn to bright metal and a dented clasp. Stored on the shelf above the folded shirts in Albrecht and Magda Pfeiffer's bedroom. Contains letters in German (in Albrecht's hand), folded documents, and a small stack of photographs including the Bamberg photograph. Hannah discovers it in April 1944 during laundry.

**Bamberg Photograph** — Photograph of two men — Albrecht Pfeiffer and Werner Grau — before a stone civic or church doorway in Bamberg, Bavaria. Both in their thirties, wool coats, hats in hand, squinting into afternoon light. Inscription on reverse in Albrecht's penciled German hand: 'A. Pfeiffer. W. Grau. Bamberg, 1921.' Stored in the Pfeiffer tin box. Discovered by Hannah in April 1944; provides the first connection between Werner Grau and the Pfeiffer family. Referenced again in ch20 and ch28.

**Bamberg** — City in Bavaria, Germany, north of Munich. Location of the 1921 photograph of Albrecht Pfeiffer and Werner Grau. Not Albrecht's hometown (his family were from farther south, near the Austrian border), but within the same general region as Ansbach (Karl Reinmar's hometown). Hannah notes the proximity without developing it.

**Grandy Soule** — Lobsterman, approximately 70 years old, Coldwater Reach. Has been fishing this harbor since before Eira's time. Owns the Maud L. Provides the key eyewitness testimony in ch15: saw an unregistered dinghy at the south pier the night before the October 1943 storm, one man coming off it toward the waterfront, did not see the man return. Does not understand the significance of what he saw and never learns it.

**Maud L.** — Grandy Soule's 32-foot lobster boat, hull dull red (faded). Bought by Soule in 1928; has worked Coldwater Reach harbor since. A fixture at the fuel dock.

**Crestview** — Lobster boat owned by Harold Oakes; on the railway at the Coldwater boatyard since June 1942 after Oakes went into the Navy. Cradle fee outstanding as of spring 1944.

**Walter D.** — The Witham family's lobster boat; sold to a buyer in Tenants Harbor in February 1944 after Lyle Witham took sick.

**Sea Star** — Lobster boat owned by Ray Coffin (Fred Coffin's younger son). Returned to the fleet spring 1944.

**Ray Coffin** — Fred Coffin's younger son; returned from Pacific theater November 1943 with two fingers missing from his right hand. Back on the water April 1944 aboard the Sea Star.

**Bertha L.** — Lobster boat owned by Pete Alley of Coldwater Reach.

**Edna C.** — Lobster boat owned by Burton Talbot of Coldwater Reach; received a new inboard engine in the 1944 refit.

**Marcus's desk drawer (locked)** — The locking bottom drawer of Marcus Klein's room desk at Henry and Lillian Crow's house. Contains the Werner Grau documents: Grau's letter to Albrecht Pfeiffer (original envelope, resealed), Sondheimer's Columbia reply with Marcus's school-paper plausible account folded inside, and a Latin dictionary resting on top. Locked as of May 1944; key in Marcus's coat pocket. This is where the assembled account of Werner Grau's death lives throughout the novel until Hannah tells Marcus the last piece in ch. 28.

**Plausible account** — Marcus Klein's private written inference about Werner Grau's death, completed May 1944: Werner traveled by small boat from Portland, arrived Coldwater night of October 11–12 1943, was on the south waterfront, attempted to find Albrecht Pfeiffer, and died before the storm's peak — cause listed as 'by drowning, by cardiac event, by the storm, by some combination of these things.' 'By some combination' is circled as the uncloseable center of the account. Stored in the locked desk drawer with the other Grau documents.

**Boston Alliance for the Arts** — Cultural exchange program for painters and printmakers that sponsored Werner Grau's 1936 US entry visa. Funding redirected 1937; visa renewed once (March 1937), final expiry March 1940. Referenced in Sondheimer's Columbia letter.

**Burton Talbot** — Coldwater Reach fisherman; dragger captain, owner of the Edna C. His sister's son, age 19, is serving in Normandy as of June 1944.

**Osgood family** — Coldwater Reach family; their son went missing in Normandy around June 14, 1944; the mother comes to the post office daily.

**Eira's unlabeled column** — The mental ledger Eira keeps for data she cannot yet classify. As of late June 1944 it holds two entries: the two dock figures from October 10–11, 1943, and Marcus Klein's question about the Pfeiffer family.

**Althaus** — Sondheimer's Emergency Committee colleague; offers Marcus Klein a German literature position at a small college outside Boston (September 1944 start) via Sondheimer; the offer is declined. Appears by name only in ch19.

**Bell buoy (channel entrance)** — Coast Guard bell buoy marking the deep line of the Coldwater Reach channel approach. Rings in a water-rhythm on the swell. Audible from Marcus Klein's room at Henry and Lillian's house when the harbor breeze comes from the southeast and the night is quiet. First distinguished by Marcus in ch19; recurs as image in ch30.

**Boston transfer offer** — German literature position at a small college outside Boston, September 1944 start, communicated to Marcus via Althaus/Sondheimer in June 1944. Marcus declines it by letter in July–August 1944. The letter says 'committed to Coldwater'; the private formulation is 'at least one further academic year.'

**bird notebook (Hannah's)** — Composition notebook begun by Hannah Pfeiffer in October 1943; records German-English bird name pairs in two columns with pencil drawings in margins. 23 entries by August 1944. Physical: covers soft from use, back cover has a water stain from September 1943. First entry: Seidenschwanz / Cedar Waxwing. The notebook is the physical token of Hannah and Karl's relationship and the vehicle through which Karl's knowledge of Werner Grau reaches Hannah in ch20.

**Habicht** — German for goshawk (accipiter type hawk that flies low and direct); distinguished in ch20 from Sperber by the wrist angle of the wingbeat. Added to Hannah's bird notebook in August 1944.

**Sperber** — German for Eurasian sparrowhawk / sparrow hawk; the hovering hawk Hannah watches over the south fence line. Added to Hannah's bird notebook in August 1944 when Karl names it.

**Er wollte kommen** — He wanted to come (German, past tense). Said by Karl Reinmar to Hannah in August 1944 referring to Werner Grau. The past tense is the chapter's load-bearing fact.

**Du weißt** — You know (German). Said by Hannah to Karl after he names Werner and uses the past tense. Not a question. Karl nods once.

**South Cove** — Small cove behind Coldwater Reach's south headland, approximately 100 yards end to end, entrance facing east. Sheltered from the prevailing swell; accumulates drifted gear. Part of Eira Bowman's annual autumn outer-cove survey route (added in her third year as harbormaster). Site where the stove-in rental dinghy connected to W. Grau is discovered in September 1944.

**The Portland Dinghy (Grau's dinghy)** — Twelve-foot flat-bottomed harbor pulling boat, last rented September 28, 1943 from a Commercial Street chandlery in Portland by W. Grau. Found stove-in (port side amidships) in the south cove behind Coldwater's south headland, September 1944, by Eira Bowman. Transom shows only '—STEN' of original name. Portland Harbor Authority registration plate survives in lower transom.

**Commercial Street Chandlery (Portland)** — Unnamed chandlery on Commercial Street, Portland, Maine, operating a small-craft rental service. Operated the dinghy last rented by W. Grau on September 28, 1943. Source of the rental record that gives Eira the name W. Grau and the address 114 Franklin Street, Portland.

**114 Franklin Street, Portland, Maine** — Address given by W. Grau on the rental record at the Commercial Street chandlery in Portland, September 28, 1943. His last known address. Referenced when Marcus eventually receives this information (indirectly, through Hannah, ch28).

**breakwater stone** — Fist-sized piece of granite broken from the breakwater elbow's cap course; gray-green, one face rough at the fracture, the rest worn smooth. Marcus Klein picks it up on October 14, 1944 and places it on the windowsill of his room at the Crow house. Present again in the novel's final image (ch30). Never explained.

**Karl's note** — A note written by Karl Reinmar in English on the back of an envelope, given to Hannah Pfeiffer at his transfer from the Pfeiffer farm in November 1944. Exact text: 'Werner Grau. From Ansbach. He is dead. I am sorry.' Below: five bird names in German and Latin, cedar waxwing (Seidenschwanz/Bombycilla cedrorum) at top, followed by Rotkehlchen/Turdus migratorius, Blaumeise/Poecile atricapillus, Habicht/Accipiter gentilis, Sperber/Falco sparverius. Hannah keeps the note. It is shown to Marcus in ch28.

**Ich weiß** — German: 'I know.' Albrecht Pfeiffer's response when Hannah tells him 'Werner Grau ist tot' (Werner Grau is dead) in November 1944. Said simply, without elaboration. Establishes that Albrecht has known of Werner's death since October 1943. This is the limit of what he will say.

**Werner Grau ist tot** — German: 'Werner Grau is dead.' The sentence Hannah says to Albrecht in November 1944 — in German, not because she chose the language but because German was what the sentence required. The language of the household's private knowledge.

**County Harbor Authority Proceeding** — Formal county board proceeding to review Eira Bowman's acting harbormaster appointment status, scheduled November 22, 1944. Official language frames it as a 'review' not a 'challenge'; Eira understands it as political — someone wants the post reassigned. The proceeding itself occurs in ch29.

**Eira's Proceeding Summary Document** — Twelve-page (plus three pages to complete) document Eira prepares for the county board hearing, covering mooring registry, revenue comparison with Owen's last two years, fuel record, safety inspection record, and casualty log. Prepared on county-header forms with the board's reference number.

**Cecelia R.** — Vessel that failed Eira's safety inspection in October 1942 (port running light out, cracked exhaust elbow); brought into compliance in eleven days; reinspected and certified on the twelfth day. The only inspection failure in Eira's eight-year record.

**Holcomb** — Hardware merchant and school board member, Coldwater Reach. Store on south end of main road. Voted against Marcus Klein's December 1944 contract renewal. Characterized by 'managed indefiniteness' — two-plus years of careful ambiguous objection to Marcus's presence, finally forced to a stated position by the first-papers announcement.

**Mrs. Vance** — Chair, Coldwater Reach School Board. Efficient administrator; voted in favor of Marcus's contract renewals.

**Rideout** — County clerk, Coldwater Reach; eleven-year tenure as of 1944. Ground-floor office in town hall. Received Marcus's letter correcting Lillian Crow's relief administration tax status classification, December 1944.

**School Service Window Placard** — White-bordered card (approx. 20x30 in.) listing Coldwater Reach men in military service, mounted in front window of the high school since November 1942. As of December 1944: 24 names, including J. CROW, Army, Pacific (added March 1944 after Marcus asked the principal).

**Spruce Street** — Street in Coldwater Reach where the Crow house stands — informal name from two old spruce trees at the corner; formal name not given in the novel. The Crow house is the fourth from the corner.

**First Papers (declaration of intent)** — The initial formal step in the U.S. naturalization process: a declaration filed at a federal district court affirming intent to become a citizen. Not citizenship itself, but a public and irrevocable statement of intent. Marcus filed his in November 1944 at the Rockland courthouse.

**Carver** — Retired vessel inspector; county harbor authority panel member for the Coldwater Reach proceeding (March 1945). White-haired, in his seventies. Voted to confirm Eira's appointment.

**Pratt** — Knox County board member who initiated the formal review of Eira Bowman's harbormaster appointment. Hostile to her confirmation. Cast the sole dissenting vote (two to one against him). Left without speaking after the finding was read.

**Haverford** — County maritime licensing office, Camden. Neutral third member of the harbor authority proceeding panel. Voted to confirm Eira's appointment. Passed the certification across the table without warmth but without hostility.

**Coombs** — Coldwater Reach lobsterman. Runs traps off Caldwell Bank. Lands catch at the cold-storage house at the pier. Encountered Eira in the Rockland county building corridor during the panel's deliberation and gave her the pier nod.

**Caldwell Bank** — Fishing ground off Coldwater Reach where Coombs runs his lobster traps.

**Mrs. Voisin** — Neighbor on Marcus Klein's walking route to school in Coldwater Reach; seen at her step with the newspaper on spring mornings (ch30).

**Kelleher (family)** — Coldwater family with a son at Coldwater High School; the Kelleher boy seen walking his bicycle to school with a flat tire (ch30).

**Garfield household** — Household at the corner on Marcus Klein's walking route to school; known to Marcus as the earliest gardeners on the block — kitchen garden in motion by late April (ch30).
