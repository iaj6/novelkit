The image surfaces: two nights ago, past midnight, two figures on the south dock — one she didn't recognize (past middle age, solid, looking toward the outer harbor), one with a farmer's build, half-turned, not a sailor's posture. She picks up her pen, holds it, sets it down. The entry stays as written. Owen's logbook is on the shelf, untouched since 1935.

## 09-the-visit
**Summary:** January 1944. Marcus's chapter. Marcus watches with the attention he brings to reading faces — a face receiving confirmation rather than discovery; not surprise but recognition of a specific kind. At the letter's midpoint (Werner's full name used in a sentence) something crosses Albrecht's face: the expression of a man receiving what he has been waiting for. Near-certainty, no proof. He returns to Henry's house through the back. Lillian is at the stove. He hangs his coat.
