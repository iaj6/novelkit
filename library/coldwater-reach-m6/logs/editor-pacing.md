# Editor — Pacing Pass
## Macro arc assessment

### Sources consulted
`brief.md`, `canon/threads.md`, `canon/themes.md`, `outline/00-chapter-map.md`, `logs/story-arc.md`, `logs/chapter-craft.md` (craft log, partial — first entry previewed; ending-mode/opening-texture data drawn from the chapter-map distribution table and story-arc digest). ---

### Overall arc shape

The five-act architecture is genuinely well-built. Act I (chs 01–05) establishes three differentiated voices without expository throat-clearing; the body is on the page before any of the three principals has been introduced, which is the right order. Act II (chs 06–11) deepens the mystery and establishes independent pressure on each POV's personal arc. The midpoint zone (Act III, chs 12–18) is the most event-dense portion of the book — the photograph, Soule's corroboration, Marcus's full assembly of the Grau account, D-Day — and it earns the title of midpoint rather than sag. The novel's structural health is better than average for a 30-chapter braid.

---

### Midbook sag: not in the middle third — in the convergence zone

The classic midbook sag does not materialize in Act III. It surfaces in early Act IV (chs 19–23), which should be the convergence phase but contains two restful, fade-ending Marcus chapters (ch 19 *Another Year*, ch 23 *One Year*) that interrupt the pressure the surrounding chapters generate. Looking at the ending sequence in Act IV: fade (19) → turn (20) → turn (21) → elliptical (22) → **fade** (23) → turn (24). The two quiet Marcus fades (19, 23) both make genuine thread moves — the decision not to leave, the stone picked up from the breakwater — but they are so interior and understated that they may not sustain reader momentum through what should be the novel's tightening spiral. This is the most actionable pacing risk in the book.

Specifically: ch 19 arrives directly after ch 18, which is itself a low-intensity harbor-traffic chapter with a resolved-beat ending. Three chapters in a row (17, 18, 19) all land without strong pressure — a D-Day scene that opens before dawn and closes quietly, a harbor routine chapter, then the transfer-decision letter. This mini-sag at chs 17–19 is the one structural stretch where the threading tables show activity (threads are advancing) but the arc-digest suggests the actual chapter-by-chapter pressure drops. Whether it reads as an earned breathing space after the midpoint cluster or as a stall depends entirely on how much internal charge those three chapters carry — which the per-chapter pass should test.

---

### Three consecutive turns (chs 13–14–15)

The chapter-map acknowledges the turn count runs over target (11 vs. 8). The most visible run is chs 13–14–15: three consecutive turn-ending chapters (*Displaced Scholars*, *The Photograph*, *What Grandy Soule Saw*). Each is a genuine story pivot, but three turns in a row risks making the reader feel the mechanism. The turns are substantively different in kind (information discovery / family revelation / physical corroboration), so the problem is less about content than about rhythm: each of these chapters ends by ratcheting forward, and without a varied close between them the ratchet can feel operated rather than earned. If any of these three chapters is also using the same closing gesture — a physical action substituted for affect, say — that would compound the pattern. Per-chapter pass should check.

---

### Hannah's exit before the final act

Hannah's nine chapters are her last at ch 28. She has no presence in chapters 29–30. This is structurally intentional — the novel opens on Eira, closes on Marcus, and Hannah's arc resolves with the transfer (ch 24) and the testimony-to-Marcus (ch 28) — but it produces a three-voice braid that becomes a one-voice close. In a 30-chapter novel, the reader spends the final 7% of pages (chs 29–30) without one of the three principals. The risk is not continuity but texture: the final act narrows from three voices to two (Eira, ch 29; Marcus, ch 30) without explicit acknowledgment that Hannah is gone. The per-chapter pass on ch 30 should verify the final chapter is doing enough work, on its own, to justify the narrowing rather than leaving the reader with a sense of a strand dropped.

---

### Climax/coda balance

This is a novel that declines a traditional climax and distributes its resolutions across Act V (chs 25–30). That is a defensible choice — it matches the book's core argument that "the story's resolutions are smaller and older than the war's ending." But it creates a specific structural test: each of the six resolution chapters needs internal momentum (something to move toward within the chapter) even though none of them is the "big" chapter. Looking at the ending-mode sequence for Act V: mid-action (25) → turn (26) → resolved-beat (27) → fade (28) → resolved-beat (29) → fade (30). This is actually varied, which suggests the chapters are not homogeneous in texture. The fade at ch 28 leading into resolved-beat at ch 29 is a good sequence — the quieter Hannah chapter sets up the clean formal resolution of Eira's proceeding. The coda (ch 30) is a single-voice literary fade, which is right for this novel. The risk is that the coda is too short and too private to feel like an adequate close after three hundred pages of braided assembly. Per-chapter pass on ch 30 is recommended.

---

### Ending-mode patterns worth flagging

From the distribution table:
- **Fades cluster in two places**: chs 02–03 (consecutive) and chs 28–30 (two fades with one resolved-beat between them). The late-book fade cluster is by design; the opening fade-pair (chs 02–03) means the novel's first three chapters end as: turn, fade, fade. The second and third chapters both landing in fade risks a lull before the story has fully ignited. Per-chapter pass on ch 03 is recommended if the opening hasn't already been stress-tested.
- **The elliptical chapters (07, 10, 16, 22)** are well-spaced — one per act through chs 06–24 — and serve the mystery's deliberate withholding. No concern here.
- **The single mid-action-cut (ch 25)** is the only chapter in the book that ends without any form of resolution or image; this is appropriate for the proceeding-anticipation chapter, but it should land with genuine tension rather than simply deferring. Per-chapter pass should verify.
