## 02-icehouse.md

**Fix applied:**
- Cognitive-framing clause removed from the bus section. Original: *"What she was thinking about was not the body — she had no image for it, had not been near the harbor — but the condition itself."* Revised to: *"Not the body — she had no image for it, had not been near the harbor — but the condition itself."* The "What she was thinking about was…" construction is the style guide's prohibited meta-commentary on cognition ("If the prose is doing its job, the noticing is the sentence, not a label for it"). The content is fully preserved; only the framing clause was dropped.

**Flags for writer judgment:**

1. **Possible anachronism — "clocking it"** (Lonzo section, first barn sequence): *"his head didn't move, but nothing in the farmyard happened without him clocking it."* "Clock" as a verb meaning *to observe/notice* is documented British slang of the period but its currency in 1943 American English (and specifically a Maine farm girl's narrative register) is uncertain. If it reads as contemporary slang, a period-safe alternative — *"without him marking it"* or *"without him catching it"* — would preserve the meaning. Writer's call on whether the word feels right.

2. **Slight register drift — "trying to determine… without being able to settle it either way"** (Lonzo's habits, first barn sequence): *"she had been trying to determine for two weeks whether he was an early riser by nature or simply wasn't sleeping much, without being able to settle it either way."* The construction is tidier and more formally analytical than Hannah's usual associative voice — it has a faint Marcus cadence (qualification, balanced clause). Not a clear violation, but if the sentence ever feels out of register on re-read, Hannah might more naturally carry this as a sensory fragment: something she keeps half-noticing rather than *determining* and *settling*. Low priority; flagged for awareness only.

## 12-ansbach.md

**Issue: German dialogue translation gloss — requires writer/compression judgment**

> *"Der Mann, der dich besuchen sollte. Er ist nicht gekommen."*
>

This is a direct dialogue gloss — the exact construction the style guide prohibits ("which meant," "as if to say"). The two English sentences should be cut.

This cannot be resolved with a word or clause substitution — it requires removing the translation lines. Flagged here for the writer or a compression-pass follow-up.
