## 08-copies-laid-out.md

**Fix applied:** Handwriting observation, para 6 — `"the downstrokes of the l and the t in 'lying'"` → `"the downstrokes of the l and the y in 'lying'"`. The word *lying* (l-y-i-n-g) contains no 't'. In cursive handwriting, 'l' and 'y' are the letters with prominent downstrokes (the 'y' descends below the baseline); 'y' is the plausible intended letter. Register requires exact physical observation; a wrong letter in a handwriting description is a precision slip in Munteanu's voice.

**Out-of-scope issue for writer's attention (logic, not voice):** Third section, the deduction about the second Thursday copy — `"That meant the second Thursday copy had probably been copied from the grey-paper copy or from something that had already introduced *beside*"`. The grey-paper copy says *by the bed*, not *beside the bed*. If it were the source of the second Thursday copy, the second Thursday copy would also say *by*. The two alternatives offered are mutually contradictory: copying from the grey-paper copy would produce "by," not "beside." The passage probably intends to say the second Thursday copy was copied from something upstream that had already introduced "beside" — with the grey-paper copy being upstream of the *first* Thursday copy, not the second. The logic of the deduction needs a sentence or two of revision; the conclusion (grey-paper is oldest, or closest to oldest) may still be correct but the argument as written does not support it.

## 16-two-branches.md

**Unfixable at word/clause level — writer's judgment required:**

Paragraph describing Viorica Damian's demeanor: "She was not frightened." This sentence directly names an emotional state. The style guide is explicit: "Fear is almost never named. The reader assembles the political situation from its effects." The behavioral work in the surrounding sentences — steady pace, no hesitation, nothing that would be visible from the intake counter — already shows the reader what they need. The named negation ("not frightened") deflates what the physical rendering accomplishes. A word-level fix would require cutting the sentence, which is compression's domain; flagged here for writer's decision.

*Applied fixes in same paragraph:* "She was wary in the particular way" → "She carried herself in the particular way" (wary names a fear-adjacent state; behavioral verb restores the cold-procedure register).

*Applied fix elsewhere:* "he thought about the fact that he was still two nodes from the answer and the answer was sitting…" → "he was still two nodes from the answer, which was sitting…" (removed meta-cognition label per style guide prohibition on "meta-commentary on cognition").
## 30-the-bracket-included.md

Chapter is in strong register throughout. Doina's first-person past voice holds — wry, honest, retrospective, nineteen. The present-tense asides ("I want to be clear," "I find I can hold that thought") are in-register for the "she survived to tell it" framing and do not constitute voice drift.

**One borderline phrase — writer's call:**

> "she had put the paper in my hand the way you pass someone the salt — before I could think of a reason to say no, which I suspect was not accidental, which I suspect was the technique of someone who had done this kind of transaction before and knew that **the window for consent is narrower than the window for refusal**."

The clause "the window for consent is narrower than the window for refusal" is the only moment in the chapter that tips from Doina's wry-concrete register into something closer to formal abstraction — a principle stated in the way a policy document or a sociologist might state it. The surrounding sentences in this paragraph are concrete (no name, no stop, no direction, the number four bus, eleven months). "The window for consent is narrower than the window for refusal" has a different texture: it sounds theorised rather than felt. Compare to the rest of the paragraph's diction: "I was cold and out and the bread was finished and the geography book was in an evidence room." That's Doina's voice. The abstract clause is not clearly wrong — she's analytical and retrospective throughout — but it's the chapter's one phrase that reads as slightly over-polished for her register. A concrete revision ("yes comes faster than no" / "the window for yes is shorter than the window for no") would land in her voice; leaving it is also defensible.
