## What the sample does well

- **Opens exactly as the guidance demands.** "The numbers come up short by twenty-three lei. Third time. The error is hers." — present tense, physical/numeric action, no scene-setting preamble. The first sentence tells you the POV register before you consciously register it.
- **Sentence-length discipline.** The vast majority of sentences fall within the 10–15 word Ilinca target. Shortest: "Third time." (2 words). "Not from interest." (3 words). No sentence in the sample breaches 20 words. This matches the exemplar cadence precisely.
- **Fear rendered as procedure, never named.** "She does not look around first. Looking around is the thing that looks wrong." is exactly the idiom the brief and style guide specify — the security reasoning is encoded in physical behaviour, not interior emotional labelling.
- **No document glossing.** She reads the paper, notes the bracket and line seven and line eight, records "The bracket is correct. The instruction inside the bracket is correct." — and stops. No sentence explaining what those lines mean to her. This is the discipline the guidance most emphasises for this book.

## What the sample drifts on

*(none)*

The sample does not drift meaningfully. No instance of fear-naming, glossing, extended interiority, atmospheric opening delay, or political-consciousness attribution. The voice is propulsive-procedural rather than contemplative: she runs the column, corrects the error, stays, retrieves the ledger, reads the document, and begins to count. Each beat forecloses the previous one. The pace is fast-to-medium for this register — measured, but purposeful, not ruminative.

One micro-observation worth naming (not a reject-trigger): "She often stays" is a slight echo of "This is not unusual" two sentences prior. The guidance says do not re-summarise. This is not pattern-level drift — it reads more like Ilinca's internal accounting (the habit of double-checking even a reassurance) — but it's the one place where a compression instinct could tighten.
