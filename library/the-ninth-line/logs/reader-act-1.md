# Reader Act 1 Assessment — Chapters 01–10

## What's working

**Voice discipline.** The three registers are fully realized and distinguishable from the first sentence of each chapter, without exception. Ilinca's close third present lands in procedural tallies: "She is on the last two lines of the October column when the knock comes" (ch 09) is a thought and a fact and a clock simultaneously — the exemplar in the brief could have been lifted from any of her five chapters. Munteanu's third past is careful, orderly, and literary in a way that feels like the state speaking in its native register, which is exactly the brief's intention: "It was the kind of finding that would not survive being described to his captain in its present form, and he understood, with a flatness that surprised him, that he was going to pursue it anyway" (ch 04) is a near-perfect realization of the character brief. Doina is the revelation. "The bridge over the Someș was always the worst part, and I say that as someone who spent two years crossing it in the dark at four-thirty in the morning, which gives me more authority on the subject than I would have liked" (ch 10) is not the wry-and-restrained funny of previous books in this pipeline; it is genuinely nineteen. "I had done the arithmetic once and been depressed by it and had not done it again" (ch 10). The geometry of her "thinking about the word *fairly*, and how it was not the same word as *certainly*, and how it was the only word I had" (ch 06) renders fear through verbal precision without naming it. Doina's voice is the first real comic register this library has produced.

**Fear rendered as procedure.** Ten chapters, not one named fear. The cold comes through the soles of Ilinca's shoes. The woman at the counter folds the dish towel in half and then in half again "until she had a square she had nowhere particular to put" (ch 08). Doina runs cold water on her wrists because "it does something to my heart rate that is worth doing in certain situations" (ch 02). The standard holds without exception through Act I.

**The dramatic irony is in place by chapter 9.** The reader watches Ilinca read N1's bracket — "*take her in. Do not wait for the morning*" — and then construct the mutant line 9 — "*If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning*" — and understands the fatal difference before Ilinca does, and before Ilinca understands it is her cellar copy that is the ancestor. The mechanism works. The irony has fuel to run on through Act II.

**The compounding mechanism is legible.** Ilinca's count advances: 2 (ch 03), 4 (ch 07), 5 (ch 09). Munteanu's copy count advances: 2 (ch 04), 3 (ch 08). The day count is clear from the dated chapter headers. Each chapter forecloses something new: the N2 branch is closed and clean (ch 07); the mutation is confirmed present before Lucia Gal (ch 08); the investigation is visible inside the mill (ch 07) and has reached the production floor (ch 10). No chapter re-measures an already-established count without new pressure.

**The documents as objects.** The grey mill-form paper with blue pencil and a foreman's signature box on the front (ch 08) is a physically distinct thing before it is data. The geography book on the shelf above the coat hooks, between pages 34 and 35 of the watershed drainage diagram, is exactly the right kind of hidden-in-plain-sight. The 1983 ledger with its brown reinforcing tape applied in February when the binding cracked (ch 01) is a state document Ilinca would produce without hesitation — which is precisely what makes it the right hiding place.

**Munteanu's frame.** He is neither villain nor sympathiser, exactly as briefed. The difference between *by the bed* and *beside the bed* as a signal of inherited variation, not copying error (ch 04), is sound police work rendered on the page. He sees the two branches; he sees the mutation is upstream. He is right, and solving the wrong problem, and the chapter never says so.

## What's not working

**The canonical N1 text is truncated in chapter 09.** When Ilinca reads the master at her desk, the bracket is rendered as:

> `[ 7 or 8 — take her in. Do not wait for the morning. ]`

The registered canonical text of N1 (record `master-n1`) reads:

> `[ 7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning. ]`

The sentence "Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why." is absent. Chapter 09 is one of the four permitted full printings of N1, so the omission is not a fragment-quotation choice — it is a drift against the canonical record. The loss is functional, not merely formal: "what to say when you get there" is part of the document's practical purpose, and its omission makes the bracket read more clinical than it should. The contrast with the mutant line 9 still lands, but the master is incomplete. This is also inconsistent with chapter 06, where Doina correctly recites the full bracket text from N14 (which matches the canonical form): "*7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning.*" The error is small but confirmable against the record.

**Chapter 07 slightly re-confirms what chapter 03 established.** In chapter 03, Georgeta tells Ilinca that Livia "has kept it to herself." In chapter 07, via Georgeta's daughter: "She says Livia is fine. Quiet. She's been at home, not seeing people... No, she kept it to herself." The new information in chapter 07 is meaningful — Miliția has not yet reached Livia, which is a genuine foreclosure — but the repetition of the core confirmation (Livia kept it, Livia has not passed it on) is slightly redundant. The chapter earns its place through the materials-discrepancy cover and the escalating pressure context, so this falls below the threshold of a structural failure. It's a soft re-measurement: not an orbiting chapter, but a chapter where a beat runs a little long before arriving at the new thing.

## Voice slips

**Chapter 09, Ilinca's analysis of the mutation.** After reading the bracket against the mutant line 9, Ilinca explains: "The woman who made that copy was being tidy. She saw nine pieces of information and she numbered them one through nine, because that is what a careful copyist does with nine pieces of information and a numbered list on the page in front of her." This is true, precise, and in Ilinca's idiom — but it is also close to the brief's explicit prohibition on thesis-stating. The brief says the argument must be "carried entirely by the tree of copies — never stated by a character." Ilinca is stating the mechanism of the mutation, which is on the edge of the book's constraint. What saves it is that she is *wrong* — she concludes the mutation entered downstream of all three copies she made, not in N4. The articulation is in the service of a false confidence, not the book's argument. It stays on the right side of the line, but it runs very close to it. A later chapter where a character correctly names the mechanism would cross.

**Chapter 02, Doina, a single phrase.** "I am not asking you to believe it changed anything. I am asking you to believe it is true." This is Doina talking to the reader, which is consistent with her first-person retrospective register — she is giving testimony. But "I am not asking you to believe it changed anything" carries a slight editorial weight, as though Doina is pre-empting the reader's moral judgment. It's one sentence and does not recur, so it does not rise to a finding, but it's the closest the book comes in Act I to a character managing the reader's response rather than reporting to them.

## Thread development in Act I

All five threads enter cleanly. Thread 1 (Ilinca's guilty knowledge) runs from false confidence through confirmation of the clean branches to the establishment of the irony gap — Ilinca concludes the error is downstream while the reader knows it is in N4. Thread 2 (Doina's silence) is well-seeded: the woman on the bus is established as unnamed and already past; the silence is passive habit before it becomes active choice; the physical threat becomes visible in chapter 06. Thread 3 (the administrative deadline) enters in chapter 04 with the 31-day clock, is felt in Munteanu's case-building pressure, and will tighten in Act II. Thread 4 (Munteanu's wrong frame) is perfectly established: he solves the genealogy problem with genuine competence and never holds the proposition that the ninth line is a warning, not a step. Thread 5 (the form of the knowledge) opens on the gap between possession and knowing in chapter 02 and is already moving toward internalization in chapter 06 when Doina finds she remembers more than she thought she did.

## Pacing

Act I moves quickly and earns its pace. The brief's floor of 1,900 words is met in all ten chapters; no chapter is padding. The physical texture — the November Someș valley, the cold in the administrative wing before six, the loom noise that makes conversation "not possible in any practical sense" — does double duty as atmosphere and clock. The A-B-A-C rotation gives the book a driving rhythm: Ilinca building her private map, then Doina's counterpoint, then Ilinca again, then Munteanu advancing from the other direction. By chapter 10, both collations are moving, the irony is established, and the investigation is inside the mill. Act I delivers its structural contract.
