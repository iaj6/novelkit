# Panel Audit

*The subject is the reviews, not the book. Claims below are checked against the manuscript.*

---

## 1. Is the panel being too generous?

Mostly no, and not uniformly. The 5.5–8 spread is wider than it looks: the thriller lens's 5.5 represents genuine rejection of the book on genre grounds, and the authenticity lens's 7.5 is more restrained than its warmth of tone suggests, since it found the most specific technical problems. The literary and ordinary lenses' shared 8 is defensible for a book with genuine formal intelligence and a flawless final chapter.

The one score that doesn't survive scrutiny is the **ordinary reader's 8**. That lens correctly diagnoses the book's most significant emotional failure — "A nineteen-year-old died. I believe it. I did not feel it." — and then gives an 8. A book that fails to make the death at its centre felt should arguably lose more points from a reader who names that failure explicitly. The 8 reads like the score was locked before the critique was finished.

The **authenticity lens** overstates its strongest praise: "Munteanu's textual analysis is impressively credible as period-appropriate forensic work" and "never telegraphs its conclusions before earning them." Both claims are demonstrably false given what I found in the Munteanu chapters (see §4 and §5). The score of 7.5 is appropriate; the language is too generous for what the text actually contains.

## 2. Which claims are grounded, and which are padding?

**Grounded claims I checked and confirmed:**

*Literary lens, on Ilinca's bookkeeper idiom as structural necessity:* Verified. Chapter 9 is the clearest demonstration: the self-indictment delivered entirely in accounting register — "Lines one through eight: hers. Correct. Line nine: not hers. The error is in the being careful" — arrives not as moral pronouncement but as the completion of a column. The prose does exactly what the lens describes.

*Thriller lens, on the Ana Petrescu death-time contradiction:* Verified against both chapters. Chapter 4 gives admission at nineteen hundred hours and death at twenty-three hundred and twelve. Chapter 29's formal determination gives admission at 14:33 and death at 18:47. Both are stated as official file facts. They cannot both be correct. The ordinary lens adds a third layer: chapter 30 has Doina say Ana "died in the morning," which contradicts both timestamps — neither 23:12 nor 18:47 is morning. The ordinary lens correctly raises this but hedges it as possibly intentional unreliable narration. Given chapter 30's rhetorical structure ("the paper said *in the morning* and she died in the morning"), presented as Doina's assembled knowledge rather than marked uncertainty, this reads as an authorial error rather than a deliberate irony.

*Authenticity lens, on "He would go to Strada Crișan in the morning" in chapter 20:* Verified. Chapter 20 (dated November 24–25) has Munteanu note that "the address on Strada Crișan" has been in his notebook "for eleven days" — meaning since approximately November 13–14. Chapter 25 (December 1) is the Population Section visit that establishes the Strada Crișan connection. Chapter 27 confirms the sequence explicitly: "The address on Dorobanților that he had spent the morning of the twenty-ninth working had yielded a woman who had moved out in February; the population register had been what gave him Strada Crișan." The Population Section visit is December 1; chapter 20 is November 24–25; Munteanu cannot have had the Strada Crișan address for eleven days in chapter 20 from a visit that occurs six days after chapter 20. The diagnosis is correct and the synthesis's recommended fix (replace "Strada Crișan" with "Dorobanților" in chapter 20) is the right repair.

*All five "finish" lenses, on the middle plateau in chapters 11–17:* Verified by structure. The Ilinca chapters in this band (13, 15, 17) each follow the same rhythm: indirect relay contact, node confirmed, N4 branch deferred. Chapter 17 contains the genuine high-tension sentence ("She cannot see whether the shape at the left margin, in that light, was a bracket or a nine") and is essential. Chapters 13 and 15 are functionally redundant against each other.

*Authenticity lens, on the geography book birth-year inconsistency:* Partially verified. Chapter 2 says the book "was published in 1978, purchased for me by my mother when I was twelve." Chapter 19 (Ilinca reading the payroll register) establishes Doina's year of birth as 1965. Born in 1965, Doina turns twelve in 1977 and thirteen in 1978. The claim is technically possible if she was born late in 1965 and the purchase occurred early in 1978, but the authenticity lens is right to flag it as tight enough to read as an oversight.

*Literary lens, on the locker mechanism contradiction (partially):* Partially verified. Chapter 2 says "The factory locker locks. I am the only one with the key." Chapter 18 says "The locker was metal, key lock, three-digit code I had changed in September." A key lock and a three-digit code are different mechanisms. This is a genuine minor inconsistency. However, the literary lens also claims chapter 10 has "the ring in my coat pocket. Three keys" as evidence of contradiction. Chapter 10's actual text says "I had the key on my ring with the hostel key and my aunt's spare" — three keys on a ring, one of which is the locker key. This is consistent with chapter 2's "I am the only one with the key" (to the locker). The literary lens is wrong about chapter 10 being part of the contradiction; chapter 18's "three-digit code" is the real discrepancy.

**Padding I tested and found hollow:**

*Comparative lens verdict: "I cannot identify a published novel that uses the textual transmission of a forbidden document this way."* This is unfalsifiable as stated and therefore uncountable as evidence. It might be true; it might not be; the reviewer has no way to know.

*Authenticity lens on Munteanu: "impressively credible as period-appropriate forensic work... never telegraphs its conclusions before earning them."* Demonstrably overstated. See §4 and §5.

*Airport lens on the opening: "efficient without being schematic."* Vague enough to apply to almost any competent novel.

## 3. Did any reviewer fail to read the whole book?

No reviewer shows signs of not having finished. Chapter-specific citations are accurate throughout (all 32 quotes verified exact, and the chapter attributions I spot-checked hold), and later chapters — 24, 27, 28, 29, 30 — are cited with specificity by multiple lenses. The thriller lens's criticisms of the climax (chapters 28–29) are argued from direct evidence, not vagueness.

One partial exception: the **ordinary lens** cites the Dorobanților/Strada Crișan confusion (chapters 12, 16, 20, 25, 27) but summarises it as "eventually resolved but so implicitly that careful readers will be confused." This is accurate but understated — the lens may not have fully tracked the investigation timeline across chapters 16 and 20 or it would likely have identified the more specific error the authenticity lens found. Whether this is incomplete reading or a decision to stay at the reader-experience level rather than the continuity audit level is impossible to determine from the review alone.

## 4. Are any two reviews suspiciously similar?

**Genuine convergence:** All five "would finish" lenses locate the plateau in chapters 11–17 and name similar chapter ranges (11–17, 12–16, 13–17, 13–15, 13–17). They reach the same conclusion through different tracking mechanisms: the thriller lens tracks pace against a clock deadline; the airport lens tracks formula repetition in Munteanu's node-visit chapters; the authenticity lens tracks chapter-ending beat patterns; the literary lens counts consecutive Ilinca deferral chapters; the ordinary lens follows the relay-chain mechanism. Same problem, five different diagnostic routes. This is real corroboration.

**Suspicious similarity: the "each/every" misdiagnosis.** The comparative and airport lenses both conclude that N1's formal full-text reproductions contain a drafting error — "every" in line 4 should be "each" because Doina's arc requires that N1 say "each." Both lenses reach the same specific wrong conclusion by the same reasoning.

I checked this against all relevant chapters. Chapter 9 (Ilinca reads N1): line 4 says "If she sleeps, wake her every hour." Chapter 28 (Munteanu reads N1 and N4 side by side): both documents say "If she sleeps, wake her every hour." These are two independent reproductions of N1 in different POV chapters, both saying "every." Chapter 14 (Doina tests herself against her copy): her copy says "each hour," and she has to fight her instinct to say "every." Chapter 30 (Doina recites from memory): she starts to say "every," corrects to "each."

The reviewers' logic was: if Doina fights to remember "each," the original must say "each." This reasoning is backwards. If N1 says "every" and Doina's copy says "each," then Doina's natural linguistic pull toward "every" is explained by N1's wording — she's correcting herself toward her copy's variant, away from the original's phrasing. This is coherent and consistent across the text. The "every/each" difference between N1 and Doina's copy is not a drafting error; it is evidence that her branch drifted on a fourth line that Munteanu never tracked.

The literary lens diagnosed this correctly: Munteanu, who obsessively tracks the "by the bed"/"beside the bed" preposition and reconstructs the whole chain from textual variants, never notices the "every"/"each" difference between N1 and Doina's copy. That is the unresolved thread, not a production error.

The panel synthesis accepted the comparative and airport lenses' diagnosis uncritically and elevated it to a "must-fix" repair: "change 'every' to 'each' in line 4 of the chapter 9 and chapter 28 reproductions." This repair is wrong. If implemented, it would break the coherent reading: with N1 saying "each," Doina's instinct to say "every" becomes inexplicable (why would her default diverge from the original?), and Munteanu's failure to note the variant between N1 and her copy disappears as a narrative thread. **The synthesis's proposed fix would introduce a new inconsistency while erasing an intentional one.**

Two lenses selecting the same specific fragment and arriving at the same wrong diagnosis through the same flawed logic is not corroboration. It is two reviewers making the same interpretive error.

## 5. The single most damaging true thing the panel is collectively underweighting

The panel unanimously praises Munteanu's investigation as forensically precise. The authenticity lens: "impressively credible as period-appropriate forensic work." The ordinary lens: "genuinely exciting procedural work." The literary lens: "architecturally necessary" and "intelligent." No lens scrutinises the investigation timeline as carefully as the investigation's own premise demands.

I did. The Munteanu chapters have not one but three interlocking timeline problems that the panel treats as separate minor issues or misses entirely:

**First (identified by authenticity lens, confirmed): the premature Strada Crișan reference.** Chapter 20 (November 24–25) has Munteanu planning to visit Strada Crișan with an address he's had "for eleven days." The narrative does not establish how he has that address until chapter 25 (December 1, Population Section visit). This is confirmed by chapter 27, which retrospectively explains that his November 29 Dorobanților visit "had yielded a woman who had moved out in February" — he needed the Population Section to bridge Dorobanților to Strada Crișan. He cannot have had Strada Crișan from eleven days before chapter 20.

**Second (partially identified by thriller lens, but misframed): the Viorica Damian double interview.** Chapter 12 (November 15) shows Munteanu going to Damian's home apartment on Strada Mihai 14/B/8, introducing himself for the first time, collecting a factory-form copy from a tin on her kitchen shelf, and receiving the Moga Rodica/Dorobanților address. Chapter 16 (November 20) shows Munteanu going to the enterprise laundry on Strada Mihai, introducing himself again, and collecting a laundry-form copy — a second physical copy from the same person, at the same address cluster, five days later. Neither scene acknowledges the prior encounter.

The thriller lens calls both scenes "written as first encounters" and calls it a continuity error. This is accurate but doesn't capture the deeper problem: chapter 16 states that the Moga Rodica notebook entry was "eight days old, from the interview with Lucia Gal." But chapter 12 (five days before chapter 16, not eight) clearly shows Damian — not Gal — giving Munteanu that name for the first time. The "eight days" arithmetic would place the name in Munteanu's notebook on approximately November 12, before any interview shown in the text could have produced it.

**Third (missed by all reviewers): the chain-of-custody attribution conflict.** Because of the above, it is unclear who actually gave Munteanu the Moga Rodica name. Chapter 12 attributes it to Damian. Chapter 16 attributes it to Lucia Gal. These cannot both be true without a prior Lucia Gal interview (on approximately November 12, eight days before chapter 16) that the narrative does not show and which would make the chapter 12 Damian interview a second, not first, encounter with the name.

These three problems are not separate editorial footnotes. They suggest the Munteanu investigation chapters were drafted or revised in a sequence that left behind conflicting address-gathering timelines — a failure of the exact kind the novel is about. The irony is specific and structural: Munteanu is a man who reconstructs transmission chains by reading textual variants and dates; the novel's own chain of transmission from draft to final text has left its own variants and dating artifacts. The novel claims forensic precision as one of its core achievements. The Munteanu chapters, read consecutively with dates in hand, do not deliver it.

The panel praises the investigation because each chapter, read individually, is convincing. No reviewer traces the address-gathering across chapters 12, 16, 20, 25, and 27 in sequence with dates logged. The synthesis addresses the Strada Crișan problem in chapter 20 and notes the Dorobanților confusion as a "clarity" issue, but does not identify the double Damian interview or the attribution conflict in chapter 16. The prescribed fix (replace "Strada Crișan" with "Dorobanților" in chapter 20) is correct but insufficient. The chapter 16 attribution to Lucia Gal, and the second Damian encounter with no acknowledgment of the first, need to be examined and resolved as part of the same rewrite pass.

The panel inherited the novel's premise — that Munteanu's investigation is meticulous — and applied it without testing it. That is the premise the panel failed to check.
