# 15 November 1984

The two copies occupied the right side of his desk. He had placed them there at ten past seven, when he arrived, in the order he was building them: Lucia Gal's copy from the eleventh on the near right, behind it and slightly above the copy he had collected from Ana Petrescu's sister-in-law on the sixth, which had spent time rolled inside something and dried with a crease in its upper quarter. To the left of Lucia Gal's copy: a gap of clean desk. The heating in the investigations room had come on overnight but was not yet warm; the radiator behind his chair made a low sound and produced the smell of hot metal without producing much else. He was the only person in the room.

He had the referral from Lucia Gal in his working notebook, on the page that began with the date of the Gal interview: the eleventh, a Sunday, the house on the far side of the rail crossing. She had given him a first name — Viorica — and a location: the laundry, she had said, and he understood her to mean the enterprise laundry facility on Strada Mihai, which handled finished goods before dispatch and was listed in the enterprise directory as a subordinate operational unit. She had not known a surname. She had been clear about this, in the way he had begun to recognize as a consistent feature of each of the four interviews he had conducted since the sixth: an avoidance of last names, a precision about first names and approximate locations, the address described rather than given. The document moved through it without a list, by design.

He signed out a personnel access form at the front desk and put on his coat and went out.

---

The enterprise administrative building was a ten-minute walk from the station, down the main road past the market. He had made this walk three times since the sixth, for different sections each time. The woman at the front desk acknowledged him with a nod and did not speak.

The personnel section occupied the second floor, three rooms behind frosted glass. He knocked and a clerk admitted him — not the woman he had seen on the fourth or the eighth, but one he had not met before. He showed his identification and stated his requirement: a worker in the enterprise laundry facility, first name Viorica, surname not known. The clerk went to the file drawers. She did not ask why.

She returned with a single card and laid it on the counter without comment. He read it in full before writing anything down. Damian, Viorica. Born 1949, Turda district. Transferred from internal production services to the enterprise laundry facility in February 1982. Registered address: Strada Mihai 14, bloc B, apartment 8.

He wrote the address in his working notebook. He thanked her and went downstairs.

---

Strada Mihai was two blocks east of the main road, past the old market building and a shuttered bakery whose sign was still up. The street ended at a courtyard shared between three residential blocs. The enterprise laundry occupied the ground floor of the first, its designation stenciled on the door: SERVICIU SPĂLĂTORIE. The smell reached him before he reached the door — heated water, lye soap, and below those, the warm chemical undersmell of processed fiber. He did not go to the laundry door. He went to the residential entrance at the far end of the same bloc and pressed the bell for apartment eight.

He waited. He pressed it again.

The outer door opened from the inside after a longer interval. The woman who appeared in the frame had dressed in the approximate direction of readiness: a grey wool robe, belted at the waist, her hair compressed flat on one side from sleep. She was between thirty-five and forty, broad through the shoulders. She took in his coat and his identification card in the same brief glance.

He told her his name and rank. He said he was sorry to disturb her at this hour. He was conducting an investigation in connection with the Petrescu matter, which had been in the district paper on the ninth. He asked whether he might come in.

She had already moved back from the door.

---

The apartment had the cold of a bloc that ran on central heating and had not received it since sometime before six. The kitchen was standard: a two-ring electric burner, a pot of water beginning to heat on the nearer ring, a row of tins on the shelf above the stove. The window had begun to fog at the lower corners. She sat across from him at the table and folded her hands and waited.

He said he was looking into the distribution of a particular document in connection with the Petrescu matter, and that he had received information suggesting she might have had access to such a document. He asked whether she had received one, and whether she still had it.

He did not describe the document.

She stood and took a tin from the shelf — the second one from the left, with a printed floral pattern, the kind that had once held tea. She set it on the table in front of him and sat back down.

He opened the tin. Inside: a sheet of paper, folded twice into quarters, the fold lines worn smooth from some months in the tin. He unfolded it and spread it flat on the table.

The paper was a factory form, written on the back. The front side showed through: column headings and the word PRODUCȚIE reversed, a date he could read only partially. On the writing side: pencil, small and regular, the letters shaped by someone who had written carefully. Nine numbered lines.

He read from the top.

---

Lines one through five he had seen before, in proportions that varied by a word here, a punctuation mark there; he did not slow down.

Line six was different.

His copy — Lucia Gal's, which he had brought folded in his inside pocket — read: *Burn nothing until she is well. Wash and keep.*

This copy read: *Burn nothing at all until she is well. Wash and keep.*

Two additional words. He wrote both versions in his notebook, side by side, and did not yet mark the relationship between them.

Lines seven and eight were as expected.

Then line nine: *7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning.*

He set the factory-form sheet to the left and found the corresponding line on Lucia Gal's copy: *If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.*

He looked at both for a moment.

The line on the left was longer — thirty-five words, roughly. It contained a sentence at the end about not waiting. The line on the right was twelve words, and it ended with the phrase *in the morning* in a position he would not have predicted from reading the line on the left. One of these versions had preceded the other, or both had descended from a third version he had not yet seen. He could determine from the texts alone neither which had come first nor what the difference meant. He could determine only that the two versions diverged, and that the divergence had entered the chain at some point between these two copies, or in a copy between them that he had not collected.

He noted both versions in his notebook, verbatim, with a line between them.

The woman across the table had not moved. She was watching him the way people watched him when they had decided the outcome of the interview was fixed — not fixed in their favor, but fixed.

---

He asked where she had gotten the document.

Her sister, she said. Her sister had given it to her.

He asked when.

She considered. Late autumn of last year, she said. November. She thought November.

He asked whether her sister was in Dej.

She said yes.

He asked for an address.

There was a pause — not a long one, but deliberate. Then she said: Dorobanților. Behind the second bloc on Eminescu.

He asked for a name and a building number. She gave them: Moga, Rodica, bloc three on the right, apartment fourteen. He wrote them in his notebook.

He asked whether her sister still lived there, as far as she knew.

As far as she knew.

He thanked her. He folded the factory-form sheet into his inner pocket with Lucia Gal's copy and stood. She stood with him. He let himself out of the kitchen and down the hallway and through the outer door without her accompanying him.

---

The cold on Strada Mihai was the same cold as when he had arrived. He walked back to the station.

---

At his desk, he laid the three copies out in order from left to right. The factory-form sheet from Viorica Damian on the far left, its printed columns showing through from the reverse, the pencil text dark against it. Lucia Gal's copy in the center — ordinary paper, black ink, a handwriting that had taken its time. The Petrescu sister-in-law's copy at the far right, with its crease at the upper quarter and its right edge curling where the paper had been wet.

He opened his working notebook.

First observation: *Line nine of the Damian copy reads, verbatim: "7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning." Line nine of the Gal copy reads, verbatim: "If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning." The Damian copy contains substantially more text in line nine. The change in wording between these two copies represents the earliest point in the current sequence at which this change can be placed.*

Second observation: *Line six of the Damian copy reads "Burn nothing at all until she is well." Line six of the Gal copy reads "Burn nothing until she is well." Difference: the addition of "at all." Both versions are consistent with independent hand-copying from separate physical sources, not with copying from the same original.*

Third observation: *The Damian copy is written on the back of an enterprise production form. The header on the printed side reads PRODUCȚIE; the date is not fully legible. The copy is consistent with having been made in a context where blank paper was not available.*

He read these observations over. He was more careful about the dosar penal, which his captain at the inspectorate would eventually review and which had to contain only what he could demonstrate.

He opened the dosar penal to the evidence section and wrote: *Physical document collected from V.O. Damian, Strada Mihai 14/B/8, Dej, 15 November 1984. Document intact. Content consistent with document family under examination. Witness cooperative. See working notebook, 15 November, for textual observations.*

He closed the dosar.

He sat for a moment with the three copies in front of him.

He had not yet established how many copies there were in total, which meant he did not know how far he was from the beginning. A document that moved through a network of this kind, through named individuals in specific relationships, without a list anywhere — a document like this did not travel through twenty hands; it was too narrow, too particular, too bound to specific contexts of trust. He had been in every house he had visited in twelve days, and none of them had felt like the house of a woman who had received this paper from a stranger.

He had the address on Dorobanților.

One copy before the Damian copy was where the chain had come from. He did not know, yet, if that was the beginning of the chain or one step into a longer sequence. He suspected — without evidence, without anything he would write in the notebook — that it was close to the beginning.

He closed the working notebook and stood and put on his coat and went downstairs.

The afternoon was grey and still, the cold sitting in the valley without moving. He turned toward the main road and walked.
