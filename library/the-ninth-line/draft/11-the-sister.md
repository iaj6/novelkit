# 14 November 1984

She does the variance three times. The third time it comes back twelve lei.

The October third-shift payroll: seventeen line discrepancies, all resolved since Tuesday, and one aggregate variance of twelve lei that has had no explanation since she found it. She has been doing the rest of the column around it, leaving the line empty, returning after other work to see if the answer has appeared. It has not appeared each time she has returned. She marks the line with a pencil stroke and turns the page.

She will find it. It is twelve lei and it has been here since Tuesday and it will be there when she finds it.

What she is thinking about this morning is the N4 branch.

She made the copy on the third of November of the year before, in the coal cellar of a building she had been brought to by someone she trusted — a connection specific to that night, not a connection before and not a connection after. The woman from that night was waiting in the coal smell. Ilinca had the pencil. The light came in through the door from the courtyard; not enough light but enough to write by if you were fast. She had written the lines while saying each one aloud, and had watched the pencil to see if it was keeping up.

She can account for eight lines from that recitation.

*Burn nothing until she is well. Wash and keep.* Line six. She had said it clearly. The woman had written it without stopping. Line six is the one that appears to go against the line before it and does not; she had been deliberate with it in the dark because of this. She can account for line six the way she can account for each of the eight: said it, watched the pencil move, line complete.

What she cannot account for is the bracket. The ninth element. She had said it. She is almost certain the pencil was still moving when she said it. She cannot say with certainty that the bracket was in the right position on the page, or in any position at all, because it is a margin note and not a numbered line and the difference between those two things in the dark, with a pencil moving fast, is a difference she could not audit in real time.

She has been proceeding on the assumption that it was correct. She has treated the cellar copy as: one transaction, her end closed. Made the copy, gave it over, left. What came after was the woman from that night's management, not a column Ilinca had any reading of.

This morning she does not believe the column ends there.

The woman from that night is not a full stop. She received the paper. People who receive this paper, and who know people who might need it, do with it what it is for: they pass it on, when the time comes, to someone they know. Ilinca has been counting on this. It is how the paper gets where it needs to go. It is also how a branch extends past a single transaction, and she has been treating the cellar branch as one transaction and nothing after, and she does not now believe that is accurate.

What she does not know: whether the woman from that night gave a copy to anyone, and if so to whom. She cannot ask directly. The cellar woman is not part of her usual network; a visit to that part of the city would be its own kind of visibility, the kind she cannot afford. She needs a route.

---

She has one.

The pharmacy is on Eminescu, near the second bloc, fifteen minutes from the mill gate. The woman at the counter knows her from the market queue and the monthly aspirin requisition and the bus stop on Thursdays. They have spoken about the bread allocation. They have spoken about the flour, about the new forms, about the bus that does not run on Saturdays when it should. They have not spoken about anything that matters.

She has needed aspirin since Monday. This is the first mid-morning she has had free.

She tells Lenuta from accounts she will be back before eleven and goes out through the rear exit, past the clinic door, which is still locked at this hour.

---

The cold on the street outside is particular to this valley in November: not at the floor yet, but past the point where the body argues about it. The boulevard trees have been finished with their leaves for several days. The pavement is wet with something that happened earlier without reaching the level of rain. She keeps her pace at the pace of a woman going somewhere she goes regularly, which she is. The market stand at the corner is closed until Thursday; she passes it.

Dorobanților runs behind Eminescu and connects to it at the north end. She does not go down Dorobanților. She goes straight to Eminescu and turns toward the pharmacy.

The pharmacy occupies a narrow single-story building between the corner post office and a shoe repair. Inside: the counter, the shelves with the permitted stock behind it, a warmth that is not present on the street. The woman at the counter has glasses repaired with thread at the right hinge and a way of standing that suggests she has been standing in the same position for a long time and has found it sufficient.

She copies Ilinca's buletin number into the register while the identity document lies open between them. She produces the aspirin. Ilinca presses the requisition form flat with one finger and signs it.

She says, while tucking the identity document away: there is a woman I've been meaning to find. She gives a description — roughly her own age, a round face, a dark coat with a grey collar, the kind you see everywhere; adds: her husband worked at the chemical plant on the south road. I thought she lived somewhere near here, on Eminescu or behind it. Do you know the family?

The woman at the counter restocks a shelf while she thinks.

She says: there is a woman on Dorobanților — just behind the second bloc on Eminescu — who recently moved her sister in from the village. The sister works at the communal laundry on Strada Mihai. That's the closest I know to what you're describing.

She says: the husband at the chemical plant, that was the main thing I remembered about them.

The pharmacy woman says: I don't know about a husband. That family, that's all I know of them.

Ilinca thanks her. She says: perhaps it's not the right person. Don't trouble yourself.

She goes out.

Three items to carry: *sister, laundry, Strada Mihai.*

She takes the longer route back to the mill — eight minutes more, away from the bloc side of town — and arrives at her desk at twelve minutes past eleven.

---

She opens the November preliminary and starts the December headers. At six minutes past noon she finds the twelve lei: a digit misread in the October second-week line transfer, a three entered as an eight in the fourth column, a small error dated to the twenty-second. She corrects the entry and rules the line. The column closes.

Then she works. The afternoon documents come through: two sets of revised production tallies from the third shift, a ministry form that requires countersignature, an internal memo about the December heating schedule for the administrative building that requires no action but has to be filed. She processes them.

At half past two, through her window, she sees the delivery boy from the pharmacy cross the forecourt below with a cardboard box held level. He goes past the personnel door, past the door she is sitting behind, and turns toward the clinic two doors down.

He does not come to her. She did not expect him to.

At twenty minutes to three, Lenuta knocks and opens the door: the clinic nurse has stepped out to the toilet, can Ilinca sign for the pharmacy delivery.

Ilinca goes into the corridor.

The box sits on the shelf by the clinic door with the delivery notice folded flat on top. She lifts the notice and reads it and signs the incoming column in the delivery ledger; while she is doing this she takes from beneath the notice a slip of paper that she folds once and puts into the pocket of her coat; she carries the box the three steps to the clinic ledge and sets it inside and comes back and returns the delivery ledger to its hook.

Lenuta is already back at accounts. The door between the two rooms is closed.

Ilinca sits at her desk. She takes the slip from her coat pocket.

The pharmacy woman's handwriting: small, regular, not hurried. Three sentences about the December aspirin allocation and the new Ministry dispensing form taking effect in January, which all mill clinics will receive notice of separately. Below that, set a little apart, one sentence that is not the same kind of sentence as the others: *Regarding the family you were asking after — the sister is at the communal laundry on Strada Mihai, I am told, Tuesdays through Saturdays.*

She reads the last sentence three times.

Then she folds the slip and places it in the ledger between the October summary and the first November sheet, where it is a piece of paper among other pieces of paper, and she sits.

---

The cellar woman has a sister. The sister is in Dej. She works the laundry on Strada Mihai, Tuesdays through Saturdays.

This is not the same as knowing there is a copy at the laundry. A sister recently arrived from the village is not automatically a woman who has received a copy of a specific document from a specific source. But: the cellar woman received a paper she would not have requested for herself, because the emergency had been resolved by the time Ilinca brought the copy to her that night — resolved or past. She took the paper anyway. People take the paper for the reason people take anything useful that they cannot buy and cannot request through official channels: because someone they know may need it and they may need to give it.

She adds the new entry. Five, and now this: six.

The problem is the laundry. She has no route to Strada Mihai. The laundry is not on her circuit; she has no contact there; a visit without a reason is the same problem as Eminescu. She needs a second route, the way she needed the pharmacy for the first. She does not have one yet. This is a problem with a solution she has not found.

She reopens the December forecast and begins the first column.

---

The shift bell rings at half past three. The building clears in its usual sequence. Accounts first: she hears Lenuta and the two junior staff go down the corridor, the familiar pattern of their conversation about the Victoriei bread shop, which is closed on Tuesdays apparently. Then the personnel office, two women who have worked there since before Ilinca arrived and who leave without speaking to anyone on the way out, which is their habit and not unfriendliness. Then, at a quarter to four, the two senior clerks at the far end of the corridor, whose footsteps she has learned to distinguish without intending to: the one with the uneven heel first, then the other.

She stays at her desk.

Her office lamp is the only one burning in this part of the building. The lamp at the end of the corridor went off when Lenuta left. The clinic door lamp has been off since three. Through the window: the forecourt is dark except for the gate lamp, and beyond it the street, and beyond that the production building with its row of small high windows lit orange from inside. From three buildings over she can hear the frequency of the night shift at full run — not individual machines but the aggregate of them, the forty-eight looms at their register.

She still needs the route to Strada Mihai. She has six nodes and a gap she cannot close from her desk.

The December first column is not going to finish itself.

She bends over the ledger and keeps working.
