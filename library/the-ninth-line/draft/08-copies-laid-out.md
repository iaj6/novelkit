# 12 November 1984

He had left the two copies on his desk the previous evening, spread flat and held at their corners by the evidence folder, and when he came in Monday morning they were as he had left them.

He took off his coat and hung it on the back of the door. Sandu's desk at the far end of the investigations room was empty; Sandu was in the field on the Cuzdrioara registration matter and would not be in until afternoon. The coal stove in the corner had been lit by whoever was on overnight duty and was burning with the damp smell of lignite that never quite cleared in November, which meant it had been going long enough to take the edge off the cold without quite producing warmth. He moved the folder to the left side of the desk and sat down.

He had read both copies on Thursday — the day he had collected them — and again on Friday before the weekend. He knew what they said. What he was doing now was not reading for content. He was reading the way he had learned, over twelve years, to read a statement taken from two different people about the same event: not for what the words said but for where the words diverged, and whether the divergence was something that would have happened accidentally or only by design.

He was not a graphologist. He was a man who had spent twelve years reading statements.

Line one of the first copy: *Keep her warm and lying down. Someone stays with her the whole night.* Small careful hand. The o in 'lying' and the o in 'whole' pressed closed by a pen held at a narrow angle.

Line one of the second copy: the same line, the same words. Different hand. Larger letters, a slight rightward lean, the downstrokes of the l and the y in 'lying' longer than was strictly necessary, as though the writer had thought they were about to run out of room and then discovered they had more than enough.

He went line by line.

Lines one through eight: close to identical. He noted where they were not. Line three on the first copy: *Count the cloths. Write the number and the hour on the paper by the bed.* Line three on the second copy: the same sentence, until the last four words — *on the paper beside the bed.* He looked at the two phrases. The instruction was the same. The prepositions were not. Someone had written *beside* where the other document, or its source, said *by.* That was either a copying error — the copyist's own preposition substituting itself for the original's — or it was a variation already present in whatever the second copy had been made from.

He had no way to know yet which of these was true.

He turned to the back page of his cardboard folder, which served as his case notes, and wrote below Saturday's entry: *L3 variant: 'by the bed' / 'beside the bed'. Two hands, two sheets, different paper stocks. L9: identical — see below.*

Line nine was the same on both copies. He read it on the first document — *If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning* — and then on the second, the words arriving in the same order, set down in a different hand on different paper, but unchanged: *If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.* He held the two sheets up briefly, each in one hand, and read the ninth line again on each.

A woman who had stayed until morning had been following a document that told her to wait for morning. What he was looking at now was something additional: the instruction had not entered the chain at the point where the first copy had passed to the Petrescu household. It was present in the neighbor's copy as well, which was a separate document obtained separately from a separate address. If the instruction was already in both copies, and both copies had been made by different people from whatever they had each received, then the instruction had been present in what they had each received. The instruction was earlier.

He closed the folder.

---

The address he needed was a three-story bloc on Alecsandri street, east of the mill workers' market. Three hundred metres. He walked.

November had gone cold and wet in the last week, the kind of damp that came off the Someș in the morning and did not lift before noon. The market was open but thin — mid-month, the queues had already moved through whatever there was to move through. A woman at the corner stand near the market entrance had something under an oil cloth. She watched him come up the street and watched him go past without moving. He did not stop. He counted the doorways until he had the right building.

Interior stair. The third floor smelled of cabbage soup and wet wool. He knocked at the indicated door.

The woman who answered had the expression of someone who had been expecting a particular knock for some number of days and had made a decision about how she would receive it. She was in her late forties, heavy-shouldered, her hair pinned close to her head, wearing a house coat. She was holding a dish cloth. When she looked at him she folded the cloth in half, and then in half again, until she had a square she had nowhere particular to put.

He introduced himself. He said he was investigating the death of Ana Petrescu and that he had questions about a document she might be able to help with.

She said: come in.

The flat was a kitchen and one other room. The kitchen had a table in the centre and two cups on it, which she moved to the counter without comment. She put the folded dish cloth on the counter beside them and sat down across from him at the table.

Her name was Lucia Gal. She worked the day shift in the second weaving hall at the AFE mill. She had worked there since 1972.

He told her: he was aware that a document of a certain kind had been circulating in the area. That he was trying to understand how it had moved. That if she had such a document and was willing to give it to him now, that cooperation would be noted in the file. Voluntary surrender was better than the alternative. He said this simply, without emphasis on the word 'alternative,' because emphasis was the thing that made people stop cooperating. He had learned this.

She got up and went to the counter. Second drawer from the left. She took out a folded sheet, carried it to the table, and set it in front of him.

He put on his gloves before he picked it up.

The paper was different from the other two copies. It was lighter, greyer, the stock of a mill production form — the front side carried official printing, department designation and output-tracking columns and a foreman's signature box, in the regulation black ink. On the reverse: the document, written in blue pencil. He had not seen blue pencil before on any version of this document. The handwriting was careful — more careful than either of the copies he had collected on Thursday, the numerals at the start of each line formed with a deliberateness that was close to elegance, the lines spaced evenly from top to bottom of the sheet.

He put it in an evidence envelope and wrote her name and address on the flap.

He asked: had she given a copy of this document to anyone else?

She said: no. She held his eyes when she said it. He wrote *denies further transmission* in his case notes.

He asked: where had she received this document?

She said: from a woman she worked with, on the production floor, in the second hall. She gave him the name without prompting. He wrote it. He wrote the hall number and the shift.

He asked: and did the woman she named live in this building, or elsewhere?

She gave him the address. He wrote it.

He thanked her. She held the door.

---

He had three copies.

He laid them side by side on his desk and weighted the corners with whatever was nearest, which turned out to be a stapler and three unopened tins of shoe polish. He read each copy through separately first, going from line one to line nine. Read separately, they said nearly the same thing. He read them again together, line by line across all three sheets.

He put the grey mill-form paper on the left, the two Thursday copies on the right, and went from the top.

Lines one through eight: close to identical across all three, with the same minor scribal variations he had already noted. Line three was the exception. The grey-paper copy said *by the bed*. The first of the Thursday copies said *by the bed*. The second Thursday copy said *beside the bed.*

He stopped.

Two of his three copies agreed in line three. The third differed from both of them by one word. He marked the positions in his notes. It was a small difference — a preposition, nothing more, the instruction unchanged — but it told him something about the arrangement. Two copies that agreed with each other in a particular line were either copied from each other or copied from the same source. A third copy that differed from both of them had either introduced the variation itself or had inherited it from a different source. He was looking at two copies that agreed, and one that did not. The two that agreed were probably closer to each other in the chain.

Line nine he read last, going left to right across all three sheets. It was the same on all three: *If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.* He had taken his pen up when he started and he set it back down without writing anything.

The instruction had been present in the grey-paper copy, which was a step earlier in the chain than the two Thursday copies. The grey-paper copy was the one the woman in the flat on Alecsandri street had given him — the woman who had named a woman on the production floor as the person she had got it from. If the instruction was already present in the grey-paper copy, it had not been introduced by Lucia Gal. It had been there when she received it. It had been present before the grey-paper copy was made.

He looked at the three sheets again. The grey-paper copy and the first Thursday copy agreed in line three — *by the bed*. The second Thursday copy said *beside the bed.* That meant the second Thursday copy had probably been copied from the grey-paper copy or from something that had already introduced *beside* — but either way, the grey-paper copy was probably the oldest document in front of him, or closest to the oldest. And the oldest document in front of him already had the morning instruction in line nine.

He turned to the fresh page at the back of the cardboard folder and wrote: *Three copies in evidence. L3 variant: 'by the bed' (x2) / 'beside the bed' (x1). L9 consistent across all three: 'take her in in the morning.' Instruction not introduced at points of collection. Present at minimum in copy acquired today (earlier in chain). Source is upstream. Next: [the name from the Gal interview].*

It was the kind of finding that would not survive being described to his captain in its present form. Captain Niculescu had looked at the file on Wednesday and asked when Munteanu expected to have something writable. He had said: by the end of the week. That had been a realistic estimate at the time. He had thought, on Wednesday, that the chain would be short — three, maybe four people, the kind of small informal thing that didn't go far and died at the edges for want of someone to carry it. The instruction in line nine had been present before the three copies he held. Before the woman on Alecsandri street, before the woman on Strada Gării, before the Petrescu household. The chain behind it was longer than three people.

He was going to have more names in this file before he was done.

He stood and took his coat from the back of the door.

The name from the Gal interview was the production-floor woman who had given Lucia Gal her copy. One step further back. One step closer to wherever the instruction had entered. She worked the same hall, the day shift. She had not been expecting him this morning, because he had not known her name this morning. Now he did.

He put on his coat and picked up his case notes.
