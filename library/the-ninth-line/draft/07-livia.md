# 10 November 1984

The November payroll forms are in the wooden tray beside the secretary's elbow, folded in half the way they come from the print room. She takes them without sitting down.

Pântelimon says: there has been some unpleasantness with the Petrescu matter. A Miliția officer has been speaking with people.

He says it as a statement in an open room. The outer door of the office is propped; two women from the personnel section are at their desks with their coats still on. He is watching Ilinca's hands.

She folds the forms once more and tucks them under her arm. A tragic business, she says. She is glad the family has someone looking into it.

She goes back to her office.

---

She closes the door and puts the payroll forms on the left side of the desk, beside the October figures. She does not open them yet.

An officer has been speaking with people. The secretary told her this in the tone of a man who has information and would like to exchange it. She gave him nothing except the obvious, which is the kind of exchange that costs her nothing and may give him something to think about later, if he is the type who thinks about things later. She does not know what type he is.

He has not come to the administrative building. If she were a name in the file, someone would have been at her door by now. The administrative building is not a production section and it is not a residential bloc. It sits on the other side of the main gate, which is a distance that means something, practically speaking, about whose names come first.

A man working outward from Ana Petrescu's house works from the sister-in-law, then from whoever the sister-in-law named, then outward from there. That is how it goes. There is a sequence to these things and she is not at the front of the sequence.

She opens the middle drawer and takes out the October summary sheet and places it to the right of the payroll forms. She does not look at either of them.

She has time. Not an abundance of it. Enough to do the next thing.

---

At nine she takes the materials-usage report from the filing cabinet behind the door. The October comparison columns for Hall B show a discrepancy on the last week of the month: the production log records sixty-eight kilograms of raw fiber allocation, but the stores ledger shows seventy-two kilograms issued over the same period. Four kilograms. Small enough to be a transcription error; large enough that it cannot stay in the month-end summary without an explanation and a countersignature.

She needs Georgeta for this.

This is the difficulty and also the solution to the difficulty. It is the second time in a week she will have occasion to speak with Georgeta. Once in a week is reasonable. Twice is one more than she prefers, for a woman who has always been careful about how often she appears in the same place as the same person. But the discrepancy is real. The stores ledger is here in her hand. She needs a production supervisor's countersignature before the month-end summary can go to the director's office, and Georgeta is the supervisor for Hall B. She has used worse reasons than this.

She writes the discrepancy figures on a separate sheet: *issued, 72 kg; production log, 68 kg; variance, 4 kg.* She clips the sheet to the October comparison figures and puts both in her document folder.

She waits until ten. She does not look at the clock between nine and ten.

---

At ten she goes to the supplies corridor.

Georgeta is already there. She is doing the quarterly inventory, which she does on the tenth of every month — Ilinca has countersigned the inventory sheets since 1978 and knows the schedule. Georgeta's clipboard is in her right hand; she is making ticks with a pencil, working along the shelves from the door end.

Ilinca sets her folder on the table near the window end and opens it to the October comparison sheet.

A materials discrepancy, she says. Hall B, last week of October. Seventy-two kilograms issued against sixty-eight in the production log. I need to know before the month-end summary which figure is correct, or whether there is a stores entry that explains the difference.

Georgeta does not stop her ticks. She finishes the shelf she is on. She sets the clipboard against the wall and comes to the table.

She looks at the comparison sheet. The last week of October, she says. We had two days of reduced intake on the spooling machines in the second bay. Some of the raw fiber arrived late. I logged it in the first week of November to keep the production records clean. If stores logged it in October because the delivery arrived in October, that is where your four kilograms are.

Ilinca writes this down. She says: I will need the delivery receipt to confirm the date. Can you get me the November intake log for the first four days?

By afternoon, Georgeta says. The materials office will have the receipt; I will send someone.

Ilinca closes the folder and clips the note inside it. She says: that should close it for the month-end summary. Then, without a pause that could be read as a pause: and how is Livia keeping?

Georgeta's pencil is in her hand. She does not tap it against anything.

Her daughter stopped by on Tuesday, she says. She says Livia is fine. Quiet. She's been at home, not seeing people.

Ilinca says: hasn't given anything to anyone?

Georgeta shakes her head, once, as if she is agreeing that the delivery receipt will settle the discrepancy.

No, she says. She kept it to herself.

Good. Ilinca picks up her folder. I'll have the countersignature form ready when the intake log comes. If you send it by two, we can close this before Friday.

She goes back to her office.

---

She closes the door. She sits in the chair.

N2 branch: Georgeta. From Georgeta to Livia — Georgeta's neighbor on her street. Livia gave the copy to nobody. She has been at home, not seeing people. No Miliția contact; Georgeta's daughter went by on Tuesday and she was fine. The branch runs from the master, then to Georgeta, then to Livia, and there it stops. A dead end is what the branch was designed to be; not every copy was meant to go further. Livia had kept it to herself for more than a year and had not used it and had not passed it and was now sitting quietly in her apartment while a man with a notepad asked questions on streets that were not her street. The branch contains no one who is in a file.

*No visitors. No telephone. If anyone asks, she has the influenza.*

She has followed this herself. The cousin, the borrowed pot, the materials discrepancy. She has not made a telephone call she did not need. She has not opened her door to anyone who was not expected. What she has done in the past week to close her accounting of the branches — the routes she took, the words she used, the questions she turned sideways — are all consistent with the instruction on line five. She knows line five the way she knows all of them. She wrote it, which is not the same as reading it: writing a thing is choosing every word separately, and she chose those words and has not needed to reconsider them.

The N3 branch is confirmed through Elena Florescu. The N2 branch closes now, with Livia. What remains is the cellar copy — the third copy she made from the master, on November third of last year, in the coal cellar behind Strada Dorobanților, by the light of the door, in ten minutes, for a woman who could not wait for a better time. She has gone over it. She knows what she wrote. She drew the bracket — two strokes and a corner — and she wrote the words inside it, and the words were the words from the master. She is certain of this the way she is certain of her own handwriting.

Four nodes in her private account. The master. The N2 branch, now closed and clean. The N3 branch, closed and clean. The cellar copy, which she holds as correct. Four entries. She cannot write any of this anywhere. She cannot write the count or the confirmation or the four-kilogram discrepancy in the same column. The private account has no ledger. It exists in the hour between nine and ten when she does not look at the clock.

The man asking questions in the production sections will work outward from what he has and will eventually reach what exists to be reached. What exists to be reached, on the N2 branch, is Livia, who is fine and has kept herself quiet and has given nothing to anyone. That branch is closed. If he reaches it, it dead-ends.

She opens the payroll forms.

---

The November columns are blank. She fills them from the October summary, adjusted for the two new employees in Hall C and the reduced hours from the repair week. She uncaps her pen and writes the first entry at the top of the column. She checks the form header. Alphabetical, surname-first, which has been the format since October per the new directive from the financial office. The entry is in the right place.

The numbers go in one column at a time. Gross hours, deductions, net. She runs the figures by hand first, then checks the tape. The tape agrees with her column for the first twelve entries and disagrees by three lei on the thirteenth. She finds the error: a transposition in the gross hours, twenty-two where the source sheet says twenty-two point five. She corrects it with a single line through the wrong figure and the right figure beside it.

She fills the column to the end of the first page. She turns to the second.

She leans back in the chair. The chair has a creak at the back joint that she has meant to report to the maintenance office since spring. She has not reported it. A creak is not a problem; it is the kind of sound that stops being heard after the first week, the kind that only a person paying close attention to her office and her habits would notice in the middle of a morning.

She sits forward and puts both hands flat on the desk.

The delivery receipt will arrive by two.

She uncaps the pen and pulls the October comparison sheet back into the light and reads the figures again from the beginning. The thing you miss is always in the line you read first, when your eye has not yet learned what it is looking for. She has learned this about herself over fifteen years and she does not argue with it.

The lamp is off. She has not needed it since the fog cleared.
