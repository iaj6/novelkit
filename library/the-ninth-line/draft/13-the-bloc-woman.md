# 16 noiembrie

The pharmacy note is in the pen-tray. She has had it since Wednesday afternoon and the words have not changed.

She takes a strip from the supply-request block in the bottom drawer — the right color, the right administrative weight, a form that is a form and not a message to anyone who does not know the mill's internal numbering. In the header she writes the date and a reference number she will not log. Below the header she writes twelve words. The twelve words ask the cousin's husband to mention, at the morning delivery, that his cousin's wife sends her regards.

She reads it back. The twelve words say nothing they cannot say. She seals the strip with the gum from a requisition envelope, folds it to form a packet the size of a ration card, and puts it in her coat pocket.

---

Her second cousin's husband delivers bread from the bakery on Strada Lungă to the laundry on Strada Mihai on weekday mornings. He does this first stop, before the market district. She has known this fact for four years and it has been in the inventory — the account she keeps of what she already holds, in the same memory where she keeps the names she does not write down and the routes she has not needed until they were needed. Last night, while the November overhead forecast would not close and the kitchen lamp made its forty-watt circle on the table, she went through the inventory and found him.

She has spoken to him twice: the christening in 1982, the name-day in April. He is not a contact. A fact in the inventory is not the same thing as a contact. A fact becomes a route when the arithmetic makes it one, and the arithmetic made it one last night, when she was doing the forecast and not doing the forecast.

She cannot telephone him. A telephone is a risk that moves in both directions: her voice going out and whatever is beside the wire coming in. She will not ask the pharmacy acquaintance again — one contact in three days on the same subject is already at the edge of visibility, and the edge is where she does not go. The bread delivery has no visible shape. It is a delivery that already happens every weekday morning, and she is adding a greeting to a delivery that already exists. A greeting is not a question.

---

The delivery boy is at the mill gate at nine o'clock. She goes down the first-floor corridor to the window and catches him before he crosses the yard. She passes the folded strip through the gap, says the bakery name and the cousin's name, says the delivery is for the laundry on Strada Mihai, and does not say anything else. He puts the strip in his jacket without asking about it. He is fifteen and he knows how to take things to where they are addressed.

She goes back upstairs. The November payroll has eight sections and a summary line.

---

The first section is the administration staff: seven people, wages unchanged since March, no entries to flag. She works the adding machine from left to right across the columns — the machine is loud in the cold, because the administrative wing has no heat before six o'clock and the mechanisms contract overnight. She records the subtotals in the left column and the totals in the right and carries each figure forward. The spool of paper accumulates at the back of the machine, a pale strip beginning to curl at the end.

The second section is the maintenance crew. Eleven people, one day's overtime from the October emergency repair to the weaving-hall pipe. The overtime is flagged and cross-referenced and correct.

Somewhere in the third section she thinks about the cellar.

Not by deciding to. She is entering the foreman wages — three people, each with a base wage and an adjustment column, the same adjustment it has been for eighteen months — and the familiarity of the entries releases her attention, the way an easy column sometimes does. She is in the cellar on Strada Crișan, November 1983, and she is copying.

Line by line she wrote it. Each line in order. Line eight: *A fever that will not come down by morning is too long.* The line is hers; the handwriting is hers; the number in front of it is hers. And below the eighth line she drew a bracket, because the ninth element was not a step in the order of the other steps — it was the element that governed all the others, the thing you had to know regardless of where you were in the list, and the bracket was the signal: this is different from what is above it. A bracket is not a numbered item. A bracket is a notation that sits outside the sequence and says: this is what you do when the sequence is not enough.

She drew the bracket in the original in October 1983. She is certain she drew one in the cellar copy also. Two strokes and a corner, the corner closing the bracket from below, because a bracket without a corner is three lines going sideways and not a bracket at all. She is almost certain she closed the corner. The light was the gap in the cellar door, six centimetres, and she was moving, and she checked the copy once before she folded it and handed it across. Line by line she checked it. She is almost certain the corner was there.

The adding machine takes the next entry. She enters it.

---

Around eleven o'clock Pântelimon knocks and opens the door in one motion, the way he does when he has already decided what he is going to say. He wants the October materials assessment. It is in the stamped folder on the second shelf, which is where stamped folders go, and she tells him this without looking up. He comes in, takes the folder, confirms it is stamped, says something about the cadres office wanting it this morning. She says: it has been there since the twenty-second. He says: ah, and leaves.

The door closes. The creak of her chair when she turns to confirm he has taken the right folder is the creak it has had since spring — the back joint, which she has meant to report to the maintenance office since May and has not.

She turns back to the payroll. The third section is closed. She opens the fourth.

---

At one o'clock she has five sections complete. She goes to the bathroom at the end of the corridor — the one bulb, the four sinks, the window painted shut above the fiber drying unit — and washes her hands under cold water, because the hot tap in this bathroom has not had pressure since October. She comes back. The desk is where she left it.

She has been running the N4 branch in the account parallel to the payroll all morning. This is not something she can stop; it is the other column, the one that has no paper form and no official header, and it runs beside whatever she is doing. 

What she knows about the branch: the master, which she holds. The cellar copy, which she made and gave and has not seen since the morning it went into a coat pocket on Strada Crișan. The sister's copy — confirmed existing three days ago through the pharmacy contact — in the possession of a woman at the laundry on Strada Mihai, a woman she has not seen and whose name she does not hold. Below the sister's copy: nothing, until today.

What she does not know: whether the sister's copy carried the bracket forward. Whether the corner she believes she drew in the cellar was correctly drawn. Whether the copy that went from the cellar to the woman to the woman's sister to the laundry on Strada Mihai has the same bracket as the one in the ledger spine on the second shelf of her office, or whether something changed. She cannot answer this without the physical objects. She does not have the physical objects.

She enters the sixth section of the payroll.

---

At four twenty the delivery boy is at the corridor window. He passes something through the gap — a torn margin from a bread delivery sheet, the bakery stamp in the corner. The note on the back is two sentences from the cousin's husband.

She reads it in the corridor.

The laundry woman had said almost nothing. She'd been between loads. She'd had a few minutes and she'd been careful. She had not given the neighbor's name to the bread man.

That was all.

Ilinca goes back to her desk. She puts the bread slip in the pen-tray beside the pharmacy note and reads it again.

*Last spring. A neighbor on her bloc. A woman with a small child.*

Last spring is April or May. Five or six months ago. The neighbor has a child, which is a household fact, which places her in one kind of life and not another. The bloc on Strada Mihai is the laundry building or an adjacent one — the laundry woman said *her* bloc, which would mean the one she lives in. A name would make this an entry that could be located. She does not have the name. The name is in the building register on Strada Mihai, which she cannot reach from here without a door and a reason to knock, and she is not the person who knocks on doors.

She puts the bread slip face-down in the pen-tray.

What she has is a node: a seventh entry in the account. A woman with a child who received a copy from the laundry woman last spring, on the same bloc as the laundry, whose name she does not hold.

The branch from the master: four nodes. The master itself, which she holds. The cellar copy, which she made. The sister's copy. The neighbor's copy. She holds one of these. She has confirmed the existence of the other three from outside, without ever touching them — the way you verify an account from the summary line without opening the individual entries. It is what she has.

She does not know what the neighbor's copy carries. She does not know if the neighbor made further copies. She is tracing forward and forward has no floor she can reach from here without a name and an address.

She opens the seventh section of the payroll.

---

She finishes at five forty. The summary line is correct. She checks the total twice, carries it to the summary sheet, and closes the payroll ledger. She puts it in the correct position on the left side of the shelf — the county finance office expects it before Friday, it will go out Thursday by the transport runner, she will confirm receipt on Friday. She marks the folder tab with the date. The procedure is the procedure.

She puts on her coat and does not take anything from the pen-tray.

---

She takes the long way home. There is a stretch on the short route without streetlights between the market junction and Bulevardul Victoriei, and November is dark before six, and this is a sufficient reason to walk the long way, and the long way is the way she walks.

The long way takes her past the end of Strada Mihai. She goes through the main road junction, past the old market hall with its padlocked shutters, down the side street with the cobblestones that have not been reset since the spring flood, past the corner where the pharmacy has been closed since August. The cold has settled the way it does by mid-November — not sharp, just final, the air's decision to stay at this temperature from now until it decides otherwise. She keeps her hands in her pockets and walks.

At the corner before the turning she stops.

Strada Mihai runs straight from here. The street is empty at this hour — the evening shift has started, the day shift has been home for an hour, the cold has moved everyone else indoors. At the far end of the street, at the ground floor of the first bloc, the laundry window is lit. A yellow square.

She stands at the corner. The laundry is lit and someone is still in there, working, at an hour that is later than the laundry's hours as she understands them from the pharmacy note.

She does not walk down Strada Mihai.

She looks at the yellow window for the time it takes to decide to keep walking, and then she turns at the corner and takes the parallel street one block over, and the laundry and the lit window are behind her.

---

She is home by six twenty. The stairwell light is off — the superintendent has not turned it back on yet. She goes up in the dark by the shape of the stairs, which she knows by the count and the lean of the banister.

In the kitchen she keeps her coat on. The forty-watt lamp is already on — she left it this morning, as she does in November, because she would otherwise arrive home to dark. The circle of light falls on the table and on her hands when she sets them flat on the surface and on the edge of the empty pen-tray. She brought nothing. The pharmacy note and the bread slip are in the pen-tray at her office desk. The payroll ledger is in its position on the shelf. The master is in the spine of the 1983 ledger on the second shelf, folded twice, where it has been since October of last year. She does not need to take it out to hold what she knows.

Four nodes on the N4 branch. The neighbor's name is not in anything she holds. Below the seventh node the branch goes somewhere she cannot trace from here.

She closes her eyes. She does not reach for the pull of the second lamp socket.
