# 18 noiembrie

The two women are at the table three seats down, and the one facing Ilinca is saying *Strada Mihai*.

Ilinca does not look up from the payroll summary. October, third section — fifty-two names in the roster column, four absences in the column to the left, dates in the correct format, certification status noted beside each. The pen moves to the next entry.

The woman facing her is from Hall B, the adjacent section. She has a green wool scarf she has worn every November Ilinca can remember; the scarf is knotted at the throat and slightly felted at the knot from repeated washing. The woman with her back to Ilinca has short grey hair, cut close, and she is not someone Ilinca knows by name. The canteen smells of boiled potato and beneath it the mill's own smell — hot-wet synthetic fiber, the close sour warmth that does not leave a building this size even in the hours when the machinery has stopped.

The green-scarf woman is talking about a friend. The friend lives near the laundry on Strada Mihai — the enterprise laundry, the ground floor of bloc 1 at the end of the street. Someone from the investigations office came to the building last week. The neighbor on the friend's bloc, a woman with a young child, third floor — she was the one they asked for. She went in on Thursday. She came home and made supper and said nothing to anyone.

*Thursday.* Thursday was the fifteenth. Day twelve.

Ilinca writes: *Absent. 4–18.X.84. Medical cert. attached.* The pen marks the column.

Viorica Damian's bloc is at the end of Strada Mihai. The neighbor on Viorica Damian's bloc, the woman with the child: she had confirmed this node through the bread relay on Day 13 — no name, just the existence of it, a woman who had asked for the copy and gotten it last spring. A G4 on the N4 branch. Munteanu has now gotten the name. Munteanu has been to the bloc neighbor and brought her in for questions.

He is in G4.

One step from Viorica Damian — the G3, the cellar woman's sister. Two steps from the cellar copy itself.

The grey-haired woman with her back to Ilinca asks something that the sound of trays on the serving ledge covers almost entirely. The green-scarf woman says: the neighbor came home pale. She made cabbage. She went to bed before eight.

Ilinca finishes the entry for absence four, draws the line beneath the third-section totals, collects the sheets, clips them with the correct clip, and sets them on the return tray. She carries the tray to the service window and slides it through the slot. The corridor back to the administrative wing is colder than the canteen; someone has left the door at the fire stair open. She can see the grey November courtyard through the gap. She closes the door as she passes.

The heating pipe is running today. She can hear it at the corner join — a low, intermittent sound, the heat finding its way through.

---

At her desk she opens the Q4 materials ledger to the page she was working before the canteen break, then does not continue it immediately.

She looks at the second shelf. The 1983 ledger sits between the quarterly labour plan compliance records and the annual balance submission, where it has been since she placed it there fourteen months ago. She does not take it out.

The bloc neighbor is now a contacted node — whatever she knows and whatever she said are in a room Ilinca cannot enter, in a file she has no way to read. Munteanu does not have the G3, not yet. He does not have the cellar copy. He is two steps from N4.

She returns to the Q4 materials ledger and writes the next line.

---

Marta's youngest daughter is eleven years old. On Tuesdays and Thursdays she walks from the school building on Strada Crișan to the tram stop at Piața Bobâlna, leaving just before one o'clock. The route passes the far end of Strada Avântului. Ilinca has been tracking this since the last week of October — timed it on a Thursday afternoon from the entrance of her building, nothing in her hands and no reason anyone would note.

At five minutes to one she is at the building door. The November sky has held the same flat grey since morning: no wind, no break in the cloud, the daylight the colour of a corridor with one working bulb. The cold when the door opens is direct. At one minute to one the daughter comes around the corner from the school direction: coat buttoned, bag over one shoulder, the dark braid Marta has been putting her hair in since she was small. She does not break stride.

Ilinca says: *Just checking that Elena is keeping the influenza quiet. Not coming in until she's well. Let us know if she needs anything.*

The daughter passes without turning. She goes around the corner toward the tram.

Ilinca goes back inside and upstairs to the administrative wing. She sits at her desk and opens the November payroll for the fourth section and writes the first name.

---

At eleven minutes past two the daughter comes back along the return route. She passes the building entrance at her usual pace and her voice carries close enough to reach through the open door:

*She says she's fine. She's been careful. And — there's a woman in the second stairwell, third floor, in her bloc. Has one. Not from Elena — from somewhere else. The woman has been asking around. About the questions.*

She is at the corner before the last word settles. Ilinca has the door handle in one hand and the cold of the stairwell at her back.

She goes upstairs. She sits at her desk. She looks at the fourth-section payroll for a moment, then folds it to the next page and continues from where she was.

---

A G4 on the N3 branch. A woman in Elena's bloc, second stairwell, third floor. She received her copy from an intermediate source — a G3 on the N3 branch that Ilinca has no name for, someone between Elena and this woman in the building Elena has lived in for six years. The N3 branch extends past Elena through a generation she cannot see, to a node in Elena's own building that Elena did not give to her.

The woman in the second stairwell has been asking around — which means she knows something has started, and she wants to understand how close it has come. Ilinca cannot reach her. She has a stairwell and a floor.

She counts: node eight.

Eight nodes held. Munteanu: three. The two counts have been moving in the same direction all month. She does not close the gap by adding a node; she only describes the shape of the thing that is larger than she thought.

She finishes the fourth-section payroll at a quarter past five, files it, collects her coat from the hook behind the door, and walks the fifteen minutes home through the November dark.

---

The lamp by the kitchen table is the forty-watt bulb on the extension cord. She has moved it so the light falls on the table rather than the counter, but at forty watts a cord's length makes a circle with edges and the edges are not far from the centre. Beyond the circle: the refrigerator, the window, the counter with the plate she rinsed when she came in and left to dry. The glass of water she filled at six she has not touched.

The pale-green grocery notebook is on the table near her right hand. She brought it out from the coat pocket and put it on the table. She has not opened it.

N2 is clean. She made N2 at her desk, in her office, in full light, with the blue-black ink she uses for fair copies. She can see that document clearly, even now: the upstroke of the y in *properly* at the end of line four, where the nib caught slightly on the paper grain; the bracket drawn below line eight in the same ink, two strokes and a right-angle corner — down, then across the bottom to form the angle, then down again — the bracket enclosing what the bracket was supposed to enclose. She was not in a hurry. She had the light she needed. She knows what she wrote in N2.

N3 she made in Elena's kitchen. Less light than her office, enough to work by. She checked it against N1 when she finished, line by line, and the bracket was there. She knows what she wrote in N3.

N4 she made in a coal cellar on Strada Crișan on the night of 3 November 1983. The cellar door was open the width of a hand — held with one foot and one elbow, because the woman who owned the cellar had not offered to hold it and there was no one else. Twenty minutes. Pencil — the one she had been carrying in the lining of the bag since October, short by November, still working. She copied eight lines. Then she copied the element below line eight.

She checked the copy once, forward, in sequence — presence, not category. Each element in the place where she had put it, in the order she had put it. She did not read it against N1. N1 was at the apartment. The cellar was not the apartment, and the woman at her shoulder could not wait any longer than she was already waiting.

She has been running the question away from this point for thirteen months.

The mutation is in the N4 branch. This she has understood since Day 9. The N2 branch is clean; the N3 branch is clean — Elena confirmed it again today through a child going to the tram. Whatever turned the bracketed ninth element into a numbered ninth line entered through the N4 branch. That is what the arithmetic shows. That is what the confirmed cleanliness of N2 and N3 requires.

The question she has not asked is: where in the N4 branch?

N1 has a bracket below line eight. She drew the bracket. Inside it she wrote — among the other words — *Do not wait for the morning.* She can hear herself writing those words in the cellar: the sound of a worn pencil on the back of a supply receipt, the cold coming through the door gap, the dark that was not complete but was nearly complete, the coal smell that has not left the inside of her nose in thirteen months. She wrote *Do not wait for the morning.* She is certain of the words.

The character that comes before the words is the one she cannot see.

A left bracket is two strokes. A nine is a circle closed at the left with a stem descending. She writes both in the course of her work — brackets in the margin columns, nines in the numerical sequences. She made the cellar copy without full light and without time to spare, and she checked it forward, in the order she had written it, and if a nine had stood where the bracket should have been, she would have read *nine* and understood it as correct. A person reading back a document they have just written in the dark reads intention, not error. The check confirmed that each element was present. The check did not confirm whether the bracket was still a bracket or had become something else in the twenty minutes and the near-dark.

She has not seen N4 since the night she handed it over. The cellar copy is on Strada Crișan, in a place Ilinca does not go, in the possession of the woman who waited at her shoulder. Thirteen months. She has been almost certain for all of them — almost certain of the bracket, of the two strokes and the right-angle corner, of the shape she knows how to make without thinking.

She picks up the grocery notebook and opens it to the first blank page. She looks at it for a moment. She closes it.

Outside the window the stairwell light on Strada Avântului is off. It went off at seventeen-thirty; it will come on again at eighteen-thirty. It is not yet eighteen-thirty. She is in the lamp's circle at the kitchen table, in the dark that the circle does not quite reach, and she cannot tell — in the account she keeps, in the column she has been maintaining for thirteen months without a single entry she could not defend — whether what she holds under N4 is a bracket or a nine.
