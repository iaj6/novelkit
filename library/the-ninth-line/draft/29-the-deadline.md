# The Deadline

5 December 1984

He had been inside the building since six. The overnight duty officer had given him a nod from behind the entry logbook and returned to his numbers. The corridor lights were at half-strength, which they were until eight, when the day shift arrived and someone switched the panel over. In the duty-room kitchen: the powder tin on the upper shelf, water from the tap that ran brown for a moment before clearing. He carried the nechezol back to his desk in the tin cup — the one with the handle that held. The ceramic mugs had handles that separated from the body without warning; he had learned this in his first month at the Dej station, twelve years ago, and had not touched the ceramic mugs since.

The investigations room was empty. His desk held, left to right: his working notebook open to 4 December, the Petrescu dosar open to the blank determination form, and the two documents face-up in the center — he had left them there when he went home the previous evening, which he did not usually do with evidence — and the eleven evidence envelopes stacked on the left edge of the desk, labeled but not yet sealed. He had the labels written from yesterday afternoon. He had wanted to see everything one more time in the morning light before closing it.

He had come in two hours before the office filled because a determination written in an empty room was complete when he signed it, and a determination written while people moved past the desk sometimes needed to be written again. He had written two over twelve years that needed to be written again. He preferred to write things once.

He wrote the section headings in the left margin, uncapped his pen, and began.

---

*Nature of the death.* Petrescu, Ana, born 1965, age nineteen. Admitted to the Dej district hospital at 14:33 on 4 November 1984, died at 18:47 the same date. Cause of death certified by the duty physician: hemorrhagic shock following complications of an illegal procedure. Initial notification to the Procuratură received 5 November 1984. No additional certification required; case opened on that date under the relevant articles.

*The document.* A handwritten document circulating without authorisation among workers and residents of the Dej municipal area. The document contains aftercare instructions pertaining to the management of complications of illegal procedures; it does not describe or specify any procedure. Through examination of twenty-two physical copies and through the voluntary testimony of its originator, it has been established that the document exists in multiple handwritten variant versions, all derived from a single source copy designated Exhibit A. The variants differ from the source by minor transcription incidentals: punctuation, line-break, numbering style. In one branch of transmission, a formatting difference in the source's ninth element propagated a material change in the rendered instruction, resulting in the version present in the Petrescu household. That branch is the subject of this determination.

*Origin.* The source copy — Exhibit A, the master — was produced by Roșca, Ilinca, born 1943, contabil-șef at the Artificial Fiber Enterprise, Dej. Authorship was attested to in a voluntary interview of 4 December 1984. The document was written in October 1983. From the master, the originator produced three copies between October and November 1983: two distributed through named individuals on separate branches of transmission, and one — Exhibit B — made on 3 November 1983.

The originator confirmed that the ninth element of the master was set down in bracket format: a marginal notation attached to the eighth line, not a numbered list item. In Exhibit B, the copy made on 3 November 1983, the same element appears as a numbered line, item 9 of nine. The originator described the conditions of that copying as insufficient light and available time. She stated that she does not know why the transcription took the form it did. She acknowledges that it did, and that Exhibit B was the product of her hand.

*Chain of transmission.* He wrote the names in order from the second generation forward, working from the notebook. He had eleven names: eight women who had spoken to him, plus the responsible party, plus the two household members at the chain's end. Against those eleven he had two entries where a holder had declined to provide a name — recorded as *Name not provided to the investigating officer.* One entry near the bottom read: *Unidentified female, bus route 4, Dej, approximately spring 1984. Contact not established.* He underlined it and moved to the next line.

The chain as established ran ten confirmed nodes from the master to the copy in the Petrescu household. Branches not connected to the fatal variant had been traced and confirmed as dead ends; they were summarised in an appendix. The central chain — from the master through Exhibit B, through two further transmissions, through the regularised copy, and through three further transmissions to the Petrescu household — had been documented through physical copy collection, voluntary interview, and the documentary evidence produced by the responsible party on 4 December 1984.

He reached the mutation section. He looked at the two documents in the center of his desk. He had read them both a dozen times since yesterday afternoon. He wrote.

*Formatting difference and its propagation.* The ninth element of Exhibit A (the master) reads, in relevant part:

[ 7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning. ]

The same element in Exhibit B (the copy made 3 November 1983) reads:

9. 7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning.

The wording was identical in both versions. The formatting differed: a marginal bracket notation in the master became a numbered list item in the copy. Two further transmissions downstream from Exhibit B, a copyist reading nine numbered lines regularised the ninth into sequence with the eighth, producing the following:

9. If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.

This wording was preserved through three further transmissions and was present in Exhibit C (node seventeen), the copy in Ana Petrescu's household at the time of the events of 4 November 1984. The introduction of the phrase *in the morning* and the omission of the phrase *Do not wait for the morning* both occurred in the course of regularisation from the numbered format of Exhibit B.

He drew a check in the margin and moved to the last heading.

*Responsible party.* Roșca, Ilinca, born 1943, contabil-șef, AFE Dej. Employment address. The relevant articles, which he had from memory. The voluntary interview; the documents produced by the responsible party in person on 4 December 1984. A note that the responsible party had appeared at the station before the end-of-day threshold at which formal preliminary charge would have been placed against the secondary holder. The secondary holder of a document on a clean branch of transmission — Marcu, Doina, born 1965 — had been released from preliminary detention on 4 December 1984, per order of the investigating officer. He wrote that sentence twice, because the first time the handwriting was not clear.

---

The determination was six pages. He read it from the heading. The facts were in the right order. The chain was documented. The mutation was described in the language he had chosen, which said what it said and nothing else. The responsible party was named and the secondary holder was cleared.

He capped the pen.

He picked up the two documents from the center of the desk. He read each one's ninth element once more before the envelopes. Every word the same. He slid Exhibit A into the first envelope and Exhibit B into the second. The labels were already written. He sealed both and set them with the other nine.

Eleven envelopes in a row on the left edge of the desk. He straightened them until the edges aligned and wrote the case number across the strip of paper along the top of the row. He wrote *Petrescu, A., 4.XI.1984 — evidence* and set the completed dosar on top, closed.

---

The file did not contain the reason the document had existed. It did not contain the prior question: why a woman in this town had written eight instructions and a bracketed ninth, what the bracket was there to prevent, what kind of decision it was designed to help someone make.

The file did not contain the reason the cellar had been dark and the time had been insufficient. It contained "insufficient light and time pressure" as the originator's own description of the conditions in which Exhibit B was made.

He had spent thirty-one days on the Petrescu matter. He had not sent up an innocent name. He had sent the correct name, through the correct procedure, on the deadline day.

He closed it there.

---

He telephoned his captain at half past nine. Two rings before the answer.

He said: the determination in the Petrescu matter is on its way to you by messenger.
His captain said: good.
He said: the evidence can be transferred today or tomorrow, whichever is preferred.
His captain said: today. Then: was it as complicated as it seemed?
Munteanu said: it was exactly as complicated as it seemed.
A pause. His captain said: good. Understood. And the call ended.

He set the phone down.

---

The dosar went with the courier at noon. At half past four he carried the evidence envelopes downstairs himself. The evidence room officer — a younger man, two or three years in the post — asked for the case name. Munteanu told him. The officer wrote it in the register. Munteanu watched the entry being written and did not speak again after that.

He went back upstairs and sat at the cleared desk for a few minutes. The notebook was in the inside pocket of his coat. The pen was in the drawer. The tin cup was on the corner, where it had been since six-fifteen that morning.

At six he put on the coat. He picked up the tin cup and drank what was left of the nechezol, which had been cold for several hours. Nobody liked the nechezol warm. Cold was not an additional problem so much as the same problem continued at a lower temperature. He set the cup on the desk and went out.

On Strada 22 Decembrie the air was several degrees below zero and the valley fog had settled back in, low along the Someș — the kind that sat from early evening until late morning, opaque at road level, thickening where the ground fell toward the water. He could make out the shape of the far hillside through the haze. He could not see the river. He turned his collar up.

The case was closed. December fifth, the day it was required to close, and it was closed on that day. Doina Marcu had been released the afternoon before. Ilinca Roșca was in the second-floor holding section, which he had not gone past when he came by it this afternoon.

He walked. His hands were in his coat pockets. The determination was in a folder on its way to Cluj-Napoca. The evidence was in a room in the basement, correctly labeled, signed for, sealed. The fog absorbed the sound of the road and the sound of the Someș below the embankment and the sound of his own boots, so that by the time he reached the corner where the road dropped toward the bridge the only thing he could hear clearly was the one thing he could not see.

He went home.
