# 24–25 November 1984

He came in before the duty officer brought her. The questioning room was on the north side of the building, facing the courtyard wall, and by eight in the morning it held only the remnant cold that the radiator had not yet addressed and a flat, grey light coming in through a window that hadn't been cleaned since spring. He set both envelopes on the table, took the contents out without removing the evidence log sheet from each, and laid them flat. He straightened both sheets so their left edges lined up and their top edges were even, and then he sat down and looked at them.

The first was Marcu's. He had spent two evenings with it already: returning to this room after the workday, switching the lamp on, sitting with both sheets in front of him and no notebook, not writing, just looking. The paper was school exercise paper — the red margin line running down the left side, seventy or eighty ruled lines per sheet, the kind sold in blocks of four at the school supply section of the Alimentara on Strada Republicii. Blue pen, medium nib, the letters slanting backward slightly, as if the writer had learned to write quickly and had slowed down for this and not entirely adjusted. Eight numbered lines, clean and evenly spaced, and then the ninth position: not a number. A hand-drawn bracket in the left margin, two horizontal strokes and one vertical, the text beside it running across two lines and ending below the lower margin of the numbered list.

*7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning.*

The second envelope held a copy from the Petrescu chain. He had several; he had chosen this one because it was the clearest to read. Standard graph-rule notebook paper, the handwriting rounder and more even, the line spacing wider. He read the ninth line: *9. If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.*

He had looked at these two ninth positions for two evenings and had not resolved what they meant for the case in any way he could write into the formal file. He had resolved what they meant for the document, which was a different thing.

The duty officer brought her in at eight forty-five.

She sat in the chair facing the lamp — the same chair she had used two days earlier, the same angle, her back to the courtyard window. She put her hands flat on the table and did not look at the two sheets.

He said: I want to show you these again.

She said: all right.

He indicated the first sheet — hers — and read the bracket text aloud. *Seven or eight — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning.* He indicated the second and read the ninth line. *Nine — if seven or eight, take her in in the morning.*

He said: two different versions of the same instruction, in the same position in the same document. One says to take her in and not to wait. The other says to take her in in the morning. Your copy says the first version.

She looked at both sheets. She said: yes. That's what mine says.

He said: I need to know where you got the copy that says the first version. The difference between the two versions goes back to wherever your copy came from — every copy above yours will also say the first version. I'm going to follow that chain either way. I'd prefer to start from the right place.

She said: I found it on the bus. I told you that.

He asked: which bus.

She said: the number four. Morning direction. Last spring. She was the only one in that section of the seats.

He made a note in his working file: *Marcu, D., second questioning. "Found on bus." Consistent with prior statement. No additional information. Unable to verify or disprove.*

He waited ten minutes and asked again: had anyone mentioned this kind of document to her before she found it. She said no. He waited another ten minutes and asked whether she'd known, when she picked it up, what she was looking at. She said she'd read it and understood what it was for. He wrote: *Confirms she read it. Confirms she understood its function. Claims no prior contact with document or its contents. Claim is unverifiable from current evidence.*

He thanked her and had the duty officer take her back.

---

He sat for a while with the two sheets on the table. The radiator had made some progress; the room was no longer actively cold, only cool in the way that rooms are cool when they've been cold for a long time and the warmth hadn't yet moved through the furniture.

The bracket was older. If you read the Marcu copy and then the Petrescu-chain copy in the order they'd been made, the bracket came first: it occupied the margin, outside the numbered sequence, set apart from the eight numbered lines. Someone, at some point, had copied the bracket document and given the bracket a number — and from that copy forward, the ninth position was a numbered line. Every copy on the Petrescu chain had the number. The Marcu copy had the bracket. Which meant the Marcu copy had not been copied from the document where the number was assigned.

He wrote in the formal file: *N14 (Marcu) — bracket format in ninth position. Divergence from numbered format in Petrescu chain pre-dates N14's entry into transmission. N14 probably descends from a different G2-level source than the numbered-format copies.*

What he did not write in the formal file was the rest of the reasoning. Because Marcu's copy was on the bracket branch, and because the mutation he was following — the numbered ninth line in its shortened form — had entered the numbered branch somewhere below its G2 source, her copy had not passed through the shortening point. She could still be a node in the transmission; her refusal to name her source was its own problem, unresolved. But the numbered branch and the bracket branch had different G2 ancestors. He needed the numbered branch's G2 ancestor, not the bracket branch's. And he had that address.

He slid both sheets back into their envelopes and put them in the drawer.

---

Mureșan knocked at half past three. He came in with an evidence envelope and set it on the desk. He said: a woman at eighteen, entry A, fourth floor, Strada Mihai. Same bloc as Damian. He'd been canvassing the building that morning as part of the production-floor sweep; she'd answered the door and handed over the document before he'd finished explaining why he was there. He'd taken a statement and sent her home.

Munteanu waited until Mureșan had closed the door. Then he put on his jacket against the cold from the courtyard window and opened the envelope.

The paper was mill stationery — AFE letterhead printed on the front, text written on the blank reverse. The paper had been folded lengthwise, once, and the crease was worn deep. The handwriting was precise, the letters slightly too large for the space, so the lines grew crowded near the right margin and the last word of each line was compressed. He read through the numbered lines.

Line six: *Burn nothing at all until she is well. Wash and keep.*

He had that phrasing from Damian's copy, and only from Damian's copy. No other document in his collection used it. He made a note in the margin of his working sheet: *"nothing at all" in line 6 — cf. Damian (N10). Probable descent from Damian or Damian's immediate source.*

He read to the end. The ninth line was a numbered line, the longer form — the same wording as the Damian copy's ninth position, different from the shortened form in the Petrescu copies.

He put the new copy in the third drawer. He wrote the count in his working sheet: six.

He checked the date on his desk calendar. November twenty-fourth. He wrote in the margin: *Damian bloc, 24 Nov. G4 on N4 branch (inferred from line 6 variant). Long form in line 9. Added to file.*

---

He stayed at his desk until past seven. When the room was dark enough that the desk lamp was the only light not coming from the street, he took all six envelopes from the third drawer and laid them across the desk in the order he'd acquired them. He did not unfold everything. He only needed the ninth line on each.

One bracket. Five numbered nines.

Of the five numbered nines, two said *take her in in the morning*. Three said the longer form, which preserved the same words as the bracket text but in a numbered line rather than a bracket. He separated them: the two short-form copies on his left, the three long-form numbered copies on his right, and Marcu's bracket copy at the near edge of the desk. Six documents in three groups and they all came from the same place.

He had been treating the numbered copies as one category, which had been useful for the structural picture but was not fully accurate. The long-form numbered copies and the short-form numbered copies had diverged from each other somewhere below the first numbered transcription. He had the short-form at G4 and G5; the long-form numbered copies were at G3 and G4. The shortening had entered somewhere between those levels. He added a note to his working sheet.

Then he opened the private notebook — smaller than the working file, not for the inspectorate — and wrote across the top of a fresh page: *24 Nov.*

Below that: *Six copies. Bracket branch: one (N14, Marcu). Numbered branch: five. Within numbered: two short form, three long form. Both branch patterns converge at a single prior document above both G2 nodes. Prior document: bracket format, inferred. Long-form numbered copies preserve bracket content in numbered position; short-form copies have shortened the ninth position below the bracket-text content. Shortening point: G3–G4 level of numbered branch, not yet identified.*

He stopped. He wrote: *Maker of prior document not yet identified. Address on Strada Crișan not yet visited.*

He looked at the last sentence. He crossed out *not yet visited* and wrote *visit pending*. The notebook was for facts he didn't hold yet.

He capped the pen.

November twenty-fifth. Ten days to the fifth of December.

He put the six envelopes back in the third drawer and closed it. He put the notebook in his jacket. He switched off the desk lamp.

He had the address. He had not gone to it because the Marcu canvass had been moving faster. The numbered branch's G2 ancestor was the person who had received the bracket document and given its ninth element a number, and that person's address had been in his notebook for eleven days.

He sat for a moment in the dark from the street. The frost was beginning on the courtyard window.

He would go to Strada Crișan in the morning.
