# 3 December 1984

The frost had held through the night. He walked from the station on Strada 22 Decembrie north through the market square and then east along the Someș embankment toward Strada Crișan, and the cold was the settled kind — dry and crystalline rather than the wet, fog-heavy cold that had come off the river in the last weeks of November. It had decided to stay. The embankment road was empty except for a woman pushing a pram with a bag of something heavy hung from the handle, and a boy on a bicycle who had to stand up on the pedals to manage the slope. The Someș below the embankment was the color of wet concrete. On the opposite bank, two lines of poplar trees, leafless.

He had not requested the official car. This was one interview on a street he could cover on foot in twenty minutes, and the car's absence would be noted wherever it went. He had not thought further than that; he was a thorough man and he was walking.

Strada Crișan ran north from the embankment for six blocks. He walked the length of it once from south to north, reading the bloc numbers from the entrance panels. Bloc eighteen was on the western side, entrance B. Someone had turned the panel sideways at some point, possibly when hanging a line; it faced the neighboring building rather than the street. He found it on the second pass and went in.

Twenty-eight units. The stairwell smelled of cabbage and damp concrete, which was the smell of every stairwell this deep into November — he corrected himself: December. Third floor left was the last door on the landing before the wall. The light fixture had been taped back to the ceiling at one point with gray insulating tape; one corner of the tape was peeling and the fixture cast its light slightly sideways. He knocked twice.

The woman who answered was somewhere in her mid-thirties. She wore a gray cardigan buttoned to the collar and her hair was in the braid she had slept in. She had opened the door to perhaps forty centimetres and held it there while she read his identification card. Her expression as she looked up at him was not alarm. It was closer to recognition — not of him specifically, but of the question he had come to ask. He could see that in the way her weight settled, very slightly, when she looked up.

He introduced himself: Locotenent Munteanu, Miliția, Investigations Section. "I am here in connection with the Petrescu investigation. May I come in?"

She held the door open.

---

The apartment was one room. A kitchen alcove on the right-hand wall with a two-burner stove and a small refrigerator; a table in the center with two chairs; a narrow wardrobe beside the far wall. The bed was made and pushed against the left wall, a folded blanket at the foot. There was a plant on the windowsill in a tin that had once held marinated peppers — part of the label was still legible. The single window looked onto the rear courtyard. The radiator beneath it was not on; she had a second blanket folded over the back of her chair. There was a cup of tea on the table that had been steaming when she opened the door and was not steaming now.

He sat in the chair that was not hers and opened his notebook.

"I will ask you directly," he said. "Have you at any time had in your possession a handwritten document that has been circulating in the community — a document concerning medical care?"

She looked at her hands. Then at him. "I had something like that. Yes."

"Do you still have it?"

"No."

He wrote this down. "What happened to it?"

"The woman who gave it to me came back and took it."

He wrote: *Document retrieved by original source.* "When was this?"

"Yesterday." She thought about it briefly. "The second. The second of December."

He wrote the date and looked at it. December 2. He had confirmed this address at the Population Section on the afternoon of December 1 — three o'clock, approximately; he had asked the registrar for the Strada Crișan bloc-eighteen ledger, had obtained the registration, had noted the address in his working book while the registrar made the required entry in the consultation log. The following day, in some part of this city, someone had climbed these stairs and removed a document she had not wanted to leave in place.

He did not say anything about this. He wrote: *Retrieval date: 2 December. Document gone the day after Population Section address confirmation.*

"Can you describe the woman who gave you the document?"

"She's older than me." The woman across the table was considering it honestly, not performing the consideration. "Forty, I would say. Perhaps a bit older."

"Where does she work?"

"At the mill. I think in administration — not on the floor. I have seen her from a distance." She paused. "I don't know her family name."

He wrote: *Mill. Administration likely. Age approx. 40. No surname known to witness.* He read these four items back. In his working notes he had three entries under the G1 profile dating from the twenty-third of November, derived from six weeks of interviews and two dead ends: *mill-connected, female, older, document originator.* What the woman across the table had just given him in three sentences was the same profile, confirmed by a witness who had met the G1 source in person. She was either telling him everything she knew, or she was giving him the perimeter of it — precisely the outer edge that would confirm his profile without allowing him to locate anyone.

He asked the next question. "Can you tell me where she gave you the document — the specific location of that first transfer?"

She looked past him at the window for a moment. "I would rather not say."

He wrote: *Refused to specify location of original transfer. Answered all other questions without sign of concealment. Consistent with protection of third party rather than personal exposure.* "I understand," he said. "Is there anything else you can tell me about her — a physical description?"

"I would recognize her face, if that is any use."

He asked two more questions: had she shown the document to anyone, had she copied it. She said no to both, steadily, without the quality that usually accompanied a rehearsed negative — the slight brightening, the half-second before the answer. He believed her. The canvass of this building had turned up nothing. The address on Dorobanților that he had spent the morning of the twenty-ninth working had yielded a woman who had moved out in February; the population register had been what gave him Strada Crișan. The document had come here and had not gone further. He had reached a terminal point.

He stood and thanked her. He told her that if she heard from the woman in question, she should contact the station on Strada 22 Decembrie. She nodded. He went out.

At the top of the stairwell he paused. The door had closed behind him without additional comment. She had given him exactly what she could give him while protecting the woman above her in the chain. He went down.

---

On the street he stood outside the entrance for a moment. The boy on the bicycle was gone. Across the embankment road, the woman with the pram was crossing the bridge in the other direction. A superintendent from one of the eastern-side blocs was scraping ice off the entrance steps with the back end of a shovel. The Someș was still the same color it had been forty minutes ago.

He thought about the consultation log.

The Population Section kept a record of every registry access: date, accessor's name, rank or office, the volume consulted. This was standard administrative procedure for a document with legal relevance. He had known about the log and had not thought about it particularly, because there was ordinarily no reason to think about it. The log was accessible to the court clerk's office, the county inspectorate, and a number of other offices with legitimate interest in civil registration matters.

He was not suggesting that anyone in the Population Section had copied the log entry and passed it to someone outside the building. He was noting that a woman who had been doing careful, deliberate work for more than a year would have developed, over that time, some sense of how information about the investigation was moving. The arrest of Marcu Doina on the twenty-second of November would have been known at the mill by the twenty-third; he was not naive about this. The canvassing of the Strada Mihai building in the second week of November would have been known, perhaps more quickly. A woman working in administration at the same mill where Marcu Doina worked nights would have her own ways of following the direction of the investigation, and those ways would not require her to consult a Population Section access log.

She had taken the document back on December 2. He had confirmed this address on December 1. He preferred not to rely on coincidences.

He turned up his collar and walked south toward the embankment.

---

He was back at his desk by half past twelve.

The Petrescu dosar was in the center of the desk. He opened it to the working notes section, found the most recent entry — the Population Section consultation, December 1, *Moga R., Strada Crișan 18/B/3-left, registered 1979* — and drew a line below it and wrote today's date.

Then he wrote: *Interview, G2 node address, Strada Crișan. Witness: female, mid-thirties, registered resident. Document confirmed received from G1 source and returned to G1 source, 2 December. G1 description: mill-connected, administration likely, age approx. 40. G1 aware of investigation by 1 December at latest. G1 has recovered G2 copy. G1 location: unknown. G1 name: unknown.*

He read the entry back.

The G1 figure had both copies now. The one she had made first, in October of last year, and the one she had made quickly in conditions that did not permit careful work — made quickly enough that something had gone wrong that did not look like anything wrong until two hands later, when a woman reading nine numbered lines had done her careful best to put them in order. The G1 figure had come back for the second copy when she understood that the investigation had its address. She had not had it destroyed in place. She had come herself. A woman going to ground did not need to come back for a second-generation copy that had already dispersed further into the network; she would leave it and move, and the copy would sit in an evidence envelope and point upward at a blank space. Coming back for it meant she intended to do something with both of them. He did not know what.

He picked up the telephone receiver. He held it for several seconds — the ambient click of the station exchange, a distant voice on one of the other lines — and then set it down.

There was nothing he could put in front of his captain that was more than arithmetic: *I have a profile. Mill administration. Approximately forty years old. She is aware of the investigation and has recovered a second-generation copy. She has had two days. I do not have her name.* His captain would say: then go to the mill and find her. And he would have to explain that going to the mill administration section without a name meant walking past a woman who would see a Miliția coat coming and would have the time to decide what to do about it — and that a woman who had taken a document back the day before he reached it would know exactly how to use that time.

He had two days.

He turned to the blank pages at the back of the notebook and wrote at the top: *5 December.* Below it: *If no voluntary approach by 1800 on 4 December — formal charge, Marcu D.* He wrote the article numbers. He looked at this for a moment, then looked away, at the radiator on the far wall, which was making the settling sound it made at this hour. Outside the window the light was already thinning — less than four hours of usable sky this deep into December, and the afternoon had not really started.

Marcu Doina had been in the holding section for eleven days. She had answered his questions in the same words each time, with the same quality of stillness. She ate and slept and did not perform distress. He did not believe she was going to change her account. He believed his arithmetic was sound and that it pointed at someone who was not Marcu Doina; he believed that someone was moving toward some kind of action, because the alternative was going to ground and she had not done that. He believed that if she was going to come to him she would come in the next two days, because two days was when the deadline became something he had to act on.

He was not certain of any of this. He was proceeding as though he were.

He turned back to the working notes section and wrote at the bottom of the entry, below the interview summary, below the G1 profile: *Two days.*

He closed the notebook.

He did not pick up the telephone again.
