# 25–26 November 1984

She fills the third column on the November audit form and checks the sum by adding upward. The same figure. She writes it in the box at the bottom of the column and sets the pen down.

The administrative wing has not had heat since Thursday. There is a coat on the back of her chair and the lamp is on although the gray November light from the window is more than enough to see by. She has brought the lamp closer because the lamp is warmer than nothing, and this is November in an office whose heating pipe runs first to the weaving hall, which takes precedence. Three days since Marcu was taken. The payroll was finished on Thursday. The materials reconciliation was finished yesterday. The November audit preparation — four forms, three of which are now on the right side of the desk — has proceeded without error. The fourth form is the output reconciliation for the third quarter: it requires checking the listed figures against three separate source ledgers, which she has already pulled and stacked on the left side of the desk in the order she will use them. She picks up the pen.

---

Gabi from the records office comes in at ten with the production-floor correction form that has been sitting in the internal post since Wednesday. She comes in person, which she does not do. She sets the form on the desk and stands for a moment in the way that means she has something to say that she will not write down.

She says: do you know the woman from near the church on Piața Libertății — the older one, Vasilica, Drăgan is the name — grey coat, keeps to herself. She hasn't spoken to anyone since the autumn. Very quiet. She heard it from someone in the building.

Ilinca says: I hadn't heard.

Gabi says: I thought perhaps you'd know her. And then she is at the door, because the records office is two doors down and she has been gone long enough.

Ilinca enters the correction form's figures into the appropriate column. The figures agree with the total she already has. She moves the form to the right side of the desk and opens the first of the source ledgers.

---

She goes to the canteen at noon and is back before twenty past. She eats at the desk: bread, Friday's meat, the last of the pickled beet. She does not open the ledger on the second shelf.

There are ten. The church acquaintance — Vasilica Drăgan, grey coat, near the church on Piața Libertății — received the copy from the bloc neighbor and has passed it to no one. Dead end. The N4 branch runs as far as she can trace it: from the cellar woman to the sister at the Strada Mihai laundry, from the sister to the bloc neighbor with the child, from the bloc neighbor to Vasilica Drăgan and no further. Four nodes below N4. Her count for the whole: ten confirmed.

She sets the three branches flat in her mind the way she would set three sheets on the desk. N2: confirmed, closed. Georgeta and her neighbor and the neighbor's acquaintance across the courtyard — no mutation. N3: confirmed, closed. Elena and her cousin and the cousin's friend on the far side of the hill — no mutation. N4: one line of descent mapped, four nodes confirmed, all four carrying the mutation. She has heard about no N4-branch copy with a bracket.

The mutation is in the N4 column. Only in the N4 column. She cannot close the N4 column because she has not seen N4.

What the N4 column tells her, set down: every node below N4 received what N4 said and transcribed it. If the numbered ninth line was in N4, they copied the numbered ninth line. They were not careless copyists; they did not introduce a change to the ninth position that was not already there to copy. A copyist who gives a number to a line that has a bracket is making a choice, and that choice leaves a mark: the bracket text sits in the left margin, outside the numbered column, and if a copyist decides to move it in, the proportions change — the margin space becomes empty, the numbered lines shift, the ninth runs longer than the eighth. Nothing she has heard about the N4-branch copies suggests this happened. They reproduced what they were given.

The error is in N4. She made N4. Three days since Marcu was taken, and the column is the same as it was before: open.

She has been holding this in the incomplete column for five weeks, since the day she confirmed the mutation is not in the N2 or N3 branches. A bookkeeper who knows a column is incomplete does not close it. What she has been doing instead is keeping it provisional, which is what you do when you know what the figure is going to be and have not yet gone to confirm it.

---

She goes to the window.

The factory yard below is flat gray — the kind of even, cold November light that strips the color out of whatever color there is and makes everything the same distance from the eye. Two workers in padded jackets cross from the weaving hall to the canteen, walking fast with their hands in their pockets, their breath coming ahead of them in short white bursts. The delivery truck that has been backed into the materials bay since morning is still there; the driver is somewhere inside. A second chimney beyond the weaving hall has started running — thin white into the fog that has settled between the factory fence and the river, barely visible against a sky the same flat color. She can see the willows at the bank, dark and bare, the branches finer than pencil lines at this distance, as a smudge at the very edge of the visible. Two hundred meters. The Someș is there. She can tell it is there by the willows and by the fog's texture, which is different over water.

She stands for a moment. Then she goes back to the desk.

She opens the second source ledger and finds the column she needs and enters the figures. She gets the same total two times running.

---

She has not seen N4. She has been to the coal cellar on Strada Crișan exactly once, in November of 1983, and she handed the copy over when it was finished without a second check, because there was no time for a second check and the first was enough at the time. She has not held N4 since. What she has instead of certainty is an inference that points in one direction, and she has been calling the inference close-enough-to-know for five weeks, and that is not the same thing.

The bracket she drew on N1 in October of 1983: she can feel the movement of the pen. Two horizontal strokes and one vertical, enclosing the text she added last, the instruction that stands beside line eight and overrides lines seven and eight when either is true. She drew it in the left margin because she had run out of numbered room; she wrote the text inside the bracket in a smaller hand to keep it clear of line eight, which says: *A fever that will not come down by morning is too long.* She drew the bracket deliberately, knowing what the two forms were for. A number is a step in the sequence. A bracket is not a step; it is what you reach for when the thing you are writing does not belong in the sequence at all. She knew this when she drew it. The shape of the bracket is in her hand the way the shape of a correct sum is in her hand: it has a feel, and the feel is distinct from a nine.

In the coal cellar, in November of 1983, she had twenty minutes and a door gap of six centimetres and a woman who could not wait. She does not know what her hand drew in the left margin of the ninth position. She has assumed for a year that she knows, and she has not looked at the copy.

---

The stairwell light is still on when she gets home, which means it is before half past five. She switches on the lamp in the kitchen and then the lamp in the other room, and the two bulbs between them make the apartment visible and not warm. The heating has not come. She can see her breath near the burner while the water takes its time. She does not take off her coat; she turns the burner up as far as it goes and stands over the warmth from it. She eats at the table: the rest of the bread, the last of the cheese, a cup of the barley mixture that has been waiting since morning. The bread is the dense end-of-week kind that requires attention. She gives it attention. When she is done she washes the plate and the cup and sets them in the rack and stands at the sink for a moment with her hands flat on its edge, looking at the dark window above it, which shows only the building across the courtyard and one lit square on the fourth floor.

She does not go back to the mill for the ledger. She is not going back tonight.

There are two possibilities, and she has been holding both for five weeks. The first: N4 has the bracket intact, and the mutation entered downstream — in the sister's copy perhaps, or in the bloc neighbor's, a hand working fast without enough light. It happens. If N4 has the bracket, the mutation is not Ilinca's. The second: N4 has a numbered ninth line. She has been calling it a possibility because she has not been to Strada Crișan to look at the left margin of the ninth position, and calling it a conclusion without seeing the copy is not something she can do with a figure she has not confirmed against its source.

Marcu has been in the questioning room for three days. The column is still open.

She goes to the window.

The street below is empty except for a woman in a dark coat moving along the far pavement with a string bag, walking toward the corner. The building across the road has a light on in the third floor — yellow behind the curtain, making a dull rectangle on the pavement below. The fog has come up from the river and sits at the level of the street lamps and blurs them into soft columns of orange.

She has never left a column she suspected was wrong without checking it. She has entered a figure incorrectly and found it on the review and corrected it. She has entered a figure and been uncertain and gone back to the source document to confirm it. An entry she has not confirmed against its source is provisional. Provisional is not closed. A closed column is a column with no outstanding questions. She has one outstanding question and it requires a visit to Strada Crișan and it requires asking to see a copy she made in a coal cellar twelve months ago. She cannot answer it any other way. The inference only goes one direction; what she does not have is the paper.

Tomorrow she will go to the cellar woman and ask to see the copy.

The light across the road is still on. She watches it.
