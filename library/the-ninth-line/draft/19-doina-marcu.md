# 23 November 1984

She is correcting the November payroll when Daniela Lungu opens the door without knocking.

Lungu is the factory committee secretary. She handles meeting minutes, internal correspondence, the directives that come down from the enterprise administration requiring annotations within fourteen days. She usually arrives with a form — the pale green of the personnel department or the off-white of the supply section — and she usually knocks. This morning she does neither. She stops one step inside the door and holds a personnel form against her forearm and looks at the desk without looking at Ilinca directly.

She says: Tovarășă Roșca. The October transport allocation correction. You'll need it for the quarterly submission.

Ilinca says: leave it on the corner.

Lungu sets it on the corner of the desk. She remains where she is. The radiator is off; it comes on at six in the evening, after the weaving hall has had the shared pipe first, and until then the administrative wing holds its cold completely. The chemicals from the fiber hall drift in through a gap somewhere above the window frame — a wet-acrid smell that Ilinca reported to the facilities division in January and again in March, both times receiving a form confirming that the matter had been registered. She has stopped reporting it.

Lungu says: I heard something this morning. Through Aurel in personnel.

Ilinca keeps her pencil on the paper. She says nothing.

Lungu says: one of the night-shift girls was taken in yesterday afternoon. She pauses. She says: Marcu. The one from the hostel on Strada Vasile. She had — a document, apparently.

Ilinca writes a number in the column. She looks at it and sees it is the number she meant to write. She says: I see.

Lungu goes. The door clicks shut.

---

Ilinca does not move for a moment. Through the south-facing window, the November morning is flat and pale, clouds that have come in low off the plateau and are not going anywhere. The looms are running in the hall beyond the administrative wing wall; she can feel the particular vibration of loom hall two, which has been running rough since mid-October, a tension problem that has been on the maintenance schedule since she reported it and has not been addressed. She has not reported it a second time.

She finishes the line she was on. She counts the remaining lines on the payroll sheet: three. She picks up her pencil and sets it down and picks it up again.

Doina Marcu. The name is in the payroll register the way every name is in the payroll register, in the column assigned to night-shift production workers, loom hall two. Started two years ago; came from a village outside town and was placed in the factory hostel. Monthly salary, corrected in June when the enterprise posted the wage schedule adjustment. Signed the mandatory health examination form in September — a handwriting that slants slightly to the right. Year of birth: 1965.

She has never spoken with her. She has never given her anything.

She knows Doina Marcu by the trace she left in forms that Ilinca is required to maintain and required to produce on demand. That is all.

The payroll sheet is waiting. Somewhere in loom hall two the rough vibration runs on. Outside, the clouds are sitting on the ridge above the river and the light is not going to improve before dark, which is four hours away.

---

If Munteanu went to Doina Marcu with her name already written in his notebook, he came to her through a branch. Not a canvass. Lungu said: *the one from the hostel on Strada Vasile*. An investigator running a general canvass of the mill's night shift would have spoken to many people; many people would have been mentioned. Lungu mentioned one, because there was one, because a single name had been given to Munteanu by someone below it on the same line.

A name before the visit.

Ilinca has the N2 branch: Georgeta, one neighbor in the next bloc, two nodes, both confirmed, end of transmission. Neither of them is a night-shift worker in loom hall two.

She has the N3 branch: Elena, two women Elena gave copies to, and the G4 node she traced — the woman in Elena's bloc whose daughter had asked. That G4 node is not a factory-floor worker. The N3 names she has collected in two months of careful relayed inquiry do not include a night-shift worker in loom hall two.

That leaves the N4 branch.

---

The N4 branch. She has mapped it at a distance, working through bread-queue acquaintances and delivery-relay contacts, counting nodes on a branch she has been unwilling to approach at the center. She has traced three G3 and G4 nodes below the cellar copy. The G5 node she added last week is the church woman near the market, whose name she does not know. Not one of those nodes is a night-shift worker in loom hall two.

Which means the branch is longer than she mapped. One of the G4 nodes she found — the bloc neighbor with the child, or the pharmacy acquaintance — gave a copy to someone Ilinca has not traced. And Doina Marcu is that untraced node. Or Doina is on the N3 branch, in the territory Ilinca has not fully walked, a G4 or G5 node below a G3 she never identified.

Either way: Munteanu, coming up from Doina, goes to whoever gave Doina her copy. Then to whoever gave that person theirs. One step at a time, backwards along a line. If the line is N4, it terminates at the cellar copy. And the person who made the cellar copy is Ilinca.

If the line is N3, it terminates at Elena's copy, then at N3, then at N1. And the person who made N1 is also Ilinca.

She has understood for two weeks that both branches terminate at her. What Lungu gave her this morning is the thing that was on the path before the terminus. A name from the payroll register. A year of birth. A hostel on Strada Vasile.

---

She does not know which branch Doina is on. She does not know whether Doina's copy carries the mutation. If Doina's copy is on the N3 branch, it has the bracket; it is clean; the mutation never passed through it. If it is on the N4 branch, it may or may not carry the mutation, depending on which generation it is and which path from the cellar copy it followed.

Munteanu has the copy — he would have it, that is the only reason a name would be in his notebook before the visit. He can see what it says. If the copy has the bracket, he can see she is on the clean branch. Whether that matters depends on whether he understands what the bracket means. Whether it matters further depends on whether understanding it is enough to release someone from custody.

Possession is enough for a charge. A clean copy is still a copy.

There is a nineteen-year-old girl who was not at work this morning, who is in a room somewhere on Strada Republicii, and who is there because she is a node in a network whose origin is the desk Ilinca is sitting at. Whether Doina's copy is clean or corrupted does not change that fact.

The nineteen-year-old does not know who made the first paper. She may not know how many generations she is from it. She is in the room because she has it, or had it, and because someone followed the line to her.

Ilinca cannot determine which it is without asking, and asking is the one action she cannot take.

---

If she went to the woman on Dorobanților. If she asked to see the cellar copy. If the cellar copy showed what she has been afraid for two weeks it shows — the numbered ninth line where the bracket should be, written in a coal cellar by the strip of light from a door, by a hand that was working quickly because there was no time to work slowly. If all of that is true, then she could take two pieces of paper to the Miliție station on Strada Republicii. N1 and N4. The master and the copy. She could lay them side by side on a desk and say: here is what the ninth element is, and here is the copy where it stopped being that and became a line in a list. Here is the date I made it. November 1983. Here is the difference I did not see when I checked it once and folded it and gave it away. He would look at the bracket and then at the numbered line and he would understand the origin.

The girl from the hostel on Strada Vasile would go home.

Ilinca would not go home.

She knows this the way she knows the quarterly balance — not because she has worked through it step by step in this moment but because the arithmetic has been sitting in her since the fourth of November and she has checked it enough times that she does not need to check it again. The sum is the same every time. Two documents in front of Munteanu. One resolution. One accounting.

---

She gets up from the desk and takes the 1983 ledger from the second shelf. The standard Ministry of Finance form-book, four hundred pages, binding retaped in February with brown tape after the spine cracked during the winter. She sets it on the desk. She opens it to where she left off this morning. The spine lies flat. In the fold at the spine, N1 is there — she can see the pale edge of it, the paper that has been folded in a dim place for over a year and has gone slightly yellow at the crease. She does not need to take it out. She knows what it says. She wrote it in October 1983 at this desk, at this lamp, and she has read it perhaps forty times since then. She knows the bracket. She knows its three strokes — two horizontals and a vertical — and she knows the lines inside it and she knows the last line, which she added last because she was afraid of leaving it out, which she put in the bracket because it was not a step in the sequence of steps and she needed to make that visible. She can read it without unfolding the paper.

*Do not wait for the morning.*

She looks at the November payroll actuals. The column has three entries left to verify. She closes the ledger.

She sets it back on the second shelf in the correct position, spine forward, between the quarterly labour plan compliance records and the annual balance submission. She sits down at her desk. The chair creaks at the back joint, as it has since spring. The payroll sheet is where she left it. The pencil is where she left it. Outside the south window the November light is the same as it was an hour ago.

Twenty days since the fourth of November. Thirteen days to the fifth of December.

The lamp is on. The ledger is on the shelf. The pencil is in her hand and she holds it without moving.
