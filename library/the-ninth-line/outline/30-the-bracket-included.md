# Chapter 30 — The Bracket Included

**One-line summary:** FINAL CHAPTER — Doina walks out of the Miliția holding facility; she has nothing; she tests herself; she can recite all nine elements, bracket included; she walks along the Someș in the fog.

**POV:** Doina Marcu — first person past  
**Date:** 5–6 December 1984 (Days 32–33)  
**Setting:** The Miliția station exterior; the street; the Someș riverbank  
**Chapter shape:** Single scene  
**Ending mode:** Literary fade  

---

## Goal / obstacle / beats

**Doina's goal this chapter:** None, in the narrative sense. She walks out. She goes.

**Obstacle:** None, in the narrative sense. She is released and she walks. The chapter is not built around an obstacle; it is built around what remains when the obstacle is gone.

**Beats:**

1. **The holding facility, morning.** An officer she doesn't know hands her back her coat, her identity booklet, and the bread she had in her pocket when they brought her in (stale now). He does not hand back the geography book. She does not ask for it. She signs something. She goes out.

2. **The street.** She has been inside for twelve days. She stands on the step outside the Miliția station and she does not know what day it is. She works out from the cold and the light that it is morning, probably early. The fog is down on the Someș, two streets over.

3. **She thinks about what she doesn't have.** The geography book is in the evidence file. The copy of the paper inside the geography book is in the evidence file. Everything she's been carrying for eleven months is gone. She has her coat. She has her identity booklet. She has the bread, which she eats standing on the step, stale and better than nothing.

4. **She doesn't know what happened to Ilinca.** Nobody has told her. The paperwork she signed said she was released following the determination of the case. She can infer from this that something happened, but she doesn't know what. She knows someone came forward because otherwise she would still be there. She doesn't know who came forward. She might, later. Not now.

5. **She tests herself.** She recites the document from memory. She does this standing on the step, very quietly, the bread finished. She begins at line 1.

   *Keep her warm and lying down. Someone stays with her the whole night.*  
   *Small sips of water, often. Nothing else by mouth for six hours.*  
   *Count the cloths. Write the number and the hour on the paper by the bed.*  
   *If she sleeps, wake her every hour and speak to her until she answers you properly.*  
   *No visitors. No telephone. If anyone asks, she has the influenza.*  
   *Burn nothing until she is well. Wash and keep.*  
   *Two cloths in one hour is too many.*  
   *A fever that will not come down by morning is too long.*

   She pauses before line 9. Then she says the bracket. She says all of it — *seven or eight, take her in, say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why, do not wait for the morning*. She says it correctly. She says it in the bracket. The bracket included.

6. **She walks.** She doesn't go back to the hostel yet. She walks along the river instead, the Someș bank where there is no path exactly but there is ground that will hold her. The fog is on the water. December fog in the Someș valley: thick, white, sitting on the surface of the brown water. The water is running fast from the rains.

7. **She thinks about the paper.** The paper is in an evidence envelope in the Miliția's file room. It is state property now. The copy that came from the woman on the bus is now the property of the state, which does not understand what it has. It cannot be passed further. The chain that passed it is, as far as she knows, over. She doesn't know what happened to the woman on the bus; she doesn't know if she's safe; she's not going to try to find out.

8. **She thinks about Ana Petrescu.** Not for long. She didn't know Ana. She knows what happened. She thinks about it the way she thinks about most things: straight, without lingering. A girl died who shouldn't have. The paper that was supposed to help her had been changed in a way no one intended. The change traveled down the chain because the chain was working in the dark in a hurry for very good reasons. She doesn't say this. She thinks it and walks.

9. **The last beat.** She is walking along the river. The fog is around her. She has the whole document in her head — not written, not foldable, not findable, not confiscatable — and she is nineteen years old and she will be nineteen for several more months and she has no particular plan for today except to walk along the Someș until the fog comes up or she gets cold, whichever happens first.

---

## Outcome / turn

The chapter ends walking. The fog is on the river. What she is carrying is not paper. It cannot be copied wrong. It cannot propagate a mutation through eleven generations. It cannot be taken from her without taking her. The paper is what killed Ana. What she is carrying is what was in the paper. These are not the same thing.

## Continuity notes

- **CRITICAL:** The ending is not ambiguous. Doina is released. Ilinca is taken. The paper is gone. The memorized line is what survives. The drafter must not soften this — not by having Doina feel hope, not by hinting at anything resolving. Doina walks. That is what she does.
- **CRITICAL:** Doina does NOT know Ilinca's name or role until, possibly, later (after this chapter). The chapter takes place on the day of her release. She knows someone came forward. She doesn't know who. She doesn't find out in this chapter. The chapter is not about that.
- The first-person past voice: Doina's voice is the voice of someone who survived to tell this. The retrospective narrator is present throughout the book, but most present in this chapter. There should be a quality of: *I am telling you this from somewhere on the other side of it. I survived. Here is what it was.*
- **The bracket:** the drafter should reproduce the ninth element as Doina recites it from memory. Not as a block quote — as her speaking it, in motion, on the step. The form is memory, not document. The bracket is there because she read it right. She is the archive now.
- Ana Petrescu: Doina thinks about her briefly (beat 8). She didn't know her. She knows what happened. She thinks about it straight, without lingering. This is the voice of someone who has integrated the knowledge and is walking with it.
- The woman on the bus: Doina does NOT name her even in the retrospective narration. The silence holds. The woman is safe. Doina will never know if she's safe; she will not try to find out; the silence holds.
- The fog: the Someș valley fog in December. This is the same fog from the book's opening — November fog, December fog, the same fog the mill workers have been walking through all month. The last image is Doina in the fog on the river, carrying what she carries.
- **DO NOT end on a declarative statement about the book's meaning.** The chapter ends physically — Doina walking, fog, the river, whichever comes first (the fog coming up or getting cold). The argument is carried by the tree and by this final image. No character, including the narrator, names it.
