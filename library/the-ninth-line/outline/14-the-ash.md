# Chapter 14 — The Ash

**One-line summary:** Doina memorizes N14 in full by testing herself, burns the test copy in the ashtray, and sits for a while looking at the ash; the geography book still has the original.

**POV:** Doina Marcu — first person past  
**Date:** 17 November 1984 (Day 14)  
**Setting:** Doina's hostel room; the hostel communal bathroom  
**Chapter shape:** Bottle  
**Ending mode:** Coda  

---

## Goal / obstacle / beats

**Doina's goal this chapter:** Find out if she can remember the whole document from memory without looking at it.

**Obstacle:** She doesn't know if she wants to know the answer — because if she can, the geography book becomes something she could destroy, and destroying it means deciding that destroying it is the right thing, and she hasn't decided that.

**Beats:**

1. **Hostel room, her day off.** The room is cold; district heating came on for half an hour and stopped. She is in bed with her coat over the blanket. She has been thinking about the geography book since she woke up, which is to say she has been thinking about it since she woke up every morning for the past two weeks.

2. **She decides to test herself.** She finds a scrap of paper in her bag — it's the back of a utility calculation form, which is real paper she has a legitimate reason to hold. She tries to write down all nine elements from memory, without looking. She goes through lines 1 through 8. She gets line 4 slightly wrong on the second try. She gets it right on the third. Line 7: correct. Line 8: correct. She pauses before line 9.

3. **Line 9 from memory.** She writes the bracket. She writes what's inside it: *7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning.* She finishes writing it. She looks at it. She thinks: that's right. That's what it says.

4. **She goes to the hostel bathroom.** Nobody is there at this hour. She burns the test paper in the ashtray that lives on the windowsill — the communal bathroom has a window that opens over the yard; she burns the paper with a match and watches it go. It makes very little ash. The toilet flushes the rest.

5. **N14 PRINTED IN FULL — THIRD FULL PRINTING (of five total).**

   Back in her room, she takes the geography book out of her bag (she brought it home from the factory this one time, specifically for this) and reads N14 through once more, checking her memory against the original. Everything matches. The bracket is on the page exactly as she wrote it from memory. The document is in her head as well as in the geography book. She puts it back between pages 34 and 35.

   ```
   1. Keep her warm and lying down. Someone stays with her the whole night.
   2. Small sips of water, often. Nothing else by mouth for six hours.
   3. Count the cloths. Write the number and the hour on the paper by the bed.
   4. If she sleeps, wake her every hour and speak to her until she answers you properly.
   5. No visitors. No telephone. If anyone asks, she has the influenza.
   6. Burn nothing until she is well. Wash and keep.
   7. Two cloths in one hour is too many.
   8. A fever that will not come down by morning is too long.
      [ 7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why.
        Do not wait for the morning. ]
   ```

6. **She puts the geography book back.** She has brought it home and now she takes it back to the factory in her bag. It will go back in the locker tomorrow. She thinks: the paper is destroyed and not destroyed. The original is still there. She has a copy in her head. She hasn't decided what this means. She has only established the facts of it.

7. **The coda.** She sits on the cot with the ashtray in her lap, looking at the small pile of ash that used to be her handwriting. She is nineteen years old and she has just memorized a document that tells you when to stop managing a medical emergency at home and go to the hospital, and the context in which she memorized it is that following it correctly may save someone's life and following the wrong copy may end one, and she has been sitting with this context for two weeks without knowing what to do about it.

---

## Outcome / turn

The chapter ends with Doina sitting on the cot with the ashtray, ash on her lap, N14 in the geography book, the whole document in her head. She doesn't draw any conclusions. She is nineteen. She cleans out the ashtray and puts it back on the bathroom windowsill.

## New question raised

Now that Doina knows the document by heart, what is the relationship between the paper and the knowledge? Is the paper still necessary? And if she has both, what is she going to do?
