# Chapter 4 — The File Opens

**One-line summary:** Munteanu opens the case, drives to Ana's house, takes N17 from the sister-in-law, then lays it alongside a second copy he finds the same day; the differences between them are the most interesting thing on his desk.

**POV:** Vasile Munteanu — third person close, past tense  
**Date:** 6–7 November 1984 (Days 3–4)  
**Setting:** The Miliția district station; the road to the Petrescu house; Ana's house; a neighbor's apartment; Munteanu's desk  
**Chapter shape:** Single scene (one extended day of investigation)  
**Ending mode:** Turn  

---

## Goal / obstacle / beats

**Munteanu's goal this chapter:** Open the file, collect the physical evidence, and take an initial statement from the people who were present.

**Obstacle:** The sister-in-law is terrified. The neighbor is more terrified. The evidence is a piece of paper that, in terms of what Munteanu is expected to care about, tells him almost nothing.

**Beats:**

1. **The station, early morning.** The file was assigned to him yesterday as an administrative matter: a young woman, nineteen, died at the Spitalul Orășenesc following what the hospital's notification classified as "complications." Munteanu's captain has asked him to look at it; it will need either a determination of criminal liability or a formal closing. Munteanu takes the folder — thin, hospital form, death certificate, the names. He reads it in his car before driving.

2. **Driving to the Petrescu house.** The Someș is to his left; the valley fog has not lifted. He notes this the way he notes everything, without purpose. The Petrescu family lives on Strada Gării, two blocks from the station. He parks on the street.

3. **Ana's house — the sister-in-law (tentative name: Rodica).** She opens the door before he reaches it. She has been expecting this. He introduces himself: Locotenent Munteanu, Investigations Section. He says he is sorry for her loss, and means it in the particular way he means things — as a registered fact about what is appropriate in this room. He asks her to sit.

4. **The statement.** She gives him what she has: Ana came home ill on the night of the third; she stayed with her through the night; by noon she knew it was serious; she did not take her to the hospital until the evening of the fourth. She hesitated because going to the hospital is its own kind of problem. She does not say this, but it is in everything she says. He takes notes. He asks: was there a piece of paper? She says yes and goes to get it. N17. He takes it from her in an evidence envelope. He does not read it in front of her.

5. **He asks her who gave her the paper.** She says: her neighbor, a woman in the next bloc. She gives the name. He writes it.

6. **The neighbor's apartment, the same afternoon.** The neighbor (tentative name: Vera) is even more frightened. Her hands are not still. He asks for her copy; she retrieves it from somewhere inside the apartment — a drawer in the kitchen, or a book — and gives it to him. He receives it in a second evidence envelope.

7. **Back at his desk, the station.** He opens both envelopes. He lays N17 and the neighbor's copy (a G4 or G5 copy on the same branch) side by side on his desk. He reads them, not rapidly. Line 9 on both copies: the same wording — *if 7 or 8, take her in in the morning*. He begins to read the other lines. Line 4 is worded slightly differently between the two copies. The handwriting is different. These are not duplicates. One was copied from the other, or both were copied from something further up.

8. **The recognition.** He has seen forged documents before; he has seen copied documents before. He has not before seen a document that varies between copies in the way a genealogy varies between generations — each generation preserving everything essential, acquiring small errors, losing small things. He stacks the two copies and holds them up to the lamp. His thought is flat: whoever wrote the first one is still out there.

---

## Outcome / turn

The chapter ends with the observation that the differences between copies are *inherited* — his term for it, not a diagram, not a word he says aloud. He picks up a pen. He opens the folder to a fresh page. He begins with the sister-in-law's neighbor's name.

---

## New question raised

If each copy is descended from the one before it, where does the chain stop going back? And why does line 9 say *in the morning* when line 8 already accounts for what to do with a fever that lasts through the night?

## Continuity notes

- **DO NOT quote N17 in full in this chapter.** Fragment only — the drafter may quote line 9 ("take her in in the morning" or similar) and perhaps one other line, but the full document text of N17 is printed in Chapter 9 only, adjacent to N1. If Munteanu reads the full document to himself in this chapter, the drafter should summarize it in prose rather than block-quoting it.
- Munteanu is not a monster and not secretly on anyone's side. He is a good policeman who has found an interesting problem. His interiority runs in case procedure: what will survive being described to his captain, what won't, and whether the distinction matters.
- The sister-in-law: her name is not established in canon. The drafter can use "Rodica" tentatively, but note that this is a placeholder — the architect may have set a specific name.
- Munteanu's voice is third person PAST (not present). Deliberate, restrained, procedural. This is the pipeline's own default register — the house voice. It should read slightly more literary than Ilinca's; it is the voice of the state's instruments.
- The chapter ends on a flat recognition, not a dramatic one. "He picks up a pen." The flatness is the register.
- **SAFETY:** The document Munteanu reads is aftercare only. He reads it as a piece of evidence that tells him something about the chain of transmission; he does not find in it anything medically specific. His observation is about the *differences* between copies, not the document's content.
