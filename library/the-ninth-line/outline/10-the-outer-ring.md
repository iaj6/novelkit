# Chapter 10 — The Outer Ring

**One-line summary:** Doina at work two weeks after Ana's death; two women from the floor have been called in; she speaks to no one; after the shift she walks home in the river fog.

**POV:** Doina Marcu — first person past  
**Date:** 13 November 1984 (Day 10)  
**Setting:** The mill production floor; the break room; the road home along the river  
**Chapter shape:** Multi-scene  
**Ending mode:** Literary fade  

---

## Goal / obstacle / beats

**Doina's goal this chapter:** Get through the shift without attracting attention; understand how close the investigation is.

**Obstacle:** The mill floor is a place where everyone knows everyone's business, which means everyone is currently pretending not to know what they know. This is exhausting in its own way.

**Beats:**

1. **Pre-dawn transit to the factory.** Doina walking on the bridge over the Someș. Fog down on the water. The smell of the mill from two hundred meters — the hot-wet chemistry of celofiber production meeting the November cold. She has been on the night shift for two years; she knows this smell the way she knows her own locker. It is not a bad smell. It is just the smell of being here, which is where she is.

2. **On the floor, first hours.** The loom noise makes private talk impossible. She prefers it this way. She watches the other women and makes a private inventory of who looks like they haven't slept. Three candidates. She doesn't know what happened to them; she only knows what their faces say, which is that something has happened.

3. **The break room.** She gets her tea and sits at the far end of the table. A woman she knows from her bloc — Marta, who works the adjacent loom — sits down nearby and says, without looking at her: they took Floarea and Geta in on Monday. Both from the floor, both from the section that does the finishing. Doina says: what did they ask them? Marta says: what do you think they asked them? She picks up her tea and leaves.

4. **Doina processes this.** Floarea and Geta. She knows Floarea by face; she has worked beside her for six months. She does not know if Floarea has a copy of the paper. She does not know if Floarea knows anyone who has a copy. She does not know if Floarea knows Doina exists in this context. She may not. The network was never a network in the sense of everyone knowing everyone else. It was: one person knowing one person, and the paper traveling between those links.

5. **Second half of the shift.** She works. She is good at this, as it turns out — at keeping her hands moving when her mind is somewhere else. The loom does not care what she is thinking.

6. **After the shift, the road home.** Light coming up over the hills behind the factory. The fog is still down on the river. She walks along the Someș, which is faster but also quieter, fewer people. She thinks: the geography book is in her locker. The locker is locked. The copy is in the geography book. The man in the good coat has been asking questions for two weeks. He has been to two women on her section. He has not been to her locker. She does not know if "not yet" is the same as "not going to."

---

## Outcome / turn

The chapter ends with Doina on the road beside the Someș in the cold, fog rising off the water, walking toward the hostel. The chapter has not answered whether she is safe. It has answered only that she doesn't know whether she is safe, which is not the same thing.

## Continuity notes

- Floarea and Geta are minor characters — they exist to show the mill being questioned, not to become recurring figures. They may be mentioned again but don't need names beyond these.
- Doina does not know that the women being questioned are on a different branch of the network (the N4 branch) rather than her own branch. She just knows they were questioned.
- The fog on the Someș is a recurring atmospheric detail. Establish it here as the November valley fog that persists through the morning.
- **The geography book is in her FACTORY LOCKER** — this is the key continuity fact that ch 18 depends on. Confirm here. The drafter should have Doina note explicitly that it is at the factory, not at the hostel.
- The `concealing` epistemic stance: Doina knows her copy is clean and knows where she got it (the woman on the bus). She does not name the woman on the bus, even in her first-person narration, because she is protecting her. The first-person narrator of ch 10 is the Doina who survived and knows the whole story — but even in retrospect, she does not name the woman on the bus. The silence holds in the narrative voice too.
