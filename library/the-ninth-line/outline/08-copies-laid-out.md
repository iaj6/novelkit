# Chapter 8 — Copies Laid Out

**One-line summary:** Munteanu traces one step backward from N17, collects a second physical copy, and lays all his copies side by side; the differences are inherited, not random, and line 9 is consistent across everything he holds.

**POV:** Vasile Munteanu — third person close, past tense  
**Date:** 11 November 1984 (Day 8)  
**Setting:** The Miliția district station; his desk; a woman's apartment in the mill district  
**Chapter shape:** Multi-scene  
**Ending mode:** Mid-action-cut  

---

## Goal / obstacle / beats

**Munteanu's goal this chapter:** Trace one generation backward from the copies he has, and confirm or deny his hypothesis that copy differences are inherited rather than random.

**Obstacle:** The woman whose name the sister-in-law's neighbor gave him is frightened and may not have a copy anymore. People have been burning things.

**Beats:**

1. **His desk, morning.** He has the two copies he collected in ch 4. He reads them again before going out. He is looking at the differences between them with more method now: he has made a small notation — not a diagram, not labeled, but a written observation in the margin of his case notes: *variants consistent in line 9; variants differ in lines 3, 4 (handwriting)* and one other minor variation. He is not a graphologist. But a man who has spent twelve years reading statements knows when handwriting has been generated by two different hands.

2. **He goes to the next name.** The woman whose name the neighbor gave him lives in a three-story bloc near the mill workers' market. He knocks at ten in the morning when most workers on the night shift are home. She opens the door and he can see she has been expecting something like this.

3. **The interview.** She is cooperative — her cooperation has the texture of someone who has decided that the safest behavior is to give the Miliția exactly what the Miliția is looking for. She has a copy. It is in a kitchen drawer. She gives it to him. He takes it in an envelope.

4. **At the station, the comparison.** He now has three copies (N17, N16 from ch 4, and this new one — a G4 copy on the poisoned branch, let the architect assign the specific node). He lays them on his desk and weights the corners with what is nearest — a stapler and some tins of shoe polish. Read separately they say roughly the same thing. Read together they do not. The differences between copies 1 and 2 are smaller than the differences between copies 2 and 3. This means copies 1 and 2 are closer together in the genealogy, and copy 3 is further back. This is not a complicated observation; it is simple arithmetic.

5. **Line 9.** On all three copies: *in the morning*. The exact words vary slightly — one copy says "if 7 or 8, take her in in the morning," another has a different verb — but the concept is consistent: morning. A woman who was supposed to go to the hospital before morning was following a copy that said *morning*. This means the morning instruction didn't enter the chain here, in these copies. It was upstream of all of them.

6. **He writes the next name.** The woman whose copy he collected today tells him she got hers from a woman she works with on the floor. That name is in the notes. He picks up his coat.

---

## Outcome / turn

The chapter cuts in the motion of picking up the coat — Munteanu has three copies, a name, and a direction. He is going backward faster than he expected. He is also recording more names per day than he expected. He notes, without arriving at a conclusion about what to do with the observation, that the copies circulated widely and that closing the full chain will mean many more names in this file before he is done.

---

## New question raised

Munteanu's case is now clearly something more than an administrative formality. The chain he is tracing is real and extensive. His captain does not yet know how many names are going to end up in this file.

## Continuity notes

- **DO NOT quote any of these copies in full.** Munteanu's chapter 4 and 8 are both "fragment only" chapters. The full text of any node is reserved for chapters 9 (N1 + N17), 14 (N14), and 28 (N1 + N4). Fragments from Munteanu's copies should be line 9 excerpts or minor differences in lines 1–6 — enough to show the comparison without printing the document.
- The stapler and tins of shoe polish: this is from the brief's exemplar passage for Munteanu's voice. Use exactly this detail — "he laid the four sheets side by side on the desk and weighted the corners with whatever was nearest, which turned out to be a stapler and three unopened tins of shoe polish." Adapt as needed for three sheets rather than four.
- Munteanu's interiority: case procedure. What will and will not survive being described to his captain. He is interesting in this puzzle the way a man is interesting in a puzzle that fits the shape of his brain. He is not emotional about it.
- The mid-action-cut ending: he picks up his coat and the chapter ends. He is going to knock on another door. The reader doesn't see that door until chapter 12.
- The woman he interviews today may have received her copy from someone on the N4 → G3 branch. The exact node identity is for the architect.
