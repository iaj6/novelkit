# Chapter 12 — Fourth Generation

**One-line summary:** Munteanu traces backward to N11 (the regularised copy, G4); the mutation "in the morning" is documented in his case notes; one more step back will put him in the third generation.

**POV:** Vasile Munteanu — third person close, past tense  
**Date:** 15 November 1984 (Day 12)  
**Setting:** An apartment in the mill district; the Miliția station; his desk  
**Chapter shape:** Multi-scene  
**Ending mode:** Cliffhanger  

---

## Goal / obstacle / beats

**Munteanu's goal this chapter:** Trace backward one more generation from the copies he has and identify the source of the "in the morning" wording in line 9.

**Obstacle:** The woman who holds the copy he needs to trace has not yet been formally identified. He has a referral from the woman he interviewed in ch 8, but referrals at this stage of an investigation are incomplete: she only knows a first name and a section of the factory.

**Beats:**

1. **The station, morning.** He lays out his copies again. He has three (N17, N16, and the G4/G5 copy from ch 8). He also has the two names he's written down from the trailing referrals. He thinks about the shape of what he's building. He is not building a diagram. He is building a list of names in order of their distance from the original, and he is working from the far end.

2. **Finding the next woman.** He goes to the mill's personnel section with a formal request: he needs to identify a night-shift worker by her given name and section. The personnel officer is nervous but compliant; this is what personnel officers do when the Miliția comes. He gets the full name and bloc address.

3. **The interview, mid-morning.** He goes to her apartment. She is not home; she is on night shift and has been sleeping. She wakes up when he knocks and opens the door in her robe. He is correct with her: Locotenent Munteanu, Investigations Section. He would like to speak with her about a matter related to the Petrescu case. She sits down without being asked.

4. **The copy she holds.** She has it. It is in a kitchen tin with a sugar label. She hands it over without his asking — she had clearly been preparing herself for this moment. He takes it: N11. This is the regularised copy. He does not know it by that designation; he knows it as the fourth copy he has held, and it is substantially further back in the genealogy than his previous copies.

5. **He reads N11.** He does not read it aloud. He reads it with the same method as the others: line by line, margin to margin. Line 9: *If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.* The same. The mutation was present in this copy. He makes a note.

6. **The observation he writes.** Back at his desk: he writes, in his case notes (not in the formal dosar penal, which his captain will eventually see — in his own working notes): *The instruction "in the morning" is present in all four copies examined. It is consistent with a mutation introduced at or before generation 4 of the copy chain.* He has now framed the problem correctly without using the word "generation" or any analogy he hasn't invented. He just says: *at or before this copy.*

7. **The next name.** The woman told him where she got N11. The name she gave is a woman who works the laundry on Strada Mihai. He writes it. He picks up the telephone — a risk, but a small one at this stage — and confirms the address from the Population Section.

---

## Outcome / turn

The chapter ends with Munteanu writing the address: the laundry on Strada Mihai. He is about to leave when his case notes catch his eye — specifically the word *generation*, which he did not write in the official notes but thought in the interval. He stands at his desk. He is in G4 territory. One more step and he will be in G3. G3 is one step from the beginning.

## Continuity notes

- **`copy-n11` is the regularised copy.** Its line 9 reads exactly: `9. If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.` This is the fatal mutation. The drafter must use this exact wording when Munteanu reads it. N11's lines 1–8 are identical to N1. Only line 9 differs.
- **DO NOT quote N11 in full.** Fragment only. The drafter should quote line 9 and perhaps one other line in fragment, showing comparison — but NOT the full document text.
- Munteanu does not understand what the mutation *means* (the bracket vs. the numbered line; the override vs. the step). He simply notes it is consistent. He is `unaware` of `ninth-line-is-a-warning-not-a-step` and will remain so for the entire book. He sees a pattern in the copies; he does not understand the semantics of the pattern.
- The laundry on Strada Mihai: the drafter should use this as the address Munteanu plans to visit next. The connection to the cellar woman's sister (the G3 node Ilinca identified in ch 11) should be visible to the careful reader — both investigations are converging on the same person.
- The working notes vs. the formal dosar penal: Munteanu keeps working notes for himself (a personal copy-book) and a formal dosar for the procuror. The distinction matters — his working notes are more candid; the formal file has to be defensible upward.
