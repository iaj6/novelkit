# Chapter 29 — The Deadline

**One-line summary:** December 5 — the deadline day — Munteanu writes his report; the file is complete; the copies go into evidence; Doina has been released; he closes the folder.

**POV:** Vasile Munteanu — third person close, past tense  
**Date:** 5 December 1984 (Day 32)  
**Setting:** The Miliția station; Munteanu's desk  
**Chapter shape:** Interiority-heavy  
**Ending mode:** Coda  

---

## Goal / obstacle / beats

**Munteanu's goal this chapter:** Write the formal determination and close the dosar penal.

**Obstacle:** There is no practical obstacle. The case is resolved. The obstacle, if there is one, is procedural precision — getting the formal language right — and perhaps something else he is choosing not to name.

**Beats:**

1. **His desk, morning.** December 5. He was given until today. He is at his desk before the office fills. He has the formal dosar penal open in front of him. He also has his working notebook, the evidence envelopes, and his coffee (nechezol, which nobody likes, which is what there is).

2. **The report.** He writes the determination. The formal language requires: the nature of the death (complications of an illegal procedure), the document involved, the origin of the document, the chain of transmission, and the responsible party. He writes it carefully. He has been writing reports for twelve years. He is good at this.

3. **He describes the chain.** He names the nodes he has confirmed: the names of the women who held copies, in order from the origin down to Ana's household. He has names for most of them. The ones who refused to give names are recorded as such. The woman on the bus — Doina's source — is recorded as "unidentified, bus route 4, spring 1984. Contact not established." He does not push on this. Doina's name is in the file but not as the responsible party. She is listed as a holder of a document on a clean branch. She has been released.

4. **He describes the mutation.** He has two documents on his desk: N1 (the master) and N4 (the cellar copy). He describes the mutation in his report: the ninth element of the document was transcribed by the originator as a numbered line rather than a marginal notation, in a second copy made under conditions of haste and insufficient light. The second copy propagated through the chain. Two generations later, the regularized wording of line 9 introduced the instruction "in the morning." He writes this as fact. He does not editorialize.

5. **What he sees on his desk.** The two documents are there. The bracket on N1 and the nine on N4. He has read both a dozen times since yesterday afternoon. He is a careful reader. He knows the words of both versions by now. He also knows what line 8 says — "a fever that will not come down by morning is too long" — and what line 9 (in the bracket version) says: *do not wait for the morning*. He knows this. He does not write anything in his report about what the mutation meant in practical terms. That is not the report he is writing.

6. **He puts the copies in evidence envelopes.** All eleven copies he has collected. N1 and N4 last. He labels each envelope with the chain position and the holder's name. He seals them. He puts the envelopes in the evidence file.

7. **He thinks about what the file is.** The file is complete. It contains the origin, the chain, the mutation, the death, the responsible party. Everything required by procedure is in it. Everything the captain needs to close the case is in it. He is a competent policeman and he has done his job correctly.

8. **He thinks about what is not in the file.** The file does not contain the reason the document existed. It does not contain the reason women needed to pass a piece of paper hand to hand rather than going to a hospital. It does not contain the reason the cellar was dark and the time was insufficient. These things are not his file's job to contain. He knows this. He is not comfortable with it and he knows that too.

9. **He calls his captain.** He says: the determination in the Petrescu matter is on its way to you by messenger. The captain says: good. He says: was it as complicated as it seemed? Munteanu says: it was exactly as complicated as it seemed. The captain says: good.

---

## Outcome / turn

The coda: Munteanu closes the folder. He straightens the case materials on his desk. He drinks what is left of the nechezol, which is cold and does not improve. Outside the window, the Someș valley. The file goes out. The case is closed on the deadline day. He did not send up an innocent name. He sent up the correct one. He is not comfortable. He goes home at six.

---

## New question raised

Nothing — this is the coda. The case is closed. What remains is Doina walking out, which is the final chapter.

## Epistemic events (this chapter)

- The reader holds all propositions as known. Munteanu holds `ana-copy-descends-from-ilinca-cellar-copy` as `knows`. He holds `ninth-line-is-a-warning-not-a-step` as `unaware` throughout — even in this chapter, he writes about the mutation as a transmission error, not as a semantic error. He has closed the case correctly and he has not understood what the case was about in the way the reader understands it. This is the book's final irony: the man who solved the tree never saw what was in it.

## Continuity notes

- The coda ending: quiet, physical, no resolution that resolves anything larger than the file. Munteanu closes the folder, drinks the cold nechezol, goes home. The book does not end here; ch 30 ends it. But this chapter should feel like one door closing before another opens.
- The chapter does NOT describe what happens to Ilinca beyond: she has been taken, she is in Munteanu's file as the responsible party. What happens to her from here is not in Munteanu's chapter.
- The chapter does NOT describe Doina walking out. That is ch 30. If Munteanu notes that she was released, it's procedural and brief.
- Do NOT have Munteanu articulate the book's argument. He does not know what the document was for, what it meant, or what the state's role was. He knows what his file says. The file is a correct description of a transmission chain and a mutation.
- The chapter should be written at Munteanu's register: procedural, careful, restrained. The moments of discomfort (beats 8 and 9) should be rendered as flatness, not as emotion.
- The nechezol: a physical anchor. He drinks it cold. Nobody likes it. It is what there is.
