# Chapter 5 — Elena

**One-line summary:** On her day off, Ilinca traces N3 through a second-hand message to Elena; she also thinks about the cellar copy, believes it was correctly made, and lies down without lighting the second lamp.

**POV:** Ilinca Roșca — third person close, present tense  
**Date:** 8 November 1984 (Day 5)  
**Setting:** Ilinca's apartment; the market queue; a cousin's flat on the production-floor district  
**Chapter shape:** Interiority-heavy (she spends most of the chapter thinking, with physical errands as the frame)  
**Ending mode:** Literary fade  

---

## Goal / obstacle / beats

**Ilinca's goal this chapter:** Trace N3 without approaching Elena directly.

**Obstacle:** Elena works a different shift and lives on the other side of the river. Direct contact is riskier than contact through a mutual acquaintance. The acquaintance may not understand what she's carrying.

**Beats:**

1. **Her apartment, morning.** Day off. She does not sleep late; she does not sleep late in November under any circumstances because the bloc heating comes on at seven for two hours and she wants to be warm while it lasts. She drinks nechezol standing at the kitchen window. The Someș is behind the fog. She thinks: what she knows. N2 branch: the supervisor and Livia. That is two nodes, both confirmed. N3 branch: Elena, production floor. She needs to reach Elena.

2. **The bread queue.** She joins it before dawn — there are already a dozen people ahead of her. She knows three of them by face; she does not know which of them would mention to their bloc committee that they saw Ilinca Roșca in the bread line on a Thursday when Ilinca normally goes on Tuesdays. She does not think about this. She stands in the queue. The cold is not unusual.

3. **She finds a route to Elena.** Her cousin — a woman who works on the production floor, no connection to the document — was at Elena's bloc last month for a nameday gathering. Ilinca goes to her cousin's flat in the afternoon, ostensibly to return a borrowed pot. The errand is real; the pot is real. While she is there, she asks her cousin to tell Elena that Ilinca said to keep the influenza quiet and not come to work if she's unwell. The message is innocent. Its meaning is: do not say anything to anyone, and everything is fine. Her cousin will deliver it. Her cousin does not know what it means.

4. **The cousin's flat, the reply.** The reply comes back within an hour via the cousin's daughter, who runs it around the corner: Elena says she is fine, she has been careful, she hasn't seen anyone she doesn't know. This means: N3 is safe. Elena has not been contacted. N3's branch is accounted for.

5. **Walking home.** She has now traced N2 and N3. Both branches are clean. Which leaves the cellar copy. November 3, 1983. She does not say the woman's name to herself. She thinks about the cellar. The door cracked to the street lamp's light. The woman waiting. The hurry. She counted the lines. She put the bracket. She copied the bracket. She was in a hurry and she was careful and she copied it correctly. She is almost certain.

6. **Her apartment, evening.** One lamp on. She sits in the kitchen. The adding machine is at the mill; here there is only a pen and a notebook she uses for grocery calculations. She thinks about writing something down and does not. She lies down on the bed without eating. The second lamp stays off. She is almost certain.

---

## Outcome / turn

The chapter ends without action — Ilinca lying in the dark, almost certain. The gap between *almost* and *certain* is the size of one night in a coal cellar in November 1983, but she doesn't know that yet.

---

## New question raised

Does *almost certain* mean *correct*? Ilinca has been careful all her working life and has never been wrong. She is not accustomed to the possibility that she could be wrong in a way that does not show up in a column.
