# Style — The Ninth Line

## POV and tense

Three POVs, each with its own grammatical contract:

- **Ilinca — third person close, present tense.** Her interiority moves in columns and tallies. The present tense is there because she does not know how her collation ends. Sentences are short and declarative under pressure; longer only where the counting takes time.
- **Doina — first person, past tense.** She survived to tell it; the reader should feel that as ground before it is confirmed. Longer sentences; room for digression; wry, honest, nineteen.
- **Munteanu — third person close, past tense.** Deliberately the pipeline's own default literary register — restrained, procedural, careful. The irony is never explained: the state's investigator speaks in the voice the model reaches for by default.

These three voices must remain genuinely distinct throughout. A chapter header is a date only; the voice must be legible before the end of the first sentence.

## Register

Cold, exact, and physical. Weather. Paper. Hands. The smell of a synthetic-fiber mill in November — hot-wet chemistry and the cold coming in through every door. Short declaratives under pressure; longer sentences only in Doina's voice, where being nineteen allows digression.

Fear is rendered as procedure: what characters do with their hands, which door they use, what they do not write down, what they say is the influenza. Fear is almost never named. The reader assembles the political situation from its effects — the reporting requirement, the monthly examination, the queue, the fact that a telephone is a risk — not from any character's explanation of it. **Nobody explains the regime to anyone else, because everyone already lives there.**

## Narrative distance

Close third for Ilinca and Munteanu. First person for Doina. In all three cases: the narrative distance lives *inside* the character's professional or personal idiom. Ilinca's interiority runs in accounting categories. Munteanu's in case procedure and what will and will not survive being described to his captain. Doina's in the voice of someone recounting something they are still figuring out.

## Dialogue

Dialogue is sparse but load-bearing. Characters in this book do not talk to manage their feelings; they talk to transact information or, more often, to withhold it. Dialogue tags are minimal — said, asked, no more. No dialogue is followed by a paragraph interpreting it.

## Chapter structure

No prologue. No epigraph. No framing device. A date is the only chapter header. Chapters are not self-contained episodes; they are stations in two simultaneous collations. Chapters in Ilinca's and Munteanu's voices are procedural; Doina's chapters are the only ones with room for reflection.

Endings: mixed by design. Quiet endings where the chapter wants them; hard cuts where it does not. Do not settle into a uniform closing mode.

## Quotation from the documents

**The full text of any node is printed at most four times across the entire book**: the master (N1) early, Ana's copy (N17) beside it at the reveal-to-reader, Doina's clean copy (N14) once, and the master and cellar copy (N4) together at the climax. Everywhere else: fragments only, attributed to the specific node the scene's character holds. A fragment quoted must be the fragment as *that character's copy* renders it — which may differ by exactly one word or formatting choice from every other node. This is the book's central discipline.

Before re-quoting any node in a chapter, `read_record` it and reproduce it exactly. **The 22 nodes differ from each other by design; no editor or repair pass may reconcile, harmonise, or "correct" any variant toward any other.** The line `9. If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.` in `copy-n11` and `copy-n17` is correct as written. It is the murder weapon.

## Failure modes

The AI-author patterns most tempting in this story's register. The drafter should actively avoid these; the compression pass should cut them.

- **Sentence-after-the-sentence** — an image lands in a declarative; the next sentence glosses what it means. This register wants the image to sit without explanation. The glossing sentence is almost always the one to delete.
- **Meta-commentary on cognition** — "She registered." "He noticed." "She felt the familiar weight of." The prose already shows what the character is doing; labelling the cognitive act softens the cold-procedure register this book depends on.
- **Post-scene subtext naming** — a paragraph after a scene that names what the scene was *really* about. This book's argument is carried entirely by the document tree, never by a character — or narrator — articulating it. Any sentence that begins to say what the mutations mean is an instance of this failure.
- **Thesis restatement** — an abstract sentence re-stating the book's argument about state culpability, conditions, or what the paper means. The brief is explicit: "that is the argument of the book, and it must be carried entirely by the tree of copies — never stated by a character." The narrator obeys the same rule.
- **Redundant transition / re-summary** — a paragraph at the opening or close of a chapter that recaps what the reader already knows about where the collation stands. The two ladders (nodes held, days elapsed) are the clock; the reader tracks them. Restating them in prose is padding.

## Lean-into patterns

Patterns the drafter and editors should NOT treat as failures, because this register depends on them.

- **Variants that differ from each other by a single word or a formatting mark** — the 22 document nodes intentionally diverge. Across-chapter differences in how a line appears are not continuity errors; they are the book's mechanism. Never smooth them.
- **`copy-n11` and `copy-n17` saying "in the morning"** — this is correct. It is the fatal mutation. Any pass that edits this line toward the master is destroying the book's subject.
- **Doina's longer, wry, digressive sentences** — her register is not the book's default. A compression pass should not shorten Doina's chapters toward Ilinca's cadence. The length is the nineteen.
- **Procedural repetition in Ilinca's voice** — she counts the same kinds of things more than once. Counting is her idiom; it is not redundancy when it is in character.
