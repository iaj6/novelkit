# Bibliography — The Ninth Line

All sources consulted during the research phase. Sources marked **(fetched)** were directly
retrieved and read; sources marked **(search-summary)** were not successfully retrieved but
contributed via web-search result excerpts. The distinction matters: fetched sources are more
reliable; search-summary sources should be verified if their claims become load-bearing.

- `[cite:01]` — "Decree 770." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decree_770.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: core provisions of the decree, mandatory monthly gynecological
  examinations, removal of contraceptives from sale, pregnancy monitoring requirement.

- `[cite:02]` — Multiple secondary sources synthesized from web-search excerpts, primarily:
  "Illegal Abortion: Lessons from Romania" (brokenwallsandnarratives.wordpress.com);
  "Romania ends compulsory childbearing," PubMed/NCBI (pmid:12222213); "The human cost of
  unsafe abortions," Our World in Data (ourworldindata.org). **(Search-summary.)**
  Used for: year-by-year maternal mortality figures (132.1 in 1980; 137.4 in 1985; 169.4 in 1989),
  the statistic that 87% of maternal deaths were due to illegal abortion, the Securitate hovering
  at hospitals, the 1984 action plan described in search results.

- `[cite:03]` — "Abortion in Romania." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Romania.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: physician obligations (24-hour notification to prosecutor), the
  Disciplinary Board for Health Personnel, how sympathetic doctors falsified diagnoses, the
  figure of 9,000+ women dead 1965–1989, the 1986 formal requirement for annual workplace
  exams (and the earlier de facto monthly standard), the statistic that the 1989 rate was the
  highest ever recorded in Europe.

- `[cite:04]` — "Guardians of the Decree: The Hidden World of the Anti-Abortion Enforcers."
  Decree Chronicles (decreechronicles.com). https://decreechronicles.com/guardians-of-the-decree-the-hidden-world-of-the-anti-abortion-enforcers/.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: 1984 secret action plan (approved by Minister Homoștean); the placement
  of 54 doctors as Securitate informants in Cluj County; Securitate agents stationed near operating
  theatres; interrogation of dying women at bedsides; the five-day withholding of treatment case;
  the 83 doctors and 187 health workers indicted 1967–1973.

- `[cite:05]` — "Searching in History: Decree 770 of Ceausescu." Blog post.
  https://searchinginhistory.blogspot.com/2014/01/decree-770-of-ceausescu.html.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: penalty of 1–3 months imprisonment for physicians who failed to report;
  women required to name the person who helped them before receiving treatment; mandatory monthly
  pregnancy test at workplace; women who self-induced abortion faced 6 months to 2 years
  imprisonment.

- `[cite:06]` — "Romanians suffer — one 40-watt lightbulb per household — to pay nation's debt."
  Christian Science Monitor, 25 January 1984.
  https://www.csmonitor.com/1984/0125/012524.html.
  **(Fetched — contemporary primary source.)** Used for: direct eyewitness account of Bucharest
  in January 1984; one 40-watt bulb per household; food prices increased 35%; broken eggs in paper
  cups; freezer-burned chickens from a van; police and armed soldiers on every street corner;
  government's 50% energy consumption reduction target; "no protests."

- `[cite:07]` — "1980s austerity policy in Romania." Wikipedia.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980s_austerity_policy_in_Romania.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: electricity and district heating cuts; hot water limited to one day/week;
  indoor heaters banned (whole-building power cuts for violations); neonatal deaths from incubator
  power cuts in winter 1983; standard of living fallen 19–40% by 1983; December 1983 new dietary
  program for 1984.

- `[cite:08]` — "Adidași and Nechezol: Food Deprivation in Communist Romania."
  The Romanian Table blog. https://theromaniantable.wordpress.com/2021/02/25/adidasi-and-nechezol-food-deprivation-in-communist-romania/.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: specific ration quantities (500g pork/beef, 1kg chicken, 10 eggs, 100g
  butter, 1kg oil, 1kg sugar, 500g cheese per month); nechezol as coffee substitute; adidași
  (pig feet); soy salami; Vietnamese shrimp chips; criminalization of hoarding in 1981; urban vs.
  rural access differential.

- `[cite:09]` — "Cartela or monthly ration in Communist Romania." Blog post.
  https://a.zamo.ca/2022/01/cartela-or-monthly-ration-in-communist.html.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: the December 1980 formal rationing decree; monthly and three-month card
  variants; the "Rational Nutrition Programme" 2,700–2,800 calorie claim; bread rationing timeline
  from 1982; practical experience that even ration quantities were often unavailable.

- `[cite:10]` — "Bread rations in '80s Romania." 2celsius.org.
  https://2celsius.org/media/bread-rations-in-80s-romania/.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: 300g/day bread ration; non-transferable ration tickets; "bread walks"
  as family institution; rations continued until Christmas Eve 1989.

- `[cite:11]` — "Dej." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dej.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: location (Cluj County, 60km north of Cluj-Napoca); river confluence;
  population figures (32,345 in 1977; 41,216 in 1992); elevation approximately 285m; salt mining
  at Ocna Dej; communist regime "brought fundamental transformations"; former mayor Cornel Pop
  died in prison 1953.

- `[cite:12]` — "Someș." Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Some%C8%99.
  **(Fetched.)** Used for: the river's total length (415 km); the confluence of Someșul Mic and
  Someșul Mare at Mica, ~4 km upstream of Dej; the river's course through Dej, Jibou, Satu Mare;
  alluvial gold in the Someșul Mare valley; 1970 flooding.
