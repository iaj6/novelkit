# Characters — The Ninth Line

## POV characters

### Ilinca Roșca — POV 1, third person present
**Age:** 41  
**Role:** Chief bookkeeper (contabil-șef) at the textile mill in Dej  
**Background:** Born 1943; grew up in the Someș valley. Has worked the mill's accounts for fifteen years. Unmarried, lives alone in a two-room apartment on the third floor of a bloc a fifteen-minute walk from the factory. She has never been caught doing anything.

**Inner drives:** Ilinca's mind runs in columns: incoming, outgoing, balance. She organises information the way she organises ledger entries — every item has a place, a provenance, a corresponding document. This is not coldness; it is how she manages terror. The paper exists because she could not stop thinking, after a woman she knew did not go to the hospital in time, about the decision and whether it could be made better. She made it better. She made it worse. She does not know yet.

What she wants is to undo one night in a coal cellar without undoing everything that followed from it. She will discover, chapter by chapter, that the two things cannot be separated. The climax is her choosing not to separate them — handing Munteanu both documents, the master and the cellar copy, which exonerates Doina and ends Ilinca.

**Voice:** Close third person, present tense. Spare declaratives. Her interiority runs in procedures and tallies — "line nine is not" is a thought and a fact simultaneously. She notices things in the order she notices them, which is the order she can act on them. She does not name her fear; she catalogues its symptoms.

**Relationships:**  
- The women she has handed copies to, personally, over two years — each known by name, each a counted entry in a private ledger she has never written down  
- The woman in the coal cellar (November 3, 1983): unnamed, unnamed deliberately; Ilinca knew her, made the copy fast, did not check it after  
- Munteanu: does not know him until Act III, when he comes to the mill

### Doina Marcu — POV 2, first person past
**Age:** 19  
**Role:** Night-shift worker at the textile mill  
**Background:** Born 1965; from a village outside Dej, moved to town two years ago when the factory offered her a place in a workers' hostel. She is intelligent and bored, which is a dangerous combination under the circumstances. She has never been in any trouble that mattered. She has a copy of the paper she has never used, folded inside a geography book, because the woman on the bus gave it to her before she could think of a reason to say no.

**Inner drives:** Doina wants to survive and she wants to be the kind of person who deserved to. She is funny — not funny the way characters in this library have been, which is wry-and-restrained, but genuinely funny, capable of a digression that is also the truth. Her first-person past tense is the voice of someone who survived and is recounting it straight, without the benefit of having been smart at the time. Her silence about the woman on the bus is not heroic self-sacrifice; it is the thing the world she lives in trained her to do, and she is not fully sure why she is doing it even as she does it.

**Voice:** First person past. Longer sentences than Ilinca; room to digress; wry observation that lands sideways on the terror. She can say things that would read as dark comedy if the reader did not already know about Ana. She is never arch; she is nineteen and honest.

**Relationships:**  
- The woman on the bus: never named, never found, never appears; Doina's silence about her is the source of Doina's danger and the book's sharpest irony  
- Ana Petrescu: did not know her; knows of her only as the reason Munteanu is knocking on doors  
- Ilinca: on the same shift rotation; knows her as the bookkeeper, no more, until Act III

### Lieutenant Vasile Munteanu — POV 3, third person past
**Age:** 34  
**Role:** Miliția lieutenant, Investigations Section, Dej district  
**Background:** Born 1950. Career Miliția officer; joined at 22. Married, two children; lives in a Miliția-assigned apartment. He is not a party enthusiast. He is a man who is good at his job and has organised his life around the fact of being good at his job, and who has not looked too carefully at what his job serves.

**Inner drives:** Munteanu is a puzzle-solver, and the copies are the most interesting puzzle he has been given. He has worked out that differences between copies are inherited, not random — that a family of documents can be collated, like a genealogy. He is right. He is pursuing the truth, in good faith, which is the thing that makes him dangerous rather than merely menacing. He understands, with the flatness the brief names, that his arithmetic will lead him somewhere his captain will not like, and he is going to follow it anyway.

What he cannot see — what the reader sees and he does not — is the difference between solving the tree and deciding what the tree means. He never holds the proposition `ninth-line-is-a-warning-not-a-step`. He solves for origin, not for culpability in the way the book means culpability. He is wrong about what to do with Doina not because his arithmetic is wrong but because his arithmetic is the wrong arithmetic.

**Voice:** Third person past. Deliberate; the pipeline's own default register. Restrained and literary. He thinks in cases, precedents, what will and will not survive being described to his captain. His interiority is orderly and procedural in a different way than Ilinca's — hers is a private discipline she chose; his is an institutional one he has inhabited for twelve years.

**Relationships:**  
- His captain at the Inspectoratul Județean in Cluj-Napoca: the deadline is the captain's deadline, not Munteanu's; the captain wants a name on the file  
- Doina: the arrest, around chapter 19, is the most power he has over anyone in this book and the moment his arithmetic most visibly fails  
- Ilinca: he does not know she exists until chapter 22 or later; when she comes to him, it recontextualises everything

## Non-POV characters

### Ana Petrescu
**Age:** 19 at death (November 4, 1984)  
Ana is never a POV character and never appears in a flashback scene. She exists in the book as: a name, a house, a hospital admission time, and her sister-in-law's account. She is the fact the book is built around.

### Ana's sister-in-law (name to be established in drafting)
The woman who had the copy and followed it. Not a recurring character; appears in accounts, not in scenes. Not to be named until she is needed.

### The woman on the bus
Never identified. Never found. Never appears. Doina's silence about her holds throughout. The reader never learns her name or what she looks like. Her copy is N3 (or a descendant of N3); she does not know and will never know that it is clean.

### Ana's husband / the Petrescu family
Present in Munteanu's chapters as people he has already spoken to, whose accounts he has already taken. They are paper in his file.

### Munteanu's captain
Offstage pressure. He appears as the source of the 31-day deadline and as the bureaucratic gravity that will take the case somewhere colder if Munteanu does not resolve it. He is not a villain; he is the system at a higher register.
