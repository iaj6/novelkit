# Pitch — The Ninth Line

Romania, November 1984. Eighteen years into Ceaușescu's Decree 770, which banned abortion and contraception and made pregnancy a matter of state accounting, women in the mill town of Dej pass a piece of paper hand to hand. Not instructions for ending a pregnancy — what to do afterwards. How to keep a woman warm, what to count, when the number is too many, and the one thing the paper exists to say: when to stop managing it at home and take her to the hospital, and what to say when you get there.

On the fourth of November a nineteen-year-old named Ana Petrescu arrives at the district hospital in Dej. She dies four hours later, nine hours after she should have come. The copy of the paper her sister-in-law followed said to take her in *in the morning*. The original, written by forty-one-year-old mill bookkeeper **Ilinca Roșca** and folded into the spine of a ledger she is legally required to produce on demand, says no such thing.

What sits between the original and the dead girl is a chain of twenty-two handwritten copies, five generations deep, each a physical object distinguishable by paper stock, handwriting, and the small errors of transmission. Two people are collating that chain from opposite ends: Ilinca, quietly, because a woman she knows may die next; and **Lieutenant Vasile Munteanu**, thirty-four, county militia, who has worked out that the copies are not identical, that the differences are inherited, and that a family of documents can be climbed to its source. He is right. He is also faster, because he can knock on doors.

**Nineteen-year-old Doina Marcu**, night-shift mill worker, has a copy folded in her geography book. It came from a woman on the bus whose name she doesn't know. Her copy is clean — still has the bracket that matters. Munteanu reaches Doina before she can prove that. The book ends in the only place it can: Ilinca handing Munteanu the original and the cellar copy she made herself, the one where the bracket became a numbered line. Ilinca is taken. Doina is released. The paper is gone. The ninth line — the bracket included — is memorised.
