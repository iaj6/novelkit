# Drafter guidance — The Ninth Line

## Voice register

This book runs three registers simultaneously and they must be kept distinct without comment. Ilinca's voice is close-third present, procedural, short declaratives — Alan Furst's apartment-search chapters in *The Polish Officer*: cold material surfaces, the sequence of a person doing something careful under surveillance. Doina's voice is first-person past, wry, digressive, honest in the way that nineteen and smart in the wrong century is honest — the register of a working teenager who is smarter than her job and is telling you what happened, including the parts where she was not smart at the time. Munteanu's voice is deliberately the pipeline's default literary register: restrained third-person past, carefully procedural, the register Herta Müller uses for the institutional world minus the surrealism. The voice you reach for without trying is the one Munteanu speaks in. That is not a coincidence; that is the joke. Do not explain the joke.

The first sentence of any chapter must tell the reader which POV they are in. Voice is the chapter header's replacement.

## Sentence-length expectations

- **Ilinca chapters:** average 10–15 words. Under pressure, shorter. A sentence over 25 words in Ilinca's chapter is a warning sign.
- **Munteanu chapters:** average 15–22 words. His deliberateness earns longer sentences; they should feel like a careful man thinking through the next step.
- **Doina chapters:** average 15–25 words, with latitude for a sentence that goes longer when the digression earns it. A 40-word sentence in Doina's voice is fine if it is doing Doina things (wry enumeration, honest backtracking). The same sentence in Ilinca's chapter is an error.

## Chapter-opening conventions

**Ilinca:** Open on a physical object or action, present tense, no scene-setting preamble. "She unfolds it on the desk between the ledger and the lamp" — the action is already in progress. Do not begin Ilinca chapters with weather or location establishment.

**Doina:** May open on observation, memory, or physical situation — but within two sentences she is her own subject, speaking in first person about what she was doing or thinking. Do not open Doina chapters on abstract statement.

**Munteanu:** May open on procedure, object, or environment — he is a man who arrives somewhere and takes inventory. The opening can be a little more deliberate than Ilinca's; he has more institutional confidence.

**In all three voices:** No chapter opens on an interior thought about the regime, Ceaușescu, or the political situation. The state is present through its instruments and effects, not through a character's meditation on it.

## Chapter-ending conventions

**Do not adopt a uniform closing mode.** The brief is explicit. Across 28–30 chapters, the distribution should include hard cuts (mid-action-cut, cliffhanger), literary fades (cold image, weather, physical sensation), and resolved beats, distributed by what the chapter's material wants. A Doina chapter may end wryly; an Ilinca chapter may end on a number that does not add up; a Munteanu chapter may end on the flatness of deciding to pursue something his captain will not like.

**One constraint:** The two chapters that mark the Act I close (the reader seeing N1 and N17 side by side) and the Act III climax (Ilinca handing over the copies) must end in distinct modes — this is the book's spine and the chapter-craft log should show those endings as different from each other.

## POV strategy

One POV per chapter. Rotation A–B–A–C (Ilinca–Doina–Ilinca–Munteanu) through Acts I and II, compressing to A–C–A–C in Act III. Do not introduce a chapter break within a chapter to switch POV. The three voices must be legible without a label; if the voice is not immediately clear, rewrite the opening.

## Dialogue density

**Low.** Characters in this book transact information or withhold it; they do not speak to manage their feelings. Ilinca's chapters may have extended sequences with no dialogue at all — she is working alone. Munteanu's interrogations should be mostly his interior accounting of what the person is not saying, with the actual exchange compressed. Doina's chapters carry the most dialogue, but it is quoted carefully — she is recounting conversations, not replaying them at full length.

Dialogue tags: `said` and `asked` only. No tags that interpret the speech (no "she admitted," "he confessed," "she acknowledged bitterly"). No tag followed by a paragraph interpreting what was meant.

## Comic timing and tonal balance

Doina is the only character in this book who is allowed to be funny, and she is funny in the way that survival humour is funny — she names the absurdity as a way of continuing to live in it. This is not comic relief; it is Doina's idiom. A Doina chapter that has no wry observation in it has flattened her register. But the comedy is always in *her* situation, not in the system — she is not satirising the regime, she is noticing what it is like to be nineteen inside it.

Ilinca and Munteanu are never funny. Munteanu may observe something with the flatness of a man who is surprised by nothing, but that is not comedy; that is register.

## Anti-defaults — what the drafter must actively avoid in this register

1. **Do not open chapters on extended interiority.** Ilinca does not begin a chapter thinking about the decree or what Ana's death means. She begins on a paper or a number.

2. **Do not gloss the documents.** When a character reads from a copy, write the fragment; do not then write a sentence explaining what the fragment means to them. The meaning is in the gap between copies, not in a character's reaction to a line.

3. **Do not name fear.** "She was afraid" is not in this book. What is in this book: the door she used, the hand she kept still, the number she counted twice.

4. **Do not explain the tree.** No character narrates the structure of the network to another character. No character uses the word "tree" or "chain" or "network" or "family" (in the genealogical sense). The structure exists in the objects and the hands; it is never on a whiteboard.

5. **Do not equip characters with political consciousness.** Nobody in this book gives a speech about the regime. Nobody is a mouthpiece. The state is present the way weather is present — it is the condition, not the subject of anyone's monologue.

6. **Do not let Munteanu become a villain or a hero.** He is a competent policeman. He is not secretly on anyone's side. He is doing his job with the tools and the arithmetic he has. Do not editorialize about him in the narration.

7. **Word count floor: 1,900 words per chapter, target 2,000–2,200.** The pipeline's native tendency is to write short. This is a three-POV literary thriller with 22 registered documents; a chapter that comes in at 1,200 words has dropped material. If a chapter is short, extend the scene — more procedural detail, more document surface, more physical environment — not more interiority.

8. **Before quoting any document in a chapter, call `read_record` on that node's recordId and reproduce the relevant fragment exactly.** Drift between a character's copy and the registered canonical text is the error this book most needs to prevent.
