## Premise

Romania, November 1984. Eighteen years into Decree 770, which banned abortion and contraception and
made pregnancy a matter of state accounting, women in a mill town in the Someș valley pass a piece of
paper hand to hand. It is not instructions for ending a pregnancy. It is what to do afterwards —
how to keep a woman warm, what to count, what number is too many, and the thing that matters most:
when to stop managing it at home and take her to the district hospital, and what to say when you get
there. Going to the hospital is itself dangerous. Every doctor is required to report. The paper
exists because the decision to go is the decision that kills people when it comes too late, and
because nobody can be told twice.

**Ilinca Roșca**, forty-one, keeps the books at the textile mill. She wrote the original — eight
numbered lines and a ninth written across the margin in a bracket, because she added it last and
there was no room. She has never handed the paper to anyone she did not know by name, and she has
never kept a list of who has it, because a list is the thing that hangs everyone. What she keeps
instead is the master: one sheet, exact, folded into the spine of a ledger she is legally required
to produce on demand.

On the fourth of November a nineteen-year-old named **Ana Petrescu** dies in the district hospital
in Dej, four hours after arriving and about nine hours later than she should have. Her sister-in-law
had a copy of the paper. She followed it. The copy she followed says to take her in *in the morning*.

**Doina Marcu**, nineteen, works the night shift at the mill and has a copy folded inside a
schoolbook. She has never used it. Hers came from a woman on the bus whose name she does not know,
who got it from someone else. When the militia begin working outward from Ana's house, Doina's copy
is four generations from the master and two branches away from the one that killed Ana — and she
cannot prove that, because she cannot say where hers came from without naming the woman on the bus.

**Lieutenant Vasile Munteanu**, thirty-four, of the county militia, is given the death as an
administrative matter and turns it into a real one. He is not a monster and he is not secretly on
anyone's side. He is a competent policeman with an unusual idea: the copies are not identical, the
differences are inherited, and if he collects enough of them he can arrange them into a family and
walk up it to the woman who wrote the first. He is right. His arithmetic is sound. He is running the
same collation Ilinca is running, from the opposite end, and he is faster, because he can knock on
doors.

**The book's answer, fixed and delivered in full: the mutation that killed Ana entered the chain in
a copy Ilinca made herself.** On the third of November of the previous year she copied the paper in
a coal cellar by the light of the door, in a hurry, for a woman who could not wait — and she
transcribed the bracketed marginal ninth line as a ninth numbered line. Nothing was lost. Every word
was correct. But a line in a list is a step, and a line in a bracket is an override, and two
copyists later a woman doing her honest best to make a clean list of nine steps put the ninth in
sequence with the eighth and wrote *in the morning*.

Nobody in this chain was careless. The woman who wrote *in the morning* was being tidy. The woman
who copied in the cellar was being fast because the alternative was being late. The state made
speed, darkness, and no-second-chances the conditions of the work, and then the work failed the way
work fails under those conditions. That is the argument of the book, and it must be carried entirely
by the tree of copies — never stated by a character.

## The case, pinned

These are fixed. The architect may not relocate them.

1. **The master (N1) is Ilinca's**, written October 1983. Eight numbered lines plus a ninth written
   across the margin inside a hand-drawn bracket. The bracket is the whole book.
2. **The poisoned ancestor (N4) is Ilinca's own second copy**, made 3 November 1983 in a coal cellar.
   Its only difference from N1: the ninth line is a numbered line 9 instead of a bracketed marginal.
   Every word identical. This is a *formatting* mutation, and it is fatal.
3. **The fatal mutation (N11)** happens two hands later, made by a woman being careful: reading nine
   numbered lines, she regularises the ninth into the sequence of the eighth, importing *in the
   morning* and dropping *Do not wait for the morning*.
4. **Ana's copy (N17)** descends from N11 and carries the mutation unchanged.
5. **Doina's copy (N14)** descends from N3, a different second-generation branch. It is clean — it
   still has the bracket. Doina is innocent and cannot prove it.
6. **Munteanu's collation is CORRECT and lands on Doina** — not through error, but because Doina's
   is the copy he can physically reach, and a clean node four generations down still points upward.
   He is not wrong about the tree. He is wrong about what to do with it.
7. **Ilinca can exonerate Doina only by producing the master and the cellar copy** — which proves the
   mutation is hers, and hands Munteanu the top of the tree. She does it. This is the climax and it
   must happen ON THE PAGE, not offstage.
8. **The ending is not ambiguous.** Doina is released. Ilinca is taken. The last chapter is Doina,
   carrying nothing, having memorised the ninth line — the bracket included. The paper is what killed
   Ana; the memorised line is what survives. Do not soften this into hope, and do not withhold it.
9. **No character ever draws the tree, explains the tree, diagrams it, or uses the word "tree".**
   The collation happens in kitchens, on a bus, in a mill office, in a militia room with copies laid
   on a desk — in objects and hands, never on a whiteboard. Any chapter in which a character narrates
   the structure of the network to another character is a failed chapter.

## The documents

**N1, the master, is CANONICAL VERBATIM TEXT.** Register it under recordId `master-n1`, canon tier,
before any chapter is drafted. It is reproduced exactly as follows, including the bracket line and
its indentation:

```
1. Keep her warm and lying down. Someone stays with her the whole night.
2. Small sips of water, often. Nothing else by mouth for six hours.
3. Count the cloths. Write the number and the hour on the paper by the bed.
4. If she sleeps, wake her every hour and speak to her until she answers you properly.
5. No visitors. No telephone. If anyone asks, she has the influenza.
6. Burn nothing until she is well. Wash and keep.
7. Two cloths in one hour is too many.
8. A fever that will not come down by morning is too long.
   [ 7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why.
     Do not wait for the morning. ]
```

**The three load-bearing variants are also CANONICAL VERBATIM TEXT**, registered pre-draft under
their own distinct recordIds. Registering each variant under its OWN recordId is required, not
stylistic: `findRecordDivergences` fires only on re-registering ONE recordId with two bodies, so a
family of intentionally-different documents is safe from the audit **only** if every node is its own
record. Never re-register a recordId.

- **`copy-n4`** — Ilinca's cellar copy. Lines 1–8 identical to N1. The bracketed line becomes:
  `9. 7 or 8 — take her in. Say she began bleeding on her own and you do not know why. Do not wait for the morning.`
- **`copy-n11`** — the regularised copy. Lines 1–8 identical. Line 9 becomes:
  `9. If 7 or 8, take her in in the morning.`
- **`copy-n17`** — Ana's copy, from N11. Identical to `copy-n11` in every line. It is a separate
  record because it is a separate physical object with its own provenance, and because the book
  prints it beside the master.

The remaining eighteen nodes are the architect's to generate, one per recordId, under the rules in
`## The tree`. Their mutations must be *incidental* — a spelling, a dropped comma, a line-break, a
hand-numbering style — and must never touch lines 7, 8, or 9. Only the poisoned branch touches the
ninth line, and only in the two ways pinned above.

**Quotation ceiling.** The full document is printed **at most four times** in the book: the master
early, Ana's copy beside it at the reveal-to-reader, Doina's clean copy once, and the master and the
cellar copy together at the climax. Everywhere else, fragments only. This is a hard limit. A prior
run in this library re-quoted a registered document four times as filler and readers named it the
book's worst tic; `editor-pacing` also treats a re-quoted document as a padding signature.

**Before re-quoting any node in a later chapter, `read_record` it and reproduce it exactly.** The
audit does not detect in-prose drift — prevention is the mechanism, and a post-run certificate is
the proof.

## The tree

Twenty-two nodes, five generations, all registered as records:

- **G1** — N1, the master (Ilinca).
- **G2** — three copies: N2, N3, **N4** (Ilinca's cellar copy, the poisoned ancestor).
- **G3** — six copies.
- **G4** — seven copies, including **N11** (the regularised one, from N4) and **N14** (Doina's, from N3).
- **G5** — five copies, including **N17** (Ana's, from N11).

Record the parentage with `relation.assert` as `copied_from` (child → parent), and record each
hand-off as `knowledge.learn` with `basis: told_by` and `basisEntity` set to the woman who physically
handed it over. That teller field is the transmission chain — it is what makes this a chain and not a
star, and it is readable back via `who_knows`.

Give each node a `document`-kind entity and assert its physical facts (`date`, `author`, and where
relevant `paper_stock`, `ink`) so a node can be identified in prose by its object properties, not by
a label. The nodes must be distinguishable to the reader as *things* — a school exercise sheet, a
carbon, the back of a mill dispatch form — long before they are distinguishable as data.

**~60 in-prose quotation sites across 30 chapters, each attributable to exactly ONE node.** A
fragment quoted in a scene must be the fragment as that character's copy renders it. This is the
book's central discipline and the reason it needs the machine.

## Who knows what

Epistemic capture is ON. Use these canonical proposition slugs verbatim. Both sides of a gap must
share ONE slug or the irony query returns nothing and the engine silently disappears.

- **`ninth-line-is-a-warning-not-a-step`** — the load-bearing proposition, and the one the book turns
  on. `@reader` moves to `knows` in the chapter that prints the master and Ana's copy adjacent
  (Act I close). Ilinca is `unaware` from chapter 1 and must be pinned `unaware` *as of that same
  chapter*, not only at the chapter she finds out. She moves to `knows` at the cellar recollection.
  Munteanu never holds this proposition at all — he is solving a different problem.
- **`ana-copy-descends-from-ilinca-cellar-copy`** — Ilinca `unaware` → `suspects` → `knows`.
  Munteanu `unaware` throughout, then `knows` only when she hands it to him.
- **`doina-copy-is-clean`** — `@reader` `knows` from Doina's first chapter. Munteanu
  `wrong_believes` from the arrest onward. Doina `knows` but cannot prove it; she is `concealing`
  the woman on the bus, which is *why* she cannot. This is the library's first real use of
  `concealing`, and it must do dramatic work: her silence is the thing that keeps her in the cell.

**Operative bookkeeping note — seed each rung under the chapter where it lands.** A knowledge event's
id is `know:{chapter}:{knower}:{proposition}`, so two stances recorded for the same knower on the same
proposition under the SAME chapter collapse to whichever was written last. An arc seeded entirely
under a single canon pseudo-chapter loses every rung but the final one. Stamp `unaware` at the chapter
it starts, `suspects` where it turns, `knows` where it lands.

Call `dramatic_irony` before committing any chapter in the back half. The gap between the reader and
Ilinca on `ninth-line-is-a-warning-not-a-step` should be live and continuous from the Act I close to
the cellar recollection, and it should close there. If the query returns empty, the slugs have
drifted apart and the book's engine is not doing anything.

## Structure

Three POVs, no prologue, no epigraph, no framing device. A date is the only chapter header.

- **Ilinca — third person PRESENT.** Close, procedural, hands and paper. She is a bookkeeper; her
  interiority runs in columns and reconciliations. Present tense because her collation is happening
  now and she does not know how it ends.
- **Doina — FIRST PERSON PAST.** Nineteen, night shift, funny in a way nobody in this library has
  been allowed to be. Past tense because she survives to tell it, which the reader should feel as
  reassurance long before it is confirmed.
- **Munteanu — third person past.** Deliberately the pipeline's own default register — the house
  voice, restrained and literary — because the state is the thing that speaks in the manner this
  library defaults to. That is the joke, and it is never explained.

Rotation **A–B–A–C** through Acts I and II, compressing to **A–C–A–C** in Act III as the two
collations close on each other. Roughly: Act I chapters 1–9 (the death, the paper, the reader is
given the poisoned line); Act II chapters 10–21 (both collations build; Doina is arrested around 19);
Act III chapters 22–30 (the cellar, the choice, the handover, the release).

The two collations must be legible as a race without anyone calling it one. The reader should be
able to say, at any point in Act II, how many nodes each of them holds.

## Length and shape

- **28–30 chapters. No chapter under 1,900 words. Target ~62,000 words.**
- This is a **floor**, not the usual ceiling. Stated deliberately: this pipeline consistently writes
  under its plan (a recent 20-chapter run planned ~2,500/chapter and delivered ~1,200), and a
  three-POV braid that comes in at 35,000 words will have dropped a thread rather than tightened one.
- Length must never flex below the **structural contract**: every pinned item in "The case, pinned"
  lands, all three POVs run to convergence, and the ending is delivered in full.

## Compounding mechanism

Two measurable ladders under every chapter, moving in opposite directions:

1. **Nodes held.** Ilinca's collation and Munteanu's each gain copies. Every chapter, one of the two
   counts changes, and the reader knows both. Neither ever loses a node — this is monotonic and it is
   the clock.
2. **Days since Ana died**, against the fixed administrative deadline at which Munteanu must either
   charge someone or send the file up — which would move it from a lieutenant who wants the truth to
   an office that wants a name.

Each chapter's worsening must be a **new foreclosure, not a re-measurement**. A chapter may not
re-walk a copy the reader already holds or re-take a count already taken. When a node is acquired,
something becomes impossible: a woman is now known to the militia, a branch is now closed, a name is
now on a list. Name in each chapter-map row the specific prior chapter it advances.

## Voice

Cold, exact, and physical. Weather, paper, hands, the smell of a mill in November. Short declaratives
under pressure; longer sentences only in Doina's chapters, where nineteen has room to digress.

Fear is rendered as procedure — what people do with their hands, what they do not write down, which
door they use — and almost never named. No one in this book explains the political situation to
anyone else, because everyone in it already lives there. The reader assembles the state from its
effects: the reporting requirement, the monthly examination, the queue, the fact that a telephone is
a risk.

Closing register may vary freely; quiet endings where the chapter wants them, hard cuts where it
does not. Do not adopt a uniform closing mode.

## Audience and register

- **Target audience age range**: adult literary / adult genre — a literary thriller that keeps its engine
- **Voice pace**: fast to medium. This is the pipeline's weakest measured axis and the primary thing
  calibration must fix. If the calibration sample reads contemplative, it has failed — re-run it.
- **Reading-grade target**: college, but plain. Short words, exact nouns.
- **POV count intent**: three, as specified above, with three genuinely distinct registers
- **Chapter-ending convention**: mixed by design

## Exemplar passages

Deliberately not pasted copyrighted prose. Instead, two precise reference points for the architect's
own knowledge, plus original samples showing the target.

**Reference points** (use your knowledge of these, do not imitate their plots): the procedural
coldness of the apartment-search chapters in Alan Furst's *The Polish Officer*; the domestic
close-third of Herta Müller's Romanian-village work, minus the surrealism. For Doina, the wry
first-person of a working teenager who is smarter than her job.

**Ilinca — third present, target register:**

```
She unfolds it on the desk between the ledger and the lamp and does not touch it after that. The
paper has been wet at some point and dried badly; the fold has gone furry. Line by line it is hers.
Line nine is not. Line nine sits square in the column with a number in front of it, and the bracket
she drew around it in October is not anywhere on the page. She counts down the list twice. Eight
steps and a warning is one thing. Nine steps is another thing entirely, and she is looking at the
second one.
```

**Doina — first past, target register:**

```
The woman on the bus had a face like a closed shop and she put it in my hand the way you pass someone
the salt. I kept it in the geography book because nobody has ever once opened my geography book,
including me. For eleven months it was just a thing I owned, like the coat. I want that on the
record, because of everything that came after: I never used it, I never showed it to anyone, and I
could not have told you what was written on line nine if you had held me upside down.
```

**Munteanu — third past, the house voice, deliberately:**

```
He laid the four sheets side by side on the desk and weighted the corners with whatever was nearest,
which turned out to be a stapler and three unopened tins of shoe polish. Read separately they said
the same thing. Read together they did not. It was the kind of difference that would not survive
being described to his captain, and he understood, with a flatness that surprised him, that he was
going to pursue it anyway.
```

## Constraints

- **SAFETY, ABSOLUTE.** The circulated document is aftercare and when-to-seek-help ONLY. No method,
  no substance, no dose, no procedure, no route of administration, at any node, in any chapter, in
  any variant, in canon, in the outline, or in the store. Ana's own pregnancy and its ending happen
  entirely off the page and are never described. The fatal mutation is a loss of urgency, never a
  wrong quantity. If any phase finds itself specifying medical technique, it has misread this brief.
- **Protection carve-out — binding continuity facts.** The audit, editor, and repair passes never
  read brief.md. The architect MUST write into `canon/continuity.md` as numbered binding facts: that
  the 22 documents *differ from one another by design*; that no editor or repair pass may reconcile,
  harmonise, or "correct" any node toward any other; and that `copy-n11` and `copy-n17` say
  *in the morning* deliberately. Echo the same into `canon/style.md` under lean-into patterns. Without
  this, a continuity pass will helpfully repair the book's subject.
- **`git commit draft/` after the drafter phase, before the editor.** `editor-continuity` runs inside
  `cdk run`, is handed the previous chapter for adjacency, is licensed to rewrite a single
  contradictory sentence, and writes back to `draft/` in place. Two adjacent chapters quoting
  one-word-different variants is exactly what it is built to flatten, and without a pre-editor commit
  there is no baseline to diff against.
- Ana is never a POV and never a flashback scene. She exists in the book only as a name, a house, a
  sister-in-law's account, and a hospital admission time.
- No torture, no interrogation set-piece, no Securitate melodrama. Munteanu's power is paperwork and
  the ability to knock on a door in the afternoon, and that is frightening enough.
- The woman on the bus is never identified, never found, and never appears. Doina's silence holds.
- Nobody delivers a speech about the regime. No character is a mouthpiece.
- Romanian words only where a translation would be false (names, the mill, food, official forms), and
  never glossed in-line.

## Research scope

The researcher grounds the world and the procedure. It must **not** research the medical content of
the document — that is fiction by design and is fixed above.

```
- Decree 770 (1966) and its enforcement through the mid-1980s: the reporting requirement on
  physicians, workplace gynaecological examinations, the demographic apparatus, documented maternal
  mortality
- Daily life in a Transylvanian mill town in 1984: rationing, power cuts, queues, heating, the
  texture of a November in the Someș valley
- County militia procedure and the paperwork of a suspicious-death file: who is notified, what is
  written, how a case is escalated from a lieutenant to a county office, and on what timetable
- Informer practice at the neighbourhood level and how ordinary people managed the risk of being
  reported by a neighbour
- Romanian textile-mill bookkeeping in the period: the ledgers, the forms, what a bookkeeper was
  required to produce on demand
- Naming conventions, forms of address, and household vocabulary for the region and period
```
