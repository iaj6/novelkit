## 02-the-wagon-road

### Sentence-after-the-sentence / Deadpan without annotation

**Cut 1:** After Brann's deadpan punchline ("I just said the word"), the next sentence glossed the joke landing.
- Removed: `Pip appeared to find this deeply unfair.`
- Reason: Brann's line is the beat; the annotation kills the compression. Lean-into pattern: deadpan without annotation.

**Cut 2:** After Pip's speechless reaction (opened mouth, closed it), the sentence continued to describe his expression in analytical detail.
- Removed: `with an expression that was not quite indignation and not quite admiration and was occupying the space between them at considerable discomfort`
- Kept: `He turned to Tess.`
- Reason: Mouth-opening-and-closing already performed speechlessness; the expression description glossed what the action showed.

**Cut 3:** After Pip's flat observation ("That wasn't standard"), three sentences decoded his delivery and explained his manner.
- Removed: `He said it the way he'd say anything. The way he'd say: looks like rain, or: that's not a grain cart. Just his read on a situation, delivered to no one in particular, directed at the road ahead.`
- Reason: The line lands as deadpan. Annotating how deadpan it was is the exact failure the lean-into pattern guards against.

**Cut 4:** After "So where'd his rider go," — a sentence labeled the line "Not a question," which the punctuation (comma, no question mark) already conveyed.
- Removed: `Not a question.`
- Kept: `He was looking at the ford, at the far bank, at the empty path leading back up the opposite slope.`
- Reason: Sentence-after-the-sentence glossing punctuation that speaks for itself.

**Cut 5:** After Brann's gesture was fully described (slow raise, palm out), a sentence translated the gesture into words.
- Removed: `The kind of gesture you made when you wanted to say: I see you.`
- Reason: The gesture description already conveyed its meaning; the translation is redundant annotation.

**Cut 6:** After "The figure turned and walked away. Not running." — a sentence paraphrased "Not running" at length.
