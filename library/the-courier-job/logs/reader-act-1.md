# Reader — Act 1 Assessment
## Chapters: 01-departure, 02-the-wagon-road, 03-the-ambush, 04-the-guild-token

---

### Overall Impression of This Act

Act 1 does the hard work of a debut act efficiently: four distinct voices assembled and legible within the first chapter, all five threads planted by chapter 4, and a genuine action set piece in chapter 3 that earns its place on the brief's terms. The prose is mostly in the right register. The character work is the act's strongest asset — Brann, Iri, and Pip all arrive as people, not descriptors, within their first few pages. The act's most serious problem is two chapter endings that fail the brief's hard cliffhanger contract. That's a structural fix, not a prose fix, and it needs to be named directly.

---

### Brief-Adherence Checks

**Chapter 01 — Departure**
1. Would a 13–15-year-old finish? Yes. Fast, dialogue-forward, ends on a hook.
2. Sole POV? Yes. Clean.
3. Opens with dialogue/action/bold declarative? Yes — *"You look awful," Mara said.*
4. Ends on cliffhanger? **Yes.** *"That man works for the Guild."* Pip's four words, no joke in them — correct cut.
5. Dialogue ratio? ~45%. Acceptable for an opening chapter that has setup work to do.

**Chapter 02 — The Wagon Road**
1. Would finish? Mostly. The barn scene slows slightly for the target age.
2. Sole POV? Yes, with one passage worth watching (see below).
3. Opens correctly? Yes — drops mid-conversation.
4. Ends on cliffhanger? **No. Literary fade.** The watcher has already walked over the hill. The chapter ends: *"her right hand had found the bag's strap and closed there, tight and specific, and she hadn't told it to."* This is post-resolution interiority — the threat has dissipated, the chapter ends on residual anxiety rendered as sensation. Per the brief: not permitted. The peak of this chapter's tension is Brann raising his hand at the watcher. Cut there, or cut when they start walking faster.
5. Dialogue ratio? ~40%, low end. The road sections rely on narration.

**Chapter 03 — The Ambush**
1. Would finish? Definitively yes. Best chapter of the act.
2. Sole POV? Yes, with one borderline passage (see below).
3. Opens correctly? **Best opening of the act:** *"Pip had been talking since the first morning out of Drey, and when he stopped, the road sounded wrong."*
4. Ends on cliffhanger? **Yes.** Hoofbeats from the east. Brann's hand finds his belt knife. Hard cut. Correct.
5. Dialogue ratio? ~30-35%, but this is an action chapter — lower is appropriate and the brief exempts it.
6. Action physical/concrete? **Yes.** The Brann knife-catch, the wrist turn, the elbow. Iri's pallor as a lamp running dry. The boot throw. Pip operating one-handed. Fully achieved.

**Chapter 04 — The Guild Token**
1. Would finish? Yes.
2. Sole POV? Yes.
3. Opens correctly? Yes — *"It was a merchant."* Perfect resolution of the ch3 cliffhanger.
4. Ends on cliffhanger? **No. Soft ending.** Iri says *"I know that seal... In places I used to know."* Brann says *"We should move at dawn."* She goes back to her bread. *"Nobody asked what places."* The revelation's peak is *"I was wrong to be surprised they sent this many"* — the chapter should cut there, or at Brann shutting the conversation down with something that sharpens rather than dissolves the tension. As written, the chapter ends after the deflation of the moment, with everyone settled at a fire. Literary fade in YA clothing.
5. Dialogue ratio? ~40-45%. Adequate.

### What's Working

**Character voices are distinct and consistent.** Pip arrives fully formed in chapter 1 — *"I'm not late, you're all early. Those are different things"* — and never wavers. Brann's compression is earned: *"You already said everything. I just said the word"* (ch2). Iri's accidental literalism in the barn scene (correctly identifying the grandmother, deadpan) is exactly the character the brief calls for. Tess's dryness and speed feel right throughout.

**The Mara opening is exactly right.** The briefing in ch1 does triple duty: establishes the contract, characterizes Mara through tone (facts, no coddling, no sentiment), and lands *"Deliver it whole. Whatever happens on that road, deliver it whole"* without annotation. This line carries Thread 3 without explaining it. It's the act's best prose beat.

**POV discipline is mostly excellent.** The milestone scene in ch2 — *"He'd been looking at something else before she looked up. He had moved his eyes to the road. These were simply facts about what his eyes had done; Tess was certain enough of this that she didn't bother checking it"* — is the best single POV construction in the act. It names exactly what Tess can and cannot know, and trusts that precision to be interesting. Defend this passage.

**The ambush is physical and earned.** The bird lift before the fight starts ("Then they weren't. All of them. One wheeling lift, gone over the treeline before the sound reached the road.") is the right kind of concrete signal. Iri's cost is rendered from the outside — pallor, shaking, needing Pip's coat — not announced. The boot throw is Pip operating at exactly his level: competent and absurd, which stops being absurd when it works.

**Thread planting is efficient and non-labored.** By the end of ch4, all five threads are in motion with no detours to explain themselves. Iri's glance at the bag's seal in ch1 (ch1, final third — *"a door opened and closed before she'd seen what was behind it"*) and the watcher on the hill and the checkpoint seal-logging in ch2 are laid in without announcement.

**The Wicken Street story** (ch2, barn scene) is a good Pip character scene. *"I think about that sometimes. / He didn't say which part."* That's the moment the joke stops being one. The guidance says to defend this pattern; it's here and it's right.

---

### What Isn't Working

**Two chapter endings fail the brief's hard cliffhanger requirement.** Chapters 2 and 4 both end on quiet, inward beats rather than forward-pulling cuts. Chapter 2's ending belongs to adult literary fantasy; chapter 4's ending dissolves what should be a sharp cut. This is not a style preference — it is a structural violation of the brief. Both endings need revision.

**One over-annotation of a character beat.** In ch4 (wound-tending scene): *"That was it. Possibly the entirety of Brann's vocabulary for someone did a useful thing and I am glad of it."* This glosses Brann's *"Thank you"* — it explains the joke after it has already landed. The style guide names this pattern (sentence-after-the-sentence). Cut or shorten.

**One borderline POV passage in ch3.** After Iri's working expires: *"He looked at Brann: one man down, the other pinned against the cutting bank. He looked at Iri: upright, shaking, hands in Pip's coat, the expression of someone who has done something expensive and is prepared to do it again."* "He" is the second attacker; the colon-constructions render what he sees when he looks, not what Tess observes. The description of Iri's expression specifically (*"the expression of someone who has done something expensive and is prepared to do it again"*) could belong to the attacker's assessment, not Tess's. Worth tightening — reframe as Tess watching the attacker look, then describing what she herself sees.

### Thread Development in This Act

All five threads enter correctly and are advanced per the plan:
- **Thread 1 (Tess/Brann):** Brann's wordless waiting (ch1), the bag-check noticing (ch2), the methodical arm-wound-tending (ch4). Correctly building without announcement.
- **Thread 2 (Tess/Iri):** Intimidation established (ch1), cost witnessed (ch3), Iri's *"places I used to know"* begins the crack (ch4). On plan.
- **Thread 3 (Mara's knowledge):** *"Deliver it whole"* planted (ch1), *"Get the bag!"* begins the gap between what Mara said and what she knew (ch3). On plan.
- **Thread 4 (Pip's Asterhall):** Entrance deferred appropriately; his badge identification (ch4) shows city knowledge without surfacing the backstory.
- **Thread 5 (Enforcement network):** Guild rider (ch1), checkpoint logging / watcher (ch2), *"not road guild — enforcement wing"* (ch4). Clean build.

---

### Pacing Assessment

Chapters 1, 3, and 4 move correctly. Chapter 2 has one slow pocket: the barn night scene (Wicken Street story) is the act's longest quiet stretch. It earns its place as character work — the party is learning to sit in the same room — but for the target reader it's the place most at risk of attention lapsing. The chapter's ending weakness compounds this: if the chapter slowed for the barn scene and then ended on a quiet resolved beat, it loses twice in a row. The ending fix is the priority.

---

### Verdict on Act 1

The act is working. The cast is alive, the threats are real, the bag already means something. The two chapter-ending failures are the only structural problems, and they are fixable. The prose is in the right register with minor drift. Act 2 inherits five threads in good shape.
