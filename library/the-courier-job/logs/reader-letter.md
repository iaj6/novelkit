# Developmental Editor's Letter — *The Sealed Bag*

**Review date: 2026-05-21**

---

## Overall Impression

At its best, *The Sealed Bag* is exactly what it set out to be: a lean, fast, banter-forward YA party fantasy with four characters you can tell apart in the dark, a MacGuffin that turns out to matter precisely and retroactively, and an ending that opens rather than closes. The bones are sound. The five-thread structure is executed with more precision than this kind of book usually manages, the Tess/Iri resolution in chapter 11 is the best thing in the draft, and the final sentence of chapter 12 is the rare ending that genuinely earns what the book has asked of the reader. The register is mostly right. The cast is mostly alive. This is a draft that can be revised into the book it was meant to be without a structural overhaul.

What stands between this draft and that version: a recurring failure on chapter endings that the brief prohibits without exception, two places where the POV contract is broken, and three extended interiority passages in the later chapters that drift into the literary adult register this book is not permitted to occupy. These are real problems. A 13-year-old reader's attention will not wait for a revision that hasn't happened yet.

---

## What's Working

**1. The character voices are fully differentiated from chapter 1 and hold across all twelve chapters.**
Pip arrives complete — *"I'm not late, you're all early. Those are different things"* — and his voice does not drift. Brann's compression is earned and consistent: the deadpan readable only after a beat, the important thing said once and not elaborated. Iri's accidental literalism (correctly identifying the grandmother in ch 02's barn scene; *"I intend to eat while I decide to do it"* in ch 05) is executed without annotation throughout. For a book that runs single POV through a four-person ensemble, having three supporting characters this externally legible is a structural achievement. The brief asks that you tell these four apart through voice and action; the manuscript delivers.

**2. The Mara departure scene.**
Chapter 01's briefing earns everything Thread 3 needs to carry. *"Deliver it whole. Whatever happens on that road, deliver it whole"* lands without explanation and stays resident in the reader's memory through the ambush, through Vel's "testimony," through the re-casting in chapter 11. The line does triple duty — establishes the contract, characterizes Mara, plants the thread's explosive — and it does none of it by announcing itself. This is what the whole book's exposition wants to do, and this scene demonstrates it can.

**3. The chapter 11 re-casting is specific and earned.**
The enumeration of every enforcement network touch-point — the ledger entry, the badge, Vel's exact word *"testimony,"* the pale cut face of the bridge supports — is the moment most susceptible to vagueness, and the draft executes it with precision. Each planted detail from Acts 1 and 2 is recovered by name. The reader who paid attention can feel the architecture snap into place. This is the brief's hardest technical requirement (check 9: the retroactive re-casting must be specific) and the manuscript meets it.

**4. The vendor stall moment in chapter 10.**
*"She didn't decide anything. She leaned, and he met her, and it was brief, and she felt her nose hit his chin, which was genuinely embarrassing, and then it was over."*
The nose-on-chin detail is the single best craft decision in Thread 1. It keeps the moment funny, specific, and YA-appropriate; it keeps the romance in subplot; it lets both of them move to the gate queue without ceremony. This passage should not be changed.

**5. "Your master is brave" and the pre-prepared letter.**
Two moments in chapter 11 land with exactly the right compression. Iri's four words close Thread 2 — the most personal thing she says in the book, delivered flat, with nothing added — and the chapter earns it because it was built through action, not speech, for eleven chapters prior. The letter reveal (*"Since before we left Drey." / "Since before we left Drey."*) gives Pip his full-competence moment through repetition alone, no commentary required. Both are the book doing what the brief's exemplar authors do: trusting the line.

**6. "She took one step."**
The final line is correct in register, in brevity, and in refusal. It does not tell us which direction. It does not tell us what she decided. The scene before it — the unsent letter in the empty kit case, the dawn moment on the threshold — earns the ending without announcing what it means. Leave it alone.

---

## What Isn't Working

**1. Three chapter endings fail the brief's hard cliffhanger contract.**
Chapter 02 ends on post-resolution interiority (*"her right hand had found the bag's strap and closed there, tight and specific, and she hadn't told it to"*) after the watcher has already walked over the hill. The threat dissipated before the cut; the chapter ends on residual anxiety rendered as sensation. Chapter 04 ends with everyone settled at a fire after Iri's *"places I used to know"* revelation has deflated rather than sharpened — the peak of the scene's tension is Iri's *"I was wrong to be surprised they sent this many,"* and the chapter continues past it into quiet. Chapter 07 ends with *"She was later fairly sure Iri had finished the sentence anyway. She would never know for certain"* — which is charming, resolved, and explicitly prohibited by the brief. All three are literary fades. These are structural violations, not stylistic preferences, and they will cost readers at exactly the moments the book most needs to pull them forward.

**2. The POV contract is broken twice.**
Chapter 06's presentation of Vel's interiority is stated as omniscient fact: *"She meant it. She had done the arithmetic on this situation and arrived at an offer she considered fair. She wasn't performing... She was making the offer she would have wanted to receive."* Vel's motivation is reported as true, not framed through Tess's reading of her face or manner. The brief is explicit: insight about another character is Tess's inference, not a narrator's report. Chapter 08's final line (*"He could tell"* — Pip's read of Tess) similarly presents omniscient knowledge as fact in a single sentence. Both need reframing as Tess's observation.

**3. Three extended interiority passages exceed what the register permits.**
The wardworking sequence in chapter 07 runs approximately 700–800 words with minimal dialogue and substantial technical interiority about chalk adhesion and anchor-point geometry. The re-casting enumeration in chapter 11 runs approximately 600 words of continuous Tess interiority with no external action or dialogue breaks — the style guide flags 4–6 sentences as the ceiling. The decision-mulling in chapter 12 runs roughly 250 words without external event; the letter-writing section immediately after compounds this with a lyrical fragment-list passage. The content in all of these passages is correct and necessary; the length and form are not.

**4. Register drift into literary adult fantasy in chapters 07 and 12.**
The wardworking sequence reads as the Rothfuss register — technically accomplished, magic-system detailed, meditative — rather than the Bardugo register the book targets. The letter-writing sequence in chapter 12 (*"West end of Drey, the well, the lamp in the window. The herbs drying on the kitchen side of the worktable — three bundles in late summer, two in spring, always the same ones, always hung the same way"*) is beautiful adult literary prose and is precisely not what this book is. Craft excellence in the wrong register is not success for *The Sealed Bag*; it is the specific failure mode the project guidance names explicitly. A 13-year-old reader who has been moving fast for eleven chapters is the most likely person to set the book down here.

**5. Sentence-after-the-sentence over-annotation.**
Chapter 04 glosses Brann's compressed *"Thank you"* with *"That was it. Possibly the entirety of Brann's vocabulary for someone did a useful thing and I am glad of it"* — the joke has already landed; the gloss kills it. Chapter 12's *"comfortable silence"* paragraph (*"The silence was the comfortable kind. Tess had not expected, three weeks ago at a pre-dawn departure from a village square, that a seventeen-year-old blacksmith's son from Drey would be someone she could be quiet with"*) names what the Brann/Tess thread has been showing for twelve chapters. This is the pattern the style guide calls post-scene subtext naming. Both instances undercut what they mean to celebrate.

## Pacing Assessment

The book's pacing is strong through chapters 1–6 and 9–11, with two problem zones. Chapter 07 (The Pavers' Country) is the midbook sag: the wardworking sequence slows to near-stop, the chapter ends on a resolved note, and two of the threads advancing in this chapter do so at the quietest possible pace. This is where the target reader is most at risk of losing momentum. The sag is compounded by chapter 05's borderline ending, which means the reader enters chapter 07 without a strong enough forward pull from the previous chapter.

The second problem zone is the first two-thirds of chapter 12. The quiet mode is structurally appropriate for a closing chapter, but the decision-mulling and letter-writing passages fall below the speed the register can sustain. The last place to lose a 13-year-old is the final chapter.

Chapters 03, 10, and 11 are the book's best-paced chapters. The ambush runs without a wasted beat. Chapter 10 moves nearly without a dead spot from note-reading through gate checkpoint cliffhanger. Chapter 11 fires its major events at speed despite the length of the re-casting block.

## Character Assessment

**Tess**: Alive and earned throughout. The book's single-POV discipline rewards her: we are close enough that her specific fear (dismissed as a practitioner, not as a person) reads clearly without announcement. Her arc from *professionally cool hiding intimidation* to *alliance with Iri* tracks correctly.

**Brann**: Alive and earned. His compression works because it has been consistent — when he says something, it lands, because he hasn't been filling silence. The twelve-chapter accumulation of small actions (coat, pace adjustment, *"Real,"* five words at the end) is exactly how Thread 1 needed to be built.

**Iri**: Alive and earned. The accidental literalism is consistent and correctly unannounced. Her magic costs are visible and real from chapter 03 forward. "Your master is brave" closes Thread 2 in four words because the draft built to it for eleven chapters.

**Pip**: Alive. His competence surprise — the reader thinks they know what he's for (comedy) and discovers he's the person who clears the enforcement checkpoint — is the book's best structural reveal. Thread 4 is correctly refused: acknowledged, not opened.

**Mara**: Alive in one chapter and effectively alive in seven through the weight of *"deliver it whole."* Correctly rationed.

**Sable Orr**: Functional. Professional and careful, not cold. Her page time is right.

---

## Thread Assessment

All five threads develop as specified and reach their designated endpoints. **Thread 3 (What Mara Knew)** is executed with particular precision: the re-casting in chapter 11 is specific, the unsent letter is the correct emotional endpoint, and the thread closes without answering Tess's question — which is exactly what it should do. **Thread 4 (Pip's Hidden Asterhall)** is correctly acknowledged and refused; the ch 12 partial disclosure (V was okay, Pip didn't die) is the most honest thing Pip says on the entire road. **Thread 5 (Enforcement Network)** builds coherently through every act; every planted element is recovered in ch 11 by name.

No thread is dropped. No thread is over-resolved. The weave design holds.

---

## Voice Assessment

The voice is in the right register for approximately 85–90% of the manuscript. Character dialogue holds its YA-contemporary coding throughout all twelve chapters. The bold declarative interior (the Novik cadence) is used correctly throughout. Short chapter-ending lines are defended well where they appear. The principal failure mode is **register drift in sustained prose passages** — the wardworking sequence (ch 07), the layered smell inventory at Asterhall's gate (ch 10, defensible but borderline), and the letter-writing sequence (ch 12). In all three cases, the prose moves toward the meditative, image-inventory mode of adult literary fantasy. This drift does not appear in motion — it appears specifically in moments when the narrative wants to slow down and linger, and in those moments it reaches for the wrong register. The fix is not to remove the slowing; it is to slow in the right way for this book: shorter sentences, physical interruption, a dialogue beat.

---

## Suggested Revisions

**1. Replace the three failing chapter endings.**
Chapter 02 should cut at Brann raising his hand at the watcher — before the watcher walks away, before Tess's hand finds the strap. Chapter 04 should cut at the chapter's moment of sharpest tension before the fire-scene settles: *"I was wrong to be surprised they sent this many"* is a candidate, or the moment Brann shuts the conversation down in a way that sharpens rather than resolves. Chapter 07 should cut mid-sentence — before Tess falls asleep, mid-Iri-implication about what the bag builds, on the interrupted information rather than on the retrospective smile at having missed it.

**2. Repair the two POV violations.**
Reframe the Vel interiority block in chapter 06 through Tess's external observation: what does Tess see in Vel's face and manner that makes her infer Vel means it? Replace the assertion with the evidence. Reframe *"He could tell"* in chapter 08 as Tess's inference: *"She could tell he could tell"* restores POV without changing the beat.

**3. Compress the three extended interiority passages.**
The wardworking in chapter 07 needs approximately 30% compression and should reintroduce a dialogue beat (Pip, Brann, or Iri saying one thing) in its middle third to break the uninterrupted thought-sequence. The re-casting block in chapter 11 needs two or three external interruptions — Sable writing something, Brann shifting his weight near the door — to give Tess's enumeration a rhythm break without changing what she's thinking through. The decision-mulling and letter-writing in chapter 12 should be condensed by roughly one-third each and re-grounded with at least one physical action beat before the unsent letter lands.

**4. Cut the over-annotation instances.**
Chapter 04: cut *"That was it. Possibly the entirety of Brann's vocabulary for someone did a useful thing and I am glad of it"* — Brann's *"Thank you"* stands alone. Chapter 07: cut the parenthetical about proving something; the ward-laying already demonstrates it. Chapter 12: cut the *"comfortable silence"* paragraph; the scene has shown it.

---

## What to Leave Alone

**The vendor stall kiss in chapter 10.** The embarrassing nose-on-chin detail is perfect. The brevity is correct. The immediate return to the gate queue is exactly right. Any revision that lingers longer, adds sentiment, or smooths away the embarrassment will make this worse. Do not touch it.

**Pip's fire-side unguarded moment in chapter 08** — *"I know people at that checkpoint. They might not be glad to see me,"* followed by his face dropping the performance at day's end, followed by *"If I say run, you run."* This is the most compressed revelation of Pip's actual interior in the book, and it is compressed precisely right. The subtext carries without annotation. Leave it.

**The ambush chapter (ch 03) in its entirety.** The opening line (*"Pip had been talking since the first morning out of Drey, and when he stopped, the road sounded wrong"*) is the act's best. The physical action is concrete and specific. The magic cost is rendered from the outside. The boot throw is the right absurdity in the right register. The cliffhanger cuts correctly. This chapter proves the book can do what it says it can do.

**"Your master is brave."** Four words. Nothing added. Nothing should be.

**"She took one step."** The direction withheld. The book ends here, correctly. Do not add a sentence.
