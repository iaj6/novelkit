## 02-the-wagon-road.md

**Chapter ending — potential literary-fade violation (writer's call)**

The final paragraph reads:

> "Somewhere between watching the figure and not watching it anymore, her right hand had found the bag's strap and closed there, tight and specific, and she hadn't told it to."

This is strong prose and correctly renders fear through physical action rather than labeled emotion. However, the chapter's final beat is internal and quiet — the threat has passed (the watcher walked away), the party has resumed walking, and the last line is Tess registering her own unconscious grip. No threat is arriving, no revelation is left unanswered, no action is cut at peak tension, no line of dialogue changes the situation.

The style guide requires every chapter to end on a cliffhanger or mid-action beat and explicitly prohibits literary fade ("ending on mood, reflection, or a quiet resolved moment"). This ending leans into the latter.

**One possible reading in the rule's favor:** if the chapter ending is construed as "a threat arrives or becomes undeniable — cut at the moment of recognition," then Tess's involuntary grip is the moment she can no longer tell herself nothing is wrong — which would satisfy the contract. That interpretation is defensible but not certain.

**Suggested options for the writer:**
1. Cut the final prose paragraph entirely and end on Pip's dialogue line: *"That wasn't standard." / Nobody responded. / They kept walking.* — this is a harder, more active stop that still doesn't fully resolve the threat, and it ends on action rather than interiority.
2. Keep the current ending if you read Tess's involuntary grip as the chapter's recognition-of-threat cut. It is the right emotional moment; the question is whether it satisfies the structural contract.

No word-level fix was applied here — this requires a structural decision.

## 04-the-guild-token.md

**Compression miss — sentence-after-the-sentence gloss on Brann's dialogue (writer's judgment required)**

After Brann says "Thank you" following the arm-bandaging, the draft continues:

> "That was it. Possibly the entirety of Brann's vocabulary for *someone did a useful thing and I am glad of it.*"

This is the "sentence-after-the-sentence" failure mode from `canon/style.md`: a punchy line lands, then the next sentence (or two) glosses it. "Thank you" is the line; the italicised paraphrase is the annotation. Per the style guide, Brann's deadpan should land without translation.

Compression should have cut the gloss. The voice pass cannot delete sentences, so this is flagged for the writer. The fix is to cut "That was it. Possibly the entirety of Brann's vocabulary for *someone did a useful thing and I am glad of it.*" entirely — "Thank you," he said." is sufficient, and the annotating cadence undercuts Brann's documented deadpan voice.

**Fix applied this pass:**

- `"the kind of thing that felt necessary and slightly wrong in equal measure"` → `"the kind of thing that was necessary and slightly wrong in equal measure"`. The `felt` construction labels interiority; the style guide requires physical signal or flat declarative inference instead. `was` converts this to Tess's dry declarative read, which is on-register.
## 09-the-crossing.md

**One fixable voice issue found and repaired.**

### Meta-cognition labeling — two consecutive "thought about" frames (repaired)

Style guide failure mode: *"Meta-commentary on cognition — 'She noticed,' 'she registered,' 'she realized she was thinking about.' This labels the observation and loses it."*

The passage as drafted:

> Tess thought about the two or three seconds where the current had stepped aside.
>
> She thought about Iri in the water, crossing without the rope — the rope had gone with Tess, the line was fouled.

Both sentences put a cognitive-labeling frame ("thought about") around observations the chapter's established rhythm can carry as direct fragments. The pattern is already present in this chapter: "Under." / "Dark. Loud. Cold so complete it stopped being a sensation." / "Hard. But solid, fixed to the riverbed, solid." Converting these two instances to bare fragments:

> Two or three seconds where the current had stepped aside.
>
> Iri in the water, crossing without the rope — the rope had gone with Tess, the line was fouled.

This removes the meta-cognitive label, puts the inference on the page directly, and matches the chapter's fragment rhythm. No other changes made.

**Chapter is otherwise clean.** Dialogue voices (Brann deadpan-minimal, Pip competent-jokey-then-silent, Iri literal, Tess dry-observational) are all on-register. No exclamation marks, no adverb-heavy tags, no interpretive glosses, no labeled emotion ("felt" appears once for physical proprioception, acceptable). Opening and closing are both on-register per the style guide.

**Flagged for writer judgment — item 1:**  
Italicized addendum after Pip's dialogue:

> `"Nobody uproad would read this." *Nobody from uproad.*`

The unattributed italicized line is ambiguous: it could be (a) Tess registering Pip's self-correction of his own phrasing, rendered as free indirect thought, or (b) a narrative gloss on what Pip meant — which would run afoul of the "dialogue glosses" prohibition. If it's (a), the intent may be clearer with attribution ("*She caught the correction: nobody from uproad*") or simply cutting the italicized addendum. If it's (b), it should go. Compression pass left it in; the writer should decide which reading is intended.

**Flagged for writer judgment — item 2:**  
`"watching everything, asking questions of nothing"` (Iri moving through the vendor stalls)

`asking questions of nothing` is an abstract, slightly purple construction that sits at the edge of Tess's register. Tess's voice runs dry and compressed; this phrase reaches for the literary in a way that strains it. Suggested alternatives stay at the word level: `watching everything, asking nothing aloud` or `watching everything, silent about all of it`. The existing phrase is not a hard violation — it survived compression — but it doesn't read like Tess's diction and may want revisiting.
