The bag was heavier than it looked.

Tess had expected that. Mara had told her: leather, sealed, no questions asked. She hadn't mentioned the weight, but that was Mara — she answered what she thought you could use and left you the rest to figure out on your own. Tess shifted it and the stone floor was cold through her socks. She'd been up since before the bells.

"Weavers' Guild seal," Mara said, setting a candle on the table so Tess could see it. Dawn was coming through the shutters in thin lines. The cottage smelled like dried willow bark and the faint salt-cold of the well outside. "Don't pick at it."

"I wasn't going to pick at it."

"You're touching it."

Tess moved her thumb. The seal was wax, embossed, red-brown in the candlelight. A mark she didn't recognize exactly — she'd seen guild marks before on the supply invoices Mara got twice a year. This one was different. More intricate, or maybe just older. She would have needed another thirty seconds to really look.

"Named recipient in Asterhall," Mara said. "Three weeks east. The contract pays for four. You're the courier of record."

"I know. You told me."

"I'm telling you again." Mara's voice was the same voice she used for most things — steady, not unkind, not particularly interested in what Tess thought about it. She was pulling Tess's kit bag out from under the workbench: chalk, salt, herbs, cord, all of it wrapped the way Mara had shown her. "Brann carries. The girl Iri does the real magic. Pip knows the city when you get there."

The way she said *real magic* landed the way it always did — like a fact, not a judgment, which somehow made it worse.

"What's in the bag?" Tess asked.

"Nothing that concerns you yet."

That was the whole answer. Tess had known it would be. She took the kit, settled the straps. The sealed bag went over her left shoulder and the kit over her right. For a moment she just stood there in Mara's kitchen while the candle threw their shadows long against the wall.

Mara crossed the room. She took Tess's hand — the left one, resting on the sealed bag — and pressed it flat against the leather.

"Deliver it whole," she said. "Whatever happens on that road."

Tess waited for the rest of it.

There wasn't any.
