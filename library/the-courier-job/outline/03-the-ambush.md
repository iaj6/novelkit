# Chapter 03 — The Ambush

**One-line summary**: Five guild enforcers hit the party at a river-bend ambush; the party survives on Brann's body, Iri's costly magic, and Tess's improvised ward; the attackers specifically shouted for the bag, which means this was never about money.

---

## Chapter Goal

Deliver the book's first physical action set piece: fast, specific, physical, costly. Every party member does something concrete. The attack is clearly targeted at the bag. The chapter ends before the party can fully process what happened.

---

## Scene Breakdown

### Section A: The Approach (~600 words)
- The road rounds a river bend — the road dips between two rises, river close on the left, trees on the right. Sight lines in both directions are cut to thirty or forty meters.
- Pip notes the geography without seeming to notice it: he starts walking slightly closer to Brann. Tess doesn't register this consciously until later.
- One sign before it starts: a bird — flock of river birds, lifting from the trees on the right. Brann slows. He's about to say something.
- Then it happens.

### Section B: The Ambush (~2,400 words)
- Five attackers emerge: three from the right ditch, two from behind. Coordinated, pre-positioned. Not a mob — they've done this before.
- They are dressed like road laborers. They are not road laborers.
- The opening beat: one goes straight for Tess — specifically for the bag. Not for her purse, not for Brann's pack (which is larger). The bag. He shouts "Get the bag!" to his nearest colleague.

**Key retroactive clue planted here**: The attacker says "Get the bag!" — not "Get the money" or "Get the girl." This sounds like tactical efficiency in the moment. In ch 11, it re-casts as proof the enforcement wing told them exactly what to take.

- **Brann**: anchors. He puts himself between Tess and the two attacking from the right with an efficiency that is not flashy — he is simply in the way, and then people are having a hard time getting past him. He is fighting two at once and holding both to a stalemate, which is enough. He takes a hit — a blade across the left forearm, not deep, not nothing.
- **Tess**: no combat magic. She has a kit, not a weapon. What she does: while Brann holds the line, she drops to one knee, tears open her kit, and draws a chalk ward in the dirt of the road — fast, rough, the kind of hedge-pattern she normally does in three hours but this version takes thirty seconds and is barely functional. What it does: slows one attacker stepping over it, just enough — the man's foot catches, he stumbles, buys two seconds. It's not magic as a superpower. It's magic as a small useful fact.
  - Note for drafter: Tess is scared. She is processing at speed. She does not have time to label being scared. She is doing the next thing.
- **Iri**: the working. She hasn't spoken yet. Then she does — one word, or something that isn't a word — and the two attackers flanking Tess's right simply stop moving. Not knocked out, not killed: stopped, as if their legs have forgotten the instruction. They are held for eight or nine seconds. It is long enough.
  - The cost of this: Iri is immediately wrong. Her color goes wrong — the particular pallor that means she's paying from somewhere deep. Her hands shake. She keeps her feet but she needs Pip's shoulder to do it.
- **Pip**: not a fighter. But he spots the one attacker who is staying back — watching, not engaging — and realizes that one is holding a horse. Their escape route. Pip does not fight the man; he throws something (a stone from the road, his boot, the thing he called "insurance") that startles the horse badly enough to make it bolt. The escape route is gone. The watcher swears and chases the horse. Now it's five against four with no reinforcements and no retreat.
- The turn: with Iri's working holding two, Brann holding two, and the fifth chasing his horse, Tess's thirty-second ward already starting to fail — the math changes. The two held by Iri snap free when the working expires; Iri is already down on one knee. But Brann has gained enough position that one of the original two attackers has backed away (a pragmatist: the odds have shifted). The remaining two engage but now they're at a disadvantage, because Brann has moved and Tess has gotten off the road and Pip has found a branch.
- One attacker goes down and stays down. One gets away — he bolts east, up the road ahead of them. One is alive and not running, sitting in the road ditch with a hand pressed to his leg.
- They stand in the road. The river keeps going.

### Section C: The Immediate Aftermath (~600 words + ending)
- Assessment: Brann's arm wound — a cut, bleeding, not arterial. He is looking at it the way he'd look at a wrong turn: mild inconvenience, correctable.
- Iri: sitting against a tree, pale, hands around her knees. She has eaten all the food anyone offers and she's still shaking.
- Pip: inventorying what they recovered. He is talking, but slower than usual. He picks up a short sword that one of them dropped and weighs it thoughtfully.
- Tess: checks the bag. It's there. Seal intact. She feels the weight of it on her back and has a sudden sense of it being both heavier and more specific than before.
- The man in the ditch is alive and not talking.
- Tess's ward in the road: chalk smeared from the fight, barely readable. She looks at it and knows it's exhausted. That's roughly one-quarter of her chalk.
- Then: hoofbeats from the east. Fast. Getting louder.

**ENDING (mid-action cut):** The hoofbeats are coming up the road — from the direction the one attacker fled. Fast enough to mean something urgent. Brann is already on his feet. Iri is trying to stand. The chapter cuts before the rider comes into view.

## Character Beats

- **Tess (Act 1 competence moment)**: The thirty-second chalk ward. It is rough, limited, barely functional — and it works. This is hedge magic doing what hedge magic can do: a small useful thing under pressure. Tess should not be triumphant about it. She is doing the next thing.
- **Brann (Act 1 competence moment)**: Physical anchor. He holds two attackers to a stalemate, takes a cut, and repositions during Iri's working with tactical instinct he doesn't explain. Stranger mode with Tess but she is watching him closely now.
- **Iri (Act 1 competence moment)**: The major working that turns the fight. Its cost is immediate and visible. The drafter should describe Iri's working as a physical event — something in the air changes, then she pays for it. No explanation of the mechanism. The cost is the character beat.
- **Pip (Act 1 scene-maker)**: Not a fighter. Does the thing a non-fighter can do: removes the enemy's strategic asset (the horse) and the watcher attached to it. His move is specific and slightly chaotic. It works because it had to.

## Action Sequence Notes (per style guide)

- Physical and concrete. Specificity over abstraction: not "there was a fight" but "Brann caught the second man's wrist mid-swing and turned it, and the knife hit the dirt."
- No slow-motion interiority during the fight. Tess processes in fragments, in sequence, in the order events happen.
- Iri's working is described in terms of what it does to her and what it does to the attackers, not its mechanism. Magic is a visible cost, not a superpower.
- The ward in the dirt: describe what Tess draws (rough circle, two chalk lines), what it costs (a quarter of her chalk, plus the time when she's exposed), what it does (one attacker stumbles on the threshold). Small, real, specific.

## Subplot Beats

- **Tess/Brann**: She watches him work in the fight the way you watch someone do something they're clearly good at. No words. She will remember the efficiency of it. Stranger mode — but the boundary of stranger is shifting.
- **Tess/Iri**: First time Tess sees what Iri's power costs. The pallor, the shaking, needing Pip's shoulder. Tess's intimidation has a new component: she didn't know it looked like that.

## Continuity Notes

- Brann sustains a wound: left forearm, cut, non-arterial. It must be present in ch 4.
- Iri's major working depletes her: she needs food and rest; this is her state at the start of ch 4.
- Tess's kit: one-quarter of her chalk used. First degradation of the kit.
- The bag is present and intact throughout; Tess checks it immediately post-fight.
- One attacker escapes east. This is the person who sent word ahead — relevant to ch 5's setup.
- The "Get the bag!" shout: this is the retroactive clue for ch 11. Plant it clearly enough to be credible in retrospect, not so heavily as to feel obvious in the moment.
- Magic used: Tess (hedge ward, crude, one use), Iri (major working, costly). Both observable costs.

## New Facts This Chapter Introduces

- The attackers are coordinated and targeted — they wanted the bag specifically.
- Iri's major magic has immediate physical consequences: pallor, trembling, physical need.
- Tess can improvise hedge magic under pressure, at reduced effectiveness and higher material cost.
- One attacker escaped east ahead of the party.
