# Chapter 06 — The Rival Party

**One-line summary**: A professional party of four presents as fellow travelers and then tries to buy the bag, and then tries to take it; Iri burns herself dry ending the confrontation; the rival leader drops "testimony" before retreating, and then Pip sees more on the ridge.

## Chapter Goal

Deliver the book's second physical action set piece — different in character from ch 3. Ch 3 was a sudden ambush by numbers; this is a professional standoff that escalates to force. The rival party has more discipline than the ambushers. The second retroactive clue is planted: the word "testimony." The chapter ends before the party knows whether they're surrounded.

---

## Scene Breakdown

### Section A: The False Companions (~900 words)
- Day 11. The road east of Long Crossing is thinner, quieter. Fewer merchants, more road marker posts, the river now at a distance.
- The rival party appears from a side road junction, four of them, presented as a legitimate traveling group: two fighters, a person with a ledger (reading as a factor or accountant), and their leader — a woman named Vel (Tess won't learn her name until she says it herself). Well-armed in a mundane way. Horses. More gear than the party.
- They fall into pace beside Tess's group: "Same road. Safer in numbers. Bad stretch coming up." Their manner is friendly and confident. They've done this before.
- The party does not tell them to leave. This would be unusual; strangers travel together. But:
  - Pip keeps up a perfectly normal-sounding conversation with Vel's ledger-person and comes away with nothing useful, which means they offered nothing, which is suspicious.
  - Brann watches the two fighters. Not confrontationally — just tracking them the way he tracks anything that might become a problem. They clock him doing it.
  - Iri says almost nothing to the new group. Exactly nothing, actually.
- They reach a roadside clearing — oak stand, a stone well (dry), good shade. The kind of spot that travelers use for midday rest because it's been used for midday rest for fifty years.
- They stop. They eat. Vel sits near Tess.

### Section B: The Offer (~600 words)
- Vel does not waste time. She is not a person who wastes time.
- "I know you're carrying a sealed contract bag," she says. "I know where it's going and roughly what's in it." She names the fee she'll pay: twice the contract fee. She says it the way people say things they expect to be accepted.
- "You're four teenagers on the road with a bag that people are willing to hurt you for. Hand it over. Take the money. Go home. Nobody gets hurt, and that's genuinely the better outcome for you."
- She means it. She is not performing. She genuinely thinks this is the rational outcome.
- Tess says no.
- Vel: "That's going to make this harder."
- Tess: "Probably."
- A beat. Vel signals. The two fighters stand.

### Section C: The Confrontation (~1,800 words)
- The rival party is more disciplined than the ch 3 ambushers. Their fighters know what they're doing. Their goal is not to hurt the party — it's to separate Tess from the bag, which is harder than it sounds.
- **Brann**: holds position, physically. He's in front of Tess. The two fighters push at him and he does not move in a way that suggests surprise — he moves in a way that suggests he has been expecting this. He takes hits but doesn't go down. He is buying time.
  
  **(Brann Act 2 competence moment)**: Physical anchor, extended. He holds both fighters long enough for Iri to work.

- **Pip**: Not useless this time — he has the short sword from ch 3 and he's placed himself between the ledger-person (who turns out to also be a fighter — of course) and Tess. He is not good with a sword. He is, however, hard to corner because he is small and completely unpredictable. He disarms the ledger-person not through technique but through bewildering chaos: throwing the sword sideways, catching the return, going under a swing no sensible person would go under.
- **Tess**: Cannot do combat magic. She is holding the bag, staying behind Brann, watching for an opening. She throws chalk at the nearer fighter's face — it's not a charm, it's powder in the eyes — and gets three seconds of him being blinded. She uses the three seconds to back into the tree line where there isn't a clear approach line. This is Tess being smart about having no good options.
- **Iri**: The major working. She waits until Brann has held long enough that the fighters are properly committed and cannot easily disengage. Then she works — and this time the cost comes faster and harder than in ch 3. The working takes both fighters off their feet (not holds like ch 3, but something more — a push, a force, the kind of thing that ends fights). Both fighters go down. One gets up slowly; the other doesn't get up quickly.
- Iri is on her knees before the working is finished. She makes no sound. She just folds.
- The standoff: Vel has not engaged physically. She watched. She is now looking at her two fighters and making the arithmetic that says this has stopped being profitable.
- She meets Tess's eyes. She says: *"You have no idea what you're carrying. Someone's testimony. You know that? You're going to die for someone's testimony."*
  - Note for drafter: she means this as a taunt, or possibly as an actual attempt to get Tess to reconsider. She is not wrong about the facts. The word "testimony" should land as strange — it's specific. Not "secrets" or "evidence" or "contraband." Testimony.

**Key retroactive clue planted here**: "Someone's testimony." In ch 11, when Tess learns the bag contains seven sworn depositions, this re-casts as Vel knowing exactly what the bag contains.

- Vel withdraws her fighters. They go — not running, walking with controlled speed. Professional. They don't give their backs immediately.

### Section D: Aftermath + Ending (~600 words + ending cut)
- Iri is on the ground. She is breathing. Her hands have stopped working as hands — they are shaking so badly she cannot use them. She needs help up.
- Pip helps her up. He doesn't make a joke. This is notable.
- Tess checks the bag. Intact. Sealed.
- Brann has blood on his shirt — not from his arm wound but from something new, a bruise breaking on his cheek. He touches it and seems mildly surprised it's there.
- They need to move. Everyone knows it.
- But Pip is looking at the ridge that runs above the road, north side. He is very still.
- He says, flat: *"There are more of them. On the ridge. I can see them — four, maybe five. They were up there for the whole thing."*

**ENDING (mid-action cut):** The ridge. Above the road, watching. They may have just seen what Iri can do. They may also be about to flank. The chapter cuts before the party decides or moves.

## Character Beats

- **Tess**: Keeps her head. The chalk-throw is not clever magic; it is using what she has. She moves into the tree line under pressure, which is good tactical thinking with no resources. She refuses Vel's offer without grandstanding — she says "no" and that's all.
- **Brann**: Extended physical anchor. Takes hits. Does not go down. Buys exactly enough time. After the fight he touches his bruise with the expression of someone doing an inventory.
- **Iri (Act 2 competence moment)**: The major working that ends the confrontation. It costs her immediately — she goes down before it's finished. No speeches. No drama. She does it and pays the price.
- **Pip**: Chaos-fighter. Unpredictable in close quarters in a way that compensates for lack of technique. His flat delivery of the ridge observation is the chapter's final beat.
- **Vel**: The book's most clearly-framed antagonist so far. Professional, calm, and genuinely convinced she's offering Tess a better option. Her use of "testimony" is specific and should feel exact — not a rhetorical flourish but a slip of precise language.

---

## Subplot Beats

- **Tess/Brann**: After the fight, Tess is close enough to see the new bruise on his face. She doesn't touch it; she doesn't offer to tend it. She notices. He notices her noticing and says nothing. This is the reluctant-ally mode expressing itself through acute awareness they don't name.
- **Tess/Iri**: Iri on the ground, Pip helping her up. Tess watches Iri accept Pip's help without argument — which is not something Iri does easily — and understands that Iri is more depleted than she's showing. The intimidation thread has a second significant beat: Iri keeps deploying this power despite knowing the cost. That requires watching to understand.

---

## Continuity Notes

- Iri's major working is her second significant deployment: she is now deeply depleted. She needs food, water, and real rest; she cannot work again for at least twenty-four hours. This must carry into ch 7.
- Tess used chalk as a thrown substance (not a charm). This is not magic — it's a material being used as a tool. Her kit chalk supply is further reduced.
- The bag is intact throughout; Tess checks it immediately.
- The rival party retreated; they have not been neutralized. They are still on the road.
- The ridge watchers: unresolved at chapter end — this is the cliffhanger. They are not addressed again until the party has moved; ch 7 can open with confirmation they escaped.
- "Testimony" — this is the second retroactive clue. The word must be rendered precisely; Vel is not guessing, she knows.

---

## New Facts This Chapter Introduces

- The rival party is professional and pre-informed — they knew about the bag, its destination, and made a specific offer.
- Vel uses the word "testimony" — she knows the bag's specific contents, not just that it's sealed and valuable.
- The enforcement wing has a two-layer operation on this road: the first ambush, now a rival party, plus watchers on the ridge.
- Iri's power has limits: a second major working in a week depletes her severely.
