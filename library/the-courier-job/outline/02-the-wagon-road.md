# Chapter 02 — The Wagon Road

**One-line summary**: The party finds its road rhythm through banter, minor friction, and three days of walking; a guild checkpoint officer logs them oddly; a loose horse and a motionless figure on a hill confirm someone is tracking them.

---

## Vitals

| | |
|---|---|
| **POV** | Tess |
| **Tense** | Third-person limited past |
| **Setting** | The Wagon Road East — river valley, rolling farmland, a guild road-toll checkpoint |
| **Time** | Days 2–4 of the journey (the first week's rhythm) |
| **Target words** | ~3,200 |
| **Chapter shape** | Travel-plus-encounter (travel banter establishes party dynamics; the checkpoint is the encounter; the surveillance is the closing beat) |
| **Ending mode** | Recognition cut |

---

## Chapter Goal

Establish the party's travel dynamic through dialogue and small action. Deliver the first concrete sign that the party is being watched — not dramatic, not violent, just undeniable once Tess adds it up. End with confirmation: someone is tracking them.

---

## Scene Breakdown

### Section A: Travel (~1,200 words)
- Day 2 opens in motion. The party has cleared Drey's county. River valley road, broad and merchant-traveled. Wagons pass in both directions; toll-booth at the county line takes a small crossing fee and waves them through without interest.
- Travel banter establishes the party's rhythm:
  - Pip talks. He has theories about every wagon they pass. His read of people is instinctive and specific — he narrates the wagon that has too much military canvas for a merchant, the woman on the gray horse who is reading a ledger while riding, the boy running parallel to the road in the field (he bets the boy is cutting across to tell someone they're coming).
  - Brann listens. Occasionally he says one word that clarifies everything Pip said. Pip doesn't miss a beat.
  - Iri walks slightly apart. She is not antisocial; she is merely not calibrated to continuous banter. She interjects once with something factually precise that is technically on-topic and stops all conversation for three seconds.
  - Tess is watching and taking notes internally — who these people are, what they notice, what they don't say. Her internal narration here is observational and dry.
- Night 2: barn space, paid. Pip tells a story about a merchant he once followed for three days across a city who turned out to be following someone else. It's funny and may be true. Brann asks one question at the end. Iri asks a different question that is not what anyone expected.
- Day 3: Tess checks the bag's seal during a rest stop. Still intact. She does this quietly; Brann notices and doesn't say anything. Tess notices that he notices.
- The bag's weight: the bag is on Tess's shoulder. She's aware of it constantly. When she sets it down to rest, she keeps one hand near it. She begins to be annoyed at herself for this.

### Section B: The Checkpoint (~1,000 words)
- A guild road-toll checkpoint at a road junction — stone booth, a ledger, an officer in blue-gray road livery (not full enforcement blue, but guild-marked).
- Routine examination: contract papers checked, party listed, a small road-toll paid. The officer is professional. He asks the standard questions.
- Then: he asks to record the contract's seal number. He opens his ledger to a page that already has several entries. He copies the number carefully.
- Tess watches. Brann watches. Pip is studying the road ahead.
- Tess asks, casually, if that's standard. The officer says it's standard for sealed contracts above a certain value threshold.
  - Note for drafter: He is not hostile. He is not threatening. He is simply doing something that is slightly non-standard in a way that is easy to explain away. Tess does not press it.
- They're waved through. The checkpoint officer goes back to his ledger.
- Out of earshot: Pip says, "That wasn't standard." He says this the way he'd say the weather.
- Iri says nothing. Brann says nothing. They keep walking.

### Section C: The Surveillance (~800 words + ending)
- Day 4. The road turns along the river. They pass a crossroads where the road dips toward a ford.
- A horse stands at the ford: gray, saddled, no rider. It is nibbling at the river grass without particular urgency.
- The party recognizes the horse: it matches the one the rider in ch 1 was on. (Pip clocks this immediately. Tess takes a moment. Brann doesn't say anything about recognizing it, but he slows his pace.)
- Pip: "So where'd his rider go?"
- No answer. They keep moving, faster.
- The road climbs slightly above the valley. From the high point, looking back: a figure on a hill, off the road, standing still. Watching the road in their direction.
- Brann sees it first and doesn't say anything. He jerks his chin.
- Everyone looks.
- The figure does not move while they watch. Then, when Brann points — just raises one hand, nothing dramatic — the figure turns and walks away quickly. Not running. Walking, but the pace of walking-as-fast-as-possible.
- Tess keeps walking.

**ENDING (recognition cut):** The figure is gone over the hill. The chapter ends on the fact of it — that the figure left when it was seen, which is something you do when you're watching someone and you want them to know it without making a scene. Tess doesn't have language for it yet, but her body does: her grip on the bag tightens, and she doesn't consciously decide to tighten it.

## Character Beats

- **Tess**: Observer mode. She's building a picture of each party member through their road behavior. Her narration is dry and quick, moving over external facts and occasional inferences. The bag is a constant low-level physical awareness. She has not yet told anyone about Mara's "deliver it whole" warning.
- **Brann**: Operates in the landscape. He is the first to spot the watcher on the hill, communicated with a chin-jerk. He asks one question at the campfire story. He notices Tess checking the bag. He does not perform any of this; he just does it.
- **Iri**: Walks apart, interjects with something precise and conversation-stopping. Her one interjection should be factual and funny in its literalism — something about the merchant wagon's canvas that is correct and irrelevant.
- **Pip**: The checkpoint read is his moment here — he knows something was off and says so after the fact, without fanfare. He also identifies the horse. He is never the last to notice. This is road competence, not city competence.
- **Nobody** talks about the watcher on the hill after the chapter ends. That's the chapter's whole ending.

---

## Subplot Beats

- **Tess/Brann**: He notices she checks the bag. She notices that he notices. Zero words about it. This is the beginning of their attention to each other — purely observational, nothing personal.
- **Tess/Iri**: Nothing yet beyond Tess's professional guardedness. Iri's precise interjection should be the sort of thing that is mildly exasperating if you're trying to set the register of banter, but Tess finds herself mentally noting that Iri was technically right.

## Continuity Notes

- The bag's seal is still intact; Tess checks it.
- The guild checkpoint officer records their seal number — not standard, but explainable. This is the second signal that the guild's enforcement infrastructure knows about this delivery.
- The horse at the ford is the same breed/color as the rider in ch 1 (gray). It is enough detail to connect but not so explicit as to over-explain.
- Magic is not used in this chapter.
- Tess's kit: intact, baseline.

---

## New Facts This Chapter Introduces

- Guild road checkpoints routinely record contract papers; the seal-number logging is slightly non-standard.
- The party is being passively tracked — someone is watching their road progress.
- Pip's read: "That wasn't standard." He is the party's first line of interpretation for social strangeness.
