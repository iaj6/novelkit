## Scene Breakdown

### Section A: The Note (~700 words)
- Morning. The shepherd's hut. Everyone is drier and colder and has slept badly on old straw.
- Pip shows the note to the group. It is short: *"Turn back. The advocate is watched. Last chance. —V."* The handwriting is in a Asterhall street-dialect shorthand that Pip reads easily.
- The letter "V" as a signature. Pip is quiet about who V is. He says: "I know the hand. It's — someone I knew. In the city." He doesn't say more. He's not hiding it dramatically; he seems to be deciding how much to explain, and then deciding less is correct.
- The content: "the advocate is watched." Sable Orr's office is under observation by enforcement wing assets.
- What this means: they can still deliver — but the delivery needs to avoid the obvious approach. The advocate's public-facing office may be watched; there may be a secondary way in. Pip thinks he knows one.
- Pip: "V is telling me how to get there without being seen. That's what 'last chance' means — last chance to use the back way before they lock it down."
- The decision: go forward, use Pip's route. Nobody argues. They pick up their packs.
- This is also the chapter's quiet statement of theme: they have all, at multiple points, had a last chance to stop. None of them have taken it. They are here because they decided to keep being here.

### Section B: The Approach (~800 words)
- The road broadens. Wall-vendor stalls appear on both sides — people selling food, cord, boot repair, information. The stalls are the unofficial beginning of Asterhall.
- The city smell: tanneries, coal-smoke, a river smell distinct from the road river, human density. It hits before the walls are visible. Pip breathes it in like someone coming home — and then, when Tess looks at him, it's clear he's also bracing for something.
- Iri is walking more steadily (she's been recovering since last night). She looks at the wall-stalls with the focused attention of someone who is cataloging, not browsing.
- Brann: the city makes him quieter than the road, which seems impossible given how quiet he already was. He is watching the people the way he watches the terrain.
- Tess: the walls become visible. Stone, high, old. The city gate is large and there is a queue of carts and travelers. The guild checkpoint tower above the gate has a blue-tabard officer visible at the window.
- She has been carrying the bag for eighteen days. She shifts it to the other shoulder.

### Section C: Tess and Brann — The Peak Moment (~700 words)
- Iri and Pip move ahead toward the queue. Iri has questions about the checkpoint process; Pip is explaining. They don't notice Tess and Brann are no longer keeping pace.
- The moment arises from stopped motion: Tess has paused to check the bag's strap, which has been rubbing. Brann stops because she stopped.
- They're off to the side of the road, briefly: a break in the vendor stalls, a half-minute of not-moving in a world that has been moving for three weeks.
- Brann says: "You did it."
- Tess: "Haven't delivered it yet."
- Brann: "You got it here."
- A pause. Tess is aware of how close they're standing — travel compression, not chosen proximity, but she's aware of it now in a way she wasn't for the last seventeen days.
- One of them moves. The outline allows either interpretation: this could be a kiss (brief, awkward, both of them surprised at themselves) or an explicitly avoided kiss (one of them starts to move, the other doesn't, the moment passes and both of them know exactly what it was). The drafter decides the specific execution. What is fixed: the moment is explicit — it clearly happens or clearly doesn't happen, and both of them know which it was.
- It lasts a moment. The chapter acknowledges it and moves immediately. Pip is calling them from the queue. They go.
- After the moment: nothing is said. Nothing needs to be said. The chapter doesn't dwell.

### Section D: The Gate and Checkpoint (~900 words + ending cut)
- The main gate into Asterhall. The queue moves in ten minutes; routine examination at the gate — city entry toll, party size, reason for visit.
- Pip handles the gate officer. He has practiced this: "Contracted delivery, Weavers' Guild contract, recipient named in the documents, four days early actually." He's breezy and specific and the gate officer is bored and waves them through.
- Inside the gate tunnel: the city opens. Sound doubles. Tess has a moment of sensory overload — this is the largest city she has ever seen, by a factor of approximately thirty.
- But there's a checkpoint inside the gate tunnel, separate from the gate officers: two guild enforcement officers in full blue tabards, with a ledger, conducting a secondary examination of sealed-goods couriers. This is not standard procedure. This is new.
- One officer is routine. The other one takes longer with their contract papers. He is reading carefully.
- He looks at the bag.
- He looks at Tess.
- He signals his colleague.
- He puts his hand on the bag — not grabbing, not yet. Just: his hand is on the bag. "I'll need to inspect the contents."

**ENDING (recognition cut):** His hand is on the bag. The chapter ends here — before any of the party has responded, before Pip has opened his mouth, before Tess has decided anything. The bag is still in Tess's hands. The officer's hand is on it. That's where the chapter stops.

## Character Beats

- **Tess**: The approach to Asterhall is described through her senses — city-first experience, the scale of it. The Tess/Brann moment is rendered through her physical awareness. The checkpoint is her nightmare: after eighteen days, this is where it could end.
- **Brann**: "You got it here." He is not one for speeches. This is as close to one as he gets. In the moment after, he is present. The chapter doesn't belabor what happens in the beat.
- **Iri**: Walking more strongly. Ahead of the others with Pip. She is useful in logistics mode: her precise questions about checkpoint process help Pip fine-tune his approach.
- **Pip**: The note gives him a mission — he knows the back route to Sable Orr, he knows who warned them, and he has a plan for the checkpoint. At the gate he is at his best: specific, breezy, practiced. The checkpoint officer stumps him for now.

## Subplot Beats

- **Tess/Brann (peak moment)**: Executed at the vendor-stall gap, brief, with immediate forward motion. The chapter does not return to it. In ch 12, neither of them names it. That's the ending-mode constraint: it happened, it matters, the chapter moves.
- **Pip's backstory (V)**: "Someone I knew in the city." V is named but not identified to the reader. This is Pip's loose thread surfacing — it will be addressed in ch 12 when he tells Tess something true about why he came back.
