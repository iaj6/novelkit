# Chapter 04 — The Guild Token

**One-line summary**: The rider is a merchant, not a threat; the party recovers and searches the captured attacker; finds a guild enforcement badge under his coat; Iri drops a sentence she doesn't finish, and the chapter ends before Tess can ask what she means.

## Chapter Goal

Resolve the ch 3 cliffhanger cleanly; give the party a moment to breathe and assess; deliver the new information (guild enforcement badge) that re-frames the attack; advance Tess/Brann into reluctant-ally mode; end on Iri saying something she doesn't complete, planting a question that carries forward.

## Scene Breakdown

### Section A: The Rider (~500 words)
- The rider from the east is a merchant: a woman, middle-aged, traveling alone with a loaded mule. She saw the dust cloud from the road fight and came to investigate. She is not shocked — she has seen road trouble before.
- Her name doesn't matter; her information does: she says this stretch of the road has been "bad lately." Guild's got enforcers out, she says. "Looking for something, people say. Nobody knows what."
  - Note for drafter: she delivers this as regional gossip, not as a warning. She doesn't know she's talking to people who do know what. Keep this beat brisk.
- She offers to ride with them as far as the next market cross. The party declines (Brann does this with two words; the merchant accepts it without taking offense).
- She goes. The party is alone with the aftermath.

### Section B: The Recovery (~800 words)
- Tess tends Brann's arm cut: she has a proper wound kit in her satchel (not hedge magic — practical medical supplies Mara insisted on). This is the first sustained physical proximity between them. It is not romantic; it is practical. Brann watches her work with the same expression he uses for watching road hazards.
- Tess is good at this. Brann notices. He says "Thank you" and that is all he says. Tess notes mentally that "thank you" is apparently Brann's entire bandwidth for expressing being glad someone did something useful.
- Iri is sitting against a tree, working through everything Pip has in his pack, which is apparently mostly food. She looks like she was dragged through something. Her hands are still trembling but less. She will not be useful for magic for the rest of today.
- Pip is inventorying what they recovered from the attackers: three short swords (he keeps one), a belt knife (he also keeps that), a road-guild ledger stub (a receipt, not useful), and several coins stamped with what he thinks is a standard guild mark.
- The man in the ditch: he is alive, leg wound, not talking. After a brief assessment, the party agrees on the obvious — they are not equipped to hold a prisoner, they are not going to wait here, and they cannot afford to do anything about him. They leave him with enough water to shout for help when the next traveler comes. This is the grimmer side of the situation, handled practically rather than dramatically.

### Section C: The Badge (~700 words)
- Before they leave, Tess goes through the attacker's coat — checking for identification, which feels both necessary and slightly unpleasant. Under the coat, inside a lining pocket: a small enamel badge. Guild mark, but a specific one. Not the general guild seal. A smaller, contained design.
- Pip sees it. He stops inventorying.
- "That's enforcement wing," he says. "Not road guild. Not trade guild. **Enforcement wing.**" A pause. "I've seen that badge before. In Asterhall. Not on merchants."
  - Note for drafter: Pip delivers this without drama. He's reading a fact. The fact is significant. His voice is the same as when he said "that wasn't standard" at the checkpoint.
- This lands for everyone. Brann: "So this wasn't random." Pip: "No." Tess: says nothing. She looks at the bag.
- New working hypothesis: the party is carrying something the guild enforcement wing wants back. This is not a revelation — Tess has been building toward this — but naming it is different from knowing it.
- Iri says: "Keep the badge. Don't show it at checkpoints."
- They move.

### Section D: Day 7 Travel + Ending (~700 words + ending cut)
- Travel: quieter than before. Pip's banter is lower-register — present but not continuous. He's processing.
- Tess/Brann reluctant-ally beat: they walk in something close to proximity. He adjusts his pace to hers (he's taller, longer stride) without being asked. She notices. She doesn't say anything. Neither does he. This is the beginning of them operating as a unit rather than two separate people walking in the same direction.
- Tess's kit assessment: she runs a mental inventory. Chalk: down a quarter from the ward. Herbs: intact. Salt: intact. Cord: intact. She can still do a full ward, but she's burning through her chalk faster than planned.
- That evening's camp: fire, real food for the first time in two days (they are near enough to a farm to buy bread and eggs). Brann is tending his own arm now and not making a face about it. Iri has gotten color back but is eating again. Pip is sharpening the short sword he kept, which is either competence or theater.
- The conversation that doesn't happen: they don't discuss what the badge means in terms of what to do differently. Brann said the operative thing ("this wasn't random") and the silence after it covered the rest.
- Then, without preamble, Iri says: *"I know that seal. I've seen it worn by people in Asterhall. In places I used to know."* A pause. *"I was wrong to be surprised they sent this many."*
- Everyone looks at her. She is eating bread and looking at the fire, not at any of them.
- Tess opens her mouth. Brann says "We should move at dawn," and the moment passes.

**ENDING (revelation cut):** The chapter ends with Iri's incomplete sentence hanging in the air — "places I used to know" — and Brann's practical interruption closing the door before Tess can reopen it. The reader knows Iri has said something significant without explaining it. Tess knows it too.

## Character Beats

- **Tess**: Practical under pressure (the wound-tending), increasingly strategic (the kit inventory, the badge assessment), still processing the ambush without naming the processing. She does not decide anything in this chapter; she gathers.
- **Brann (Act 2 reluctant-ally mode begins)**: The pace adjustment is small and specific. The "thank you" is two words. He is becoming reliable in Tess's mind — not trusted exactly, but dependable in a way she can count.
- **Iri**: Color coming back. Still depleted. Says the most personal thing she's said so far — not personal in the sense of emotional disclosure, but in the sense of revealing she knows more than she's said about the enforcement wing. Then shuts it down. This is Iri's character pattern: she volunteers information at unexpected moments, then closes.
- **Pip (Act 1 competence moment — badge ID)**: He knows the enforcement wing badge on sight. He's fourteen and he's been to Asterhall and he knows what that badge is. This is specific knowledge from his background that the others don't have and can't fake. His delivery is low-key; the significance is high.

---

## Subplot Beats

- **Tess/Brann**: Wound-tending, pace adjustment. Physical proximity without personal content. Reluctant-ally mode beginning — they are now two people who are starting to coordinate rather than simply coexist.
- **Tess/Iri**: Iri's revelation is the first crack in the distance between them. Tess doesn't know what to do with "places I used to know" but she files it. The intimidation is still there, but now it has a companion feeling: curiosity.

## Continuity Notes

- Brann's arm wound: dressed, not infected, will be present but not discussed again unless relevant.

- Magic: not used in this chapter. Both Tess and Iri are conserving.

## New Facts This Chapter Introduces

- The attackers carried guild enforcement wing badges — they were not bandits, they were enforcement assets.
- Pip knows the enforcement wing badge on sight (from his time in Asterhall).
- Iri has some prior acquaintance with the enforcement wing, from "places she used to know." She doesn't explain.
- The party now works from a shared assumption: the bag is specifically targeted, and the guild enforcement wing is responsible.
