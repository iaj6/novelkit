## Scene Breakdown

### Scene A: The Checkpoint — Pip's Move (~600 words)
- The officer's hand is on the bag. Tess has not moved.
- Pip steps forward. He produces a document from his inner pocket — not the party's contract papers, something else. He puts it in front of the officer.
- It is a letter. It has Sable Orr's office seal on it. It says, in formal advocate-court language, that the named courier party (their names) is expected at the Advocate's Court for a scheduled delivery at the time of the city's afternoon bells, and that interference with this delivery constitutes obstruction of a council-ordered proceeding.
- This letter is real. Sable Orr's reformist faction contacts anticipated this moment and prepared it. Pip has been carrying it since before they left Drey — given to him by someone connected to Mara's contacts. He did not mention this until now because he was waiting for the moment that needed it.
  
  **(Pip Act 3 competence moment)**: He had the document. He was waiting to use it at the right moment. This is Pip: the right resource, deployed at the right second.

- The officer reads the letter. His colleague reads it over his shoulder. They confer. The letter is legal. They cannot detain under its terms without committing a documentable offense against the council.
- They let the party through. The officer's hand comes off the bag.
- They walk into Asterhall.

### Scene B: The Low Streets (~600 words)
- Pip navigates. The Low Streets: narrow, loud, a market-within-a-market, people who know which direction is which without signs. Pip moves through it at a pace that makes it clear he has done this thousands of times.
- Tess sticks close. Iri follows Pip's exact path. Brann takes the wide position — he's watching their rear, which is unnecessary but habitual.
- Pip takes them through three alleys and two covered markets. At one point he stops at a stall (food, clearly; he buys four things without stopping to read the menu), hands them out, and keeps moving. They eat while walking.
- One moment: Pip glances at an alley junction and doesn't take it. He keeps going. This is the junction where V's route connects — Tess doesn't know this, but Pip knows someone is watching from the alley and he wants them to see the party pass without coming to the meeting, because the timing isn't right. This is Pip managing a social landscape Tess can't see.
- They emerge at the back of the Advocate's Court building — a stone building, civic-feeling, the back entrance less guarded than the front. The city-council seal above the door.
- Pip: "In there."

### Scene C: The Delivery (~600 words)
- The back entrance. A guard, an advocate's clerk. Pip gives their names; the clerk has them on a list. They're expected.
- Sable Orr's private office: smaller than Tess expected. Shelves of document cases, a desk that is entirely covered in organized paper. A window that faces an interior courtyard.
- Sable Orr. Mid-forties. Short hair, practical clothes, the air of someone who has been working this problem longer than the party has been alive. She looks at the four of them without particular surprise — she was told four, four appeared, this is what it is.
- She examines the bag's exterior seal before taking it. She checks the seal number against a document she has — a matching record from the reformist faction that arranged the delivery. It matches.
- She opens the bag.

### Scene D: The Discovery (~1,400 words)
- Seven scrolls, each sealed with a different personal seal. Sable opens them one by one and stacks them on the desk.
- She reads them, quickly — she is very fast at reading. The party waits.
- Tess watches Sable's face while she reads. She can't read upside-down at this distance, but she can read the advocate's expression: controlled, professional, with something underneath it that is either relief or grief or both.
- Sable sets down the last scroll. She looks at the party.
- "These are sworn depositions," she says. "Seven practitioners documenting extortion by the Weavers' Guild enforcement wing. Dates, amounts, incidents. Three years of material." A pause. "This is the case I've been building toward for four years."
- Tess: "Who are the deponents?"
- Sable reads the names. Seven hedge-witches, regional practitioners. She reads them in order.
- The sixth name is Mara Weft, of Drey.
- Tess hears her own master's name in a sentence she didn't know was coming. She has no response ready because there was no version of this she had prepared for. Her hands have gone cold. This is how she knows she's in shock: her hands went cold.
  
**The Mara revelation**: Tess knew the contract covered Mara's debts. She knew Mara arranged the delivery. She did not know Mara was one of seven practitioners who had sworn before an advocate-recorder that the enforcement wing had extorted her. She did not know that "deliver it whole" meant deliver her master's testimony whole. She did not know she had been carrying this.

- Iri, quietly: *"Your master is brave."* Four words. It is the most personal thing Iri has said to anyone on this road.

**The Re-Casting**:
- Tess thinks about the attacker shouting "Get the bag!" — not "Get the money." They knew what they were looking for.
- She thinks about Vel saying "You're carrying someone's testimony." She meant it literally. She knew.
- She thinks about the innkeeper Rossa logging their names in a ledger with an enforcement-wing seal on the cover.
- All of it was targeted. From the beginning. The enforcement wing knew about the depositions because one of the seven deponents' communications was intercepted, or there's an informer in the reformist faction, or both. They sent the ambushers first, then the rival party. They pre-positioned Rossa in Long Crossing. They damaged the bridge. They put the checkpoint inside the Asterhall gate.
- And none of it worked.
- Tess says: "Mara didn't tell me what was inside."
- Sable: "No. She wouldn't. Operational security. And because knowing could have changed your behavior in ways that made the delivery less likely to succeed." A pause. "She said you were the right person to send. She was correct."
- Sable explains what happens next: she brings the depositions to the full council in formal session. The council has jurisdiction over guild enforcement operations within city limits. She will file the case. It will take months. There may be charges. The enforcement wing will know the depositions exist.
- And then: "You are the courier of record. Your name is on the contract. The council will want to know what happened on the road — the ambush, the rival party, the bridge. The enforcement wing will also want to know your name, if they don't already."
- Sable lets that sit.
- "You can leave the city tonight," she says. "I'll tell the council the couriers completed their contract and returned home. Your names won't appear in the formal record. No one will pursue you — you're four teenagers with nothing the enforcement wing wants now that the depositions are here."
- A pause.
- "Or you can give your own testimony. Add your account of what happened on the road to the evidentiary record. It strengthens the case — significantly. A courier who was ambushed is a witness to the enforcement wing's methods."
- She looks at Tess. "That's your choice to make. I'll need to know by tomorrow morning."

**ENDING (revelation cut):** The chapter ends here. Sable has laid out the choice. She hasn't pushed it. Tess has not said anything in response. The chapter cuts — before Tess responds, before the party has a chance to react, before any of them says a single word about what they just heard.

## Character Beats

- **Tess**: Two stages. First: the Mara revelation (her hands go cold; she is in shock without knowing she's in shock). Second: the re-casting (she goes through every event of the road and watches it reorganize into something targeted and deliberate). She does not speak much in this scene; she is processing. Her silence is not absence — it is the presence of a person who has taken on too much information too fast.
- **Iri**: "Your master is brave." This is the thread's resolution — quiet, specific, personal. Iri respects hedge work, and the deposit Mara made to this process is the concrete expression of that. Iri does not explain or expand the statement. It doesn't need to be expanded.
- **Pip (Act 3 competence moment)**: The letter from Sable Orr's office, produced at the checkpoint. He had it for eighteen days and said nothing. This is Pip's city-knowledge being load-bearing in exactly the way the brief specified.
- **Brann**: He is standing near the door. He is watching Tess's face while she processes the Mara revelation. He doesn't say anything. He is there.
- **Sable Orr**: Professional, precise, not cold. She explains consequences; she doesn't demand actions. She respects that Tess is fifteen and has been carrying something she didn't know the full weight of.
