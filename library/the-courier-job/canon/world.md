# World

## Overview

A working medieval fantasy world rendered specifically, not generically. The scope for this book is deliberately tight: one village, one road, one city. The world is pre-gunpowder, pre-printing-press, with wagons, taverns, road-toll booths, guild licenses, and market towns. The register is contemporary-coded YA — characters sound like teenagers, not like medieval peasants — but the world is built from specific sensory detail, not from genre shorthand.

---

## The Village of Drey

Population roughly two hundred. A farming village three weeks of wagon-road east of Asterhall. Keeps two dozen cattle, a blacksmith's forge, a mill, an inn with three rooms, and one hedge-witch. The hedge-witch (currently Mara Weft) maintains the well charms and cattle wards: practical magic that makes the difference between a hard winter and a catastrophic one.

Drey smells like woodsmoke, fresh manure, and the salted herbs Mara hangs to dry in her window. The main road through town is unpaved and rutted in spring. Tess has lived here all fifteen years of her life. The blacksmith's forge is at the east end of the road. Mara's cottage is at the west end, near the well she tends.

## The Road: Drey to Asterhall

A three-week journey on foot or by wagon. County toll-booths at road junctions charge a small crossing fee. The road passes through:

**The Wagon Road East** — The first week: broad and well-traveled, farmers and merchants moving in both directions, the road following the river valley. Cold at night; the party camps or pays for barn space.

**Long Crossing** — A market town roughly at the midpoint of the journey. Large enough to have a proper inn (the Crossed Keys or similar), a weekly market, a road guild office, and a guild toll-booth. One of Tess's named encounters happens here.

**The Pavers' Country** — A stretch of ancient stone-paved road through rougher terrain, roughly the third week. The paving is Oldwork — no one built it in living memory. Jurisdictionally ambiguous: the Weavers' Guild claims taxing rights, but the crown's charter for this section is contested. Bandits and deserters operate here. The terrain is hilly, forested, with poor sight lines. This is where the ambush happens.

**The Outskirts of Asterhall** — The approach to the city: the road widens, wall-vendor stalls appear, guild checkpoint towers. The smell of the city (tanneries, cook-smoke, animal and human density) hits before the walls are visible.

## Asterhall

Population approximately 7,000. Seat of the Weavers' Guild. A working city, not a gleaming fantasy capital: dense streets, tenement blocks, a river-harbor for trade barges, a market district that runs every day except holy days, guild halls in the center, advocate courts near the civic hall. The guild's presence is ambient — guild seals above doorways, guild-licensed traders on every corner, guild enforcement officers in blue-tabard livery.

Notable districts:
- **The Market Quarter** — busy, loud, where Pip knows how to move
- **Guild Hall Row** — formal, guarded, expensive buildings the party has no reason to enter
- **The Advocate's Court** — where the city council hears petitions; where Sable Orr's office is
- **The Low Streets** — where Pip knows people; where the party can find lodging that doesn't ask questions

## Magic in This World

Magic exists on a practical spectrum. It is not cosmic or apocalyptic. It is a skilled trade with apprenticeship paths, materials costs, and physical limits.

**Hedge magic** (what Tess practices): small-scale, preparation-dependent, material-intensive. A well charm requires chalk patterns, salt, specific dried herbs, and three to four hours of work. Cattle wards have to be renewed at the solstices. Hedge magic is reliable if the practitioner has the right materials and time; it fails if the materials are wrong, missing, or disrupted. Tess cannot improvise hedge magic without her kit. Her kit lives in a leather satchel she carries constantly.

**Guild magic** (what Iri practices): more immediate, less material-dependent, more powerful in effect. Iri can move things at distance, create barriers, project force, affect perception. The cost is physical and visible: Iri comes out of a working pale and shaking, needing food and sleep. Her hands tremble afterward. She cannot run high-cost workings back to back. The cost scales with the working.

Guild magic is officially regulated by the Weavers' Guild. Unlicensed practitioners are subject to enforcement. Iri's status with the guild is deliberately left uncertain in the narrative — she has the training but not the current license.

---

## The Weavers' Guild

The primary commercial and magical guild in this region. Based in Asterhall. Officially charters magical practitioners, licenses traders, sets road-toll rates, and provides enforcement. In practice, its enforcement wing has been running a side operation: extorting unlicensed magical practitioners (primarily hedge-witches in rural areas) for "protection fees." This is the corruption the sealed bag documents.

The guild is not monolithic. There is a reformist faction — the people who arranged the delivery contract — and the enforcement faction that is trying to stop the delivery. This internal conflict is the background of the plot.

## Sensory Anchors

- **Drey**: woodsmoke, dried herbs, cow dung, cold well-water, the sound of the forge at dusk
- **The wagon road**: dust in dry weather, mud in wet, the creak of cart axles, toll-booth officers with their ledgers
- **Long Crossing**: crowded inn common room, meat pie, the road-guild seal above the door, unfamiliar accents
- **The Pavers' Country**: cold at night, the eerie solidity of the old stone road underfoot, forest close on both sides
- **Asterhall**: tannery stink, cook-smoke, bodies, noise, guild tabards in blue, the civic hall bells every quarter-hour
