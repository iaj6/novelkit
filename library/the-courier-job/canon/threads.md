# Threads — *The Sealed Bag*

Five threads braid this story. Three resolve (one cleanly, two with deliberate residue). Two are explicitly refused. Every chapter advances at least two threads; no chapter is dead.

---

## Thread 1: The Slow Burn (Tess and Brann)

- **Question**: Will Tess and Brann acknowledge what is growing between them, or will they carry it across three weeks of road without ever finding the words?
- **Enters**: `01-departure` — Brann arrives in the square before dawn, says four words, and sets his pack down to wait. He is a fact about this road, not yet a person to Tess.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `02-the-wagon-road` — He notices Tess checking the bag's seal; she notices him noticing. Zero words exchanged.
  - `03-the-ambush` — She watches him hold two attackers to a stalemate with methodical efficiency; the watching is new.
  - `04-the-guild-token` — She tends his arm wound; first sustained physical proximity; he adjusts his walking pace to hers without being asked.
  - `05-the-innkeeper-at-long-crossing` — Pre-dawn coordination in a dark room; four words total; they now operate as a unit.
  - `06-the-rival-party` — She notices the new bruise on his cheek; neither of them names the noticing.
  - `07-the-pavers-country` — He gives her his coat without comment; wraps himself in his blanket instead.
  - `08-two-days-out` — He says "Real" about her ward-working — the first unnecessary thing, the first thing he said that didn't need to be said.
  - `09-the-crossing` — He goes into the river after her; goes back in a second time to rig the rope for Pip.
  - `10-the-walls` — The moment at the vendor-stall gap before the city gate: a kiss or an explicitly avoided kiss. Brief. Both of them know which it was. The chapter moves immediately.
  - `11-the-advocates-court` — He stands near the door, watching her face while she hears Mara's name.
  - `12-the-decision` — His last line to her: *"Whatever you decide, I'll be there when you do it."* Five words doing the work of everything he hasn't said.
- **Resolves in**: Deliberately unresolved. The moment at the gate happened. Neither of them names it. Brann's five words cover the romantic thread without closing it. The sequel inherits this.
- **What it's about underneath**: Two people who didn't choose each other and couldn't leave discovering they trust each other with their lives before they trust each other with words. The romance stays in subplot because the book is about the job — but this thread is about how much you can mean to someone when there is still no language for it. Brann says important things when he says them; the thread is the accumulation of those things, spoken sideways, at the road.

## Thread 2: What Counts as Magic (Tess and Iri)

- **Question**: Will Tess stop experiencing Iri's guild-level power as a verdict on her own worth, and will Iri ever acknowledge that hedge work is real?
- **Enters**: `01-departure` — Iri arrives with a pack too small for three weeks and looks at the bag's seal before looking away. Tess hides her intimidation behind professional cool, a fact she is aware of and dislikes.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `03-the-ambush` — Tess sees for the first time what Iri's power costs: pallor, shaking, needing Pip's shoulder. The intimidation acquires a new component — she didn't know it looked like that.
  - `04-the-guild-token` — Iri says "places I used to know" about the enforcement wing; the first crack in the distance between them.
  - `06-the-rival-party` — Iri uses major magic a second time in a week, goes down before it's finished; Tess watches her accept Pip's help without argument and understands Iri is running on less than she shows.
  - `07-the-pavers-country` — Iri corrects one chalk line in Tess's anchor pattern — a single fingertip, without asking — and walks back to the fire. She then tells Tess that hedge patterns and guild patterns use the same underlying geometry; different names, same structure. The thread's turning point.
  - `09-the-crossing` — Iri uses magic she doesn't have to clear Brann's approach line to Tess. Nobody asked her to.
  - `11-the-advocates-court` — *"Your master is brave."* Four words. The most personal thing Iri has said to anyone on this road. Thread resolves here.
  - `12-the-decision` — From what appears to be sleep: *"What you are doesn't require their recognition to be real. But recognition is not nothing. They are different things."* The thread's closing statement from Iri's side — spoken by someone who has lost her own recognition and is not pretending it doesn't matter.
- **Resolves in**: `11-the-advocates-court` / `12-the-decision` — confirmed as alliance, and the shape of the friendship is legible by the final page.
- **What it's about underneath**: The Weavers' Guild decides what "real" magic is — and the Weavers' Guild is running an extortion operation. Tess's hedge work keeps a village's wells clean through winter. Iri's guild magic is more powerful and has a license she no longer holds. The thread asks whether the institution that confers legitimacy has any right to define it, and whether two practitioners from different tiers can recognize each other without waiting for official permission. By the end, both of them know the answer; neither says it out loud.

## Thread 3: What Mara Knew (The Weight of Trust)

- **Question**: Did Mara betray Tess by sending her into danger without telling her what the bag contained — or was keeping her ignorant the only way to trust her with it properly?
- **Enters**: `01-departure` — Mara takes Tess's hand over the bag (she does not do this habitually) and says: *"Deliver it whole. Whatever happens on that road, deliver it whole."* She does not explain. Tess files it under "things Mara is right about without explaining why."
- **Advanced in**:
  - `03-the-ambush` — The attackers shout "Get the bag!" — not "Get the money." The bag is specifically wanted. The gap between what Mara said and what Mara must have known begins to widen.
  - `06-the-rival-party` — Vel says "You're carrying someone's **testimony**." The word is specific. The bag's contents become legible in outline; Tess doesn't connect it to Mara yet.
  - `07-the-pavers-country` — Iri says: *"I've seen the kind of case this builds."* She doesn't name the document type. The thread continues to cook.
  - `11-the-advocates-court` — Sable Orr reads the sixth name: Mara Weft, of Drey. *"Deliver it whole"* meant: deliver my testimony whole. Every attack on the road was aimed at recovering Mara's deposition along with the others. Tess's hands go cold. Thread explodes.
  - `12-the-decision` — Tess writes a letter to Mara. She does not send it. It goes into her empty kit case — the case that used to hold chalk. She is not sure what she thinks, and the book ends before she is.
- **Resolves in**: `11-the-advocates-court` factually — what Mara knew is confirmed. `12-the-decision` emotionally — Tess can hold the question but cannot yet answer it. The thread closes on the unsent letter: present, real, not resolved.
- **What it's about underneath**: The deepest internal thread. Mara trusted Tess enough to send her with her own sworn deposition in the bag, into a road she knew the enforcement wing would patrol. She sent her knowing. She did not tell her because knowing would have changed how Tess carried it. Whether that was protection or condescension — whether "you are the right person to send" and "I won't tell you the full weight" can coexist as an act of love — is the question Tess cannot answer. The thread pressurizes everything the book says about adults, institutions, and what it means to be fifteen in a world that will use you without explaining itself.

## Thread 4: Pip's Hidden Asterhall

- **Question**: What is Pip hiding about why he left Asterhall, and will the city swallow him back up when they return?
- **Enters**: `01-departure` — Pip arrived in Drey two summers ago and has never explained why. He is going back.
- **Advanced in**:
  - `04-the-guild-token` — He identifies the guild enforcement wing badge on sight, from his time in Asterhall. He's fourteen and he knows what that badge means.
  - `05-the-innkeeper-at-long-crossing` — He reads the Crossed Keys as wrong within five minutes of arrival: city-honed pattern-recognition applied to a market town.
  - `08-two-days-out` — He steers the party away from a shortcut junction without explaining: *"I know people at that checkpoint. They might not be glad to see me."* His unguarded face at day's end — the first time he stops performing — and his explicit warning: *"If I say run, you run."*
  - `09-the-crossing` — A folded note appears in his coat pocket at the ford; it was placed there during the crossing chaos by someone who knew he'd be there and chose to communicate with him specifically.
  - `10-the-walls` — V's note: the advocate is watched, here is the back route. *"Someone I knew in the city."* Pip navigates the Low Streets at full competence.
  - `11-the-advocates-court` — He produces Sable Orr's pre-prepared letter at the checkpoint — a document he has been carrying for eighteen days without mentioning it. He talks them through the gate. He navigates to Sable's office. This is Pip operating at full load.
  - `12-the-decision` — He tells Tess the true thing: he came back because he owed someone. V is alive. It turned out okay. He didn't die. He cannot give her more than that, but that is the most honest he has been with anyone on the entire road.
- **Resolves in**: Deliberately unresolved. The obligation to V is discharged; Pip did not die returning. Why he originally left Asterhall is not disclosed in this book. The thread is acknowledged and refused — it waits for a sequel.
- **What it's about underneath**: Pip's jokes are armor. The thread is about what lives behind the armor: a fourteen-year-old who is the sharpest reader of rooms in the party, who has been running from something specific, who agreed to come back to the city he left and spent three weeks not letting anyone see that he was scared. His partial resolution — V was okay, the thing resolved without disaster — is the face of the question, not its answer. The backstory is the refusal: the book acknowledges it exists and does not open it.

## Thread 5: The Reach of the Enforcement Network

- **Question**: How many people did the Weavers' Guild enforcement wing position against this delivery, and when does the targeting become undeniable?
- **Enters**: `01-departure` — A guild rider at Drey's edge glances at the bag before looking away. Pip identifies him: *"That man works for the Guild."*
- **Advanced in**:
  - `02-the-wagon-road` — A checkpoint officer records the contract's seal number in a ledger; it is not standard. A figure on a hill watches the party and walks away when they look back.
  - `03-the-ambush` — Five coordinated attackers: pre-positioned, disciplined, not bandits. The shout is *"Get the bag!"* — not the money, not the girl. The bag.
  - `04-the-guild-token` — An enforcement wing badge recovered from the attacker's inner pocket. Pip identifies it. Not road guild. Not trade guild. Enforcement wing.
  - `05-the-innkeeper-at-long-crossing` — Rossa at the Crossed Keys has an enforcement-wing seal inlaid in her lodger-ledger. A rider is sent east before dawn with the party's names and route.
  - `06-the-rival-party` — A professional four-person party with pre-knowledge of the bag's contents and a specific buyout offer; watchers on the ridge above. Vel uses the word *"testimony"* — she knows exactly what the bag contains.
  - `09-the-crossing` — The road bridge is out (cut supports, not rot). Someone at the ford slips a note into Pip's pocket — the network knew their exact route and their arrival window.
  - `10-the-walls` — A secondary checkpoint inside Asterhall's main gate, specifically targeting sealed-goods couriers — not standard procedure when the party left Drey.
  - `11-the-advocates-court` — The re-casting: Tess works backward through every encounter. None of it was opportunistic. All of it was the same operation, layered: the rider, the checkpoint officer, Rossa, the ambush party, the rival party, the bridge, the ford watcher, the inner checkpoint. A sustained, pre-planned, multi-asset operation to stop seven depositions from reaching a council chamber.
- **Resolves in**: `11-the-advocates-court` — the network's full scope becomes legible; every planted detail pays off. The thread resolves with the re-casting.
- **What it's about underneath**: The enforcement wing's operation is quiet. It does not announce itself. It is a rider here, a checkpoint ledger entry there, an inn asset at the midpoint of the road. By ch 11, when Tess sees the full shape, the revelation is not a surprise — the reader has been watching it build — but it is still devastating, because it makes visible how much institutional infrastructure was mobilized to silence a handful of rural practitioners who wrote down what happened to them. This thread carries the book's "small people and big systems" theme: the depositions are seven pages of testimony from seven hedge-witches, and the guild sent riders and fighters and a professional party and a network of inn assets to stop them.
