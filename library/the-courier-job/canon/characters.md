# Characters

---

## Tess — POV Protagonist

**Age**: 15  
**Role**: Courier of record; hedge-witch apprentice  
**Appears in**: Every chapter (sole POV)

**Background**: Born and raised in Drey. Three months into her apprenticeship with Mara Weft — long enough to know what she's doing wrong but not yet long enough to do it reliably right. Has some genuine magical aptitude (enough that Mara took her on) but it is hedge-caliber: she can lay a ward, maintain a charm, diagnose a spell that's slipping. She cannot do what Iri does, and she knows it, and she doesn't like how much that bothers her. Literate because Mara's library is her primary education; she reads fast and has opinions about everything she's read.

**Inner drives**: Tess wants to be taken seriously as a practitioner. She has spent three months doing maintenance magic for cattle and wells and she knows she's capable of more. She wants the job to be hers — not because someone assigned it to her, but because she finished it. Her secondary drive is simpler: she's fifteen and this is the first thing that has ever happened to her, and she is paying attention.

**Voice**: Observational, dry, moves fast. Makes defensive jokes when uncertain. Processes external situations through rapid inference — she's quick to spot inconsistencies, slower to admit she's scared. Not naive, not particularly courageous, just stubborn when she's decided something.

**Relationships**:
- *Mara*: Respect, mild awe, some affection she wouldn't name. Mara doesn't coddle her; Tess finds this correct.
- *Brann*: Starts as cautious and slightly wary — he's older, larger, harder to read. Ends as something that functions like trust.
- *Iri*: Starts as intimidation Tess tries to hide by being professionally cool. Ends as alliance, maybe the beginning of friendship.
- *Pip*: Likes him immediately. Doesn't trust him immediately. Both of these change at different rates.

**Magic**: Hedge magic. Requires her kit (chalk, salt, herbs, cord). Preparation time measured in minutes to hours. Costs materials. If her kit is damaged or wet, she is limited. Her strongest working in the book should take a full scene to prepare and visibly exhaust her.

## Brann

**Age**: 17  
**Role**: The one who carries; the party's physical protection  
**Background**: The blacksmith's second son from Drey. His older brother will inherit the forge; Brann learned the trade anyway, for lack of anything else to learn. Took this job because his older sister accrued a significant debt and the family needs the contract fee to cover it. He did not volunteer. He did not object.

**Inner drives**: Primary: fulfill the obligation, get home, don't make things worse than they are. Secondary, quieter: he's not incurious — he notices things the others miss — but he talks slowly about them, or not at all. He's been steady in the way that people who've had to be steady are.

**Voice**: Few words. Dry. Occasionally funny in a completely deadpan way — the joke delivered without inflection, the humor visible only after a beat. He doesn't explain himself. When he says something important, it's because he's been sitting on it.

**Physical**: Large. Useful for carrying things and for being the thing bandits look at first.

**Romance thread with Tess**: Slow burn. Arc across 12 chapters: stranger → reluctant ally → noticeable ease with each other → one explicit near-moment (kiss or clearly avoided kiss) in ch 10 or 11. The thread stays in subplot. It is never the scene's primary content. Brann doesn't perform the romance; it comes out sideways, through small consistent actions.

**Relationships**: Gets along with Pip better than you'd expect (Pip talks; Brann listens; this turns out to work). Has a careful, undefined relationship with Iri — he neither underestimates nor overestimates her. Mara trusted him for this, which he treats as a serious thing.

## Iri

**Age**: 16  
**Role**: The one who can do real magic  
**Background**: Half-elven — her heritage is visible (the ears, a slight otherworldliness to how she holds herself, the occasional too-precise syntax) but she passes as human at a distance. Three years a stranger in Drey. She came alone, pays rent in advance, does not explain herself. Before Drey, she had guild training — how much, and what happened to make her leave, is not resolved in this book. She carries a persistent unresolved obligation she has not named.

**Inner drives**: To be competent without being known. She has power and skill she does not want to call attention to. She is not hiding from the guild specifically (or she is, but the book doesn't confirm this); she is hiding from being made a story. Under that: she is lonely in a way that fifteen-year-old apprentices don't notice until they do.

**Voice**: Precise. Economical. Says exactly what she means and nothing else, in the most accurate words available. Occasionally startling because she volunteers information nobody asked for, at unexpected moments, in a flat tone. She is accidentally funny sometimes because she is too literal for the situation.

**Magic**: Guild-level. More immediate and powerful than hedge magic. Visible cost: pallor, shaking hands, physical depletion, needs to eat and sleep after a significant working. She cannot run major workings back to back. She does not explain what she's doing before she does it.

**Thread with Tess**: Tess starts intimidated. The thread traces two realizations: (1) Tess comes to see that Iri's power costs Iri something real and continuous; (2) Iri quietly respects hedge work in a way she doesn't say until near the end. By chapter 11-12, they have something like an alliance or the start of genuine friendship.

## Pip

**Age**: 14  
**Role**: The one who knows Asterhall; the party's street intelligence  
**Background**: Street kid, originally from Asterhall. Showed up in Drey two summers ago, didn't explain why, didn't leave. He has urban survival skills — city navigation, reading people, knowing which questions to ask and who to ask them to — that are useless in a farming village and invaluable in Asterhall. He is actually the youngest member of the party. This matters to how people underestimate him.

**Inner drives**: He is going back to a city he left for reasons he hasn't named. Under the jokes, he is scared. He doesn't let the others see it. His primary mechanism is forward motion and humor; if he stops moving, the fear catches up. Secondary: he likes these people more than he expected to, and he doesn't know what to do with that.

**Voice**: Fast, jokey, constant. His banter is not deflection from incompetence — he is genuinely good at what he does, and the humor is how he builds connection. He is the party's reader of people and rooms. He spots the lie in someone's face before Tess has processed the sentence. When Pip isn't joking, something is actually wrong.

**Comic engine**: Pip's banter is the book's primary source of comedy. His jokes are specific — about the situation, about each party member, about the road. They sometimes land in character-revealing ways that stop being jokes. He is never the butt of the joke (he's the sharpest person in most rooms he walks into); the humor comes from his read of everything around him.

**Role in the plot**: Saves the party in ways the others can't in at least one key scene. His city knowledge is load-bearing in Asterhall. His backstory (why he left) is a loose thread the book acknowledges and does not resolve.

## Mara Weft

**Age**: 58  
**Role**: Tess's master; hedge-witch of Drey  
**Appears in**: Opening chapter (departure) and possibly via letter or flashback

**Background**: Has been Drey's hedge-witch for thirty years. Broke — the Weavers' Guild's enforcement wing has been collecting protection fees from unlicensed practitioners in the region, and Mara has been paying to keep working. The contract fee covers her debts. She arranged this contract through a guild contact; she is one of seven hedge-witches whose sworn depositions are inside the sealed bag. She knows this. She did not tell Tess.

**Voice**: Practical, unsentimental. Fond of Tess in ways she doesn't say directly and wouldn't say directly if asked. She sends Tess because Tess is the right person for this, not because she's expendable.

## Sable Orr

**Age**: Mid-forties  
**Role**: The named recipient; advocate-recorder for the Asterhall city council  
**Appears in**: Chapters 11-12

**Background**: Has been quietly building a case against the Weavers' Guild's enforcement wing for several years. The sealed bag is the evidence she needs to bring to the full council. She arranged for the delivery through the guild's reformist faction; she expected a professional courier, not four teenagers. She is not unkind about this. She is professional and careful, and she understands exactly what the depositions mean and what they cost the people who gave them.

**Voice**: Precise, professional, not cold. She explains consequences rather than demands actions. She tells Tess what she's holding and lets Tess sit with it.
