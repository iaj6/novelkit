# Plotter Guidance — *The Sealed Bag* (working title)

---

## POV Count and Rotation

**One POV only: Tess.** All 12 chapters are in her perspective with no deviation. There is no rotation. The plotter must not design scenes whose primary content requires being inside another character's head. If a scene requires knowledge Tess doesn't have, either she's present and observes it, or it happens off-page and is relayed to her later.

The party has four members, but the outline must be built entirely around what Tess can see, hear, and infer. When the plotter assigns chapter content, check: *Is Tess present for this event? Can the reader experience it through her limited perspective?*

---

## Chapter-Shape Preferences

This is an **episodic road-quest**: each chapter is one named encounter or milestone. The brief specifies chapter titles should announce the encounter ("Departure," "The Wagon Road," "The Innkeeper at Long Crossing," "The Ambush," etc.).

Chapter shapes by type (favor these):
- **Single-scene chapter**: one continuous event, start to (interrupted) finish. Most chapters should be this. The chapter's identity is the event.
- **Travel-plus-encounter**: a brief transit section (rendered in fast summary or banter-forward prose) leading to the chapter's named encounter. The encounter is 80%+ of the chapter's real estate.

Chapter shapes to use sparingly:
- **Intercut chapters** (two simultaneous events cutting back and forth): at most 1–2 in the full manuscript; only when the intercut creates genuine tension, not complexity for its own sake.
- **Interiority-heavy chapters**: chapters where the primary content is Tess's inner processing of a previous event. This register does not favor them. If Tess needs to process something, she does it while something else is happening.

---

## Episode-vs-Arc Structure

Primarily **episodic** — each chapter should feel self-contained as an encounter — but with a continuous spine:
- The sealed bag is always present and its significance grows
- The party relationship arc develops across all 12 chapters (incremental trust-building)
- The Tess/Brann subplot runs as a sustained background thread, peaking in ch 10 or 11
- The Tess/Iri thread resolves into alliance by ch 11–12

The episodic structure means each chapter should have a clear stake that is introduced and (partially) resolved within the chapter. But the chapter ending is always an interruption — the resolution is cut off or complicated before the reader gets it cleanly.

---

## Ending-Mode Distribution

**All 12 chapters end on cliffhanger or mid-action beats. No exceptions.**

Within that constraint, vary the type of cliffhanger. Suggested distribution (not prescribed sequence):

| Chapters | Ending type |
|----------|-------------|
| 5–6 | Threat arrives or recognition cut: chapter ends at the moment danger becomes undeniable, before any response |
| 3–4 | Revelation cut: information lands and the chapter ends before the response or decision |
| 2–3 | Mid-action cut: chapter stops at peak physical tension and continues in the next chapter |

Do not cluster the same type in consecutive chapters. Alternate recognition cuts, revelation cuts, and mid-action cuts to keep the reader off balance.

---

## Chapter Target Words

Target: **~42,000 words over 12 chapters = ~3,500 words per chapter average.**

Acceptable variance:
- **Action set-piece chapters (3, 6, 9)**: may run 3,800–4,200 words (the action earns the length)
- **Transition chapters**: may run 2,800–3,200 words (keep them lean; no filler)
- **Climactic chapters (10, 11, 12)**: may run 3,500–4,000 words

Do not let any chapter run below 2,500 or above 4,500. Very short chapters feel skipped; very long chapters stall the pace.

## Genre-Specific Structural Conventions

### Road-quest structure
The 12-chapter outline must follow the journey's shape:
- **Ch 1**: Departure from Drey. Establish Tess, Mara, the contract, the party. End before the party has gotten far.
- **Ch 2**: On the road. Establish travel rhythm, banter, party dynamics. First sign something is wrong.
- **Ch 3**: The Ambush. Physical action set piece. Guild enforcers operating as bandits. The party survives; the danger is now concrete.
- **Ch 4**: Aftermath and continued road. Party recalibrates. New information about what just happened.
- **Ch 5**: Named encounter at or near Long Crossing. Character-driven complication (the innkeeper, a guild checkpoint, a contact of Pip's or Iri's).
- **Ch 6**: The rival party confrontation. Second action set piece. The party learns they're being specifically targeted, not randomly robbed.
- **Ch 7**: Deeper into the Pavers' Country. The world gets stranger. Tess's magic is needed; costs her something.
- **Ch 8**: The road narrows. A quieter chapter but not a slow one — mounting pressure, Tess/Brann thread advances.
- **Ch 9**: Near-disaster set piece. Third action beat. Physical emergency (crossing, accident, element). Not combat this time.
- **Ch 10**: Approaching Asterhall. The Tess/Brann subplot peaks here or in ch 11. The party knows something is wrong but not what.
- **Ch 11**: Asterhall. The delivery. Meeting Sable Orr. Discovery of the bag's contents. The re-casting of everything that happened on the road.
- **Ch 12**: The decision. Tess decides what to do with what she knows. Open ending. Chapter ends on the weight of the decision, not its resolution.

### Romance subplot convention (Tess/Brann)
- Ch 1–3: Stranger mode. They barely speak to each other about anything personal.
- Ch 4–7: Reluctant-ally mode. They operate in coordination. Small moments. No declaration.
- Ch 8–10: Noticing mode. Something shifts. Tess notices things she wasn't noticing before. Brann says one unnecessary thing.
- Ch 10 or 11: The peak moment. One kiss or one explicitly avoided kiss. The chapter does not dwell on it. It happens, it matters, and the chapter moves on (or ends on the beat).
- Ch 12: Not resolved. They don't talk about it. The next-book hook includes it.

### Party dynamics convention
- The party must demonstrate **one competence moment per character per act** (roughly: ch 1–4, 5–8, 9–12). Each of Tess, Brann, Iri, and Pip gets a scene where they're the one who solves the specific problem. Do not let any character coast for an act without contributing.
- Pip's city knowledge must be load-bearing in Asterhall (ch 11 or 12), not just decorative.

### Bag mechanics
The outline must ensure:
- The bag is **present and unexamined** in every chapter until ch 11. It is physically with Tess (she carries it). Its weight is a physical fact the outline can use.
- The ambush in ch 3 and the rival party in ch 6 must be motivated by the bag's contents, not by random chance. The outline should plant one retroactive clue in each of these that is re-cast when Tess learns the contents in ch 11.
- The discovery in ch 11 (what's inside) must include the revelation that Mara is one of the deponents — this is Tess's most personal surprise.
